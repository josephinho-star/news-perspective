Pelosi, Omar join Trump and Schiff as unlikely alliance pushes to onshore key industry

2026-09-04 17:35

EXCLUSIVE: President Donald Trump has gained several unlikely allies, including the man who led his first impeachment trial, in a major push to bring motion picture production back to California from other states.

Sen. Adam Schiff, D-Calif., who represented studio-heavy Burbank in Congress, was joined by Reps. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., and Nancy Pelosi, D-Calif., in echoing support for Trump’s federal tax incentive plan to draw major film projects back to where it began.

"We’ve been promoting that for a long time. I’m glad he finally arrived at it," Pelosi told Fox News Digital when asked about the plan on the Capitol grounds.

Schiff tweeted earlier this week that he is "in strong agreement with the president."

The Democrat, who has accused Trump of colluding with Moscow to win the election and whom the president has responded to by calling him "pencil-neck," said it is time for Republicans and Democrats to "work together… get this done and bring the movie magic back to America."

"Congress should immediately take up and pass a federal film tax incentive to bring back these good-paying jobs that we've lost to other countries."

Omar told Fox News Digital a similar refrain, adding that she has worked separately to attract film projects to Minneapolis.

She referenced "Fargo," the 1996 Coen Brothers comedy starring William H. Macy as an example of Minnesota’s ability to host large projects. The "Mighty Ducks" series starring Emilio Estevez and Joshua Jackson also filmed heavily in the Twin Cities during its 1990s run.

"When I was in the Minnesota House, I was very supportive of Minnesota having subsidies so we can have more movies being made inside Minnesota like the movie ‘Fargo’," she said.

She said the end of subsidies from St. Paul recently "made it less fun trying to run into people who were actually doing the production."

"It could be a really cool thing [and] also bring us money to states that can use it."

Trump appointed legendary actors Jon Voight, Mel Gibson and Sylvester Stallone special envoys to Hollywood. Gibson previously told Fox News that for a recent film, it was cheaper to fly the entire crew to Europe for three days than to shoot for one day in Los Angeles.

"There’s something really wrong there," he said, adding that another actor, ex-California Gov. Arnold Schwarzenegger, tried to assuage the problem while in office but was met with "obstacles."

Last year, another Trump foil also got on board with the idea of Washington bolstering Hollywood.

"California built the film industry – and we’re ready to bring even more jobs home… Now it’s time for a real federal partnership to Make America Film Again," said California Gov. Gavin Newsom.

Fox News Digital's Brie Stimson contributed to this report.
