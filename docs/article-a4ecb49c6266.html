Mayor-elect Nithya Raman spends Thanksgiving eve feeding the homeless on Skid Row

2026-11-26 00:46

More than 3,000 pounds of turkey and 600 pies were on the menu at the Los Angeles Mission’s annual Thanksgiving celebration, which was expected to feed approximately 3,500 people.

Dinner was served on a long stretch of Skid Row that was turned into an outdoor dining room.

Raman was joined by Los Angeles County Supervisor Lindsey Horvath, who endorsed the councilwoman in her successful campaign to unseat Mayor Karen Bass.

Hollywood also turned out for the occasion, with comedian Jeff Ross and actor Danny Trejo among the celebrities dishing out traditional Thanksgiving meals.

“Oh my God, it’s wonderful. Only LA Mission could throw a party like this. It’s incredible,” Ross told the California Post.

The comedian, best known as the “Roastmaster General” for his celebrity roasts, spent hours serving gravy while reflecting on those who find themselves alone during the holidays.

“I don’t think about it as families, I think about it as people, ’cause there’s a lot of strays, kinda like me,” Ross said.

Trejo, the Hollywood actor and restaurateur best known for his roles in “Machete” and “Heat,” stressed that helping struggling Angelenos should extend beyond the holidays.

“Being here today is a culmination of what we do all year,” Trejo, who also owns Trejo’s Tacos, told The Post.

“We’re all in a big boat, and if we’re not careful, we’re gonna sink, you know? So we all gotta work together.”

Other celebrities slated to volunteer included “Beverly Hills, 90210” star Jennie Garth, actor Harry Lennix and comedian Natasha Leggero.

The Thanksgiving spirit extended beyond Skid Row.

Real estate developer and former Los Angeles mayoral candidate Rick Caruso and his family joined LAPD Chief Jim McDonnell to distribute Thanksgiving meal supplies to approximately 350 families in South Los Angeles.

The Nov. 18 giveaway continued a longstanding tradition for the Caruso family, whose charitable foundation has partnered with organizations serving disadvantaged communities for more than three decades.

“As a family, we have loved being involved in Watts and supporting underserved communities throughout South Los Angeles for more than 30 years,” Caruso said in a statement to The Post.

“These are wonderful communities, and we have been fortunate to support them in many ways, including educational scholarships and health care grants.”

The giveaway provided families with everything they needed to prepare a traditional Thanksgiving feast at home.

“One of our most meaningful traditions is providing families with all the ingredients and supplies they need to prepare a complete Thanksgiving dinner in their own homes,” Caruso said.

“Thanksgiving is about bringing families together, and we are grateful to help make that possible.”
