WNBA interviewing commissioner candidates as Cathy Engelbert’s tenure winds down

2026-11-18 00:18

Outgoing WNBA Commissioner Cathy Engelbert presided over her final league Board of Governors meeting Monday in a Midtown boardroom as the search for her replacement ramps up.

Representatives from all 15 teams were in attendance. Several new faces were in the room, too, including representatives from the three expansion franchises — Cleveland, Detroit and Philadelphia — that will join the league over the next four years.

NBA Commissioner Adam Silver also stopped in for the final hour.

The group voted on some items from last week’s competition committee meeting, though Engelbert didn’t share any details. She said points of emphasis for officiating next season won’t be determined until April.

But the biggest question looming over this one-day summit is who will stand at the front of the room next year.

Engelbert, the league’s first commissioner who oversaw historic expansion and the WNBA’s jump to mainstream popularity, announced in September that she planned to retire at the end of the year.

The search for Engelbert’s replacement, led by Excel Search & Advisory, is in full swing, with more than a dozen candidates under consideration.

Alex Rodriguez, co-owner of the Minnesota Timberwolves and Lynx, said the Board of Governors discussed what traits they’d like to see in the next WNBA commissioner.

“Someone who has marketing experience, business, someone who’s a very good leader, someone who loves basketball and loves players,” the former Yankees star said. “As a former player, I always had a great relationship with [MLB Commissioner] Rob Manfred, and I still do, and I think that’s really important.”

Engelbert said they’re “well past” the exploratory stage of the search process and have started interviews.

“The nice thing is the league’s on the map, so we have a lot of interest from a lot of different candidates with broad capabilities and backgrounds,” Engelbert said. “I’m really excited for who’s going to take over next.”

Engelbert said she’ll join the interview process once the list is trimmed down. A committee of NBA and WNBA owners will also be part of the hiring process.

Silver declined to share who’s on the committee. Rodriguez confirmed he’s not, though.

“It’s just the beginning,” Silver said.

If Engelbert’s successor isn’t identified in the next 1 ½ months, Silver could tap Deputy Commissioner Mark Tatum to take over on an interim basis. Tatum previously served as the interim WNBA president between Lisa Borders’ exit in October 2018 and Engelbert’s hiring in July 2019.

Engelbert also believes the WNBA could explore adding its own Deputy Commissioner in the near future.

“That’s something, probably in the next couple years, would probably make sense,” she said.
