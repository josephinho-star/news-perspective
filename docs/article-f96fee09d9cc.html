WHO chief urges transparency amid Russia plague reports

2026-10-07 10:42

A razor wire sits on a fence of the anti-plague institute in the city of Irkutsk on October 7, 2026. (AFP Photo)

The World Health Organization (WHO) said on Oct. 7 that it wanted more information from Russia on the death of a plague laboratory technician, and on reports of “a second employee with pneumonia of undetermined cause.”

The WHO’s chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said full transparency was needed in order to make a proper assessment of the public health risk.

He reiterated that the WHO stood ready to help Moscow with technical support, if asked.

“WHO has been actively following up on the reported death of a laboratory worker at the Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute,” he said on X.

“The individual died on Oct. 2 after being hospitalized with severe pneumonia. Initial media reports suggested pneumonic plague following an alleged accidental laboratory exposure.”

On Oct. 3 the WHO contacted the Russian authorities to get more information, under the International Health Regulations (IHR).

The IHR provide a legally binding framework defining countries’ rights and obligations in handling public health events and emergencies that could cross borders, and countries are asked to respond within 24 hours.

Russia got back to the WHO on Oct. 6.

“WHO has requested further information to clarify the cause of the severe pneumonia, the pathogen that prompted the public health measures, and media reports of a second employee with pneumonia of undetermined cause,” said Tedros.

“Timely, complete and transparent information sharing under the International Health Regulations is essential to clarify conflicting reports and enable an accurate assessment of potential public health risks.”

The WHO’s initial risk assessment from the information available estimates the risk to be moderate to low for the Irkutsk region; low for Russia as a whole; and very low for Europe.
