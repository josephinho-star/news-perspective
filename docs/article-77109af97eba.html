Democrats nominate Hakeem Jeffries to be US Speaker of the House

2026-11-18 21:13

Democrats in the US House of Representatives officially nominated Hakeem Jeffries to be Speaker, setting him on the path to become the first black lawmaker to lead the chamber.

The House will elect its new Speaker on 3 January, when the next term begins and Democrats will be in the majority.

His election to the powerful position - the Speaker is in line to succeed the president after the vice-president - is widely expected. Should he win, Jeffries will preside over his party's largest House majority in years and benefit from the Senate also being under Democrats' control.

Jeffries told reporters after the vote that the nomination was "a high honour and a distinct privilege".

As of Wednesday, Democrats will hold at least 257 seats in the House next year, which is well beyond the 218 needed for a majority. Seven races from the recent midterm elections are still uncalled.

Jeffries, who represents a district in New York, was re-elected as the party leader in a 251-11 closed-door vote on Wednesday. He has served as minority leader since 2023.

The Congressional Black Caucus, currently made up of only Democratic House members, noted the potential history-making moment when Jeffries takes the helm of the House.

"The American people, largely driven by turnout among Black communities, particularly across the south, have trusted us to make Washington work for them," said the committee's chair, Yvette Clarke, in a statement. "Now, it is our charge and responsibility to do all we can to deliver on their faith."

Despite overwhelming support in Wednesday's vote , Jeffries faced a handful of holdouts from new members in the party's left wing, who have said the party's sweeping victory in the midterm elections is a sign voters want change.

"While we have seen meaningful progress, there is still work to be done to ensure that the direction our party takes in this coming Congress reflects what our constituents are demanding of our leadership," seven incoming lawmakers, including fellow New Yorkers Darializa Avila Chevalier and Claire Valdez, said in a statement.

All congressional Democrats are gearing up for clashes with the White House once they officially take control in January.

President Donald Trump, a Republican, has signalled a willingness to work with Democrats on some issues, but has also pledged to use his veto power to reject legislation he does not like.

Trump's opposition could make it hard for Democrats to deliver on their campaign promises to address affordability and pass other reforms.

At a news conference following the vote on Wednesday, Jeffries pledged that House Democrats would "fight to lower the high costs of groceries and gas and housing", and "make healthcare affordable for every single American".

Though the party will have a large enough majority to weather defections on must-pass legislation, Jeffries will still have to navigate internal ideological divisions that have grown particularly sharp over US support of Israel and Americans' rising healthcare costs.

Many incoming Democrats, including members of the Democratic Socialists of America, won their elections by challenging the party establishment.

The Congressional Progressive Caucus, from the party's left wing, congratulated Jeffries on the nomination, adding they would "continue conversations with Leader Jeffries to ensure Democrats deliver a bold agenda for working people".

Along with shepherding legislation, Jeffries as Speaker will also have to manage the complex array of investigations that Democrats plan to launch into Trump, his family, and cabinet members now that they have regained control of the chamber's oversight powers.

Rounding out their party leadership team, House Democrats also elected Katherine Clark of Massachusetts as majority leader, Pete Aguilar of California as majority whip, and Joe Neguse of Colorado as assistant speaker.
