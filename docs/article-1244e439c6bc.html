Bill Maher says late-night comedy is ‘dead’ as far-left comedians are too one-sided

2026-11-25 03:26

Lindsay Kornick, Fox News

Published Nov. 24, 2026, 10:26 p.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

HBO host and comedian Bill Maher told Piers Morgan Tuesday that late-night comedy was “dead” due to comedians focusing too much on one-sided political jokes.

Maher and Morgan discussed the state of late-night shows since Johnny Carson retired from “The Tonight Show” in 1992. Maher remarked that Carson likely retired at the right time before hosts and even entertainers became determined to force political messaging into their shows.

“Do you think that the likes of Jimmy Kimmel and the others, by being so obviously politically partisan, have they conspired to slightly kill late-night comedy for you?” Morgan asked Maher on “One on One with Piers Morgan.”

“Well, it definitely seems dead,” Maher said. “Yeah, so I don’t think that helped. I know they don’t like it when I say that, but it’s true.”

Maher remarked that he was always political on his programs but distanced himself from other late-night hosts since they had no interest in appealing to people outside their political circle.

“Now I’m like, ‘Oh, I’m glad I’m not lumped in’ because I’m not like them at all. I’m really not. And I’m not saying they’re not talented or funny. They are. But, yeah, they absolutely have no interest in playing to half the country,” Maher said.

Morgan pointed out that an overwhelming number of Kimmel’s late-night political guests were not Republicans and that hosts like Kimmel rarely joke about Democrats.

“It’s not like the left isn’t doing a lot of stuff. And, of course, I’ve lost fans on the far left, and I don’t miss them. You know what? Because if you don’t understand what’s wrong with your own side, you are watching the wrong show,” Maher said.

Fox News Digital reached out to ABC and Kimmel’s representative for comment.

Media Research Center President David Bozell agreed with Maher, citing his organization’s history of documenting liberal bias on late-night shows.

“For years, MRC has documented just how predictable late-night television has become. The same people are always the punchline, and the same people are almost never the punchline. Somewhere along the way, the networks confused applause from a few hundred people in a studio with entertainment for millions of Americans at home,” Bozell said.

Earlier this year, Maher suggested his friendship with Kimmel was on the rocks after he criticized Kimmel’s wife, Molly McNearney, for cutting off family relationships based on whether they voted for President Donald Trump.

“This is the difference between the right and the left. It bugs me so much. My tribe is supposed to be the left, but these are the people who just can’t talk to you unless you’re exactly there,” Maher said on his “Club Random” podcast.
