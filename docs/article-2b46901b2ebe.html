Obama, in Ohio, assails 'cruelty' and indecency from Trump and his allies in the midterm campaign

2026-10-24 21:51

Former President Barack Obama on Saturday seized on what he described as cruelty and indecency coming from President Donald Trump and his Republican allies, stepping into one of the nation's ugliest midterm fights as he worked to rally Democratic voters in battleground Ohio.

Obama specifically targeted Republican gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy, who has doubled down on an ad featuring the estranged mother of Democratic rival Dr. Amy Acton. Trump and Vice President JD Vance have also defended the ad, even as Republican leaders across Ohio, including Gov. Mike DeWine, have called for its removal.

"In recent days, we've seen there is no line he will not cross, no low he will not sink to," Obama said of Ramaswamy, noting that Acton has "spoken movingly about her painful childhood and all she's experienced: abuse, neglect and homelessness."

"There's a decency we should expect out of our public officials," Obama continued. "And I don't know what's happened where the standards of behavior are so low that somebody could run an ad like that."

Obama, 65, remains the Democratic Party's best motivator nearly a decade after leaving the White House. He was set to campaign in Michigan later Saturday night, lending the Democratic Party his star power in midwestern battlegrounds that will help determine the balance of power in American politics next week.

Obama shared the stage with Acton and former three-term Ohio Sen. Sherrod Brown, who is looking to unseat Republican Sen. Jon Husted.

Although Ohio has become more reliably Republican, it's been a hotly contested battleground this year. Trump and Vance have both visited the state in recent weeks to drum up support for Husted and Ramaswamy. And the controversy over Ramaswamy's ad has ratcheted up the already high temperature.

Ramaswamy has defended his decision to press ahead with the ad as fair game. Trump aggressively defended the ad, which has become a national flashpoint in the final days of the midterms, as he voted in West Palm Beach on Saturday.

"This is a disgrace," Trump said. "The mother said that Amy Acton is a liar and she's no good. And you know what? I don't think you can get a better reference on that." He added that if his own mother had said anything bad about him, it would be front and center.

The Ramaswamy campaign video features Acton's birth mother, Donna Arthur, calling her "a liar" and insinuating she has mental health issues.

Acton, Ohio's COVID-19-era health director, has made no secret of facing homelessness and abuse as a child. Arthur lost custody of her children after their stepfather, Arthur's husband, was indicted on a charge of rape involving Acton.

The abuse allegedly happened from December 1976 to April 1978. Cledith Arthur failed to appear in court and became a fugitive. Donna Arthur remained married to him until his death in 2004.

Appearing before Obama, Acton spoke as if addressing Ramaswamy directly.

"My opponent has chosen to exploit, intentionally, my childhood to save his stumbling campaign, but it's not going to work," she said. "Decency still matters." She added: "Mr. Ramaswamy, you are not just out of touch, you are out for yourself and you are a danger to my state. Apparently there is no low to which you won't stoop."

After wrapping up in Ohio, Obama headed to a rally in Detroit for Abdul El-Sayed, a former public health official working to unite the Democratic base in Michigan after winning a divisive primary by less than a percentage point. El-Sayed is running against former Rep. Mike Rogers, a Republican.

Obama's appearance in the city, which is three-quarters Black, could bolster El-Sayed's support among Black voters. In the primary, his opponent Haley Stevens won Wayne County, which includes Detroit, by about 6 percentage points. Former Vice President Kamala Harris, the first Black and South Asian vice president, campaigned for El-Sayed in September.

El-Sayed criticized Obama after his 2012 reelection for not following through on all of his progressive campaign promises, writing that Obama "was not the right choice — he just wasn't the wrong one."

El-Sayed told reporters earlier this month that he appreciated the former president, calling him "foundational" to his ability to run for office, but he still disagreed with elements of Obama's presidency.

Michigan also has open races for governor, attorney general and secretary of state. Jocelyn Benson, the Democratic nominee for governor, is slated to appear on the ballot alongside others. Benson is running against Republican Rep. John James.

Obama will headline a rally in Georgia next week with Sen. Jon Ossoff and gubernatorial candidate Keisha Lance Bottoms. Trump is slated to speak in Georgia Saturday.

In Ohio, Obama also criticized Trump for failing to lower costs, a key campaign promise, saying the president has instead focused on building a new White House ballroom and helipad on the South Lawn.

"I don't know if you've noticed, but he seems very focused on home renovation," he quipped. "He should be on HGTV." He went on: "There's gold everywhere. He's got the White House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory."

The former Democratic president acknowledged that the nation has Trump for another two years, until his term ends. But voters are not stuck with Trump's allies, he said.

"We're not stuck with a spineless Congress and Republican governors who are handling lawlessness and carelessness and cruelty and indifference for working people," Obama said. "After two years of chaos and cruelty and corruption and incompetence … you get a chance to say enough is enough."
