Nvidia says it’s policing itself on security, exports

2026-11-17 15:17

Nvidia’s board of directors announced Tuesday it established an oversight committee focused on national security and export controls following accusations that the company’s former CEO Jensen Huang had an extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

The National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee will be responsible for “board-level oversight of the company’s national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management,” the company said in a blog post.

Suzanne Nora Johnson, who currently serves on Nvidia’s board, was appointed chair of the new committee, which is expected to meet at least quarterly, the tech giant said.

The committee is expected to work with independent third-party experts on law, global trade compliance and national security, it added. The company’s national security and global trade compliance teams will report directly to the standing committee and interim CEO Colette Kress.

The announcement comes as the company faces mounting scrutiny over its chip export policies and global work after reports emerged this month alleging Huang had an extramarital affair with a woman whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect is a Chinese spy.

The woman, Alice Huang, is a former executive of the Singaporean data center and cloud computing firm Megaspeed, which is under investigation over allegations it smuggled Nvidia’s AI chips into China despite export control bans.

Amid the flurry of reports about the alleged affair, Huang stepped down as CEO of Nvidia last Thursday.

The New York Times reported President Trump was briefed on the U.S. intelligence community’s suspicions about the onetime Megaspeed executive a year ago and chose not to anything with the information. It is not clear when or what Trump or other administration officials knew about Alice Huang’s potential connection to Jensen Huang.
