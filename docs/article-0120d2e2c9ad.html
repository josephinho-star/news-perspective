New home sales surge to highest level in nearly a year despite high mortgage rates

2026-11-25 16:06

Published Nov. 25, 2026, 11:06 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Sales of new US single-family homes surged in October as builders cut prices to attract buyers, but rising mortgage rates remain a constraint to overall housing market activity.

New home sales jumped 14.2% to a seasonally adjusted annualized rate of 701,000 units last month, the highest level since December 2025, the Commerce Department’s Census Bureau said on Wednesday.

Data for September was revised sharply lower to show sales falling to a rate of 614,000 units instead of the previously reported 676,000 units.

Economists polled by Reuters had forecast home sales easing to a rate of 650,000 units. New homes sales account for a small share of the housing market and tend to be volatile month-to-month. Sales increased 7.5% on a year-over-year basis in October. Developers have been cutting prices to stimulate demand.

A National Association of Home Builders survey last week showed about 46% of builders reported cutting prices in November, the highest share in the post-pandemic period, up from about 43% in October.

The median new house price dropped 3.2% to $391,300 in October from a year earlier, the Census Bureau said. Most of the homes sold last month were priced below $399,999.

The US-Israeli war with Iran, which is fanning inflation and driving up borrowing costs, has worsened a housing market slump that started in mid-2024.

Economists expect the housing market downturn to persist in 2027, especially with the Federal Reserve forecast to continue raising interest rates after next month’s widely expected hike to curb inflation.

The average rate on a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage has surged by about 1.3 percentage points since the US and Israel attacked Iran at the end of February. It averaged 7.28% last week, data from mortgage finance firm Freddie Mac showed.

Despite the jump in sales last month, new housing inventory remained elevated and is likely to continue hampering builders’ ability to break ground on new single-family housing projects.

The supply of new houses on the market fell to 470,000 units from 477,000 units in September. At October’s sales pace, it would take 8.0 months to clear the inventory. That was the lowest level since December 2025, and was down from 9.3 months in September.

The government reported last week that permits for future construction of single-family homes dropped to their lowest level in more than 3-1/2 years in October. Though there is a glut of new houses on the market, there is a national housing shortage, especially starter homes.
