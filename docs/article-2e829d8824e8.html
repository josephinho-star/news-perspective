Trump says he will have spent $400m to support Republican candidates in midterms

Donald Trump said that he will have spent $400m supporting Republican candidates in midterm races by the time election day rolls around on 3 November.

The staggering amount will be spent through his political operation and aligned Super Pac, Maga Inc.

“More than 300 Million Dollars has already been spent on Advertising, Direct and Indirect Voter Contact, Rallies, Rally Arenas, Telerallies, Get Out the Vote Programs, Election Integrity, and more. It is my Great Honor to do so on behalf of the Republican Party, which has done so much to bring our Country back from the Edge of Oblivion,” he wrote on Truth Social.

He cited his belief in the Republican party, but also the riots that have swept through France, as his reasons behind the spend. A reminder, Trump has repeated baseless claims that the demonstrations, which have descended into chaos and seen thousands arrested, are the result of mass migration.

In the Oval Office, Trump announced an incentive program for people to report fraud, in honor of conservative YouTuber Nick Shirley – who, you may remember, posted a video last year alleging fraud by daycare centers servicing Minneapolis’s Somali American community.

The video, initially posted in December, resulted in a deluge of anti-immigrant and anti-Somali content across social media, including in Truth Social posts by Donald Trump.

In July, Fahima Egeh Mahamud, the CEO of the daycare center that appeared in Shirley’s video, pleaded guilty to wire fraud and conspiracy to defraud the US.

Under the administration’s proposed program, people who submit information through that results in a fraud conviction could receive up to $30,000.

In the Oval Office today, Trump presented Shirley, 24, with a check for $10,000 for his work.

Kansas has not sent a Democrat to the Senate since 1932 – does Adam Hamilton have the courage, brain and heart to cause an extraordinary upset?

Just two years after Donald Trump won Kansas by 16 points in his fight to regain the White House, Republicans in the state are scrambling to retain their dominance. The battle for one of its two seats in the US Senate is too close to call, setting the stage for a potentially extraordinary upset.

Some recent polls even put Democrat Adam Hamilton on course for a historic victory. Kansans have not sent a representative of the party to the US Senate since 1932.

On the campaign trail, Hamilton regularly points to one of his state’s most famous residents: Dorothy from Wizard of Oz. Recalling a visit he made to Liberal, Kansas, and its Oz museum, he asks the audience to recall what Dorothy’s companions on her journey need. The lion needed courage, the scarecrow a brain and the tin man a heart.

Washington needs more of all three, he says, and he can deliver it. He decided to run for office after seeing the Trump administration act in ways that were “not good or kind or compassionate or humble”, Hamilton – in the energetic cadence of the pastor he is – told a crowd of business leaders in Topeka on Friday. He was waiting for Roger Marshall, the state’s Republican incumbent senator, to stand up for his state and its people.

“I would have arguments with Roger Marshall in the shower. He was not in the shower with me,” Hamilton joked. “And I would just say, Roger, please stand up for your people. You were not hired to be a spokesperson for the White House. You were hired to fight for your people.”

Hamilton’s role as senior pastor of the country’s largest United Methodist congregation comes with name recognition and speaking skills. But Marshall’s liabilities as his opponent – namely, the fact he sued hundreds of his patients for unpaid bills, resulting in the arrests of dozens of them – have contributed greatly to Hamilton’s prospects of flipping the seat.

Electorally, he’s trying to win over independents and Republicans, calling himself an independent-minded Democrat. It would be valuable, he told the business leaders last week, for Kansas to have a Democrat like him and a Republican in Jerry Moran representing them in the US Senate – working both sides of the aisle.

Speaking to the New York Times on Tuesday, Bloomberg’s advisor Howard Wolfson said: “He believes in participation in our democracy. And obviously the scale of his participation, given his resources and his means, is greater than other people’s financially. But other people are participating in different ways.”

“He is of the view that resources matter in campaigns,” Wolfson said, adding that Bloomberg “expects a result.”

The outlet reports that the former New York City major, now 84-years old, is focused on securing a Democratic majority in the House of Representatives, with at least $50m of his doantion to be spent on House races.

In response to Bloomberg’s donation, House minority leader Hakeem Jeffries said: “Mayor Bloomberg played an incredibly instrumental role in the decisive midterm election in 2018. The extraordinary support from Mayor Bloomberg will once again help Democrats flip the House,” the outlet reports.

A new survey reveals a widening “empathy gap” between Republicans and white evangelicals and the rest of the American public.

On Tuesday, the nonpartisan Public Religion Research Institute released its annual American Values Survey. The survey found that white evangelical Protestants are the only religious group in which a majority does not agree that empathy is foundational moral value (42%). Instead, a majority (54%) say empathy can be dangerous when it leads people away from a society guided by God’s truth, PRRI said.

The survey also found that Republicans who view Trump favorably are divided on the role of empathy: 49% consider it a fundamental moral value, while 48% believe it can be dangerous. In contrast, 72% of Republicans who view Trump unfavorably see empathy as a core moral principle.

The vast majority of Democrats (84%) and independents (70%) agree that empathy helps build a healthy society, compared with 54% of Republicans, the survey also noted.

House armed services Democrat accuses Rubio and Trump of 'lying' about not restricting intelligence with Ukraine

Representative Eugene Vindman, the Democratic congressman from Virginia, has slammed Marco Rubio’s recent comments that the US is not witholding intelligence from Ukraine.

“He [Rubio] is lying and so is the president when they say that they are not restricting intelligence in violation of the law,” Vindman said in an interview with CNN. “There was a law that was passed in 2024 – the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) – that said that intelligence sharing must continue, or Congress needs to be notified. They’re both lying. It’s costing lives. This is gaslighting.”

Vindman, a former US army officer whose brother – Alexander – provided key testimony that led to Donald Trump’s first impeachment, added: “It’s desperate attempt to change a narrative two weeks before an election because the president knows that it was his policies – these terrible tariffs and this war in the Middle East that have caused gas prices to spike”.

When asked how he’s certain the administration is lying, Vindman confirmed that there have been investigations into Zelenskyy’s accusations that intelligence has been disrupted.

“They’ve conducted tests and studies that clearly demonstrate that,” he told CNN. “They state very clearly that they’re not getting the early warning on ballistic missile attacks in country. It’s coming intermittent. Sometimes they get warnings, sometimes they don’t. That was not the case prior to a few days ago.”

Zelenskyy says he had 'substantive' and 'constructive' conversation with Marco Rubio, as feud with Trump continues over Russia sanctions

Volodymyr Zelenskyy said that he had a “substantive conversation” with US secretary of state Marco Rubio.

“I am grateful for his readiness to help and his clear position,” the Ukrainian president said in a statement. “First, ballistic missile interceptors, which are needed to protect lives. Second, early warning of Russian ballistic missile attacks, which is vital. Third, the situation with Starlink/Starshield, which needs to be restored.”

This comes as Zelenskyy’s feud with Trump was reignited following the US president’s decision to waive sweeping sanctions and tariffs on Russia for 60 days. Both Zelenksyy and several allies have criticized the move as a boon, ultimately, for Moscow. Trump has also chided Zelenskyy for the continued attacks on Russian refineries – calling it the primary cause of the global energy crisis that actually reached fever pitch after the Iran war started in February.

Recently, Kyiv has also said that US intelligence has been disrupted, which officials say has affected Ukraine’s ability to defend itself against Russian attacks.

Rubio told reporters in India today that “there has not been some deliberate political directive to go out and harm Ukraine”.

“I saw somebody associated with the Ukrainian government say that people in Ukraine are dying because the U.S. is denying them information. That’s scandalous. That’s outrageous,” he added.

The secretary of state said there appeared to have been undetected airstrikes against Ukraine on 13 and 18 October. The US is reviewing those incidents, he said, but believes that, to the “extent that happened”, they were the result of a “technical matter”.

“It was not the result of any sort of U.S. directive not to provide advance warning of a strike, and we are providing the same warnings to them now that we always have,” Rubio said.

Trump announces relief for farmers impacted by soaring diesel prices, as Iran war continues and midterms loom

Donald Trump has announced $8bn worth of economic aid for farmers hit by soaring diesel prices, as polling shows Republicans on course for a midterms drubbing.

The president’s announcement came 15 days before a midterm election dominated by anger over rising fuel costs caused by the US war on Iran.

Farmers in Kansas, a heavily agricultural state, have been hit hard by the consequences of the Iran war. A gallon of diesel costs nearly $6, nearly double from this time a year ago.

In August, the president upset cattle ranchers with a plan to reduce import tariffs to lower the price of beef and with aggressive immigration raids that prompted some agricultural workers to stay at home.

Trump’s announcement came during a rally in Kansas on Monday night as Republicans faced mounting anger over the cost of living and the ongoing war in Iran. As my colleague David Smith wrote last night: “The fact that Donald Trump was here at all said more about a looming electoral disaster facing his Republican party next month than anything contained in his dark and discursive 98-minute stump speech.”

Trump seemed to acknowledge there was a risk Democrats could win in the state, where no Democrat has won a US Senate seat since 1932. As he stumped for first-term senator Roger Marshall, the president told the crowd: “You’re really sort of – I hate to say it – you’re sort of tending toward the purple,” the president told about 5,000 supporters in a multipurpose arena outside Wichita, acknowledging that Kansas already has a Democratic governor in Laura Kelly. “That’s why I have to be sure that we get this guy.”
