Inertia co-founder Jeff Lawson’s next big bet is fusion: Go inside it at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026

Jeff Lawson has already helped build a company with more than $4 billion in revenue and 300,000 customers worldwide. After scaling Twilio into a public company, his next challenge reaches well beyond software: bringing fusion power to the grid as co-founder and CEO of Inertia.

It’s a striking change in direction. It also raises a question worth exploring: How much of what makes someone a successful software founder carries over when the next business depends on lasers, manufacturing, and major engineering milestones?

A $450 million raise is only the beginning

In February, Inertia announced a $450 million Series A, led by Bessemer Venture Partners, with participation from GV and other investors. The funding supports the development of powerful laser systems and the mass manufacture of fusion fuel targets — key steps in the company’s plans to turn laboratory science into grid-scale energy.

The ambition is taking physical shape. In July, Inertia opened a 50,000-square-foot headquarters in Livermore, California, with facilities for developing fuel-target manufacturing processes and laser technologies. The company also announced that it had recruited leaders from businesses, including Apple, Corning, and Waymo, to help build its manufacturing and supply-chain capabilities.

What you’ll leave with when you lean in on this session at Disrupt

Lawson’s experience spans more than one successful technology business. Alongside co-founding Twilio, he worked in early product leadership at Amazon Web Services and was the founding CTO of StubHub. His career brings a perspective shaped by building products, teams, and companies across different markets.

At TechCrunch Disrupt 2026, the focus turns to how that experience informs Inertia’s approach to talent, timelines, fundraising, and difficult engineering questions. It’s a conversation about applying hard-earned experience without assuming that the methods that worked in software will translate unchanged to fusion energy.

Consider the leadership questions underneath that transition. How do you build the right team when the expertise required extends far beyond your own? How do you set credible milestones while keeping an ambitious mission moving forward?

For founders, those questions offer a useful way into the discussion.
