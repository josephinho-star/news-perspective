Still single? Science says this surprising reason might be why you’re not dating more

2026-11-24 19:28

If you’re single and ready to mingle — but can’t find someone to match that energy — a new study might shed some light on your situation.

Researchers evaluated self-reported data around the “Big Five” personality dimensions—openness, extraversion, neuroticism, conscientiousness and agreeableness — to find the specific traits that complicate romance and often prevent people from forming meaningful romantic attachments.

Participants responded to questionnaires from Professor Elyakim Kislev of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and his research team about their personality traits and relationship history.

From their answers, researchers found a clear link:

Higher levels of extraversion, conscientiousness, and agreeableness meant a higher chance of being in a relationship. Outgoing and socially engaged men, in particular, were far more likely to have romance in their life.

But then something interesting happened. Traits that people often say they look for in partners — specifically, high levels of curiosity, creativity and a drive for new experiences— correlated more with single people than those in a couple.

Researchers specifically found that individuals who scored high in “openness to new experiences” had significantly greater rates of being single, a correlation especially strong in women.

“Openness may reduce motivation to commit, conscientiousness may enhance maintenance-relevant skills, and the Dark Triad trait may support short-term attraction skills while undermining long-term motivation or skills,” the study explained.

Professor Elyakim Kislev of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and his team of researchers published the insights after looking at self-reported personality and romantic relationship status across several years.

They analyzed data from 14 waves of a large-scale longitudinal study in Germany.

Besides curiosity and openness, the team found a less surprising conclusion: Tendencies toward psychopathy and narcissism were closely tied to staying single.

“The impulsivity, low empathy, and lack of remorse that define psychopathy are precisely the qualities most damaging to long-term partnership formation and maintenance,” the researchers explained in their study, published in the Journal of Personality.

Despite a widespread romantic crisis, the study also found traits that support romantic success.

Individuals scoring higher in “Machiavellianism” — a strategic behavioral pattern where individuals rely on self-interest over emotion or moral absolutism — are less likely to be single, though that’s only true within short-term relationships.

That’s partly because Machiavellianism, narcissism and psychopathy are considered the ‘Dark Triad’ in personality studies — but those with Machiavellian traits are more adept at flattery, which makes dating (if not long-term partnership) easier.

But just because you’re single doesn’t mean you have a personality disorder — or that you’d be any happier with someone by your side.

Lead author Kislev said that people assume being single is a universal feeling and experience, but people handle it differently, and factors like societal and familial pressures are impactful.

“We need to consider how people experience singlehood and the expectations society places on them,” Kislev wrote. “The same trait fluctuation may carry different consequences for relationship status depending on the developmental phase in which it occurs.”

Translation: Even science agrees that while you might be single, you might also be much happier in your day-to-day life — and that doesn’t mean anything’s wrong with you at all.
