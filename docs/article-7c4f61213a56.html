COP31 talks head into overtime as countries split over fossil fuels

2026-11-20 15:12

The UN’s COP31 climate talks were set to run past Friday’s deadline as countries remained divided over a draft agreement that makes no mention of fossil fuels, with disputes over climate finance and the timing of UN scientific reports also standing in the way of a deal.

Negotiators from nearly 200 countries were still seeking a compromise after two weeks of talks in the Turkish Mediterranean city of Antalya. The meeting could stretch into the weekend.

“At this point, no one is happy,” Brazil’s national secretary for climate change, Aloisio Lopes Pereira de Melo, told reporters hours before the summit was due to end.

The talks follow a year of extreme weather that included heat waves in Europe and devastating floods in Nepal. A record-breaking El Niño, a periodic warming pattern, has pushed temperatures higher. Expectations of a breakthrough have been low as governments grapple with an oil price shock and tensions stemming from the war in Iran.

Dispute over electrification

An earlier draft called for accelerating the phaseout of fossil fuels. A shorter version released Thursday by Australia, which is leading the negotiations, dropped all references to oil, gas and coal, prompting several countries to demand that the language be restored.

The new draft calls for expanding the use of electricity, saying it could strengthen energy security and reduce greenhouse gas emissions. It reflects a push by Turkey, the summit host, to raise electricity’s share of global final energy demand to 35 percent by 2035. Some countries want the text to specify that the additional electricity should come from clean sources.

“Electrification is the key to transitioning away from fossil fuels,” Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen told reporters. He said he would keep working with delegations “for as long as it takes, to get the job done.”

At COP28 in Dubai in 2023, countries agreed to transition away from fossil fuels, with no targets or timetables. Saudi Arabia and other major oil producers have resisted reopening the issue.

A group of European and developing countries, including France, Panama and the Solomon Islands, released a letter calling for “a clear transition away from fossil fuels.”

Panama’s environment minister, Juan Carlos Navarro, said “the COP can still be saved” if the deal addresses the fossil fuel phaseout and defends climate science. He has threatened to block any agreement that omits a cap on oil production.

A European Union official, speaking on condition of anonymity, told Agence France-Presse that the latest draft was “not acceptable and not ambitious enough.”

“We don’t want this COP to become a ‘fossil’ COP,” the official said.

Some countries also objected to a proposal to expand carbon capture, utilization and storage. Critics say the technology allows polluters to keep burning fossil fuels.

“We’re quite disappointed by the text,” Vanuatu’s climate minister, Ralph Regenvanu, told AFP. “We want to see the references to false solutions removed.”

Climate science and funding

Countries remain divided over when the UN’s Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change should publish its next reports. The draft also expresses “deep concern” that global warming is on course to exceed the 1.5°C limit that countries agreed to pursue under the 2015 Paris Agreement.

Developing countries say wealthy nations are falling short of their obligations to help them respond to climate change.

“We didn’t come here to beg,” Ghana’s environment minister, Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings, said. “The [African] continent will not accept an outcome that offers declarations without the resources to deliver them.”
