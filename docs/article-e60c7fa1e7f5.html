At least 8 die, more missing, as ferry capsizes off northern Cyprus

2026-08-30 23:27

A passenger ferry capsized and sank off northern Cyprus on Sunday, killing at least eight people and leaving 18 missing, officials and media reports said, as police took the captain and seven crew members into custody.

Several rescued passengers told local media a blast had gone off at the bow of the ferry before it rolled over and said the boat had an insufficient supply of life vests.

The vessel capsized 6.5 kilometers (four miles) off the coast of Cyprus, shortly after leaving a northern Cyprus port bound for Turkey’s mainland.

Images broadcast by Turkish television showed the upside-down ferry in the water, with people wearing life vests atop its orange hull.

“Some people had to jump without lifebuoys or life jackets,” one young man, Efe Multici, told CNN Türk. “It was literally a fight for survival.”

Of the 267 people aboard — 259 passengers and eight crew members — 241 were rescued, according to the latest official figures. Search-and-rescue operations continued overnight with 13 vessels and two helicopters.

The official Cypriot news agency TAK, however, said 17 were missing.

“Efforts to find the missing are continuing tirelessly,” Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan said during a public ceremony. “We hope to receive positive news from these efforts.”

The Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (KKTC) is dependent on Turkey, and Turkish Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz said on X: “We are providing all necessary assistance to the search and rescue operations.”

Ünal Üstel, prime minister of the KKTC, said the ship’s captain and seven crew members had been detained.

The KKTC’s transport minister, Erhan Arıklı, said the ferry had “completely sunk to a depth of 514 meters (1,686 feet).” The vessel’s “black box” would help them understand what had caused the sinking, he added.

But he also referred to allegations that an object might have hit the vessel.

One rescued passenger who spoke of a blast on board told local KKTC television after he was taken back ashore: “The front part exploded, they tried to turn around. And the side windows blew out. The boat started taking on water.”

The man, aged in his 50s and not named, said he spent an hour and a half in the water before being rescued. He said many people had been inside the ferry at the moment it started taking on water.

“Those who could, got out,” he said, adding that he saw the captain among the first to abandon ship “without organizing anything.”

Another witness, in his thirties, corroborated reports of an explosion. He said he had reported water leaks to the captain.

“The seats started to float, the captain kept going, and the bow exploded,” he said.

Many boats, including private ones, were involved in the rescue operation. Turkey’s interior ministry said on X that six Turkish navy ships had also been dispatched.

“All necessary preparations are being made for a swift response from rescue and health services,” the KKTC’s health ministry said in a statement.

Hospitals in the territory had been alerted to provide any needed medical attention, it added.

The ferry had departed from the northern Cyprus port of Kyrenia, known as Girne in Turkish, heading to the Turkish port of Taşucu.

The KKTC, which is recognized only by Turkey, controls the northern third of the island of Cyprus, which has been divided since 1974.

Ankara occupied that part of the island in response to an Athens-engineered Greek Cypriot coup seeking union with Greece. The territory is served with regular air and sea links to Turkey.

Turkish President Erdoğan had been due to visit the KKTC Monday on an official visit. But that appeared to have been cancelled as his office announced on Sunday that he was due to attend a regional summit in Kyrgyzstan on Monday and Tuesday.
