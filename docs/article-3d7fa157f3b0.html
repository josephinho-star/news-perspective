Badenoch condemns Reform UK’s ‘authoritarian’ press bans – UK politics live

Badenoch condemns Reform UK's 'authoritarian' press bans, saying Farage shouldn't be MP if he can't handle scrutiny

Q: [From Emilio Casalicchio from Politico] Reform UK have banned seven reporters from their conference, including the one who revealed Nigel Farage got a £5m donation he did not declare. What do you think of that in terms of free speech? And would you do something like that?

Absolutely not … Conservatives believe in free speech, where people are welcome to criticise us as much as they like.

If you look at Nigel Farage’s attacks on the press, some of them very personal … you look at the things that Zia Yusuf says about journalists constantly, you know what kind of government they would be – authoritarian and using state power to pursue opponents. [That is] what they’ve effectively been threatening.

We do not do that. It’s absolutely not something that we believe in. We believe in free speech. We believe in a free press. And yes, of course, that might be painful for us as politicians, but public scrutiny is part of the gig.

If Nigel Farage can’t handle difficult questions about his dodgy £5m, then he shouldn’t be a politician.

But that comes with, working as an MP and standing to be leader of the country.

Badenoch has not always been comfortable with press scrutiny herself. As a minister in the last government, she was criticised for her aggressive response to journalists reporting on her. But as Conservative leader she has been more tolerant of criticism, and while the Tories don’t exactly welcome negative press coverage, they are more pro-media than Reform UK.

Former Scottish Labour leader Anas Sarwar takes seat in Lords

Anas Sarwar, the former Scottish Labour leader, has taken his seat in the unelected House of Lords.

Sarward was supported by two former Scottish Labour MPs, Helen Liddell and George Robertson, as he swore the oath of allegiance.

Speaking in the chamber in a debate later, in his maiden speech, he said:

I played my part in helping the Labour Party succeed at the last general election, but our new prime minister must succeed, this government must succeed, and most importantly of all, our country must succeed. I am determined to play my part in making that happen.

There is also, in my view, a bigger fight that I can’t walk away from, and I’m determined to play my part in.

That’s what I believe is the fight for the heart and soul of our nation – it’s about who we are as a people and who we are as a country, and for me, it actually goes beyond the usual mainstream political divide.

And that is: who are we as a country? Do we continue to look out for and love our neighbour, or do we treat them with suspicion?

Are we a country that succumbs to the politics of division and hate, or are we going to make sure that we have a country and a politics that believes in unity and hope?

Sarwar announced he was resigning as Scottish Labour leader and taking a peerage in July so that he could take a job in the UK government as a trade minister.

Jenrick claims Reform UK could cut UK government borrowing costs by almost £30bn by slashing spending

Robert Jenrick, the Reform UK Treasury spokesperson, is claiming that a Nigel Farage-led government could save the exchequer almost £30bn a year by cutting UK government borrowing rates. He says this is part of a Reform UK plan to cut spending by more than £100bn.

If the cost of servicing our debt was comparable to France, we would save £28bn a year [on interest repayments]. The message from the markets is very clear: we have got to cut spending and have a credible path to cutting taxes.

The Telegraph says that the interest rate on government borrowing hit a 30-year high of 5.91%, compared with 4.1% in France.

Jenrick said Reform UK would also cut £50bn from welare, and another £20-30bn from other departments. Combined with the savings on debt interest, this would amount to savings of more than £100bn, he said. And he claimed this could happen very quickly.

In his report of the interview, Gordon Rayner says:

[Jenrick] promises he would provide that clarity – and cut the cost of borrowing to French levels – in his first 100 days as chancellor.

He plans to reassure the markets, and the public, by setting out in detail how he would reduce welfare spending by £50bn and save another £20-30bn through a combination of cuts to net zero policies, axing quangos, reducing foreign aid and trimming the number of civil servants to pre-Covid levels.

Jenrick is speaking about these plans at an event this afternoon at the business day at the Reform conference.

In July the Disability News Service, a specialist website that reports on disability issues, said the Conservative party stopped sending it press releases, and even responding to its questions, after DNS published a series of factual stories that reflected badly on the party.

The Conservatives have made cutting benefits a priority, and these plans will particularly disadvantage some people who get disability payments.

Labour criticises Lee Anderson for 'corrupt' jibe about Ukraine

Labour has criticised the Reform UK Lee Anderson for saying that Ukraine is corrupt.

I think we should support Ukraine. But I’m very dubious where the money goes, it’s a corrupt country. I’m just very, very mindful of where the money goes.

[President] Zelenskyy, he’s always coming cap in hand. Don’t blame him. He’s the leader of a country. He’s got to do that.

It’s awful what the Ukrainian people have been through. Dreadful.

The Labour government’s support for Ukraine in the face of appalling Russian aggression is unwavering. Reform’s top team seem to spend more time undermining Britain’s support for Ukraine than calling out Putin’s despicable war.

Labour stands with President Zelenskyy and the Ukrainian people. The UK has proudly done so on a cross party basis since Putin’s full-scale invasion started. Lee Anderson should stop the side swipes at our allies and give Ukraine our full support to bring this war to an end.

Thornberry says she thinks taxes will need to rise in budget

Yesterday the Resolution Foundation published a report saying taxes would probably have to go up to pay for higher defence spending.

In an interview on the World at One, Emily Thornberry, the Labour chair of the Commons foreign affairs committee, said she thought “we’re going to need to raise taxes” in the budget because of the “huge strains on the economy”.

Asked if taxes would have to go up, she replied: “I think that in the end we’re going to need to do that.”

She also claimed that “people will understand”.

In its report, the Resolution Foundation backed higher taxes partly on the grounds that current tax rates for workers are, by international and historic standards, relatively low. It said:

The UK remains in the bottom third of [33 comparable OECD countries] for the level of tax on an average worker, and the effective tax rate on a typical earner is still lower than it was before the financial crisis. Indeed, no country raises more in total tax as a proportion of GDP without also asking more of single average earners …

Looking over a longer time horizon, as previous RF analysis has shown, personal taxes remain low by historical standards. Figure 3 [see below] shows effective tax rates in the UK, at multiples of the median salary for all employees, which stood at £33k in April 2025.

The overall effective tax rate for the median employee in 2025 was still lower than before the financial crisis, despite rising in recent years – including the impact of the increases in employer NICs. Those recent rises have been particularly rapid for lower-paid employees, as shown by those at half the median, where there has been the sharpest increase. Tax rates for higher earners, those at two or ten times the median, are somewhat higher than they were in the 2000s or 2010s, but still below levels seen historically.

Badenoch claims Tories don't give peerages to people based on donations

Q: Nigel Farage said in a BBC interview yesterday that he would not give peerages to people donating to Reform UK. Is that a policy that you will follow?

We don’t give people peerages based on donations, or government contracts. I am someone who believes in meritocracy.

We give people peerages because we think they’re going to do a good job.

You look at the peerages that I have given: Gen Sir Patrick Sanders, the former head of the British Army, he has joined our benches. We have people that are serious and that are credible joining conservative benches.

Who did Keir Starmer put in? That Matthew Doyle guy and another person whose credentials turned out to be out to be completely fake.

So I’m not worried about the people who I’m putting in the House of Lords.

As Badenoch delivered this answer, some journalists reacted with astonishment. While Badenoch has tended to favour her ideological allies when nominating people for the Lords, there is a very long history of major donors to the Conservative party getting peerages. Labour and the Liberal Democrats have also given plenty of peerages in the past to major donors.

Badenoch says UK must prepare for war if it wants to ensure peace

Back to what was said at Kemi Badenoch’s press conference.

Q: [From Jonathan Walker from the Express] Are you ready to lead the country into war if necessary?

If you want a peace, you have to prepare for war. And I believe that strong countries don’t get attacked. It’s countries that look weak that get attacked.

And I look at what Poland is doing. Why are they increasing their army to 300,000? Why are they withdrawing from all these treaties that would stop them from fighting a war?

I was in Rome last week speaking to Giorgia Meloni about defence. I spoke to Prime Minister Mitsotakis in Greece about defence. Everybody is worried.

So why are we pretending that nothing’s happening? That’s what should concern us, that we have a government that’s talking about things that are not to do with national security, just their pet projects and hobby horses.

Yes, we all want cheaper bus fares, but if anything happens to a Nato country, so much damage will be done we’re not going to be getting any growth.
