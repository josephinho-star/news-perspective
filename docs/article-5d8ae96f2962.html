JONATHAN TURLEY: Dem socialist rips ‘institutionalists’ as far left turns up heat on defenders of Constitution

2026-08-29 19:29

Pennsylvania state representative and democratic socialist Chris Rabb is the latest Democrat to reaffirm his alliance with virulent antisemite Hasan Piker. What is most notable, however, is his attack on establishment Democrats as "institutionalists," a new label reflecting the radical agenda for changing our constitutional system. It is a term that is now in vogue on the far left by those who want to tear down our institutions and scrap our Constitution.

Across the country, attacks are growing on Democrats and independents who are viewed as insufficiently radical. Those of us who continue to celebrate the Madisonian democracy as creating the most successful and stable republic in history are now "cultists" and "institutionalists" who are trapped in "nostalgia."

The Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) have drawn support from many who want a literal revolution. DSA Steering Committee member Cliff Connolly declared on one podcast that they are famous for their "hatred of the United States Constitution."

Even those who are not joining the DSA are denouncing the system and lashing out at those who want to preserve it.

At The Atlantic, Sarada Peri, the former senior speechwriter for President Barack Obama, referred to institutionalists as "peculiar creatures" inhabiting Washington.

On the web, radicals denounce institutionalists who "want us to defend and support the institutions we already have."

She added that: "Many institutionalists simply have a reflexive distaste for change, preferring the enduring, if ossified, conventions that offer them inherited credibility. But perhaps in part because they are mostly old, white men, institutionalists are afforded significant deference by the media and the general public."

Rabb joined his chorus of radicals opposing institutionalists in their party. He is the Democratic nominee for Pennsylvania’s 3rd Congressional District and is now virtually assured of being a member of Congress. He is also emerging as a dominant voice on the far left.

He has established his bona fides with a growing radical movement within the Democratic Party, denouncing the Declaration of Independence, calling our constitutional system "the beast" to be slayed, and bizarrely claiming that the Framers had no notion of what "policing" means.

On Friday, Rabb tweeted "This is what the institutionalists are focusing their efforts on now: Disavowing Hasan Piker."

He added: "Not White Christian nationalists? Not the broligarchs? Not the AI overlords? Nope, the guy encouraging young people to use their collective political power for good. F--- that noise."

Of course, Piker rose to national prominence after other Democratic Socialists and radicals brought him to their states to campaign against establishment figures.

Much like the left's focus on figures who associate with Nicholas Fuentes, Piker has outraged many with claims that America deserved 9/11, American Jews are asking to be attacked by supporting Israel, and mocking the murder of figures like Charlie Kirk.

Putting aside the fact that Rabb still supports this notorious hatemonger as someone organizing "young people to use their collective political power for good," he notably denounces those opposing Piker as "institutionalists."

It is an important reveal, highlighting the radical agenda to trash the Constitution and tear down our core institutions, including the Supreme Court. Rabb has been one of those voices in calling the system "the beast" and denouncing our own revolution.

In my book Rage and the Republic: The Unfinished Story of the American Revolution, I discuss the French Revolution and its decline into what the Framers called a mobocracy.

In France, "The Terror" began not with the working class, but with educated lawyers, journalists, and even aristocrats as part of the Jacobin movement. Eventually, after the mob had finished off the aristocrats, clergy, and merchants, they turned on their fellow Jacobins in a bloodletting that claimed the lives of thousands.

Even law professors and deans joined this movement in calling for the rejection of our Constitution on the 25th anniversary of our revolution.

Mainstream figures such as Erwin Chemerinsky, dean of UC Berkeley Law School, have published books such as "No Democracy Lasts Forever: How the Constitution Threatens the United States."

The media eagerly publishes these radical views, such as the New York Times column, "The Constitution Is Broken and Should Not Be Reclaimed," in which Ryan D. Doerfler of Harvard and Samuel Moyn of Yale call for the nation to "reclaim America from constitutionalism."

Brown University’s Corey Brettschneider called the Constitution a "dangerous document" that is driving this "threat to democracy."

George Washington law professor Mary Anne Franks condemned the "cult of the Constitution" that has been defended to advance "White male supremacy."

Now, to support our constitutional system is to be an "institutionalist," a pejorative term that is as bad as being an "oligarch" in the new revolutionary lexicon.

Even establishment candidates like former Vice President Kamala Harris and California Gov. Gavin Newsom are trying to pander to the mob by calling fealty to our traditions and institutions mere "nostalgia."

It is a familiar strategy. After Yuri Bezmenov, a KGB agent who defected in 1970, revealed the four stages by which the Soviets hoped to bring about revolutionary change in the U.S. It began with undermining our institutions and values, with the help of journalists and academics.

The success of this counter-constitutional movement is reflected in the growing list of establishment figures like Kamala Harris and Pete Buttigieg throwing the Supreme Court to the mob. The problem with leading the mob is that you have to stay in front of it or be devoured by it. Packing the court is unlikely to be enough. After they come for the institutionalists, they will come for the incrementalists.

It appears that, if you are not campaigning with Piker, you could be next for the pike. El-Sayed declared in his campaign that moderates like Democratic Sen. John Fetterman should have their heads cut off and put on pikes like ogres.

So much for the "Big Tent party." For institutionalists, the message is clear. Lose your constitutional "nostalgia" or your political heads.
