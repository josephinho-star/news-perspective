What is wrong with the Chargers' offense this season? Assessing five major factors

2026-11-16 20:45

Could it get any worse than this for the Chargers? After nine weeks, they are the only winless team in the NFL. They're the first team in NFL history to start 0-8 after winning 11-plus games in the previous season, per ESPN Research. And they haven't won a game since Week 16 of last season -- 331 days ago.

While last week's 44-22 loss to Houston featured plenty of poor defensive play, new coordinator Mike McDaniel's offense entered Week 10 at 30th or worse in an alarming number of stats this season: points, touchdowns, turnovers, efficiency, Total QBR, pass block win rate, red zone drives and fourth-down conversion percentage. The Chargers went 37 straight drives without scoring a touchdown, a streak they finally broke in the fourth quarter against the Texans.

So how did we get here after all the hype surrounding McDaniel, quarterback Justin Herbert and the rest of the Chargers' offense over the offseason? Ahead of their "Monday Night Football" matchup against the Ravens (8:15 p.m. ET, ESPN), I looked at the tape for some answers. Here are five reasons why Los Angeles' offense is off to such a horrific start.

Injuries and subpar play on the offensive line

Yes, injuries have played a role here. Left tackle Rashawn Slater has been out since Week 4 with an ankle injury, right tackle Joe Alt missed two games with a neck injury, and center Tyler Biadasz went down with an ACL injury during camp after signing a three-year deal.

But the interior of the front wasn't corrected over the offseason, as Cole Strange and Logan Taylor both rank in the bottom half of all qualifying guards in run block win rate. And overall, the Chargers currently own a 37.7% pass block win rate, the third lowest in the league.

Herbert must also take accountability in the pocket, but he has been sacked 31 times this season (second most in the league behind Cleveland's Deshaun Watson). Has the scheme clicked under McDaniel? No, and more on that later. But there is a general lack of execution on tape from the offensive line, which is devoid of quality talent in key spots.

The turnovers in the passing game

Herbert's 11 interceptions, tied for the most in the league, show a regression in field vision. He has failed to identify second-level defenders in basic zone schemes. We're talking hook-to-curl drops and flat defenders, which has led to him to throw 10 interceptions and post a 17 QBR versus zone this season.

Herbert has also forced the ball into too many contested windows while taking unnecessary risks down the field. I see too many examples of Herbert playing hero ball, relying on his physical tools instead of taking the available throw. And his 19 QBR on pocket throws ranks dead last in the league.

A lack of playmakers on the perimeter

Ladd McConkey's availability is a major issue for this team. He has missed time in 2026 with rib, foot and back injuries, as well as a concussion in Week 6. He has played just one full game, recording 17 receptions for 286 yards in total.

Besides McConkey, the Chargers don't have a receiver who alters defensive game plans. Quentin Johnston is an inconsistent straight-line speed target, while Tre' Harris would grade out as a No. 3 or No. 4 for most teams.

I don't see the Chargers' wide receivers creating separation at a consistent rate versus man coverage, plus opposing defenses are playing more zone (66.2% of Herbert's dropbacks) to cap vertical routes and force Herbert to read it out with late rotation in the secondary. He has completed only eight passes of 20 or more air yards in 2026. This isn't an offense that can create explosive plays in the pass game.

Limited run game production

This summer, I was all-in on Omarion Hampton and the Chargers' run game under McDaniel. The use of 21 personnel (two RBs, one TE, two WRs) with fullback Alec Ingold and more motion/movement made perfect sense on paper. They had the tools to create advantages with numbers and blocking angles.

However, Ingold was put on IR ahead of Week 6 with a shoulder injury. And Hampton is now part of a backfield rotation featuring Keaton Mitchell and Kimani Vidal.

The 2025 first-round pick is a volume runner who wins with short-area burst and contact balance when he can get on a downhill path. However, Hampton is averaging less than 4.0 yards per carry on outside and inside zone plays, which are core concepts in McDaniel's scheme. Based on the tape, Hampton isn't a great fit as a zone runner, as he lacks high-end vision as a ball carrier. And he doesn't display the one-cut ability to dart through creases of daylight.

As a team, the Chargers are also averaging 1.9 yards before first contact, fifth fewest in the league. That puts L.A. behind the sticks, impacts McDaniel's ability to influence second-level defenders with play-action, and sets up more third-down throws for Herbert.

The disconnect with OC Mike McDaniel's offense

Coaching does matter in this league. And I don't see the fit right now with McDaniel and Herbert.

This system is built on timing and ball location. Yet, Herbert is completing 61.3% of his throws, and I don't see a rhythm-based pass game on tape. McDaniel has put Herbert in a position to use play-action (55 attempts), but he has an 11.6 QBR on those throws (31st in the league).

What would I like to see? More boot off outside zone play-action. Let's move the pocket at a higher rate, as Herbert has just 18 attempts on designed rollouts in 2026. McDaniel could also use more schemed concepts that create leveled reads, especially on throws outside of the numbers. And the three-step passing game can improve with more defined reads for Herbert, using different formations and movement to clear the pre-snap picture.

But until we see more consistent play from Herbert, and improvements up front, this system will continue to produce poor results.
