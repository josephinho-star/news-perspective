Climber dies in fatal fall on Colorado 14er south of Aspen

2026-09-08 01:24

Officials in Colorado said a climber suffered a fatal fall Sunday after scaling a towering peak south of Aspen.

The tragic incident occurred on Castle Peak, which reaches a steep elevation of 14,272 feet in the Elk Mountains, according to Mountain Rescue Aspen.

Responders said rescue team members reached the climber by 4 p.m. after the Gunnison County Sheriff's Office was notified of a fallen climber.

However, officials "determined the individual had sustained injuries that were not survivable."

The fall was apparently witnessed by nearby hikers, including Rebecca Rees, who recounted the incident in a statement posted to Facebook.

"Unfortunately, we witnessed the event. It has been an extremely sad and terrible afternoon for all involved. He did not survive," she said.

Rees said a bystander, identified as Robert, even scrambled toward the climber immediately after the fall, putting himself at risk in case there was still a chance of survival.

She added that the victim ultimately "wasn’t alone" in his final moments.

Rees said the rescue crew responded at a commendable speed. Despite the remote location, helicopter and ground teams reportedly reached nearly 13,000 feet in under two hours on a holiday weekend.

According to Mountain Rescue Aspen, officials used off-highway vehicles and helicopters to reach the remote area.

After a helicopter located the fallen climber, it returned to Aspen to pick up additional rescue personnel before taking them to the high-elevation area near the peak.

A total of 21 rescue members were sent into the field, and officials safely recovered and evacuated the deceased climber from the mountain shortly after 8 p.m.

Rees said the responders showed extraordinary compassion throughout the recovery effort.

"The response of search and rescue, the sheriff, and the coroner all made us feel like every single person matters. This young man had a story and we all care," she said.
