US adds Megaspeed to trade blacklist as Nvidia’s Huang returns as CEO

2026-11-24 00:52

The United States has added the Singapore-based company at the centre of an alleged spying scandal involving Nvidia to its trade blacklist.

The Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) placed Megaspeed International and six other firms in Southeast Asia on its Entity List on Monday, hours after Jensen Huang returned to Nvidia as CEO in a stunning comeback that sent tech stocks soaring.

Megaspeed and the other entities were determined to be “acting contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States”, BIS, which sits under the US Department of Commerce, said in a filing to the Federal Register.

The designations mean that anyone seeking to ship the entities advanced US technology, including Nvidia’s top-end graphics processing units (GPUs) used in AI, must obtain an export licence.

Megaspeed’s blacklisting comes as divisions in US President Donald Trump’s administration over China’s access to Nvidia chips have been laid bare in recent weeks amid claims Huang was romantically involved with a woman accused of working with Chinese intelligence.

While US officials have been scrutinising Megaspeed’s compliance with export controls for years, US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s department had declined to place the company on its trade blacklist, prompting recriminations from administration officials known to hold more hawkish views on China, including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

Trump felt ‘very strongly’

Trump on Monday played down the national security concerns around Nvidia as he confirmed that he had personally intervened with the company’s board of directors to push for Huang’s reinstatement.

Trump, who has cultivated a close relationship with the top Nvidia executive during his second term, told CNBC that he felt “very strongly” about Huang’s role at the chip giant and that he offered the board assurances about risks to national security.

Shaoyu Yuan, a visiting assistant professor at New York University’s Center for Global Affairs who specialises in security issues, said Trump’s intervention was the latest example of the president making policy based on personal rapport rather than a “consistent security standard”.

“The incoherence is in the wider policy. Washington blacklists a middleman for moving Nvidia chips to China while it licenses Nvidia to sell H200 chips to China directly and takes a 25 percent cut,” Yuan told Al Jazeera, referring to the Trump administration’s announcement last year that it would allow the sale of some Nvidia GPUs to China in exchange for the company handing over a portion of the revenues to the government.

“Treasury Secretary Bessent has said other departments wanted this listing for months, and Commerce acted only after the scandal became public.”

US officials have accused Megaspeed International’s former top executive Alice Huang of working with China’s Ministry of State Security in an intelligence-gathering operation targeting Huang.

Beijing has rejected the claims as “groundless speculation” and “an attempt to smear and malign China”. Alice Huang has not publicly commented on the claims, which are based on US intelligence first reported by The New York Times and the Financial Times.

Nvidia shares skyrocketed 31.5 percent following the news of Huang’s reinstatement, helping the US stock market achieve its strongest single-day performance in more than 18 months.

The benchmark S&P 500 closed up 4.7 percent, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite jumped 6.51 percent.

The rally continued in Asia on Tuesday, with key indexes in Japan, South Korea and Taiwan roaring back after a punishing sell-off the previous day.

Taiwan’s Taiex led the gains, surging 9.1 percent as of 04:15 GMT.

Japan’s Nikkei 225 was up 4 percent, while South Korea’s Kospi was 7 percent higher.

Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Index was 0.4 percent in the red.

Concerns ‘serious, unresolved’

Apart from Megaspeed International, the expanded blacklist announced on Monday identifies three firms said to be based in Malaysia, two in Singapore and one in Thailand.

BIS named the entities as MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International, Speedmatrix, Apex Enterprise Solutions, Swiftdata and Megaspeed (Thailand).

Malaysia’s Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry said in a statement that, based on official records, PT Megaspeed International is not a Malaysian entity.

The ministry said authorities would inform all data centre operators in Malaysia of the expanded blacklist and advise them against hosting or providing services to the designated firms.

“Malaysia remains committed to a secure, transparent and rules-based trading environment and will not allow itself to be used as a channel to circumvent export controls. Action will be taken against parties found to have contravened any laws,” the ministry said.

Singapore’s Ministry of Digital Development and Information and Thailand’s Ministry of Industry did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Speaking during a visit to Australia on Tuesday, Singaporean Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said the city-state did not want to be “used and abused” by companies seeking to circumvent US export controls.

Megaspeed International, which is under investigation by the US Department of Justice on suspicion of diverting high-end chips to China in violation of export controls, did not respond to a request for comment.

The Singapore-based data centre company has previously insisted that it is a “legitimate and lawful business” and that media reports about its operations contained claims that were “not supported by verifiable evidence”.

Shiva Rajgopal, a professor of accounting at Columbia Business School, said it was “extraordinary” that the Trump administration had decided to blacklist Megaspeed while also pushing for Huang’s reinstatement.

“The board reinstated its CEO before the investigations into Megaspeed and his ties to it have concluded. Today’s listing shows those concerns are serious and unresolved,” Rajgopal told Al Jazeera.

“For the chip industry, the lesson is that knowing your customer is now a board-level issue,” Rajgopal said.
