Reform suspends two officials over donations sting as Farage admits their comments ‘don’t look good’ – UK politics live

2026-09-04 14:43

Here are some more lines from Nigel Farage’s interview with LBC this morning where he gave his response to the undercover report about an apparent Reform UK plot to evade election donations rules. (See 9.07am.)

Farage insisted Reform UK had not broken any laws – but he admitted aides had said things they “perhaps should not have been said” and conceded he was “not happy” about this. He said:

The party has broken no laws. The party has not taken any dodgy money or anything like that whatsoever.

However, it is a clear case of entrapment, and they worked at it for a very, very long time, and they got a couple of our contractors to say things that perhaps should not have been said.

And as a result of that, you know, they have been removed this morning. And yeah, you know, I’m not happy about it.

He rejected the claim that the footage of him discussing a £500,000 donation with fake father-and-son donors (see 9am) showed that he was aware of a plan to circumvent electoral law. He said:

If you listen to what I said, I said, is this all above board? Is this all legal? I’ve been told it was a global family office based in London. And by the way, I was in the middle of a Clacton by-election. I turned up to see people. I knew nothing about them whatsoever. I meet people all the time.

He claimed the three polls featured in the investigation were not commissioned by Reform UK. He said:

Whilst the poll benefited Reform, it wasn’t commissioned by Reform.

It had nothing to do with Reform. It was an independent third party that did it. So that’s nothing. That’s nothing. Polls are funded by overseas people every single week. Every single week.

In fact, the report contains footage clearly suggesting the polls were carried out at at the behest of Reform UK.

He suggested Labour and the Tories had a worse record on donations because they in effect sold peerages. He said:

The Labour Party and the Conservative Party have been selling peerages for years in return for political donations. Political donations leading to big government contracts.

In the world of political funding, all sorts of things have been done over decades that are wrong.

He said the party had suspended two aides because their remarks “don’t look good”. He said:

I promise you, the polling is not even an issue. Nothing. The party has not done anything wrong, but entrapped conversations don’t look good, and that’s why we’ve taken the action we have.

The British Polling Council (BPC) said “it is clear there have been breaches” of rules governing the industry after JL Partners reportedly did not disclose that Reform UK commissioned polling it had carried out.

Undercover footage recorded by Verbatim and aired by Channel 4 appeared to show JL Partners’ James Johnson admitting to “informal rule bending” by keeping the party’s involvement hidden.

The BPC, an association of polling organisations, said on its website: “Rule 2.1 states that all data and research findings made on the basis of social or political polls conducted in the United Kingdom by member organisations that enter the public domain, must include reference to the client commissioning the survey.

“With respect to the Channel 4 News investigation of polling apparently commissioned by Reform and conducted by JL Partners, it is clear there have been breaches of British Polling Council Rule 2.1.

“According to the programme, this may have been deliberate. We are seeking further information from the BPC member concerned and expect them to make information available after further consideration.

“The Market Research Society has a wider remit than the British Polling Council and we note that the matter is being referred to the Market Research Standards Board. We will await the outcome of that inquiry.”

Farage says he is disappointed to lose his former long-term adviser Dan Jukes amid allegations about Reform UK’s finances.

Responding to a question, he says:

I don’t like it at all. I’ve worked with Dan very closely for many years, many, many years, and I described it as loose pub talk, and that’s what it was, because what he said simply wasn’t true.

He got put into a position and he should never have said what he did. I believe the investigation will find he should never have said one or two of the things that he said.

Farage has indicated that the body of the first president of France, Napoleon III, could be repatriated to France if he becomes prime minister.

Napoleon III, who was president between 1848 and 1852 and then emperor until 1870, was exiled in the United Kingdom after defeat in the Franco-Prussian war.

He died in 1873 and is buried in St Michael’s Abbey in Farnborough.

Speaking at a press conference alongside French politician and the National Rally president Jordan Bardella, Farage hinted there could be legal difficulties, but added: “If they want him, I think they can have him.”

Farage said he is “annoyed” undercover reporters were able to meet him and said Reform UK needs to professionalise after the Channel 4 story.

Speaking at a press conference, Farage said: “We have not taken an illegal penny in funding, that is important. The loose pub talk, frankly, there was a bit of boasting, a bit of jack-the-lad-dery going on there.

“If it was more serious than that, then I refer you back to the BBC’s question earlier, the names would have been put on to the programme and there would be a hell of a row. They don’t exist. Those donations don’t exist.”

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage is giving a press conference with French National Rally leader Jordan Bardella.

Asked by the BBC’s Chris Mason about the undercover report into an apparent Reform UK plot to evade election donations rules, Farage reads a pre-prepared statement that he says “should cover it for everyone”.

What is more problematic is loose pub talk, including a couple of boasts that simply were not true.

So in the video, when he says we’ve done it with X, we’ve done it with Y – well, the reason Channel 4 haven’t broadcast the names of X and Y is because it isn’t true.

Donations from the names that were mentioned had not occurred, don’t exist on the old comms site, don’t exist in our accounts.

And while I’m aware that our people were pushed and pushed and pushed into saying these things, the comments were injudicious at best.

Neither of these men was authorised to act in that way. Accordingly, they’ve stepped down pending an investigation.

Am I annoyed that these people even got to meet me? Yes I am, it should not have happened.

The two individuals involved are not even part of our fundraising team, so they should have gone through the appropriate channels.

Checks would have been made and they would never, ever have got to sit down at a table with me.

Alleged plotting by senior Reform UK aides for a US donor to fund £32,500 polling commissioned by Reform UK could amount to a criminal offence, a legal expert has suggested.

Legal commentator Joshua Rozenberg said political parties are prohibited from obtaining donations from foreign donors and must declare donations above a threshold of just over £11,000, as well as any impermissible donations such as those not from a British individual or company.

He told the BBC’s World At One:

Backing all of that is a criminal offence in the legislation that set up the Electoral Commission. That’s the Political Parties Elections and Referendums Act 2000.

And what that says is that a person commits an offence if he knowingly enters into any arrangement which is likely to facilitate, by means of concealment or disguise, the making of donations to a party by anybody other than a permissible donor.

So, in other words, if you do something to enable your party to get money from somebody who is not permitted to pay that to your party, and you do that knowingly, or if you give information to the treasurer, then you are committing a criminal offence.

He also said payment for a political opinion poll for the benefit of a political party “would be arguably a donation in kind” and would need to be registered under electoral law.

Reform UK are pushing a press release out with details of the agreement signed on stage just now, which the party claims contains a commitment that “a National Rally-led French government would accept the return of illegal migrants which have launched from French shores, entered British territorial waters and were returned to France.”

It quotes Nigel Farage saying “This is a momentous agreement and shows that both Reform UK and the National Rally are serious about stopping the illegal migrant invasion, fixing our borders, and putting our own people first.”

Farage and Bardella sign 'Entente Cordiale' agreement over small boats

Nigel Farage says Reform UK and Jordan Bardella’s National Rally are to begin a new “Entente Cordiale”. The pair of them are signing a “memorandum of understanding” to end small boats crossing the Channel. Farage says he hopes the two parties will be the next two governments, and the agreement means France will allow the UK to return people, and that something will actually come of it.

Farage describes it as “a historic document that will stop the boats for ever”. Bardella says it is “the beginning of the cooperation.”

Earlier Farage criticised the announcement from Andy Burnham and Emmanuel Macron that extra police would be deployed on beaches to deter small boat crossings, suggesting that 45,000 extra officers would be needed.

France’s next election is in 2027. The Entente Cordiale, famously, was part of the build up to the first world war.

Jordan Bardella has won bouts of warm applause during parts of his speech, notably when talking about flying the British flag high as patriots, and saying that Reform have changed the political debate in the country and will soon “change the face of the United Kingdom”

He finishes “Long live Reform, long live Nigel, long live the United Kingdom, and vive la France!”

He has then invited Nigel Farage on stage, who opens with “Jordan Bardella. And in English! How about that!”

While Jordan Bardella is getting wild applause for promising that if he and his party got into power France would no longer be a route for migrants to reach the UK, my colleague Peter Walker is at a fringe event which feature Nadine Dorries. He has posted to social media some updates, saying:

Nadine Dorries, says the Channel 4 story was “a sting”, and starts talking about how “the establishment” want Farage and Reform to fail. She seems to be hinting there a conspiracy to bring him down. Dorries when challenged says [Dan] Jukes and [James] Orr might not have known the rules and that others in the party would have checked and turned down the money.

Dorries is challenged again and says Dan Jukes is someone who just carried Farage’s bags and “organised the car”. When reminded that Jukes described himself as the future No 10 chief of staff Dorries says: “I think that was Dan getting carried away.”

There is laughter in the room as Jordan Bardella describes the historical relationship between France and its neighbour as “turbulent”, which is somewhat the understatement. He describes, however, “a true friendship” between the two peoples at the most decisive moment of history, which draws warm applause.

Jordan Bardella is up next at the Reform UK conference. He has been the president of the far-right the National Rally (RN) since 2022. He has started trying to win the room over with a tribute to Ann Widdecombe. He describes the audience as “the beating heart of the British people. A people of patriots. A people who love freedom. A people proud of their history. And a people who have decided to take their destiny back into their own hands.”

“What a victory it was,” he says of the Clacton byelection. “The establishment had more or less lined up behind a candidate with a bin on his head”. He suggests Nigel Farage took out the rubbish. The audience has maybe not warmed quite so much to this bit.

Zia Yusuf has gone a little bit Trump-ist here, promising “more change in 100 days than from any previous government in British history”, and everything he is promising is going to be the biggest in history. He is promising to deport every foreign national without a right to stay, every foreign national in prison, and end early prison releases. “If their home countries refuse to take them we will send them to serve their sentences in countries like El Salvador,” he says.

“We will rebuild our economy, re-arm our military, and ensure that Britain can compete in this era of hard power” he says. He says the fate of the country is down to Reform UK.

At the Reform UK conference, Zia Yusuf has attacked Kemi Badenoch as a “con artist”, and also repeated his attack on former defence secretary Ben Wallace over the resettlement of Afghan nationals to the UK. Yusuf previously described Wallace as a “traitor”, and said a Reform UK government would hold an inquiry into the resettlement scheme. He has described the Times and the Telegraph as “client Tory media”. He has then called for applause for the late Ann Widdecombe, who was killed in her home in July.
