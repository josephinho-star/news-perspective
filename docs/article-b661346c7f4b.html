Boeing nears deal on Turkish Airlines’ 150 MAX jet order: report

2026-09-16 12:31

Boeing is close to finalizing a delayed Turkish Airlines (THY) order for 150 737 MAX jets after the carrier threatened to switch to Airbus over engine prices and maintenance terms, Reuters reported Tuesday, citing four people familiar with the talks.

The agreement is back on track and could be signed next week, three of the sources told Reuters.

Boeing and engine maker CFM International declined to comment, while Turkish Airlines did not respond to the news agency’s request for comment.

The 150 MAX aircraft are the unresolved part of a 225-plane Boeing package that Turkish Airlines announced after President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan met with US President Donald Trump in September 2025.

The carrier placed firm orders for 50 Boeing 787 Dreamliners and took options for 25 more. Its MAX plan covered 100 firm orders for the 737-8 and 737-10 models along with options for another 50 aircraft, but completion depended on an agreement with CFM.

Two people familiar with the talks told Reuters that confirmation of the MAX order would prevent a reversal of an aircraft package promoted by the White House after the Erdoğan-Trump meeting.

Turkish media have said Erdoğan and Trump could meet again next week during the United Nations General Assembly in New York, although the White House has not confirmed such a meeting.

The dispute centered on the price of engine support and how Turkish Airlines and CFM would divide the risk of rising repair costs under a long-term service agreement, according to industry sources cited by Reuters.

CFM, jointly owned by US-based GE Aerospace and France’s Safran, makes the LEAP-1B engines used by the 737 MAX.

Turkish Airlines also sought permission to establish its own MAX engine maintenance center as part of CFM’s top service tier.

Such an agreement would give the carrier faster access to repair methods and technology, but Reuters could not determine whether the sides had reached a deal on the proposed center.

Turkish Airlines Chairman Ahmet Bolat threatened in October to abandon the MAX purchase and order Airbus aircraft if CFM did not offer acceptable terms.

GE Aerospace Chief Executive Larry Culp later criticized the carrier’s decision to conduct negotiations in public and defended the company’s pricing.

Turkish Airlines operates more than 400 Boeing and Airbus jets and is expanding its İstanbul hub as part of plans to increase its network and replace older aircraft.

Okan Baş, the carrier’s senior vice president for finance, told an aviation finance conference Monday that Turkish Airlines was considering more aircraft purchases.

The company is studying the Embraer E2 and Airbus A220 regional jets while comparing the Boeing 777X with the Airbus A350-1000 for future long-distance routes.
