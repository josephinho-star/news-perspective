Trump calls on Congress to fund new air traffic control system

2026-11-15 15:25

President Trump on Sunday asked Congress to fund a new air traffic control system following Friday’s rare nationwide ground stop for all U.S. flights.

“If Congress approves the desperately needed money for Air Traffic Control, we can get it built quickly and efficiently. It will be the BEST SYSTEM anywhere in the World,” the president wrote Sunday on Truth Social.

“Much of our current air control system is 50 years old – RIDICULOUS! We have already started the work, but it should be done all at once, not in drips and drabs. Congress must approve,” he added, before pointing blame at the Biden administration, which he said “set it back years.”

In another Truth Social post earlier Sunday, Trump wrote, “The Biden Administration was an FAA disaster. Everything they touched turned bad. Big money spent unwisely, making an old and broken system worse than when they began.”

“We have the knowledge and plan for the Flight Control Systems, we need Congress to give the money, FAST!” he continued.

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) on Friday afternoon issued a ground stop, which lasted around four hours, citing communication issues. The decision is unusual — it’s the third such instance — and disrupted travel throughout the country.

In response, members of the Trump administration and lawmakers have called on Congress to improve what they see as an outdated system.

“What we have is an incredibly old air traffic control system,” Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said at a Friday night press briefing. “Let’s not do a patchwork fix to a system that’s critical for every single family.”

Duffy then asked for $50 billion, adding, “Let’s finish this job: new hardware, new software, new telecommunication.”

Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) on Saturday dismissed any demands for investigation. He instead said in a Fox News interview that “we need further investment.”

“I mean, some of this equipment dates back to the ’80s so it’s gotta be updated and that’s our responsibility. We’re gonna work to get it done,” Johnson said.

The source of the ground stop has not been directly established.

The transportation secretary on Saturday morning pointed to a telecommunications line switch as the potential cause.

“So, what we’re working to determine was whether we had incompetence, meaning, was it user error, installator error from L3Harris, or did they install a faulty router?” he told reporters. “We haven’t determined what the fault was.”

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin rejected concerns that Americans flying were unsafe or that any bad actor was to blame.

“There was no indication as of yet that this was a nefarious attack against us,” Mullin told reporters Friday afternoon outside of the White House. “It’s just a telecommunication outage as of right now.”

There was some speculation that the rollout of the FAA’s new AI technology Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories (SMART) played a part, but Air Space Intelligence (ASI), the company which owns SMART, denied involvement.

“The current ground stop is not based on or a result of the SMART system, any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC [air traffic control],” ASI wrote on social media Friday amid the stoppage.
