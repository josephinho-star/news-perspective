Hyeseong Kim faces uncertain Dodgers future entering pivotal 2027 season

2026-11-20 01:21

When it comes to Hyeseong Kim, the Dodgers still talk in terms of hope.

That the versatile, glove-first infielder will eventually find a breakthrough with his swing. That, despite a second straight disappointing season since his signing out of South Korea, there are ways to improve his hitting mechanics and make him more of a threat at the plate.

That next year — the last before the team will have to make a decision on a two-year, $10 million option in his contract for 2028 and 2029 — the 27-year-old can finally establish himself as a full-time weapon at the big-league level.

“Hopefully,” general manager Brandon Gomes said last week, “he’ll take a step forward on the swing mechanics and [we can] get him into a really good place offensively.”

For now, however, hoping is the best the Dodgers can do.

What they thought might have been a steal of a signing two years ago has thus far turned into a frustrating flop.

And even in an offseason in which the club is searching for added infield depth and versatile defense, the Dodgers still haven’t seen enough to be certain he can help fill such a need.

“[We’re] really focused on just what we can do right now to get him to that point to go out,” Gomes said, “and be the player we know he can be.”

The player the Dodgers believe Kim can be, at the least, is one who should have a role to play on their 2027 roster.

With Miguel Rojas having retired after the end of last season, and Kiké Hernández uncertain to be re-signed as a free agent this winter, the Dodgers have holes on their infield depth chart.

They want to give more regular off-days to veterans including third baseman Max Muncy, shortstop Mookie Betts and first baseman Freddie Freeman. They would like to use Tommy Edman as more of a utility threat, which could free up playing time at second base (Kim’s best defensive position).

Alex Freeland — a former top prospect who has had his own big-league struggles the last couple years but impressed team officials with how he finished 2026 (hitting .250 with an .802 OPS after being called back up in September) — figures to soak up some of those at-bats.

The team is also searching for a right-handed-hitting infielder on this year’s free agent market, casting a wide net that could stretch from Hernández to three-time Gold Glover Mauricio Dubón.

Still, depth will be paramount for the Dodgers, who are on track to once again have the majors’ oldest lineup next season. Youth and athleticism are needed; two traits that should align with Kim’s speedy, defensively sound profile.

“He has so many skills that fit us well,” Gomes said.

And yet, Kim’s future with the club remains very much unclear, as he continues to search for ways to adapt his slap-hitting swing to the challenges of facing MLB-caliber pitching.

“Somebody with that work ethic and care and desire to be good, that’s something that we feel good about,” Gomes insisted, trying to strike an optimistic tone while discussing Kim’s status at last week’s GM Meetings in Las Vegas. “That is a guy that you want to bet on, that he’s going to go make the adjustments that he needs to.”

Then again, the Dodgers have said similar things about Kim ever since he was signed out of the Korea Baseball Organization ahead of the 2025 season — in what was a surprise move the club envisioned as its next international scouting success story.

Instead, every change the 5-foot-10 lefty has tried to make has failed to stick.

A strong start to his 2025 campaign (when he hit .410 with a 1.028 OPS in his first 27 games) quickly faded as the league began adjusting to him, resulting in a .200 batting average and .498 OPS over the rest of that season.

A nearly identical script played out in 2026, with Kim hitting .314 with an .800 OPS in his first 26 games of the season, only to quickly fall into an 8-for-46 slump that saw him relegated to Triple-A for the final four months of the year.

In 301 career MLB plate appearances overall, he has hit .271 with only 15 extra-base hits, 19 walks and a whopping 83 strikeouts.

And while he improved in many underlying metrics in 2026 — including strikeout rate, walk rate, chase rate, whiff rate and in-zone contact percentage — he still “did not have the year that he was expecting,” Gomes acknowledged.

This offseason will present another unique challenge for Kim, who won’t be able to work with Dodgers coaches once an all-but-certain lockout begins on Dec. 1.

That means, until a new collective bargaining agreement is struck and the 2027 campaign begins, it will largely be up to him to try to make the kind of improvements that have thus far proven elusive at the plate.

After all, for as much as the Dodgers continue to hope he can cement himself as a regular in the majors, they still don’t know quite what to expect entering his most pivotal year yet in the organization.

“He’s proven at times [he can hit at the big-league level],” Gomes said. “It’s just about kind of holding that consistency.”
