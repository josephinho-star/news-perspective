Taylor Swift performs ‘The Fate of Ophelia’ live for the first time in front of star-studded crowd at Academy Museum Gala

2026-10-18 12:41

Taylor Swift performed some of her biggest hits during her performance at the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures’ sixth annual Academy Museum Gala in Los Angeles Saturday evening.

The “Fortnight” singer took the stage in a strapless sparkling gown to give her first live performance of her chart-topping single “The Fate of Ophelia,” per videos shared online.

Swift, 36, also performed “Love Story,” “Blank Space” and her original song from “Toy Story 5,” titled “I Knew It, I Knew You.”

The “Bad Blood” songstress channeled Cinderella when she arrived at the “Met Gala of the West” on Saturday.

The well-heeled crowd included this year’s honorees filmmaker and composer John Carpenter, Oscar nominee Colman Domingo and Oscar winner Charlize Theron. Co-chairs of the gala, Tom Ford, Robert Rodriguez, Steven Spielberg and Kate Capshaw were also in attendance.

A-listers including Jessica Alba, Adrien Brody and Georgina Chapman, Kirsten Dunst, Regina Hall, Kate Hudson, Jessica Lange, Greta Lee, John Legend and Chrissy Teigen, Rami Malek, Demi Moore, Olivia Munn, Da’Vine Joy Randolph, Seth Rogen and Teyana Taylor were among the 2026 Academy Museum Gala host committee.

In the press release, Academy Museum Director and President Amy Homma described Swift as “an extraordinary talent whose work resonates around the world.”

“First of all, I want to say thank you to MTV for giving this platform to music videos so long ago,” Swift said in her acceptance speech. “I got to start making music videos and being a part of music videos 20 years ago, and since then, I’ve gotten to be a part of making 60 music videos, roughly 60 of them — 18 of those I wrote and directed. And I really love writing and directing music videos.

“I gotta tell you, though, the main reason why I love writing and directing music videos so much is because you, the fans, make it so much fun. You make it so rewarding because you actually care about what we’re making,” she continued.

“So I want to say thank you to everyone who ever worked on a music video that I got to be a part of. I learned something from every single person that I ever worked alongside.”

Swift joined country singer Lainey Wilson onstage toward the end of her set, with Wilson telling the crowd, “I don’t think y’all are ready for what’s about to happen,” before Swift took the stage.

Wilson, 34, said it has been “so cool” to see what Swift has accomplished since she first saw the “really, really special” singer perform at age 16.

“She is just as kind as she is talented. Y’all, welcome to the stage, my friend, Taylor Swift,” Wilson said, prompting the audience to erupt into screams.
