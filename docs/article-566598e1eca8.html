American soldier held in UK suspected of spying for Russia taken into US military custody

2026-11-17 23:05

A US soldier who was arrested in the UK on suspicion of spying for Russia has been taken into custody by the US military, police have said.

Lt Col Brandon Carroll Tyner, 41, was detained by British counter-terrorism police on 10 November in Gloucestershire over allegations relating to espionage.

The arrest was part of an investigation by UK Counter Terrorism Policing, working with authorities including the US military and the FBI.

The FBI director, Kash Patel, said in a statement on Tuesday: “This FBI has zero tolerance for anyone who betrays their oath to the United States or puts our national security at risk.

“That’s been demonstrated by the 167 counterintelligence and espionage arrests we’ve made over the last 21 months, a 53% increase.

“An army officer is in custody as the result of an espionage investigation and the close cooperation between the FBI’s Philadelphia field office and Legat London and our partners at UK Counter Terrorism Policing, MI5, UK Defence Counter Intelligence, and the US Army Counterintelligence Command.

“Together we worked to identify a potential threat to national security and take swift action.”

The suspect, a senior soldier, was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, which serves as the headquarters of Nato’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, the Guardian understands.

He was arrested on suspicion of offences under section 1 and section 3 of the National Security Act and was in custody at a London police station.

US authorities have said Tyner joined the military in 2008 as a senior human resources officer, and was deployed overseas to Iraq, Qatar and Afghanistan.

He was identified on Friday after British counter-terror police secured more time to question him.

As part of the investigation, officers carried out searches at two locations in Gloucestershire, the Metropolitan police said last week.

Assistant commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of Counter Terrorism Policing: said: “Following an extensive investigation, CTP presented our case to the CPS [Crown Prosecution Service], who in turn engaged with the attorney general’s office and American partners. Subsequently, a decision was made to pass the investigation to them to progress.

“This decision was made in line with existing international protocols.

“These are extremely serious allegations, and our collective response should send a strong message that we will act swiftly and robustly where we suspect this kind of espionage activity taking place.

“I’d like to praise the excellent work of the investigation team here in the UK to get us to this point and we will continue to support our US colleagues as they take the matter forwards.”

The investigation is not linked to any other ongoing Counter Terrorism Policing investigations.

US Brig Gen Sean Stinchon said the case “should serve as a stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath we all take to protect and defend the constitution”.

He added: “Whenever we suspect somebody of betraying the trust of their fellow soldiers and jeopardising our collective defence, we will identify you, and we will ensure justice is served.

“This effort is a great example of the remarkable partnership Army Counterintelligence Command has with army prosecutors, the FBI, DOJ [Department of Justice], and the UK.”
