Turkey seizes firms linked to ruling party official’s son over fund scandal

2026-11-17 15:40

Turkey on Tuesday put companies linked to Mustafa Yazıcı, son of a deputy leader of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s ruling party, under state management in an investigation into alleged investment fund manipulation and money laundering.

Mustafa Yazıcı owns Adil Varlık Yönetim, an asset management company, and co-founded another business covered by the takeover decisions with financier Ali Emre Ballı, who was found dead in his car last month.

His father, Hayati Yazıcı, is a founding member and deputy chairman of the Justice and Development Party (AKP) and a former minister.

The Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF), the state agency that administers seized businesses, was appointed to manage Adil Katılım Bankası, Adil Varlık Yönetim, Adil Tasarruf Finansman and Dünya Varlık Yönetim, the Demirören news agency reported, citing the İstanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office.

The decision gives the agency the companies’ management powers along with authority over their shares and securities.

Money raised through loans and securities sales with no underlying business activity supplied Adil Katılım’s founding capital, which was then invested in funds managed by Tera Portföy and used to generate profits through manipulation, prosecutors alleged.

Bank resources were depleted through loans to companies in the same group, while suspected criminal proceeds were laundered through company transfers and investment transactions, the prosecutor’s office added.

The findings drew on reports from the Financial Crimes Investigation Board (MASAK), Turkey’s financial intelligence unit, along with banking records, market records and witness statements.

The fund scandal emerged in September when investment funds could not meet withdrawal requests, prompting the Capital Markets Board (SPK), Turkey’s securities regulator, to order the liquidation of 131 funds managed by seven companies.

Investigators suspect fund operators drove up prices of shares with few buyers and sellers, inflating the value of their investments before investors sought to withdraw their money.

Adil Katılım co-founder Ali Paslı, jailed on November 11, is accused of investing 1.844 billion lira ($36.6 million) of the bank’s capital in Tera funds in 2025 and generating 2.43 billion lira ($48.2 million) in illicit gains over 84 days, the Nefes daily reported.

Paslı told prosecutors he met Ballı through Mustafa Yazıcı, denied receiving payments from the bank and maintained that he learned about the transactions only after the fund crisis.

In a separate decision announced earlier Tuesday, prosecutors ordered the seizure of all personal assets belonging to Ali Emre Ballı and İlkay Ballı and the appointment of the TMSF to manage 21 companies in which they owned shares.

The seizure covers property, bank accounts, assets at electronic payment companies, safe-deposit boxes and company shares.

Of the 21 companies, 15 are operating and six are undergoing liquidation, according to an İhlas news agency dispatch.

The businesses named in that decision include Reel Finansal Teknoloji Yazılımları, a financial software company that Mustafa Yazıcı and Ali Emre Ballı founded with equal shares, according to trade registry records cited by the Serbestiyet news website.

Mustafa Yazıcı’s financial dealings had come under scrutiny in September, when prosecutors imposed restrictions on his assets along with those of 29 other people.

The restrictions on Yazıcı and four others were lifted within hours after prosecutors reassessed statements and other evidence, although Yazıcı was prohibited from leaving Turkey.

Ballı’s asset restrictions were also removed before he was found dead in his car in İstanbul’s Çekmeköy district on October 6.

The investigation into his death was transferred to the İstanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office on Monday, the Diken news website reported.

Five other companies also appeared Tuesday on the TMSF’s list of businesses under its management: brokerage Alnus Yatırım Menkul Değerler, Altınbaş Holding, Altınbaş Kuyumculuk, Altınhas Holding and Altınhas Yatırım Holding, according to Bloomberg HT.

A court also jailed seven more suspects pending trial and released 19 subject to court restrictions, bringing the number in pretrial detention in the fund investigation to 130, DW Türkçe reported.

A separate group of 17 suspects was brought to the İstanbul Courthouse Tuesday for questioning by prosecutors, according to the Ekonomim news website.

Those jailed earlier in the investigation include former regulators, bank executives and former family minister Fatma Betül Sayan Kaya, who resigned as an AKP deputy chair in September.
