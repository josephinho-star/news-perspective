House Democrats promise a US return to global climate talks

2026-11-17 15:32

ANTALYA, Turkey—After their big gains in the 2026 midterm elections, a delegation of Democratic US House members appeared at the United Nations COP31 climate change conference Monday, pledging to rejoin the international fight against global warming.

Rep. Jared Huffman (D-Calif.), ranking member of the House Natural Resources Committee, said it is a “pivotal time” for US climate and energy policy.

“The wrecking ball of Donald Trump and this out-of-touch administration, and the temporarily empty chair of the United States at this COP31, does not represent what the Americans want or need,” Huffman said.

President Donald Trump has pulled the United States out of the Paris climate agreement twice and is trying to quit the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. The current administration has also tried to cut all funding for climate programs.

Since Trump returned to the White House in 2025, the United States has had no formal government presence at the UN talks, but was informally represented by America Is All In, a coalition including local governments, businesses, faith groups, Native American tribes, and universities.

Huffman said the midterm election shows that Americans have rejected the current administration’s efforts to “turn the United States into something like a petro-dictatorship.” The results could pave the way for the country to reestablish a formal presence at the annual talks.

Given legal uncertainties about Trump’s efforts to completely withdraw the US from the UN’s climate framework, the extent of US participation in the talks is unclear. But Huffman said that, in the past, US congressional delegations have attended the annual Conference of the Parties, as the meeting is formally known, under both Democratic and Republican majorities.

“The next time we’re here, we will be an official congressional delegation led by the leadership of the House of Representatives,” Huffman said in an interview with Inside Climate News.
