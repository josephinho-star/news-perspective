Steelers silence the Aaron Rodgers noise, cruise past Broncos to remain perfect at home on Black Friday

2026-11-28 00:26

The Pittsburgh Steelers were placed firmly under the microscope this week. Aaron Rodgers’ benching, revealing interview and subsequent departure sent shockwaves through the NFL.

Amid the shakeup, coach Mike McCarthy and the Steelers turned their attention to a high-stakes Black Friday matchup with the Denver Broncos. Pittsburgh entered Friday on a two-game losing streak, while Denver had won six straight.

But the Steelers embraced the underdog role Friday, upsetting the AFC West-leading Broncos 27-5. Mason Rudolph started at quarterback and threw a touchdown pass, while Pittsburgh’s other stars delivered key plays. T.J. Watt recovered a Bo Nix fumble in the end zone for a touchdown, and Jalen Ramsey intercepted Nix and returned it for another score.

D.K. Metcalf appeared to show his support for Rodgers after hauling in what was arguably his best catch since joining the Steelers in the first quarter. While celebrating in the end zone, Metcalf signed "stand on business, 8" in American Sign Language. The phrase refers to standing by one’s principles. Rodgers wore No. 8 with the Steelers.

While there was still the stray No. 8 jersey in the stands at a rainy Acrisure Stadium, the Steelers began emerging from the rubble behind inspired play from a dominant defense.

Nix gifted Pittsburgh an early touchdown when the ball slipped out of his hand on Denver’s second possession and bounced into the end zone while he was pressured by Watt and Nick Herbig. Watt fell on it to send a jolt through the mist.

The Kansas City Chiefs’ loss to the Buffalo Bills on Thanksgiving night opened the door for the Broncos to take sole possession of first place in the AFC West on Friday. Denver missed the opportunity and will instead settle for a tie at the top of the division standings, at least for now.

Save for a second-quarter safety by linebacker Alex Singleton when he tackled running back Jaylen Warren in the Pittsburgh end zone, the Broncos mustered little on a day that in some ways felt like a referendum on the way one of the league’s premier franchises goes about its business.

Prime Video NFL play-by-play announcer Al Michaels shed more light on Rodgers’ turbulent exit from the Steelers, sharing that Rodgers sent him a text message about his release.

"Earlier today, I heard from Aaron," Michaels said during the broadcast in the fourth quarter. "(He) said, 'A lot of talk about the sanctity of the locker room. The code or sanctity exists to protect the integrity of the locker room, not to provide immunity from accountability for its leadership.'"

During a Wednesday interview on "The Pat McAfee Show," Rodgers made a series of allegations about McCarthy’s focus and work ethic. He claimed the coach had been caught "snoring" during team meetings and had recently ended a speech by inadvertently saying, "one, two, three, Packers."

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
