For which we give thanks: The Post’s Thanksgiving message

2026-11-26 00:22

The first Thanksgiving in the New World was celebrated in 1621, nearly a year after the Pilgrims landed at Plymouth in the Massachusetts Bay Colony.

In 1789, George Washington became the first of many US presidents to formally proclaim a day of “public thanksgiving and prayer”:

I do recommend and assign Thursday, the 26th day of November next, to be devoted by the people of these States to the service of that great and glorious Being who is the beneficent author of all the good that was, that is, or that will be; that we may then all unite in rendering unto Him our sincere and humble thanks for His kind care and protection of the people of this country previous to their becoming a nation; for the signal and manifold mercies and the favorable interpositions of His providence in the course and conclusion of the late war; for the great degree of tranquillity, union, and plenty which we have since enjoyed; for the peaceable and rational manner in which we have been enabled to establish constitutions of government for our safety and happiness, and particularly the national one now lately instituted; for the civil and religious liberty with which we are blessed, and the means we have of acquiring and diffusing useful knowledge; and, in general, for all the great and various favors which He has been pleased to confer upon us.

In 1863, in the midst of the Civil War, Abraham Lincoln likewise called for a day of Thanksgiving in November:

I do therefore invite my fellow citizens in every part of the United States, and also those who are at sea and those who are sojourning in foreign lands, to set apart and observe the last Thursday of November next, as a day of Thanksgiving and Praise to our beneficent Father who dwelleth in the Heavens.

And so it was until President Franklin Roosevelt, in 1939, moved the celebration back to the third Thursday in November to stimulate Depression-era Christmas sales.

President Trump on Wednesday issued his own Thanksgiving proclamation:

As families and friends gather across our country to celebrate Thanksgiving in this 250th anniversary year of American Independence, we pledge to carry forward the spirit of faith and fellowship that marked the first Thanksgiving feast, and we renew our mission to continue ushering in the Golden Age of America so that every citizen has the opportunity to achieve their American Dream.

More than four centuries ago, 102 passengers set sail aboard the Mayflower on a daring voyage across the Atlantic Ocean that would forever change the course of humanity. Fleeing religious persecution and seeking the promise of freedom found in the New World, these brave Pilgrims endured a treacherous journey and reached the shores of New England in November of 1620. A brutal winter followed, bringing unimaginable hardship and claiming the lives of nearly half of the souls who had made the crossing. Sustained only by their unfailing faith in Jesus Christ and the bonds they formed in caring for one another, this small community persisted with remarkable strength of spirit and laid the foundations for our civilization — forging a friendship with the Wampanoag people, learning to cultivate the land, and gathering a successful harvest in the following autumn. Filled with gratitude to our Lord for these magnificent blessings, the Pilgrims joined the Wampanoag in a celebration that became the first Thanksgiving.

The faith and gratitude that sustained our forebears still remain at the heart of our Thanksgiving traditions and national character. In every generation since, these enduring values have inspired proud citizens to persevere through adversity, build a land of opportunity, and pass the blessings of liberty to their children — making our Nation stronger, our future brighter, and the promise of America greater than ever before.

On this blessed day, we celebrate the glorious land that has allowed all people to worship freely and openly since its founding, and we remember that this solemn conviction anchors our national identity: America is — and always will be — one Nation under the eternal providence and goodness of God. Through every trial and triumph, our trust in Him has strengthened our people and inspired us to carry the flame of liberty forward.

As we gather with our loved ones to celebrate the innumerable blessings of our Republic, we also pay special tribute to the brave men and women of our Armed Forces whose commitment to duty draws them far from home and family. Their courage and sacrifice secure the way of life we cherish, and we salute them for their unwavering dedication to defending our Nation and keeping the American People safe throughout this holiday season.

This Thanksgiving, we uphold a beloved American tradition older than our Nation itself. May we remain steadfast in our mission to restore American greatness, faithful in our devotion to Almighty God, and united in our resolve to preserve His blessings of freedom and prosperity for centuries to come.

NOW, THEREFORE, I, DONALD J. TRUMP, President of the United States of America, by virtue of the authority vested in me by the Constitution and the laws of the United States, do hereby proclaim Thursday, November 26, 2026, as a National Day of Thanksgiving. I encourage all Americans to gather in homes and places of worship and offer a prayer of thanks to God for our many blessings.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand this twenty-fourth day of November, in the year of our Lord two thousand twenty-six, and of the Independence of the United States of America the two hundred and fifty-first.
