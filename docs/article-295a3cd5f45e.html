European Parliament seeks EU action over Turkey-Libya maritime deal

2026-11-26 14:30

The European Parliament on Thursday asked EU governments to consider targeted sanctions on people or organizations that violate an EU member’s maritime rights by implementing a 2019 agreement between Turkey and Libya.

In a resolution adopted in Strasbourg, lawmakers called the maritime deal “null and void” and said it violated the rights of other countries under international law.

They also urged the European Commission, the EU’s executive arm, to review funding for Turkey and Libya and cut or redirect any money that supports such violations while protecting humanitarian aid.

The parliament asked EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas and the commission to report within three months on diplomatic efforts, safeguards for EU funds and ways to protect the maritime rights of Greece, Cyprus and other EU members.

Lawmakers called on Turkey and Libya to scrap the maritime deal and related oil and gas agreements.

The resolution also called for stronger maritime security in the eastern Mediterranean, citing the EU’s Aspides naval mission, which protects shipping in the Red Sea, as a model.

The agreement, which Turkey signed with Libya’s then-government in Tripoli, draws a boundary between areas where the two countries claim rights to resources in the eastern Mediterranean.

Greece says the boundary ignores the maritime rights of Greek islands such as Crete, while Turkey maintains the deal complies with international law.

Libya’s eastern-based House of Representatives approved the pact on October 20, reversing years of opposition.

The House published the agreement in its official gazette on Wednesday as Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan met with eastern Libyan military commander Khalifa Haftar in Ankara.

Erdoğan is expected to travel to the Libyan capital of Tripoli for an Africa-Turkey summit on December 8 and 9.
