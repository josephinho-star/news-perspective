Gavin Newsom faces furious backlash from Dems over abortion veto

2026-09-25 17:36

The Democratic governor on Sunday vetoed AB 2164, the Care Without Fear Act, which would have restricted future governors from granting extradition requests from other states targeting people for legally protected abortion and gender care.

“In his letter explaining the veto, Newsom tries to describe this bill as if it’s really extreme and suggests that the decisions on extradition need to lay within the discretion of the governor, but 18 other states have already passed similar legislation. So how extreme could this be?”

The issue came to a head in January, when Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill announced an indictment against Sonoma County physician Dr. Rémy Coeytaux for allegedly providing abortion pills to a Louisiana resident. The state’s governor, Jeff Landry, sought Coeytaux’s extradition.

Newsom exercised his authority as governor to block extradition of Coeytaux for prosecution in Louisiana, but he rejected AB 2164. Coeytaux could not be reached for comment Thursday.

The bill — authored by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D-Orinda) — would have barred specified extradition demands involving legally protected health care, subject to existing statutory requirements. It also would have extended protections to people whose assistance with care was lawful in another U.S. jurisdiction.

“While I share the author’s views on the importance of protecting these important healthcare services, extradition is an exclusively executive function,” Newsom wrote in his veto message.

“When discretionary, that discretion is appropriately placed with the governor.”

The veto leaves existing restrictions on arrests and cooperation with outside investigations intact. But advocates say the governor’s remaining discretion creates uncertainty that legislation could reduce.

“California cannot call itself a safe haven for reproductive freedom while leaving abortion providers just one election away from extradition,” Pavitra Abraham, director of state campaigns for Reproductive Freedom for All, told Mother Jones.

Newsom’s explanation focused on protecting the powers of whoever replaces him.

“I am troubled by the precedent that would be set if an outgoing administration agreed with the Legislature to erode the executive authority of an incoming governor based on their shared policy preferences, regardless of how righteous and well-intentioned those actions may be,” he wrote.

Newsom’s office said he had nothing further to add when reached for comment on the criticism.
