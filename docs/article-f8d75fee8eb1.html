TOGG shareholders confirm talks that could give Turkish state control

2026-09-01 14:37

Three shareholders in Turkey’s Automobile Joint Venture Group Industry and Trade Inc. (TOGG), the company behind the Togg electric vehicle brand, have confirmed talks over a share transfer which if completed as reported, would give the Turkey Wealth Fund (TVF) and Turkcell, which the fund controls, a combined 69 percent stake and control of the automaker.

Vestel Elektronik and the AG Anadolu Group Holding confirmed in disclosures to Turkey’s Public Disclosure Platform (KAP) late Monday that talks concerning their TOGG shares were underway.

Vestel said its board had not adopted a decision finalizing a transaction, while the Anadolu Group said no binding agreement had been signed. The Vestel and Anadolu Group filings were marked as delayed disclosures.

Turkcell, an existing shareholder and prospective buyer, confirmed that talks related to the transaction were continuing and named TVF as the proposed co-buyer.

The filings came after Reuters reported that Vestel and the Anadolu Group planned to sell their combined 46 percent stake to TVF and Turkcell. The agency cited three sources familiar with the talks.

Vestel, the Anadolu Group, Turkcell and commercial vehicle manufacturer BMC Automotive each own 23 percent of TOGG. The Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Turkey (TOBB) owns the other 8 percent.

Turkcell’s existing stake and the 46 percent under discussion would give Turkcell and TVF a combined 69 percent if the transaction proceeds as reported.

TVF owns 26.2 percent of Turkcell and controls its board through privileged shares that allow it to appoint five of its nine directors. The deal would therefore put a 69 percent stake in the hands of the wealth fund and a company it controls, although the state would not directly own all those shares.

Retired Turkish ambassador Selim Kuneralp described the proposed structure as a reversal by a government that has sold many state assets to private investors.

“If the claims are true, the company’s majority shares will have passed to the state,” Kuneralp wrote. “I fear this is a clear sign that the Togg experiment has failed.”

Investors also reacted differently to the prospective buyers and sellers. Turkcell shares fell 8.16 percent on Monday, triggering two circuit breakers, while Vestel and the Anadolu Group gained.

Turkcell recovered about 1.5 percent by midday Tuesday but was still 6 to 7 percent below its closing price before the Reuters report. Vestel and the Anadolu Group retained part of their gains, according to market data.

TOGG is not publicly traded, but figures disclosed by its shareholders show that it lost 14.61 billion lira ($303 million) in 2025 after a loss of 13.75 billion lira in 2024. Its losses decreased in the first half of this year.

The automaker sold 39,020 vehicles in Turkey last year. Sales rose 30 percent from a year earlier to 25,848 vehicles in the first seven months of this year.

Hakan Doğu, a former Renault Turkey chief executive, argued before the share sale report that TOGG could not reach profitability at its projected annual production of about 50,000 vehicles.

The company would need to produce between 200,000 and 300,000 vehicles a year to make a profit, Doğu told the Mesele Ekonomi YouTube channel on August 28.

TOGG was founded in 2018 after President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan called on Turkish companies to develop a passenger car financed and owned by Turkish investors. His government has promoted Togg as part of its plan to develop Turkish-owned technology and reduce reliance on imported vehicles.
