Hurricane Trudy strengthens to Category 2 as it approaches Mexico coast

2026-10-17 22:22

Trudy strengthened to a Category 2 hurricane as it moved toward Mexico's southwestern and west-central Pacific coast on Saturday, the U.S. National Hurricane Center said.

A hurricane warning was in effect from Manzanillo, Colima, to Cabo Corrientes, Jalisco. The latest NHC data showed the Category 2 hurricane, with maximum sustained winds of 100 mph (155 kph), about 205 miles (330 kilometers) south-southeast of Cabo Corrientes in Jalisco state and 115 miles (180 kilometers) south-southwest of Manzanillo, moving northwest at 10 mph (17 kph).

Meanwhile, Typhoon Champi strengthened into a Category 4 weather system as it moved toward Guam.

Trudy's center was expected to approach the Mexican coast on Saturday and pass near it into Sunday. After the latest warning, Mexico's government issued a tropical storm warning for Las Islas Marias and discontinued a similar warning for its coast south of Lazaro Cardenas.

The Mexican Navy urged mariners and coastal residents to exercise extreme caution because of high waves, heavy rain, and the risk of rising rivers, flooding, and landslides in vulnerable areas.

Colima authorities said risks remained low but warned that heavy rainfall could cause damage because successive storms had already saturated the ground along the Pacific coast.

Trudy was expected to bring 4-8 inches (10-20 centimeters) of rain through the weekend, with isolated totals of around 15 inches (38 centimeters).

Also in the Pacific, Champi strengthened into a Category 4 storm as it moved west toward Guam at 15 mph (24 kph).

By Saturday night local time, Champi was about 25 miles (40 kilometers) southeast of Guam, with maximum sustained winds of 140 mph (225 kph), according to the U.S. National Weather Service.

The storm was forecast to pass south of Guam early Sunday local time and continue intensifying through the day. Guam and Rota remained under a typhoon warning, while Tinian and Saipan were under a tropical storm warning and typhoon watch.

The governors of Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands both requested emergency disaster declarations on Friday. President Donald Trump's approval would authorize federal support and funding for emergency protective measures.

Airports, harbors, and some health facilities in the territories were closed. Resources were unlikely to reach the islands for three to five days, according to Saturday updates from the Federal Emergency Management Agency.

The territories are still recovering from Super Typhoons Sinlaku and Bavi, which struck in April and July, respectively.
