The most expensive cities in the US to raise a family — here’s where NYC lands

2026-11-21 18:20

New York City has earned the dubious distinction of being the most expensive place in America to raise a family, according to a new study — with parents shelling out nearly $147,000 a year in rent and childcare costs alone.

The eye-popping findings come from a new survey by personal loan experts Achieve, which examined the cost of family life in 50 US cities — and found that raising little New Yorkers comes with one seriously grown-up price tag.

Researchers looked at three wallet-draining factors: rent for a three-bedroom apartment in the city center, average childcare expenses and a basket of very basic groceries.

Each city was then assigned an affordability score out of 40, with higher scores indicating more affordable places to live.

And when the numbers were crunched, the Big Apple landed at the bottom of the pile with an 8.6 out of a possible 40.

Families hoping to squeeze into a three-bedroom apartment in the heart of NYC can expect to fork over a jaw-dropping $8,856 a month in rent.

Childcare adds another $3,365 per month.

Even a bare-bones trip to the supermarket won’t offer much relief. A basket of staples costs $173, and includes everyday essentials like milk, bread, rice, eggs, cheese, chicken, beef and a handful of fruits and vegetables.

But New York parents aren’t the only ones feeling the financial squeeze.

Oakland, Calif., grabbed second place on the least-affordable list, with families facing average monthly rents of $4,312 and childcare bills of $2,726.

The Golden State city has the most expensive grocery basket in the study, at $202.18 – and even those could only feed baby birds.

Its overall affordability score was just 14.65 out of 40.

Miami came in a not-so-sunny third, proving that living in paradise doesn’t come cheap.

Families looking for a three-bedroom rental in the Florida hotspot can expect to cough up $6,060 a month, while childcare adds another $1,801.46.

Even basics at the grocery store will set them back $161.72. They landed a score of 16.05.

So much for fun in the sun.

Boston landed in fourth place, where parents are also getting whacked by sky-high housing and childcare costs.

A three-bedroom apartment in the city center averages $5,918, while childcare runs $2,900.09.

Throw in $171.64 for a basic basket of groceries, and it’s easy to see why Beantown earned a dismal affordability score of 16.56 out of 40.

The nation’s capital, Washington, DC, grabbed fifth place with a score of 17.97.

Los Angeles took sixth place, with families paying a whopping $5,496.99 a month for a three-bedroom apartment and another $1,676.09 for childcare.

A basic grocery cart totaled $149.62, scoring them a lowly 18.24.

Seattle followed in seventh (18.39) where parents are paying $4,784.62 a month in rent and a staggering $3,012 for childcare — the third-highest figure in the study.

The Emerald City has one of the priciest grocery baskets, at $173.05 a month, giving it an affordability score of 18.39.

Denver, Colo., took eighth place, followed by Portland, Ore., in ninth, and Philadelphia, Pa., rounding out the top 10.

Their affordability scores were 19.49, 19.26 and 19.66, respectively.

On the other end of the spectrum, cash-strapped moms and dads have a few places where raising a family doesn’t require a small fortune.

El Paso, Texas, which scored an impressive 30 out of 40, emerged as the most affordable city in the study with an average of $1,708 in rent and $574.50 in childcare.

Tucson, Ariz., followed at a close second, scoring 27.89 out of 40. Rent averaged $2,122.26, with $1,100 going to childcare.

Pulling in the third, fourth and fifth spots are Omaha, Neb. (27.15), followed by Austin, Texas (26.74), and Louisville, Ky. at 26.58.
