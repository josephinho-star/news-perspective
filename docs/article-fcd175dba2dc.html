Syrian president appoints former SDF commander Mazloum Abdi as adviser

2026-08-28 10:54

Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa has appointed Mazloum Abdi, the former commander of the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF), as an adviser to the presidency, days after Abdi announced the dissolution of the group following its integration into the Syrian army.

“Mustafa Khalil Abdi is appointed as adviser to the presidency of the republic,” said Decree No. 164 of 2026, issued by al-Sharaa and published by Syria’s state-run SANA news agency.

Abdi, also known as Mazloum Kobani, led the SDF for years as the US-backed force became Washington’s main local partner in the fight against the Islamic State group.

He announced on Tuesday that the SDF had dissolved as an independent military organization after its forces were integrated into brigades of the Syrian army.

The move followed an agreement reached with al-Sharaa in January to integrate the SDF’s military forces and the Kurdish-led civilian administration in northeastern Syria into state institutions.

The agreement strengthened Damascus’ control over northeastern Syria and ended the separate military and administrative structures that had underpinned Kurdish-led self-rule there.

US special envoy to Syria Tom Barrack welcomed the agreement on Tuesday, saying it showed “what real integration looks like” and praising the inclusion of former Kurdish leaders in state institutions.

Abdi said the dissolution followed “the completion of the process of integrating our forces into the brigades of the Syrian army.”

The SDF was formed in 2015 with US backing and played a central role in the campaign against the Islamic State group, including the battle for Kobane.

It later became the main military force of the Kurdish-led administration that controlled large parts of northern and northeastern Syria during the civil war.

The integration process accelerated after clashes in January in which Kurdish-led forces lost significant territory to government troops, while Washington moved closer to the new leadership in Damascus.

Kurdish-language classes approved

The appointment came as Syria’s Education Ministry authorized Kurdish-language instruction in public and private schools in areas with significant Kurdish populations.

Kurdish will be offered as an optional subject for two classes a week across all grade levels beginning in the 2026-2027 academic year.

The decision implements a January decree by al-Sharaa recognizing Kurdish as a national language and allowing it to be taught in schools.

The decree also described Syrian Kurds as an integral part of the Syrian people, annulled measures stemming from the 1962 census in Hasakah province that had deprived many Kurds of citizenship and declared Nowruz a national holiday.

Abdi said last week that although the integration of the Kurdish administration into the Syrian state had been completed, efforts to secure constitutional guarantees for Kurdish rights would continue.

Kurds are estimated to number around 2 million in Syria and are concentrated mainly in the northeast, with significant communities also in Aleppo and Damascus.
