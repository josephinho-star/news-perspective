Anthropic shares fall after blockbuster opening as wider tech sell-off continues

2026-11-20 17:36

Anthropic's shares have dropped back towards their initial price after a soaring first day on the stock market, as a wider tech sell-off deepens.

The fall in tech stocks continued to weigh on markets, with the S&P 500 down more than 2% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 0.7% by lunchtime.

Both US indexes touched correction territory, a point where they are trading at 10% or more down from a recent high.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite Index also fell more than 3.4% by the middle of trading on Friday.

Despite a positive first day of trading, Anthropic's shares fell on Friday, dropping more than 12% to about $85 by lunchtime, close to its initial public offering (IPO) price of $83.

The artificial intelligence (AI) company's introduction to the Nasdaq stock exchange on Thursday was a crucial test for the industry and was largely seen as a success, with trading starting at $111.50.

The maker of the Claude chatbot raised a record $81bn in its initial public offering (IPO), but its share price moved significantly in the first day of trading.

The shares peaked at $115 on Thursday, before easing to end the day up 17% at $97.17.

Analysts said volatility was to be expected in the first days or even weeks of Anthropic's trading, as the company was the first frontier AI firm to go public so had no direct peers.

Some have also noted the similarities with Elon Musk's SpaceX, which at the time of its debut in June was the largest IPO on record. Shares in the rocket and AI company experienced large swings in its first few weeks of trading.

Ed Moya, a strategist at market intelligence firm AlphaSense, said Anthropic's blockbuster IPO did not appear to have restored confidence in the broader AI market.

While AI demand was still very strong, Moya told the BBC that investors were trying to answer the question of how much were they willing to pay for that growth.

"As we've gone through the past past few weeks, it seems that there's been more risks that have surfaced," he said.

"Right now you're starting to see Wall Street is trying to shift from rewarding growth at any price, to demanding stronger cash flow and a clearer path to profitability."

Shares in some of the largest US tech companies also fell.

Nvidia and SpaceX were both down more than 6%, while Tesla shares fell more than 3% and Meta was down 3.4%.

Apple, Amazon and Microsoft experienced smaller falls, while Alphabet was up by 1.2%.

Europe's main stock markets - which contain fewer tech stocks - were also trading lower. Germany's Dax index fell 2% and the UK's FTSE 100 was down 0.5%.

Earlier on Friday, tech-heavy Asian markets saw steep falls.

South Korea's Kospi and Japan's Nikkei 225 indexes closed down about 5%, with shares in memory chip makers Sk Hynix, Kioxia and Tokyo Electron, and other tech firms such as Samsung Electronics, seeing losses.

Taiwan's stock market dropped about 6% on Friday, taking the weekly fall to more than 17% and what was reported to be its biggest weekly fall since 1990.
