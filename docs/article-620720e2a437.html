UCLA unbeaten no more after Bruins lose QB and fall in triple OT

2026-11-22 00:27

ANN ARBOR, Mich. – Finally, there was a situation too dire, adversity that could not be overcome.

A UCLA football team that had always found a way this season faltered in the shadow of perfection.

Seeking a program-record 11th victory, the fourth-ranked Bruins lost their quarterback in overtime Saturday and then lost the game against Michigan, 18-16, in triple overtime for their first setback of the season.

After the injury departure of quarterback Ty Dieffenbach on the first offensive play of the extra period, Michigan eventually scored in the third overtime on quarterback Bryce Underwood’s pass to Jalen Hoffman in the corner of the end zone.

Madden Iamaleava, who had replaced Dieffenbach, spun away from one defender on UCLA’s possession in the third overtime before lofting a short pass that fell incomplete.

There were ominous signs from the start.

The Wolverines ran for 24 yards on their first offensive play. Dieffenbach threw a shaky opening pass that fell incomplete, one of many to come while facing relentless pressure.

Seeking a program-record 11th victory, the Bruins (10-1 overall, 7-1 Big Ten) never got comfortable against a team that matched their intensity despite a .500 record entering the day.

The Wolverines (6-5, 3-5) even rivaled UCLA’s energy while both teams bounced around on the field before the start of the fourth quarter as The Killers’ “Mr. Brightside” blared over stadium loudspeakers.

Everything about the game just seemed a little off for the Bruins.

UCLA found itself facing its first fourth-quarter deficit of the season. It came courtesy of Michigan quarterback Bryce Underwood firing a 6-yard touchdown pass to Mason Bonner in the corner of the end zone on third and goal with 12:55 left.

Known for their second-half dominance – the Bruins had entered the game outscoring opponents, 185-77, after halftime – there was little besides blunders late when the game got tight.

Dieffenbach lost his handle on the ball while cocking his arm back to throw early in the fourth quarter, giving Michigan the ball at the Bruins’ 15-yard line and leading to the game’s first touchdown.

What a disaster.

UCLA could go from No. 4 in the College Football Playoff rankings to on the cusp of being left out of the 12-team field, depending on how far the selection committee decides to drop the Bruins.

The Bruins now also must beat USC next weekend to clinch a spot in the Big Ten title game.

Dieffenbach’s fumble before he was even hit turned the momentum squarely in MIchigan’s favor, giving the Wolverines the ball in the red zone.

They punched it into the end zone five plays later to take a 10-3 lead.

Michigan running back Savion Hiter singlehandedly outgained every UCLA counterpart combined, rushing for 102 yards in 24 carries.

His hard-charging runs sustained drives and helped Michigan win the time-of-possession battle while keeping the ball out of UCLA’s hands.

UCLA heads back to the Rose Bowl to face rival USC next weekend with plenty on the line for both teams. While the Bruins try to improve their CFP positioning, the Trojans will seek to stay in contention for their first CFP appearance.
