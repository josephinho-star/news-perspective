Lakers are ‘real’ deal — even Victor Wembanyama thinks so

2026-11-28 09:41

Like the rest of us, Victor Wembanyama was skeptical of the Lakers.

The darlings of the NBA entered Friday’s game against the Spurs with the top record in the NBA at 15-3. But they hadn’t played the league’s most elite teams. They hadn’t really proven themselves.

So, Friday was a temperature check. It was a harsh dressing room mirror that would expose their flaws in front of a national television audience.

How did they do?

“As a team I wasn’t sure what they were built of, at least from the start of the season,” Wembanyama acknowledged. “But it’s real.”

The Lakers lost, 99-96. But the final score was far less important than what transpired.

The Lakers led by as much as 20 points. They bottled up Wembanyama in the first half, holding him to just eight points on nine shot attempts and five rebounds. Over that period, Luka Dončić had as many rebounds as Wembanyama (five) while scoring more than twice as many points (19).

It was incredible.

The Spurs reached the Finals last year. Wembanyama is unequivocally considered the next face of the league once the 41-year-old LeBron James retires. This was about as big of a test as the Lakers could get.

And at the beginning of the game, they were jaw-droppingly good.

Then things fell apart.

The Lakers were outscored in the third quarter, 30-14. Over that period, they were outshot from the field, 48.1% to 25%, and from beyond the 3-point line, 37.5% to 15.4%. They unraveled, culminating in Keldon Johnson making a 3-pointer with 1.8 seconds left to knot the score at 68-68 heading into the fourth quarter.

The next 12 minutes were a dogfight.

Both teams wanted this.

Wembanyama and Dončić, who are in a two-horse race for the MVP Award, knew the stakes.

Everyone on the court was acutely aware of the chatter this game would generate. They knew this contest meant so much more than another regular-season game in November.

Ultimately, the Spurs clawed a few inches ahead as the final buzzer sounded.

After Wembanyama’s sluggish start, he finished with 25 points, 16 rebounds, four steals and two blocked shots. But he had as many field-goal attempts (25) as points.

Dončić had a game-high 31 points, six rebounds, eight assists and three steals. But he went 1-for-13 from beyond the arc.

Both of them shined. Both of them struggled. The Spurs likely prevailed because they’ve experienced moments like this before.

They were three wins away from a championship last year. If they hadn’t blown double-digit leads in all five of their games against the Knicks in the Finals, they would’ve been the reigning champions.

If anything, it was incredibly impressive that the Lakers did what they did Friday. They have nine new players. They weren’t supposed to be this good.

And on Friday, they were ready for this test.

They came out of the gate with the requisite intensity. They slammed the accelerator. From top to bottom, nearly everyone was starring in their roles. Heck, Dončić even looked good when he took on the Herculean task of guarding Wembanyama in moments.

But the Lakers ran out of gas. They sputtered. They couldn’t sustain that for 48 minutes.

It happens.

Overall, the Lakers should be proud of themselves. This proved they can compete with the best teams in the league. They are contenders.

No one was down after this loss.

Said Lakers coach JJ Redick: “I think the spirit of our group was awesome.”

Added Dončić: “I think, you know, we could obviously win this game. I don’t think we played great and we were there. One-possession game, away game against San Antonio, which is a great team.”

If that was a preview of the Western Conference Finals, can it be May yet?

If that was what happens when Wembanyama and Dončić go head to head, can we figure out a way to add a one-on-one showcase to All-Star weekend?

This was fun. This was theater. This was thrilling.

The Lakers have officially arrived on the scene.

They lost but they won the respect of the NBA.

As Wembanyama said, the hype is real.
