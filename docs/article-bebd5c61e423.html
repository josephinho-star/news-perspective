Trump demands probe into preelection GDP data, alleges ‘purposeful’ error

2026-11-26 15:35

President Trump on Thursday called for an investigation into economic data released ahead of the midterm elections after the federal government revised its estimate of third-quarter growth upward.

The president, in a Thanksgiving Day post on Truth Social, alleged without providing evidence that the initial gross domestic product (GDP) figures were deliberately understated.

“Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation,” Trump wrote. “This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error.”

The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) said Wednesday that the economy grew at an annualized rate of 4.3 percent between July and September, up from its initial estimate of 3.4 percent.

GDP estimates are routinely revised as more comprehensive data becomes available. The BEA is scheduled to release another estimate next month.

“The American Economy under The Trump Administration is doing FAR BETTER than the good numbers already released,” Trump continued in his post.

The demand for a probe follows a tough midterm election cycle for Republicans, who lost control of both the House and Senate as Democrats made affordability a central focus of their campaigns.

The economy was a top concern for voters, many of whom expressed frustration with rising prices. Trump’s sweeping tariffs and the economic fallout from the war with Iran also weighed on Americans.

While the economy has continued to grow, fueled in part by investments in artificial intelligence, inflation remains stubbornly high.

Separate data released Wednesday showed the personal consumption expenditures price index — the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation gauge — rose 3.8 percent annually in October, in line with expectations but well above the central bank’s 2 percent target.

The data has also fueled expectations that the Fed could raise interest rates at its meeting next month after holding them steady in October.
