Deep-sea search begins amid negligence claims in Cyprus ferry disaster

2026-09-01 12:34

A Turkish Navy deep-sea vessel began searching off northern Cyprus on Tuesday for 20 people missing after a ferry disaster that killed eight, as survivor accounts, court records and claims by maritime experts raised questions about the ship’s condition, the decision to sail and the crew’s response.

The high-speed catamaran Filo Jet left Kyrenia on Sunday for Taşucu in southern Turkey with 259 passengers and eight crew members aboard. It took on water, capsized about four nautical miles from shore and sank to a depth of 514 meters. Officials said 239 people were rescued after two people counted among earlier rescue figures were found to be missing.

The Turkish Navy rescue and salvage ship TCG Işın reached Kyrenia on Tuesday and began work to locate and inspect the wreck. The ship carries sonar and an underwater vehicle that can operate at depths of up to 3,000 meters, allowing crews to search the ferry and recover its voyage data recorder.

The operation could also establish whether passengers were trapped in compartments as the ferry overturned. Videos appeared to show people inside the vessel while other passengers waited on the upturned hull.

Survivors said they heard noises from below before water entered the passenger area. Serkan Kunduracı told Turkey’s Demirören News Agency that crew members inspected the leak and told passengers that a loose gasket had caused a minor problem. Another survivor, Deniz Özpolat, said passengers could not find lifejackets and that doors were closed as they tried to escape, forcing some to break windows, The Associated Press reported.

Police told a Kyrenia court that they had received statements alleging the crew was warned about water entering from beneath the vessel but continued the voyage. Other testimony accused crew members of failing to help passengers or explain how to use the lifejackets. A defense lawyer maintained that the crew lowered lifejackets and rafts and helped with the evacuation.

Survivors also alleged that the captain and other crew members left the ferry before passengers had been evacuated. Northern Cyprus Public Works and Transportation Minister Erhan Arıklı promised an investigation into claims that doors were locked and the captain abandoned the vessel.

The captain, seven other crew members and a company official were ordered detained for three days while police investigate the possibility of death caused through carelessness and negligence. Police are also examining the conduct of company shareholders and port officials.

The crew told the court that waves broke part of the ferry’s bow before it capsized. They maintained that weather reports allowed the voyage and that the vessel carried the permits and certificates required for international service.

Questions about the ferry’s structure arose after naval architect Erdal Kılıç said he inspected the vessel for about 50 hours in 2016 following a grounding near Taşucu. Kılıç said radiographic and ultrasonic tests showed “very serious structural damage” to its bow and that its sandwich construction required repairs.

Kılıç alleged that the vessel’s owners did not accept his proposed repairs and later had two areas of damage repaired by people who lacked the required skills.

The 26-year-old vessel had faced earlier safety concerns under its former name, Iris Jet. An İstanbul court ruling issued in 2024 found that 29 defects and rule violations identified during surveys in 2018 had not all been corrected at the time of a disputed sale.

Those findings describe the vessel’s condition in 2018 and do not establish its condition when it sank. The ferry later underwent repairs, and police told the court that authorities inspected it on June 2 and found it fit for international service.

A new question arose when an online check of a certificate number released by the government returned an “invalid” result. Arıklı could not explain the entry during a television interview but said the ferry had undergone annual inspections and that he possessed a report describing work carried out at a shipyard.

Investigators are also checking the captain’s qualification, which was issued by Honduras. Arıklı rejected claims that the captain lacked a license, citing his 25 years of experience, but later said the document’s validity would form part of the investigation.

Turkey’s Chamber of Naval Architects (GMO) called for an independent inquiry into the ferry’s stability, watertight compartments, hull, loading, maintenance history and inspection records. The chamber said certification alone could not ensure safety throughout a vessel’s operating life and cited requirements under the International Maritime Organization (IMO) International Code of Safety for High-Speed Craft (HSC Code).

The ferry’s Saturday voyage had been postponed because of bad weather, but officials deemed conditions suitable when it sailed on Sunday. Former ocean-going watch officer Cem Aykut claimed residual swells made the departure unsafe for a high-speed catamaran, while Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhürman cautioned against assigning the disaster to the weather before the investigation is complete.

The government suspended Filo Denizcilik’s services and removed the port officials who approved the voyage from their posts. The company said it was cooperating with government agencies and had formed a crisis team but did not respond to the allegations about maintenance or crew conduct in its public statement.

Northern Cyprus declared three days of mourning. The northern part of the island declared itself the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (KKTC) in 1983 and is recognized only by Turkey.
