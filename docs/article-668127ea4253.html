Stream It Or Skip It: ‘The Hunting Wives’ Season 2 on Netflix, where Sophie and Margo can’t hide from their secrets

2026-11-26 20:30

Season 1 of Netflix’s unexpected smash hit The Hunting Wives was nuts, with secrets flying, and people dropping like flies. Season 2 takes place in that finale’s aftermath, but new mysteries are cropping up in East Texas.

THE HUNTING WIVES SEASON 2: STREAM IT OR SKIP IT?

Opening Shot: The sheriff’s department, including sheriff Jonny Baskin (Branton Box), surround a huge house, and Baskin tells whoever’s inside that they know they’re holding Margo Banks (Malin Akerman) hostage. We then flash to see Sophie O’Neill (Brittany Snow) holding a gun.

The Gist: One week earlier, we see Margo introducing her husband Jed (Dermot Mulroney) at a campaign event; he’s running for governor and the couple is putting on a unified front, but after finding out about Margo’s affair with Sophie, she’s been banished to the pool house. Sophie and her husband Graham (Evan Jonigkeit) are separated but in counseling.

Sophie has vowed to stay away from Margo since finding out that Margo killed Abby Jackson (Madison Wolfe), but Sophie has a secret of her own: She ran over Margo’s brother Kyle (Michael Aaron Milligan) and dragged him off into the woods. She has nightmares about it that start with her having sex with Margo. Margo, of course, is trying and failing to get in touch with Kyle, especially after Zelda Moffitt (Dale Dickey), the leader of a biker gang who Kyle was associated with, threatens her if she can’t find him.

Meanwhile, Callie (Jaime Ray Newman) and Jonny are the stars of a new reality show that Callie wants Margo to be a part of; the two of them have gotten “close” again since Margo and Sophie split. The executive producer of the show, Nadia Kelly (Kim Matula) seems to have a keen interest in the Abby Jackson case. Brad Thompson (George Ferrier) is at Baylor, haunted by the death of his mother Jill (Katie Lowes). Finally, deputies Wanda Salazar (Karen Rodriguez) and Walter Flynn (Hunter Emery) investigate the death of another teenager who was found buried in the woods.

What Shows Will It Remind You Of? The Hunting Wives, created by Rebecca Cutter and based on the novel by May Cobb, is Desperate Housewives with the addition of streaming-TV-level sex.

Our Take: Given how crazy the ending of the first season of The Hunting Wives was, with its high body count and secrets being revealed left and right, it’s hard to think of just how much more outrageous the show will go. Somehow, though, Cutter and company have managed to figure out how to do just that. It helps that this show isn’t trying to be anything more than it is, so even when it threatens to cross the border into Sillyville, it’s less of a departure than it might be on other shows.

The show still more or less revolves around Akerman and Snow, whose chemistry with each other was off the charts in the first season. They’re at odds in the first episode, but even the scene where Margo tries to apologize to Sophie in Sophie’s car has some spark. Callie and some of the other side characters are starting to develop their own personalities, but things are still mostly centered around Margo and Sophie, which is fine with us, given how much we enjoy the shit-stirring performance by Akerman and the wide-eyed innocent-but-not-really performance by Snow.

Performance Worth Watching: Akerman continues to play Margo as both sexy and troubled, able to stir it up while warding off her own problems at the same time, like the threat against her by the biker gang.

Sex And Skin: Yes, lots of both.

Parting Shot: Sophie digs out Kyle’s phone, which she’s been storing under the floorboards in her bedroom, and listens to the increasingly agitated messages from Margo.

Our Call: STREAM IT. Is The Hunting Wives an absolutely bonkers show? Yep. But it owns that bonkers nature and wears it with pride, and things are set up to only get crazier in Season 2.
