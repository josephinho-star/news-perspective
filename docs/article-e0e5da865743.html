The Pennsylvania farm behind a White House Thanksgiving turkey tradition

2026-11-22 08:55

OREFIELD, PA – Every Thanksgiving table has its traditions: Stuffing, mashed potatoes, cranberry sauce — and, of course, the turkey.

For more than six decades, one Pennsylvania farm has played a central role in one of the country's most high-profile Thanksgiving meals: the one served at the White House.

Jaindl Farms, a family-owned operation in Orefield, Pennsylvania, has supplied turkeys to U.S. presidents dating back to Dwight D. Eisenhower.

"We supplied every president since '59. There were years that we did not supply the turkey, but every president, every single president at one time or another got our turkey," said David Jaindl, owner of Jaindl Farms.

The business dates back to 1933, when Jaindl's father and grandfather started raising turkeys.

"My father and grandfather started the business. My father bought five turkeys from my grandfather," Jaindl said.

What began with just five turkeys eventually grew into a major operation producing award-winning birds.

Then, in 1959, the farm received a special request: two turkeys for the White House.

"Eisenhower was the first president to receive the turkey from us in a bag," Jaindl said.

The tradition continued through generations of presidents. While Americans may be most familiar with the two turkeys that receive a presidential pardon each Thanksgiving, the White House also receives two additional birds from Jaindl Farms for the holiday meal.

Despite the special delivery to 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue, Jaindl said the turkeys selected for the president are no different from the ones available to customers at the farm.

"It's the same turkey. I don't say, 'Look out for the best turkey that comes off the line. Don't do that. Pick two turkeys out and put them in a bag,'" he said.

For Jaindl, supplying the White House is a tremendous honor. But after decades of building the family business, what makes him most proud is seeing the next generation carry it forward.

"It's very important that my family works with me and other family members so all my children, five children, one girl and four boys, all working the business," he said.

And when it comes to what happens to the presidential turkeys after Thanksgiving, there is another tradition.

In recent years, Presidents Barack Obama and Donald Trump donated the birds supplied by Jaindl Farms to charity, continuing a holiday tradition of giving back.

For the Jaindl family, however, the biggest Thanksgiving tradition may be the one that started nearly a century ago: keeping the farm in the family.
