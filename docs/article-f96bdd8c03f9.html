Philip Rivers fights back tears during emotional intruduction as NC State coach

2026-11-17 05:17

An emotional Philip Rivers was introduced as the new head coach of NC State on Monday and had to fight back tears while answering questions.

It was a full-circle moment for the former NFL quarterback, who made a name for himself as a four-year starter for the Wolfpack from 2000 to 2003.

“I’ve got a lot still to give and there ain’t no better place or any place that I’d really want to give than right here at NC State,” Rivers told reporters during Monday’s news conference after getting emotional and having to briefly pause.

Rivers is taking over the program after Dave Doeren was fired last month amid a 3-4 start to the season, which was Doeren’s 14th at the helm of the program.

NC State athletic director Boo Corrigan began talking with Rivers about the job the day after Doeren was let go.

Rivers will begin his role as head coach after the 2026 season and had a message for Wolfpack fans on Monday.

“I don’t have the manual, and I don’t have the book, or the past years in college or the NFL to say, here we go, this is what we about to put together,” Rivers said. “But I have it right here [pointing to his head] and here [pointing to his heart] and I’ve seen it right here. We’re going to flood the [football ops] building with coaches and players that love football, that have high character and are committed to a relentless pursuit of victory.”

Rivers told a raucous crowd on Monday that the NC State job was the only college football coaching job he was going to take.

“This was it. There was no other college job that was coming for me, but had there been one, it would’ve been an easy no. This was the only thing we were thinking about from a college standpoint,” he said.

Rivers’ head coaching experience is limited.

He coached the last six seasons at St. Michael Catholic High School in Fairhope, Alabama, where his son Gunner Rivers has played.

Rivers’ son will come with him to NC State next season.

And he has a 17-year NFL career to lean on as well as he goes into the college football ranks to coach.

“I have this saying: the win-loss record goes to the quarterback and the head football coach and [I’ve experienced both],” Rivers said. “I probably got too much credit when we won and probably got too much blame when we lost. It’s going to be about a great staff, a great locker room and a great group of people all in this thing together.

“I took great pride in responsibility in that was how it was looked at. I never learned the ‘I’ part of it. It’s our team, it’s our football program, it’s our university. And that’s the way we’ll communicate and operate in the building.”
