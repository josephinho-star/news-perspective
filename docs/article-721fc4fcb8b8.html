France jails 8 Turkish nationals over handgun shipment from Germany in probe tied to İstanbul gang

2026-09-16 14:59

French judges jailed at least eight Turkish nationals pending trial in an arms trafficking case after police intercepted a van carrying 150 handguns, Agence France-Presse reported on Tuesday.

The shipment was traveling from Hannover, Germany, to the Paris region, Le Parisien reported.

Investigators suspect some of the men have ties to the Daltonlar, an İstanbul-based crime group.

Police found the weapons in six sports bags after stopping the van on September 10 on a highway in Ain, a department in eastern France.

The handguns were copies of Glock pistols, investigators said.

The network under investigation is accused of importing the weapons into France since at least 2025 and selling them for €1,300 to €2,800 each.

Police detained 12 men, most of them Turkish nationals, on September 10 and 11 in the Paris region, Marseille and other parts of France.

Seven Turkish nationals between the ages of 19 and 37 were put under formal investigation in Paris on Monday on suspicion of arms trafficking and criminal conspiracy.

All seven were jailed pending trial that evening.

An eighth Turkish national had been detained at Paris’s Gare de Lyon on August 24 while carrying about 30 Glock copies in his bags.

He was put under formal investigation and jailed pending trial on August 28.

That brought the known number of Turkish nationals jailed in the case to eight.

France’s National Organized Crime Prosecutor’s Office is leading the investigation but declined to comment.

Investigators are checking whether the arms route extended to Spain, Italy or Belgium.

They are also examining whether weapons reached the Marseille-based DZ Mafia drug network or the outlawed Kurdistan Workers’ Party (PKK), AFP learned.

Some weapons tied to the case may have been used in an attempted killing in Marseille linked to the DZ Mafia, a source close to the investigation told AFP.

A lawyer for a Kurdish man jailed on Monday said his client did not belong to the Daltonlar.

The lawyer, Mikaël Benillouche, said the man first hid weapons as a favor to people in his community and was later beaten and forced to sell them.

The Daltonlar have been accused of murder, armed attacks, extortion and drug trafficking by Turkish prosecutors, who say it uses social media to recruit young people.

An İstanbul court sentenced two senior Daltonlar figures to 12 life terms each in December 2025 and handed down prison sentences to 258 other defendants.

The case involved 362 defendants and was one of Turkey’s largest organized crime trials.

The group operated across Turkey and Europe and used social media and music videos to promote itself and find recruits, prosecutors said.
