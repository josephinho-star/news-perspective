Apple plans coding and manufacturing academies in Turkey

2026-11-10 16:29

US technology company Apple on Monday announced plans to open five developer academies across Turkey, a developer center in İstanbul and a manufacturing academy in the capital of Ankara.

The investment aims to support software developers, entrepreneurs and manufacturers and expand Turkey’s role in app development. Apple did not disclose an investment amount in its announcement.

Apple plans to open the five developer academies over the next three years, with the first set to open in İstanbul in 2027. The academies will offer free, nine-month programs in coding, design, marketing, project management and artificial intelligence for developers, designers and entrepreneurs.

The company will also open an Apple developer center in İstanbul in 2027, offering developers, entrepreneurs and startups access to Apple’s technical resources and specialists. The center will organize workshops, in-person events and individual consultations to help developers improve their apps. It will join Apple’s five existing developer centers worldwide.

In 2028, Apple plans to open a manufacturing academy in Ankara, offering free training and consulting to small and medium-sized businesses, students and entrepreneurs. The facility will be Apple’s second manufacturing academy worldwide and its first outside the United States.

The academy will provide training in machine learning, predictive maintenance, automation and robotics, along with ways to reduce waste and improve production efficiency. Apple aims to help Turkish businesses adopt AI and other manufacturing technologies.

The announcement came during a visit to Turkey by Apple Executive Chairman Tim Cook, who arrived in İstanbul on Sunday. During his visit Cook toured the Grand Bazaar with Turkish content creator Cem Başak and visited Apple’s Bağdat Caddesi store, where he met with employees and fashion designer Bünyamin Aydın, founder of the Turkish brand Les Benjamins, the Anadolu news agency reported.

Cook traveled to Ankara on Monday, where he visited Anıtkabir, the mausoleum of modern Turkey’s founder Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, before meeting with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan at the presidential complex. Industry and Technology Minister Mehmet Fatih Kacır also attended the meeting, which was closed to the press.

Erdoğan said the two discussed the future of digital technology and Apple’s support for Turkey’s app economy. He added that he hoped the meeting would benefit both Turkey and Apple.

Cook said in Apple’s announcement that the company had served customers and communities in Turkey for 35 years and wanted to support a new generation of entrepreneurs. He added that Turkish developers were creating apps used around the world.

Apple has operated developer academies since 2013 and has 19 worldwide.
