49ers’ Brock Purdy offers edge at QB vs all but NFL’s most elite passers

2026-09-07 12:00

There may not be a more improbable quarterback story in NFL history than Brock Purdy’s. He was the 262nd and final selection of the 2022 draft — the player annually handed the Mr. Irrelevant label — and opened his rookie season buried behind Trey Lance and Jimmy Garoppolo.

He was not drafted to save the 49ers. He was barely expected to play.

Then both quarterbacks ahead of him were injured, Purdy stepped into the huddle and the franchise’s trajectory changed. He won his first 10 regular-season starts, helped carry San Francisco to the NFC Championship game as a rookie and returned from a major elbow injury the following season to lead the 49ers to the Super Bowl. Along the way, he set the franchise’s single-season passing yardage record, led the NFL in passer rating and earned a Pro Bowl selection.

This is no longer a cute Mr. Irrelevant story or a temporary hot streak powered entirely by Kyle Shanahan’s system. Purdy has started 45 regular-season games, won 30 of them and added five playoff victories. He has become the stabilizing force at the center of everything the 49ers want to be: efficient, aggressive, connected and capable of competing for a championship.

Last season offered another reminder of his importance. Purdy was limited to nine games after suffering a turf toe injury in the opener, and San Francisco went 7-2 when he started. Mac Jones deserves considerable credit for going 5-3 in Purdy’s absence and helping the 49ers reach 12 victories. He kept their season afloat. But there is a difference between surviving without Purdy and reaching their ceiling with him.

With Purdy, the offense operates with greater rhythm, conviction and big-play potential. Without him, the margin for error narrows, and the 49ers become more dependent on their supporting cast and Shanahan’s ability to manufacture answers. Jones proved San Francisco could still win without its starter. The larger question is whether the 49ers can win everything without him. The answer is probably no.

That is why Purdy’s health is the defining variable of San Francisco’s 2026 season. If he stays on the field, the 49ers will enter most weeks believing they have the better quarterback — or at least one capable of matching the man on the opposite sideline.

But not every week. The schedule includes established legends, elite stars, fascinating reclamation projects and young quarterbacks still trying to define themselves. So, where does Purdy fit among them? Here is our ranking of every opposing quarterback the 49ers will face and who owns the edge in each matchup:

1. Matthew Stafford — Rams, Weeks 1 and 14

It is almost unfair. At 38, Stafford is playing some of the sharpest football of his career. His command of the position — mentally and physically — is complete, and nothing about his game suggests an imminent decline.

Purdy is plenty good. Stafford remains the standard on San Francisco’s schedule.

2. Patrick Mahomes — Chiefs, Week 16

Mahomes is returning from a serious late-season injury, which could affect his play or effectiveness early in 2026. By Week 16, however, the reasonable expectation is that he will be much closer to his familiar form — assuming there are no setbacks.

Until somebody definitively takes the crown from him, Mahomes gets the benefit of the doubt.

3. Justin Herbert — Chargers, Week 15

Herbert has performed like a top-five quarterback for years. Now the Chargers are getting both starting tackles back after they missed most of last season, and former 49ers assistant and Dolphins coach Mike McDaniel has arrived with ideas for making Herbert even more efficient.

Better protection plus a more creative plan for Herbert is a frightening combination.

4. Jayden Daniels — Commanders, Week 6

Daniels was brilliant as a rookie before an injury-plagued second season interrupted his ascent. If he is healthy and back in rhythm, he could easily rank among the first three quarterbacks on this list.

His talent is undeniable. His health and effectiveness by Week 6 are not.

5. Dak Prescott — Cowboys, Week 10

When healthy, Prescott has lived somewhere between the top 12 and top five at his position. Injuries, coaching instability and an uneven supporting cast have too often complicated the conversation around him.

Strip away the noise and the bottom line is simple: Prescott is a problem. San Francisco’s defense will have its hands full.

6. Sam Darnold — Seahawks, Weeks 5 and 12

Darnold’s career revival has gone from improbable to historic. His season in San Francisco under Shanahan, Klint Kubiak and the 49ers’ staff helped him rebuild his game, and that journey culminated in Darnold leading Seattle to a Super Bowl championship last season.

He is no longer a reclamation story. He is an accomplished division rival — and he may still have room to grow.

7. Jalen Hurts — Eagles, Week 17

Something felt off in Philadelphia last season. The Eagles’ approach too often restrained Hurts instead of emphasizing the powerful arm and dangerous legs that make him unique.

A fresher, more aggressive plan could unlock him again. Until we see it, Purdy and the 49ers get the narrow advantage.

8. Bo Nix — Broncos, Week 4

New Denver playcaller Davis Webb is expected to loosen the reins on Nix. Sean Payton frequently managed him through three quarters, then asked him to rescue games in the fourth. Webb appears more willing to let him attack from the start.

Now we find out whether Nix can thrive with more responsibility — or whether the guardrails were helping him more than anyone realized.

9. Kirk Cousins or Fernando Mendoza — Raiders, Week 9

The biggest question is who will take the snaps for Las Vegas by then. Cousins opens the season as the starter, but the Raiders did not select Mendoza first overall to keep him permanently parked on the sideline.

Ideally for Las Vegas, Mendoza has taken over by Week 9 and begun showing why the franchise invested so heavily in him. But a rookie making an early start against San Francisco — or an aging Cousins trying to hold off the future — still favors the 49ers.

10. Kyler Murray — Vikings, Week 11

Murray gets the best opportunity of his career in Minnesota under Kevin O’Connell. He will be surrounded by talent, led by Justin Jefferson, and paired with a coach known for putting quarterbacks in position to succeed.

Now Murray has to prove Arizona was the problem — not him. The setting is better, but the burden of proof remains his.

11. Jaxson Dart — Giants, Week 13

The Giants believe Dart can become their long-term answer, but his second season must begin with self-preservation. His competitiveness is admirable. The unnecessary collisions he invites are not.

Dart has to stop treating every scramble like a test of courage and start protecting himself like a franchise quarterback. Will he? Until he does, the 49ers have the edge.

12. Tua Tagovailoa or Michael Penix Jr. — Falcons, Week 7

Penix continues working back from knee surgery, while Tagovailoa arrives from Miami looking to restart his career. That leaves Atlanta with two talented quarterbacks and no shortage of uncertainty.

Either player could stabilize the position. Either situation could unravel. At the moment, volatility defines the Falcons’ quarterback room.

13. Malik Willis — Dolphins, Week 2

Willis is one of the NFL’s most intriguing quarterbacks entering 2026. He attempted only 89 passes over his final two seasons in Green Bay but maximized those opportunities by completing 78.7% of them with eight touchdowns and no interceptions.

Now comes the hard part: reproducing that efficiency over a larger sample without Green Bay’s supporting cast. The upside is fascinating. The uncertainty is impossible to ignore.

14. Jacoby Brissett, Gardner Minshew or Carson Beck — Cardinals, Weeks 3 and 18

Arizona’s quarterback situation is the murkiest on San Francisco’s schedule. It could be Brissett. It could be Minshew. It could eventually be Beck.

Beck offers the most upside, but even that projection requires a leap of faith. No matter who emerges, this is the clearest quarterback advantage the 49ers own.
