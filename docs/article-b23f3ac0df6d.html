Farage hints £72m donations to Reform UK won't be legal under retrospective measures in elections bill when it becomes law

Nigel Farage has hinted that the donations to Reform UK worth £72m might not be legal under the legislation to tighten elections law currently going through parliament.

He made the apparent concession in an interview with the BBC in which he strongly objected to the government’s plan to make a new law banning Britons who live abroad, or who have recently relocated to the UK, from giving more than £100,000 a year to political parties retrospective.

It seems likely that the law would apply to Ben Delo and Christopher Harborne, the two cryptocurrency billionaires who have each pledged £36m to Reform UK.

In an interview, Chris Mason, the BBC’s political editor, asked Farage if he was sure that both donations were compliant with the law now, and “how we expect it soon to be”.

Both these donations are 100% compliant with the law today. Of that there is no question whatsoever.

Mason pointed out that Farage was saying “today” and he asked if Delo and Harborne were both resident in the UK on 25 March – after which the retrospective restrictions on donations by new arrivals will kick in when the representation of the people bill becomes law.

I’m not going to go into their particular details. All I’m going to say is, with the law of the land as it stands, they are both compliant.

Now there is talk, and Angela Rayner was talking about this yesterday, there is talk of them retrospectively declaring donations illegally.

Well, that simply cannot be. If a government was allowed to do this, you know what would happen? There’d be a response the other way. We or the Conservatives would say, right, if you’re going to stop legitimate donors like this, we will then say to the trade unions, you can’t give money. And if you do that, you have collapsed the whole political system.

In fact, there has not just been “talk” of making the cap retrospective. The bill legislating for this has gone through the Commons, and MPs debated amendments creating these exact laws on Wednesday 2 September.

While laws applying retrospectively are rare, they are not unprecedented. Reform UK wants to pass retrospective legislation itself. For example, it plans to abolish indefinite leave to remain as an immigration status, including for people who already have it.

Farage’s position is different from the one adopted by his party over the weekend when Robert Jenrick, the Treasury spokesperson, said that Reform UK was confident the Delo and Harborne donations were “entirely in line with the law as it is and, as far as we know, the law as it’s going to be in the future”.

Minister says elections bill designed to ensure votes of 'crypto bros' don't count more than voters of ordinary workers

In the Lords, peers have just started the second reading debate on the representation of the people bill. Sharon Taylor, a local government minister, is opening on behalf of the government.

She starts by saying she wants to make a reference to the “events of recent times”.

She recalls catching the train early in the morning with tradespeople, public sector workers and small business owners heading into work early and she goes on:

It’s a fundamental principle of our democracy that every one of their votes is exactly equal to the votes of millionaires, crypto bros and industry magnates, and it is that which sits at the front and centre of this bill.

We must put politics back under public control.

We all share concerns about the rise of big money and its ability to dominate public power.

At its heart, this bill is about renewing the foundations of our electoral system so that it remains fair, secure, transparent and accessible in the years ahead.

Rayner ended her speech at the TUC praising Andy Burnham. She said she was glad to hear him “talk about doing things the Labour way”.

And she said, as a former care worker who has campaigned for a better care system, she was particularly pleased about Burnham’s commitment to reform adult social care.

As a former care worker myself, I have waited my whole life for a prime minister to speak about social care the way Andy has, understanding what care workers do and the conditions that they face.

Rayner said the government would not let “any foreign dictator or fascist oligarch” buy British politics.

And I tell Nigel Farage, you can’t pay your way out of accountability to the people of this country.

So let me be clear. We will defend our democracy and society from any foreign dictator or fascist oligarch who seeks to divide our country and buy our politics.

But we will never undermine the rights of working people to organise collectively and democratically.

Rayner says trade union donations to Labour 'most highly regulated political funding we have'

Rayner criticises Reform UK for suggesting its donations from cryptocurrency billionaires are like the contributions from trade union members to Labour.

How dare they try to compare money from a couple of crypto billionaires to union members’ subs?

The most highly regulated political funding we can have.

It couldn’t be more different from a couple of offshore billionaires trying to buy our democracy.

Rayner says crypto donations to Reform UK show Nigel Farage won't defend workers' interests

Rayner stresses the links between the Labour party and the union movement.

The Labour party was not simply born out of the trade union movement.

We’ve grown up together side by side. Our histories are shared and so are our futures.

Together, we serve working people for generations, and together we must fight the next fight into the future, too.

She says Labour and the unions are like family. They don’t always agree, but they stick together.

Reform UK would repeal the Employment Rights Act, she says.

They voted against the Employment Rights Act, and if elected, they’d repeal it in a heartbeat. Along with the Equalities Act.

Think of what that means. Millions of workers no longer protected from exploitative zero-hours contracts. Millions of workers, but waiting three days to be paid when they’re sick. Millions of workers who could be fired and rehired on worse terms and conditions.

And if you needed proof of whose side they’re really on, they have given it to us this weekend.

Nigel Farage’s sugar daddies made their money in crypto, but there’s nothing cryptic about their motives. They know full well that a Reform government will act in their interests, not the public interest.

Now, when these Reform donors say they want nothing, they mean it. No workers’ rights, no renter’s rights, no social protections, no safety net, no regulation, nothing to stand in their way.

Angela Rayner, the housing secretary, is addressing the TUC conference.

She says it is three years since she was last giving a speech here.

She says she delivered on the pledge to have a workers’ rights bill ready 100 days after Labour won the election, and she thinks the unions for their help with this.

TUC calls for law saying strike ballots must have 50% turnout to be valid to be abolished

The government is being urged to abolish the law on a threshold for union industrial action ballots and any link to bringing in e-balloting, the Press Association reports. PA says:

At the TUC conference, Matt Wrack, general secretary of NASUWT, said if the 50% threshold applied in public elections, many of the current cabinet would not be in parliament. The rule says strike ballots are only valid if they have a turnout of at least 50%.

Wrack told delegates: “Attacks on union rights have been central to delivering the agenda of the banks, the bosses, the billionaires, privatisation and inequality – and serve only to shift power to those at the top.

“That is why we welcomed Labour’s pledges before the last election. At last, we could have a government willing to start to turn the tide on workers’ rights.

“However, trade unions still face 50% ballot thresholds. We were promised that legislation to abolish that act would be introduced within 100 days of the election. So why, two years down the line, is the 50% threshold still in place?

“I don’t know how ministers count 100 days but by standard arithmetic, two years is significantly beyond the timescale set out in promises to voters.”

Wrack said it was “scandalous” that the UK was in breach of various ILO (International Labour Organisation) conventions on industrial action.

He added: “It is scandalous that UK workers do not enjoy an actual right to strike, including the right to take solidarity action.

“This lack of rights exacerbates all the factors which create such an unequal, bleak and dystopian world offering no future for millions.

“No more delays, abolish the threshold immediately.”

Delegates backed the union’s call for the immediate removal of both the 50% ballot threshold and any linkage between the removal of the threshold and the implementation of e-balloting.
