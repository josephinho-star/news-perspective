Democrat Haley Stevens endorses Abdul El-Sayed’s Republican rival Mike Rogers

2026-11-01 18:55

Congresswoman Haley Stevens, who lost the Michigan Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed his Republican rival, Mike Rogers, in an extraordinary rebuke of her party’s nominee on the eve of the midterm elections.

Her endorsement came in a video posted to X after she attended an event with Rogers at the predominantly Black Perfecting church in Detroit on Sunday. Stevens’s endorsement was the culmination of an escalating feud between the former primary rivals that has roiled one of the most fiercely contested Senate contests of the cycle.

The endorsement was met with immediate pushback from Democrats, who condemned Stevens for her “betrayal”.

Neera Tanden, the former senior adviser to president Joe Biden, wrote on X: “Standing with Mike Rogers is a betrayal of everything Haley Stevens has fought for her entire career. It’s also a betrayal of all of her supporters that believed in her. I hope Abdul El-Sayed has a strong victory on Tuesday and that all Democrats and like-minded independents support vote for him.”

Michigan’s Democratic party chair Curtis Hertel said: “Haley Stevens’ decision to campaign with Rogers – a man who has repeatedly sold out Michigan’s working class, wants to pass a national abortion ban, and has pledged to be a loyal vote for Trump’s agenda – shows that she has abandoned everything she claims to care about. We reject the attempts by Stevens and bad-faith actors to divide our party and undermine our momentum, and will not allow these eleventh-hour distractions deter us.”

Some of the people who worked for Stevens’s campaign also reportedly quit following her Rogers endorsement. Her long-time chief of staff, Justin German, and her communications director, Logan Basch, were among those who left, according to CNN correspondent Manu Raju.

Stevens had lost by a close margin in this year’s primary, and for months had refused to openly campaign for El-Sayed, as had his other opponent Mallory McMorrow. But top Democrats had brokered the truce between El-Sayed and Stevens. After the two had sparred viciously in the primary campaign Stevens extended him a tepid endorsement, and this weekend was scheduled to join El-Sayed at a predominantly Black church in Detroit’s North End.

Those plans have quickly unraveled over an issue that divided Stevens and El-Sayed perhaps more than any other: Israel.

Later last week, Stevens, a staunch supporter of Israel, made clear that her appearance alongside El-Sayed was preconditioned on his denouncing of an outside group’s campaign ad that had blamed US support of Israel for the rising cost of living. El-Sayed declined the request, accusing Stevens of needlessly reopening debates from a settled primary.

As the feud escalated, El-Sayed’s campaign went further, formally disinviting Stevens from his campaign events in the closing days of the campaign. Debbie Dingell, a Michigan congresswoman, later told CNN she had been the one to tell Stevens “this isn’t working” when it came to Stevens’s appearance with El-Sayed on Sunday. Dingell added that the tensions between the two were a distraction and that “there’s nothing to be gained by the two of them being in the same place”.

Earlier on Sunday Stevens had put out a statement that said she expected El-Sayed to win and telling people to vote. But just hours later she appeared with Donald Trump’s Senate pick and in one comment said she supported Rogers’s purported future investment in Black businesses.

As the vicious political firestorm has unfolded, Democratic commentators have also pointed out that Stevens’s fiance, Jason Turkish, has been condemning El-Sayed publicly while supporting Rogers. Turkish, who is also vocally pro-Israel, has donated heavily to Democrats in the past, but this year also donated to Republican candidates including Susan Collins.

On Sunday, Senate majority leader Chuck Schumer didn’t name Stevens but pushed a unity message.

“Senate Democrats are united in our support for Abdul El-Sayed, because we know he will fight for the economic interests of working families in Michigan, unlike Mike Rogers, who is a tool of Donald Trump and billionaire donors. We Democrats equally united in our total revulsion at the racist, Islamophobic, and over-the-top false attacks on Abdul by Republicans. These attacks are evidence of the total bankruptcy of the Republican Party today, who resort to ugly and un-American fearmongering because they have nothing to offer on the problems that concern people today.”
