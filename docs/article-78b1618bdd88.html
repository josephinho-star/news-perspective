Wheelchair-bound man dies from injuries sustained in mysterious shooting 36 years ago: ‘Suffered constant pain’

2026-11-19 01:41

A man left paralyzed after a 1990 Brooklyn shooting — who decades later delivered harrowing court testimony against the Chelsea bomber — died from the gunshot wound he suffered in the unsolved attack, police said Wednesday.

Cort Cheek, 67, was allegedly shot in the abdomen by a mystery gunman outside 57 Lincoln Road in Prospect Lefferts Gardens on June 23, 1990. He was struck by a stranger who approached Cheek as he was walking home, according to sources.

It remains unclear, however, whether there was a dispute that led to the shooting, sources said.

Cheek died on April 29, when a home aide found him slumped over on the floor inside his Chelsea apartment. EMS pronounced him dead at the scene.

On Friday, the medical examiner ruled it a homicide, determining complications from the old gunshot wound caused his death.

No arrests were ever made — and the case remains open, according to police.

The shooting had left Cheek paralyzed from the waist down.

Bound to a wheelchair, he went on to live at Selis Manor on West 23rd Street and 6th Avenue — a housing facility for the blind and disabled — placing him directly in harm’s way when terror struck.

Cheek was outside his building on Sept. 17, 2016, when homegrown terrorist Ahmad Rahimi detonated a pressure cooker nearby.

The blast sent shrapnel and debris flying through the air, leaving 31 people injured. Luckily, Cheek wasn’t harmed in the explosion.

Prosecutors determined Rahimi was inspired by ISIS and al Qaeda. He was arrested following a bloody shootout with police in Linden, N.J.

Taking the witness stand in Manhattan Federal Court during Rahimi’s 2017 trial, he gave haunting testimony about the moment of terror.

“I heard sirens going off, people screaming,” Cheek said.

“I thought it was doomsday,” he told the court, comparing the “double boom” to “the end of the world.”

Surveillance footage shown by prosecutors depicted Cheek fleeing from debris in his wheelchair.

The Chelsea bombing was accompanied by a twin blast in Seaside Park, New Jersey, which failed to injure anyone.

Cheek was a member of the New York City Community Chorus, which mourned his death in an Instagram post on May 20.

“Cort was a member of our Chorus for more than 25 years. He was a frequent soloist. He met us at a street fair all those years ago, and had been looking for a musical outlet,” the post read.

“Cort suffered constant pain and severe health limitations, but lived with generosity, humor, and optimism. He was the soul of our Chorus. We will miss him.”
