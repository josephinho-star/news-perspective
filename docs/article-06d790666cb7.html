Turkey ready to meet Saudi military needs amid Houthi attacks: FM

2026-09-20 20:42

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan has said Ankara would be prepared to meet possible Saudi military needs, particularly in technical areas, as Yemen’s Houthis intensify attacks on the kingdom.

Fidan made the remarks during an interview with Turkish broadcaster NTV on Saturday, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

He said the attacks posed a serious threat to Saudi Arabia’s territorial integrity, sovereignty and infrastructure and that Turkey had responsibilities toward the kingdom under a three-way defense agreement that also includes Pakistan.

“If there are military requirements, particularly in certain technical areas, we would have no difficulty meeting them,” Fidan said.

He did not say that Riyadh had formally requested military assistance or specify what kind of technical support Ankara might provide.

Fidan said the three countries were holding consultations that had not been made public and were jointly assessing the situation.

Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement on August 7. The agreement stipulates that an armed attack on one member will be treated as an attack on all three.

The pact brings together Turkey, which has NATO’s second-largest military, Pakistan, the only Muslim-majority country with nuclear weapons, and Saudi Arabia, one of the world’s largest oil exporters.

Fidan said efforts to establish a secretariat and an affiliated military headquarters were almost complete, with the deployment of personnel the remaining step before the headquarters becomes operational.

He described the alliance as a “game-changing” platform.

Foreign and defense ministers and military chiefs from the three countries met in İstanbul on August 31 for the first meeting of the agreement’s Strategic Political and Defense Committee.

They agreed to establish a secretariat in Saudi Arabia, initially headed by a Pakistani official for a three-year term, and to take further steps toward making the alliance operational.

No member had publicly requested the activation of the agreement or announced a combined military response to the Houthi attacks.

A senior Turkish official said earlier that the pact’s defense commitments were not yet legally in force in Turkey and that it could be submitted to parliament for approval in October.

Fidan’s remarks marked a shift from Ankara’s earlier public response, which had been limited to diplomatic support for Riyadh.

Turkey last week condemned what Saudi Arabia described as a Houthi drone attack targeting Mecca and reiterated its support for the kingdom’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and security. The Houthis denied targeting the holy city.

The Iran-backed group on Saturday claimed missile and drone attacks on what it called “sensitive targets” in Riyadh and a separate operation against Saudi Aramco facilities in the Red Sea city of Yanbu.

Saudi Arabia said it intercepted a ballistic missile targeting Riyadh. Residents reported hearing explosions, while flames and a large plume of smoke were seen near King Khalid International Airport. There were no immediate reports of casualties.

The Houthis have stepped up attacks on Saudi cities, energy infrastructure and shipping since declaring a maritime blockade against the kingdom in July. The escalation has threatened oil exports and shipping through the Red Sea at a time when traffic through the Strait of Hormuz also remains disrupted.

Fidan said Saudi Arabia should be kept out of the broader conflict between the United States and Iran because Riyadh had not sought to become involved.

He said new proposals had been conveyed to the parties in an effort to end the fighting but warned that the economic effects of the conflict were becoming increasingly difficult to tolerate.

Turkey, which depends heavily on imported energy, faces growing economic pressure from disruption to regional energy routes, Fidan said.
