GOV JEFF LANDRY: SpaceX bets on Louisiana as America’s next frontier takes off in the South

2026-08-29 11:00

For generations, Americans looked west when they thought about opportunity. Today, they should look south.

Louisiana has always powered America. We fueled the nation with energy, fed it through our farms and ports, built ships that defended it, and manufactured the products that helped it grow.

Now, we are helping lead America’s next frontier: space.

With SpaceX choosing Louisiana for its newest chapter, our state is taking its place at the center of the economic and technological race that will define the 21st century. This is more than a win for Louisiana. It is a win for America.

For many years, Louisiana saw transformative investments land somewhere else. We watched other states attract the industries of the future while too many of our own left in search of opportunity.

Not anymore.

SpaceX did not choose Louisiana by accident.

It chose Louisiana because we made a deliberate decision to compete.

Over the past two years, we have cut taxes, reduced bureaucracy, modernized economic development, strengthened workforce training, and created an environment where major employers know they can build and succeed.

The results speak for themselves.

Louisiana has secured more than $250 billion in announced private investment. Meta is building one of the largest artificial intelligence data centers in history in North Louisiana. Hyundai Steel is investing billions in advanced manufacturing along our River. And Louisiana continues to solidify its position as America’s energy powerhouse through transformative LNG investments — including Woodside’s $17.5 billion Louisiana LNG project, Venture Global’s $18 billion expansion, and Commonwealth LNG’s $13 billion facility.

Together, these projects are creating thousands of jobs, strengthening American energy security, and cementing Louisiana’s role as a global energy leader.

Louisiana is not betting its future on a single industry. We are building an economy that leads in energy, manufacturing, technology, and now aerospace.

SpaceX is the latest chapter in that story.

Some will see rockets. I see what they make possible.

I see high-paying jobs for Louisiana workers. I see engineers, welders, electricians, fabricators, software developers, and skilled tradespeople building the future right here. I see students who once believed they had to leave Louisiana to pursue careers in technology and innovation realizing they can build those careers at home. I see communities gaining investment, growth, and optimism. Most importantly, I see Louisiana becoming a state that attracts talent instead of exporting it.

That matters because the greatest challenge facing our state has never been a lack of resources. It has been a lack of opportunity.

For decades, Louisiana was known for what we were losing. Today, we are becoming known for what we are gaining.

The proof is already visible.

Louisiana recently recorded population growth for the second consecutive year. Young adults accounted for most of that growth. Our labor force participation rate recorded one of the largest increases in America as thousands more Louisianians entered or reentered the workforce.

People are choosing Louisiana again.

SpaceX did not create that momentum.

It confirms it.

This project joins a growing list of investments that send the same message to the country and the world: Louisiana is open for business, ready to compete, and prepared to win.

For too long, Louisiana was told to think smaller. We were told the industries of the future belonged somewhere else.

SpaceX proves otherwise.

The same state that helped America reach the moon through generations of aerospace manufacturing is now helping shape the future of space exploration itself. That should make every Louisianian proud.

More importantly, it should make every American optimistic.

Because when companies choose to build in Louisiana, they are choosing to build in America.

The future of American energy, artificial intelligence, manufacturing, and now space exploration is being written in places willing to compete for it. Louisiana is competing.

Louisiana is winning.

And if SpaceX’s decision proves anything, it is that America's next frontier runs straight through Louisiana.
