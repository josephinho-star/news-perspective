The best oily skincare products I swear by to control shine and breakouts

2026-09-01 14:51

Among all the other daily stressors we may run into — finding an acne spot treatment, picking a makeup remover that actually work — dealing with oily skin is one we want to rid for good.

Unlike dry skin, where eczema may abound, oily skin feels slick and looks shiny (which, can literally put a damper on your day).

With a smorgasbord of skincare products to choose from — toners, retinol creams and serums, the whole nine yards — we turned to two board-certified dermatologists to give us the 411 on the most sought-after products you’re likely searching for at the moment.

- Best Face Wash: CeraVe Acne Control Cleanser

- Best Serum: Alpyn Niacinamide & Nettle Firming Serum

- Best Moisturizer: Medicube 3H Relief Cream

- Best Sunscreen: Supergoop! Mineral Mattescreen SPF 40

- Best Sheet Mask: Biodance Refreshing Sea Kelp Real Deep Mask (4-Pack)

“You can notice that you have oily skin if you have enlarged pores, acne or blackheads,” Marina Peredo, MD, a board-certified dermatologist specializing in aesthetic dermatology and associate clinical professor at Mount Sinai Hospital with 31 years of experience, told the New York Post. “Those who have oily skin typically have an overall shine to their face and break out on the T-zone and cheeks.”

Conversely, combination skin has a similar-yet-different texture.

“Combination skin is common because sebaceous glands are more abundant in specific areas of the face, along the nose, lower forehead, and upper cheeks, commonly referred to as the T-zone,” Nava Greenfield, MD, board-certified dermatologist at Schweiger Dermatology Group and clinical instructor at Mount Sinai department of dermatology who specializes in acne, told The Post. “In those locations skin can appear more oily but may look normal to dry in places where sebaceous glands area more sparse.”

When it comes to finding the best ingredients for oily skin, our experts recommend looking for products with niacinamide, which absorbs the oil on the skin, retinol which promotes cell turnover and eliminates clogged pores, plus salicylic acid, which helps fight acne.

How can I tell if I have oily skin?

“The easiest way to tell if you have oily skin is to observe your skin for a day,” Sapna Palep, MD, board-certified dermatologist and co-founder of Journelle Skin, shared. “Start by washing skin in the morning and don’t apply any skincare products or ingredients.”

If your skin feels slick, look shiny or glossy, or reflects light, then you likely have oily skin. “Some people with oily skin have oily skin all over, some only have oily spots in portions, most commonly the T zone (forehead, nose and chin),” she added.

What are the best ingredients for oily skin?

“Salicylic acid is one of my favorites because it is oil-soluble, meaning it can penetrate into the pore and help remove the oil and dead skin cells that contribute to clogged pores and acne,” Whitney Hovenic, board-certified dermatologist, MOHS surgeon and co-founder of sunscreen brand SPOOGE, shared.

Niacinamide is another great ingredient. “It can help with the appearance of excess oil while also supporting the skin barrier, which is especially helpful because people with oily skin tend to overdo exfoliation,” Dr. Hovenic explained.

She also recommends retinoids, which are excellent for oily, acne-prone skin because they help normalize cell turnover and prevent clogged pores. Adapalene is a good over-the-counter option, per Hovenic, that causes less irritation, as it tends to get better absorbed into the areas where skin oil is being produced.

“I also like ingredients such as azelaic acid, particularly when oiliness comes with acne, redness or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation,” Hovenic said. “And, don’t forget barrier-supporting ingredients like ceramides and hyaluronic acid. Oily skin still needs hydration!”

What are the best types of serums for oily skin?

“I recommend lightweight, fast-absorbing serums that provide targeted benefits without leaving the skin feeling heavy or greasy,” Lindsey Zubritsky, MD, board-certified dermatologist and member of the Elizabeth Arden Dermatologist Collective, shared with The Post. “Oily skin still needs hydration and barrier support, so ingredients like ceramides can be particularly beneficial.”

What are the best types of moisturizers for oily skin?

“Oily skin still needs moisture, and skipping moisturizer can actually contribute to an imbalanced skin barrier,” Dr. Zubritsky saiad. “Look for lightweight, non-greasy formulas that provide hydration and barrier support without feeling heavy or occlusive.”

What should I look for in an SPF for oily skin?

Daily, broad-spectrum SPF is essential for every skin type. “For oily skin, I recommend looking for a lightweight formula that feels comfortable and absorbs easily, since the best sunscreen is one you’ll actually use consistently,” Zubritsky said.

Should I use skincare devices or facial treatments on oily skin?

“As many people with oily skin also tend to have acne breakouts, blue light LED therapy is beneficial to add to your skincare regimen,” Dr. Palep shared. “Blue light kills acne-causing bacteria and helps regulate sebum production.”

Adding a clay mask to your weekly routine is a great way to absorb excess oil as well. “I wouldn’t do a clay mask more than once a week, and if you find that your skin isn’t as oily when you add moisturizers and other products to the skin, drop it down to using it two to three times a month,” Palep recommends.
