Schengen states form group to review visa procedures for Turks

2026-11-20 14:20

Schengen countries’ diplomatic missions in Turkey have formed a working group to review visa procedures for Turkish citizens following talks with Ankara, Turkish diplomatic sources told the TRT Haber state broadcaster on Thursday.

Turkish Foreign Ministry officials met with representatives of Schengen countries, the European Union delegation and the European Commission in Ankara on Wednesday. The Local Schengen Cooperation group, which reviews visa procedures and application data, met with ministry and commission representatives participating for the first time.

Turkey requested more visa appointments, faster decisions, fewer documents and simpler procedures for applicants who had previously received Schengen visas.

Ankara also called for broader use of the EU’s “cascade” rules, which allow travelers who comply with visa conditions to obtain multiple-entry visas valid for progressively longer periods of time.

The European Commission adopted more favorable rules on July 15, 2025, for Turkish citizens living and applying in Turkey. Eligible applicants can qualify for one-year visas, followed by three-year and five-year visas, depending on their travel history and passport validity. Visits are still limited to 90 days in any 180-day period.

The rules exclude Turkish truck drivers applying in their professional capacity. Turkey separately requested uninterrupted Schengen access for drivers and changes to stay limits that it says disrupt trade.

Schengen consulates in Turkey processed approximately 1.27 million visa applications in 2025, up from 1.17 million in 2024, an increase of about 8 percent. Turkey ranked second globally after China for applications submitted.

Multiple-entry visas accounted for 68.7 percent of visas issued in Turkey in 2025, up from 65 percent in 2024. The rejection rate rose from 14.5 percent to 14.6 percent.

Appointment shortages had encouraged visa consultancies operating outside the law, prompting operations coordinated with the Justice, Trade and Interior ministries and legal proceedings, the Foreign Ministry said.

Visa difficulties for Turkish students admitted to EU universities had decreased compared with the previous two years, with few complaints received this year, ministry officials added.

Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said Turkey had a Schengen visa approval rate of 85.4 percent, citing figures for 2023 through 2025, during a parliamentary budget committee meeting on Thursday.

Applications from Turkey were increasing, while political competition over immigration in Europe was also affecting applicants, he added.

Fidan described visa-free travel as the long-term solution. Draft measures to meet the six outstanding EU requirements were nearing completion and would come before parliament after talks on the government’s 2027 budget, he said.

Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz announced in August that several ministries had begun work on those requirements. The outstanding issues concern personal data protection, counterterrorism laws and implementation of an agreement requiring Turkey to take back migrants who entered the EU through its territory.

The European Parliament’s lead lawmaker on Turkey, Nacho Sánchez Amor, criticized Ankara in March for seeking to expand access to special passports that allow visa-free travel rather than completing the reforms needed to extend that access to ordinary passport holders. Six of the original 72 requirements were still unmet, he said.

In a resolution adopted on June 17, the European Parliament called on Turkey to meet those requirements and urged EU member states to devote more resources to reducing administrative barriers for applicants. Turkey’s Foreign Ministry rejected the broader report, describing its assessments as politically motivated and based on misinformation.

The EU launched its visa liberalization dialogue with Turkey in December 2013 along with the migrant readmission agreement. Another outstanding requirement concerns an agreement allowing the exchange of personal data between Europol, the EU’s law enforcement agency, and Turkish authorities combating serious crime and terrorism.

Turkish citizens with ordinary passports need visas for short visits to the Schengen area, which comprises 29 European countries. Fidan and EU Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos welcomed the rules easing access to multiple-entry visas during talks in February, but the European Commission’s Turkey report, published this month, found no progress in meeting the six outstanding requirements for visa-free travel.
