US midterm elections 2026 live updates: Trump defiant about brutal Republican losses and acknowledges Iran war’s unpopularity

2026-11-04 19:09

Donald Trump says that Vance's comment about Iran war kneecapping Republicans in catastrophic midterms are 'largely true'

Donald Trump responded to JD Vance’s comments today that the war in Iran was ultimately the reason behind the catastrophic Republican losses in the House and Senate in this year’s midterms.

“I think it’s largely true because it drove the gasoline up a little bit short term,” Trump said. “But at the same time … I’d have to do it again because long term, it’s the only answer.”

The president said that he felt the decision to begin the conflict was unavoidable. “I thought the oil would go up a lot more,” Trump said. “You have no choice. You have to do it. You can’t let Iran have a nuclear weapon.”

Trump once again insisted that if he were on the ballot Republicans would have won their races, which in many cases they lost to Democrats in historic landslides.

“They just couldn’t transfer that love,” Trump said. “There was such love at those rallies. It was incredible. We weren’t able to transfer the love to candidates.”

House Democrats will convene starting Nov. 18 to vote on the caucus’s leadership under the new Democratic majority, according to a memo circulated by Democratic House caucus chair Pete Aguilar and and Ted Lieu.

Major races we're still waiting on, and when we might know the result

There are a few key races that we’re still waiting on for the final result, including the final two Senate contests.

In Mississippi, 96% of the vote has been counted in the nail-biting US Senate race between Democrat Scott Colom and Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith. As of now Colom is leading by just 65 votes. We could get the final call any minute, in what could be an historic flip. Democrats haven’t won a US Senate seat in Mississippi since 1982, and Trump carried the state by 22 points in 2024.

In Alaska, the fierce competition for US Senate is still under way and too close to call. As of now, 74% of the vote has been counted and Democrat Peltola is leading the GOP incumbent Dan Sullivan by less than a point. A reminder, Alaska uses ranked choice voting to determine a winner. This means that the winner needs to receive 50% of first choice votes. If that doesn’t happen, the candidate with the fewest “first preference” votes is eliminated, and their supporters’ votes are reassigned to their second choice. That process will continue until one candidate wins a majority. As a result, it means that the final result could take longer than any other race.

Meanwhile, in the Los Angeles mayoral race, the battle between the Democratic incumbent, Karen Bass, and her progressive challenger Nithya Raman, a city council member, is also one to watch. Only 63% of the vote has been counted so far, and Raman has a narrow lead.

Trump also said that he was not considering replacing Pete Hegseth as defense secretary. “I think he’s doing a very good job,” the president said.

This comes after reports circulated that Marsha Blackburn could appoint Hegseth to finish out the remainder of her US Senate seat in Tennessee, after she won her contest for governor.

“I actually think he’d be very popular if he did that. But I haven’t heard that at all,” the president added.

Donald Trump didn’t give a clear answer about whether he knew JD Vance was going to post about the Iran war costing Republicans control of Congress.

“I know how he feels about it. I think it was. I think it was fine. I thought it was good,” Trump said, while remaining belligerent about his decision to start the conflict. “I can tell you that as far as that war is concerned, I would have done nothing different.”

Florida’s governor, Ron DeSantis, moved quickly today to position himself among the leading pack of 2028 Republican presidential hopefuls by attacking party colleagues who lost seats in the midterms bloodbath.

Bucking the national trend, but just barely, Florida voters elected Republicans to the US Senate and governor’s mansion yesterday, achievements DeSantis boasted were because of his eight-year term as governor that ends in January.

“People can look at Florida and show how you can deliver big results and how you can build a durable coalition,” he told a press conference in Tallahassee.

“Why did Florida hold up? Why were we able to hold the line? The answer is we’ve built up a record of achievement. We’ve earned trust from our voters that even in the worst situation that you can imagine politically, we were able to prevent the worst from happening here,” he said.

DeSantis had harsh words for Republicans in Washington who he said failed to deliver campaign promises, including releasing the Epstein files and cleaning up corruption.

“We ran as Republicans on saying we need to do that, and then you actually have people in the Congress voting against release when the attorney general said she had all this horrific stuff on her desk and that people were going to be held accountable,” he said. “It’s untenable when you have people trading stock. Why don’t they ban congressional stock trading? It would be a breath of fresh air.”

DeSantis’s run for the 2024 presidential nomination, won by Donald Trump, ended in humiliation when he dropped out early. Speaking to reporters today, he said he would “talk about anything we’re doing” in terms of the 2028 contest after he is termed out of office on 5 January.

Analysts see DeSantis as all but certain to run again. In a blistering podcast interview yesterday, he attacked Trump’s handling of the Iran war, the economy and the president’s second-term immigration crackdown.

Top House Democrat says party will hold hearings and pass regulations on AI, datacenters

Ted Lieu, a California representative and vice-chair of the House Democratic caucus, said that Republicans had made a mistake by failing to regulate AI while they controlled Congress – and that Democrats would step in to change that.

“Two things will change next term,” Lieu said. “We will hold public hearings on AI. We will pass legislation, reasonable guardrails, on AI.”

Democrats, Lieu said, would specifically take a closer look at guardrails around the building of new datacenters, after the breakneck pace of construction for those facilities drew the bipartisan ire of communities around the country.

That pledge sets Democrats up for a potential showdown with Donald Trump, who has vowed to veto any Democrat legislation and who has harshly criticized the idea of reining in AI development.

Trump avoids speaking about stunning Republican losses in first public event post-midterms

Donald Trump is addressing members of the press for the first time since Republicans lost control of Congress in a stunning midterm defeat.

The president has yet to mention Democrats flipping seven Senate seats and securing 229 seats in the US house, with more victories expected.

Instead, Trump has focused on speaking about his East Room ballroom project, the new helipad on the White House lawn and his various “beautification” projects around Washington DC.

Pressed on whether the Democratic House caucus would pursue the impeachment of Donald Trump upon assuming a majority in the chamber, Hakeem Jeffries seemed to downplay the possibility

“I haven’t had a single member of the House Democratic caucus talk to me about impeachment,” Jeffries said to a group of reporters. “We’ve been relentlessly focused on affordability and accountability.”

Still, Jeffries did assert that a Democratic-controlled House of Representatives would assert its authority to rein in the Trump administration.

“The days of the House of Representatives functioning like a wholly owned subsidiary of the Trump administration are over. House Democrats are going to lead a Congress that does serve as a check and balance on an out of control executive branch.”

Asked separately whether he would consider investigating or impeaching Pete Hegseth, Trump’s Defense Secretary, Jeffries responded dismissively – “Is Pete Hegseth still around?” – before moving on to the next question.

Hakeem Jeffries said that neither he nor Chuck Schumer has spoken with Donald Trump since Republicans’ brutal defeat in the 2026 midterm elections.

“I have not heard from President Trump,” Jeffries told a group of reporters. “It is my understanding that leader Schumer has not heard from President Trump.”

Democrat Christina Bertrand Hines flips red Michigan House district

Democrat Christina Bertrand Hines has flipped the solidly Republican 10th congressional district in Michigan, which is now held by John James – who lost his governor’s race to Democrat Jocelyn Benson.

Bertrand Hines beat her Republican challenger, Michael Bouchard, for control of the district, which a GOP representative has held for more than a decade.

Hakeem Jeffries: Democrats have been handed a mandate 'to make life more affordable'

Speaking to reporters on Wednesday, the House minority leader and soon-to-be speaker of the House, Hakeem Jeffries, characterized the midterm election results as a “stunning rebuke of Maga extremism.”

“Mike Johnson was correct,” Jeffries continued, referencing the now-outgoing Republican House speaker. “The polls were wrong. It wasn’t simply a blue wave, it was an American tsunami.”

In his remarks, Jeffries highlighted what he said would the party’s main priority now that it has control of legislative branch. The American people, Jeffries said, had tasked House Democrats with “a relentless effort to make life more affordable”.

That included, Jeffries said, reversing cuts to Medicaid, extending Affordable Care Act tax credits, undoing Trump’s tariff regime and ending Trump’s “reckless and costly war of choice” with Iran.

Mississippi US Senate race still on knife's edge with 96% of votes counted

The US Senate race in Mississippi is still on a knife’s edge, with Democrat Scott Colom leading Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith by just 43 votes, with 96% of the final votes counted.

If he’s successful, Colom would be the first Democratic senator that Mississippi has sent to Washington since 1982.

We’ll be keeping an eye on the final result, which would be the eighth Senate flip for Democrats in this astonishing midterm election, and in a state which Donald Trump carried by 22 points in 2024.
