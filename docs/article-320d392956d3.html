Turkey partners with Danish company to develop thorium nuclear fuel

2026-09-07 14:38

Turkey’s nuclear research agency has signed a memorandum of understanding with Denmark-based Copenhagen Atomics to develop domestic production of thorium compounds for advanced nuclear reactors, the Energy Ministry said Monday.

The agreement focuses on producing materials that meet nuclear fuel standards, particularly for use in molten salt reactors, which use liquid salts to carry nuclear fuel and transfer heat. Turkey hopes the research will help it use its thorium deposits and eventually become an international supplier of nuclear fuel materials.

Turkey has thorium-bearing deposits in Eskişehir in central Turkey and Malatya in the east, among other locations. The Energy Ministry says the country has considerable potential but faces technical challenges in exploiting those resources.

The Turkish Energy, Nuclear and Mineral Research Agency (TENMAK), which operates under the ministry, signed the memorandum in the presence of Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar.

The announcement did not specify an investment amount or a timetable for commercial production.

Copenhagen Atomics is developing a modular molten salt reactor concept called “Onion Core,” in which liquid salts carry nuclear fuel and transfer heat. Under the agreement, the company and TENMAK will assess how Turkish research facilities, scientists and industrial companies could contribute to the technology.

Bayraktar said the agreement would support research in using thorium in small modular reactors, which he described as “the new era of nuclear energy.”

“Thanks to the abundant thorium reserves in our country, we aim to eliminate our dependence on foreign nuclear fuel,” he said, adding that the cooperation could eventually lead to a domestically developed product suitable for commercial use.

Thorium requires other nuclear fuel to start the process that allows it to generate energy. Inside a reactor, it can be converted into uranium-233, which can sustain a nuclear chain reaction. Developing and processing thorium fuel remains costly and technically demanding, according to the International Atomic Energy Agency.

Copenhagen Atomics says its first test reactors, planned for 2028, and initial commercial reactors, targeted for the 2030s, will use enriched uranium along with thorium. Those are the company’s targets rather than a schedule for the Turkish partnership.

The agreement comes as Turkey seeks to expand nuclear power to diversify its electricity supply and reduce reliance on imported fossil fuels.

Russia’s state nuclear corporation Rosatom is building Akkuyu, Turkey’s first commercial nuclear power plant, in the Mediterranean province of Mersin. Its four planned reactors will have a combined capacity of 4.8 gigawatts.

Russian President Vladimir Putin said during talks with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan last week that Akkuyu’s first reactor would begin operating this year. Turkey is also pursuing plans for nuclear plants in Sinop on the Black Sea coast and the northwestern Thrace region.
