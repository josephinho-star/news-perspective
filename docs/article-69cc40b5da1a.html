Big wave surfers line up to ride ‘raw power of the Pacific’ during Super El-Niño swells

2026-11-16 21:12

While this year’s Super El Niño storms are battering the California coast and destroying homes and infrastructure, surfers are finding a silver lining: waves so epic that pro athletes from around the world are making last-minute journeys to experience them.

Big wave surfers converged on northern California this Saturday to ride massive, storm-driven swells at the famously dangerous Mavericks Beach, an event some surf fans called “Monster Day.”

Kai Lenny, an American pro surfer, Clément Roseyro, a French big wave surfer, Jamie Mitchell, an Australian big wave surfer, Lucas “Chumbo” Chianca, a Brazilian pro surfer, and Luca Padua, a local star surfer who is from Half Moon Bay, were among those who risked the waves this weekend.

Lai wrote on Instagram that he “nearly didn’t make it” to Mavericks over the weekend after “all flights were grounded nationwide”, but that he was one of the lucky few to make it to San Francisco, which is just 20 miles north of Mavericks, near Half Moon Bay, California. Justine Dupont, a French pro surfer, wrote on social media that she had been heading to Mavericks, but was “locked in the Boston airport”, the Washington Post reported.

The conditions on Saturday were so rough that even some experienced big wave surfers decided not to risk it: “If the waves are 60ft and you’re not feeling 100%, the stakes are just way too high,” Bianca Valenti, an American pro surfer who has surfed Mavericks many times, said in a social media post.

Surfer Ridge Lenny, Kai’s brother, called the day “very wild” and “frankly terrifying”, in an Instagram post.

Others described the Mavericks El Niño surf as “no joke” and “survival mode”.

Mitchell, the Australian big wave surfer, found himself crushed by one of the waves on Saturday, and though he triggered his inflatable safety vest, the power of the ocean was so strong that the vest “ripped off”, photographer Fred Pompermayer wrote on Instagram. “This is the raw power of the Pacific.”

It “took all my years of experience to survive that”, Mitchell wrote on Instagram.

But some of those who risked the six-story waves, such as Lenny, produced jaw-dropping footage of their exploits.

“Well hello El Niño ... it’s good to see you,” surf filmmaker Tucker Wooding wrote.
