Zelenskyy says Ukraine was behind drone attacks on Russian ships off Libya

2026-11-23 07:37

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Ukrainian forces sank a Russian warship in Benghazi and also hit a cargo ship used to transport weapons off the coast of Libya, hailing an “exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia’s war”.

Ukraine’s president said in a statement on Monday that the Merkury, one of Russia’s newest warships, sank after Ukrainian forces launched seven naval drones on Sunday. A KA-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed in the attack.

He confirmed that Ukraine’s forces also attacked the cargo ship Angara. Libya’s eastern health ministry in Benghazi said all crew members from the cargo ship had been rescued and were receiving treatment after Sunday’s attack.

Authorities in eastern Libya said the warship was docked in the port of Benghazi when the explosion occurred.

“We firmly reject the use of our territory or territorial waters as an arena for settling scores or as an extension of external conflicts,” Saddam Haftar, the deputy commander-in-chief of the eastern Libyan administration’s forces said.

“The security and sovereignty of Libya are a national responsibility that leaves no room for political posturing or the exploitation of incidents to propagate accusations and incite fear,” he said.

Reporting from Moscow, Al Jazeera’s Yulia Shapovalova said that Russian authorities have not yet made an official comment on the drone attack on the warship.

“According to the Libyan side, the ship was returning to Russia from its mission and stopped in Benghazi for refuelling,” our correspondent said. She said it was not clear if there were any casualties.

“Russian military infrastructure is Ukraine’s target, not just in Russia, but pretty much throughout the world,” she said.

The United Nations Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL) expressed “serious concern” over the drone attacks, calling for “an immediate, independent and transparent investigation”.

“Libya cannot afford and must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts,” UNSMIL wrote on X.

The eastern administration did not identify who was behind Sunday’s attack but said Libya was “not a party to the Russian-Ukrainian war”.

The oil-rich nation of Libya has been divided between the government in Tripoli and a rival administration in the east since the overthrow of longtime ruler Muammar Gaddafi in 2011.
