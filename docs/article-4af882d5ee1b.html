Novak Djokovic brought to tears after body betrays him in stunning first-round upset at US Open

2026-08-31 04:08

Betrayed by his body and brought to tears, Novak Djokovic’s run at this U.S. Open is done before it got started.

The Greatest of All Time knocked out by Father Time.

And by Argentine Mariano Navone, who won 7-6 (5), 5-7, 4-6, 6-2, 6-1 in a 4-hour, 36-minute marathon. Djokovic waved to the fans as they cheered, and made a heart symbol as he left Arthur Ashe Stadium. Will it be for the last time?

Exactly 21 years to the day after Djokovic’s U.S. Open debut, it’s fair to ask whether this was his finale in Flushing. That’s how ugly it looked and how compromised he was.

Djokovic had won 37 straight first- and second-round matches here in the U.S. Open. He hadn’t lost this early in any major in 20 years, since a first-round exit in Australia in 2006.

On Sunday, Djokovic made 70 unforced errors. He fought through at least three bouts of nausea, cramps, needed an inhaler, a medical timeout and two pills. But once his back seized up and his serve disappeared, this went from a match to a requiem.

And for the 48th-ranked Navone, the biggest win of his life.

“Novak is the GOAT in this sport. Beating him at the U.S Open, Arthur Ashe, is one of the first moment I have in tennis,” Navone said. “When you’re a kid and you’re thinking to play tennis, the biggest stage in the tennis world is very important for you. When you have the chance to play against him, you wish and you dream to beat him in these kinds of settings because you grew up with that. So yeah, it’s a dream come true. It was a pleasure.”

Djokovic refused to retire but was in tears down the stretch.

The 39-year-old was not just under the weather but, some will say, over the hill. He’d thrown up and needed treatment two weeks ago in Cincinnati, suffering an upset loss and acknowledging he’s been suffering with a mystery medical condition; many will suggest the ailment is simply called age.

On Sunday, Djokovic vomited multiple times on the court and the towel box, then suffered from left leg cramps and used an inhaler on the fourth set changeover.

His back was the final straw. Djokovic had a break lead in the fourth set when it tightened up. He only took one more game the rest of the way.

After taking a quick 3-0 lead early, he started to struggle physically.

Up 5-4, Djokovic called for a trainer who gave him a pill. It didn’t keep Navone from taking the first set. Djokovic sailed a forehand long to give Navone set point; and when he drilled a forehand into the net, the Argentine had cashed in.

In the second set, Djokovic started to move better and seemed more engaged. Down 4-3, he won a 29-shot rally with a backhand down the line to go up 40-15, then held to knot the set at 4-4. He reeled off four straight pinpoint serves to hold for the set in cutthroat fashion, then took the third set 6-4.

After getting a cramp in his left foot during the fourth set, Djokovic’s back seemed to tighten. He got broken to go down 4-2 with his serve completely toothless, often in double digits. That’s when Navone took the last two games and the set 6-2.

Djokovic took a medical timeout. A trainer was ready to rub some sort of cream on his lower back, while a doctor gave him more pills. But after having taken the lead off free points he garnered on his first serve, that edge was now gone.

Navone pounced on that Achilles’ heel and ended Djokovic’s stay in Flushing.

It remains to be seen if he’ll ever have another.
