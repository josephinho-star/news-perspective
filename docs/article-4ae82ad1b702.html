Australian politics live: Two Nationals kicked out of question time as Taylor slams ‘dirty deal’ with Greens; NSW responds to fire ant threat in Sydney

Two Nationals kicked out as Taylor slams ‘dirty deal’ on tax

Angus Taylor has accused the government of cutting a “dirty deal” with the Greens, after Labor struck an agreement with the minor party. The Greens will help pass Labor’s 30% minimum tax on discretionary trusts, in exchange for increasing the excise on private jet fuel.

Taylor asks the prime minister when he will “end his war against small businesses in this country?” Anthony Albanese basically says: at least one party is open for negotiation:

This is the mob who just said with the vote that was held in this house on private health insurance, that will reduce the expenditure by making the rebate for 64-year-olds and 65-year-olds for private health insurance the same, except for pensioners, which are being carved out. They not only voted against it, they’ve said they will reverse it.

He doesn’t get far in before Nationals MP Pat Conaghan is kicked out for interjecting too much.

As the PM continues, the opposition tries to make a point of order, which Albanese claims is just for social media:

We can follow the TikTok obsession of those opposite as well with their points of order to try to get grabs.

Soon after, the deputy Nationals leader, Darren Chester, is also booted.

Tehan calls Burke a ‘Dibber-dobber’: are we back in primary school?

Tim Wilson comes to the despatch box next and asks the treasurer, “inflation went up again to 4.9%, higher than expectations. Treasurer, why should Australians trust a word you say?”

Jim Chalmers says it was worse under the Coalition.

They’ve got a lot of nerve asking us about inflation when it was much higher on their watch, nerve asking us about savings in the budget when they vote against savings in the Senate.

Manager of opposition business, Dan Tehan stands up to make a point of order, saying Chalmers hasn’t answered the question.

Leader of the House, Tony Burke counters and says for some reason that AngusTaylor has interjected 17 times in two minutes. (“One for every rate rise”, shouts Liberal MP GarthHamilton.) Slightly more bizarrely, Burke says 14 of those interjections used words while three were “non-verbal sounds, just grunting”.

Tehan then stands back up and calls Burke a “dibber dobber”.

‘Of course I will’: Marles agrees to meet Bathurst veterans

Over to the crossbench, Andrew Gee asks the defence minister, Richard Marles, if he will meet with Bathurst veterans who want one third of an army reserve base being sold by the government to be retained as a veterans wellbeing centre.

Gee has to restart his question so Marles – who is speaking to the Solomon Islands delegation – can do a little run back to hear it.

Marles actually answers the question immediately, saying he will meet the community.

The short answer to the question is of course I will. I will reach out to your office to organise an opportunity to do just that.

He then justifies the government’s plan to sell off defence estates across the country, including the one in Bathurst, and takes the opportunity to lay any blame he can on the Coalition.

The estate audit made the point that today defence was wearing the burden of a failure over decades to not make difficult decisions. Well, we are making the difficult decisions. Make no mistake, they [the Coalition] contributed to that, absolutely.

The opposition makes a point of order, saying the question had nothing to do with the Coalition. Milton Dick tells Marles to stick to the question, and the Coalition to stop injecting and “provoking” the deputy PM.

Marles finishes his answer attacking the Coalition anyway.

‘Zero savings, not a dollar’: Albanese calls out opposition’s budget record

Angus Taylor is back a the despatch box and sticks to inflation, asking when the prime minister will stop using the wars in Ukraine, the Middle East, and Donald Trump as an “excuse” and “start taking responsibility for forcing Australians to pay more for absolutely everything?”

Anthony Albanese says the opposition leader hasn’t been listening because the treasurer told the house on Monday that inflation was a problem before war broke out in Iran.

We acknowledge of course that this isn’t the sole source. We haven’t said that. We’ve said the opposite. We have said there are inflationary pressures before then as well.

Indeed, we understand and those opposite should know what inflationary pressures look like, because inflation had a six in front of it when we came to office and their last opportunity to hand down a budget, with fiscal responsibility, it had a really easy figure to remember – zero. Zero savings, not a dollar.

Solomon Islands brings largest ever delegation to House floor

Following the signing of a landmark treaty with Solomon Islands today, 20 of the nation’s representatives, including the prime minister, Matthew Wale, are sitting in the house for question time.

They’re such a big delegation the house has had to bring in extra chairs for them. Milton Dick confirms it’s the largest delegation to ever sit on the floor of the house!

After some less than ideal inflation data, I’ll give you one guess what the opposition leader’s first question is.

I’m sure you’ve got it: Angus Taylor asks, “Will the prime minister finally admit his government’s out-of-control spending is driving up inflation and making life harder for families and small businesses?”

The prime minister, Anthony Albanese ,says the numbers are tough but the government has been preparing for them, and that the war in the Middle East has made the economic situation much worse.

Today’s numbers reflect what a lot of Australians are feeling, that right now things are really difficult. Though it doesn’t make it easier, it is what we have been preparing for … As the Treasurer said, things could get harder before they get easier. Our government is determined to do the work to get inflation down and to ease interest rates here at home.

Now, the challenges that we face they won’t be solved overnight. We will get Australians through this and make sure we come out stronger on the other side.

Andrew Hastie to take acting shadow immigration role

Before question time begins, Angus Taylor announces that Andrew Hastie will be the interim shadow home affairs and immigration minister, as Tasmanian senator Jonno Duniam marks his final day in Parliament.

Taylor will likely do a reshuffle and to bring someone else onto the frontbench, but I wonder whether Hastie will permanently assume that role.

OK, so today has actually turned into a massive day.

The Greens have just announced they will support the government’s legislation to impose a minimum 30% tax on discretionary trusts in exchange for increasing the excise on private jet fuel.

I’ll be honest, I did not see that coming.

Greens leader, David Shoebridge, says it is “disgraceful” that ultra-wealthy Australians using private jets only pay 3c a litre in excise.

We have forced the government to end the lurk where the ultra wealthy are paying one-fifteenth of the tax on the fuel that they use to fill up their private jets than ordinary motorists; we have forced the government to come to an arrangement where those ultra wealthy, when they’re flying around the country in their private jets, whether it’s Gina Rinehart or Clive Palmer or Pauline Hanson, are now going to be paying the same effective rate for when they fill up their tanks as ordinary Australians do in a cost of living crisis.

Coalition confirms it would repeal digital duty of care laws

Following Andrew Hastie’s slightly more cautious wording this morning, the Coalition has confirmed it would repeal the digital duty of care laws if it wins government.

In a statement, the shadow communications minister, Sarah Henderson, insists the laws will censor free speech.

We fear Labor’s digital duty of care will be virtually impossible to comply with given the dangerously vague and subjective definitions of harm. Labor is walking blindly into a legal nightmare of protracted investigations, costly litigation, and regulatory uncertainty.

Albanese government is being ‘defeated by inflation’: Wilson

The shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, has squarely put the blame of today’s higher inflation numbers on government spending.

This is not exactly a surprise – he’s been blaming the government almost entirely for rising inflation for months.

He’s now labelled it “persistent government inflation”.

Inflation has defeated this treasurer and the Albanese government. They said last year that we had turned the corner on inflation and that we were facing a different directional interest rates … I am deeply concerned about the lift in unemployment going up to 4.9% when the Reserve Bank said that we wouldn’t get there till the end of next year.

They don’t understand that they are one of the key drivers behind inflation, which is destroying confidence and hope, particularly for the small businesses of this country.

Lead contamination in Broken Hill childcare centres to be tested

Early learning centres in Broken Hill will face testing after revelations 10 centres had lead levels up to 10 times the national limit.

The New South Wales Environment Protection Authority (EPA) said about 30 centres attended by children aged 5 and under would receive mandatory environmental testing for lead and the watchdog would compel remediation and cleanup works where needed.

The EPA’s interim chief executive, Nancy Chang, said the testing and remediation program was in response to these long term problems and aimed to address the concerns of the local community. She said

We know that children under 5 years of age are more likely to be at risk of harm from lead because of their hand to mouth behaviour, touching and crawling on dusty surfaces and their young bodies absorbing a higher proportion of lead than adults as they grow.

It follows questions by the Greens MLC Cate Faehrmann this year that revealed soil testing had detected lead levels at up to ten times the national threshold at 10 centres. Four of the centres had subsequently been remediated.

Faehrmann said the response was “long overdue but also too little and too late to prevent generations of children suffering life-long developmental issues from lead poisoning”.

The treasurer has said Australia’s economy is facing an “especially difficult” period while pointing to government forecasts that things will get better in 2027.

Jim Chalmers was asked if the country faced an “economic emergency” when speaking to reporters in Canberra just now. He said:

This is a very serious moment for our economy, and we will respond in substantial ways.

Chalmers said the combination of economic issues Australia was facing had motivated his statement on Monday, which he described as “probably the most substantial preview, that I’ve seen in this building, of a mid year update”.

But he noted that Australia’s jobs market had enjoyed a “better experience” than other countries and pointed to Reserve Bank predictions that wages would grow faster than prices next year.

2026 has been an especially difficult year for people. We expect this inflation, for a range of reasons, to hang around for a bit longer than anyone would like.

But we also expect inflation to come down in the target band in 2027. We expect real wages growth, in the Treasury and RBA forecast, to return in 2027. We expect our economy to continue to grow.

Chalmers: ‘no amount of savings will make inflation go away’

Jim Chalmers has said no amount of budget cuts would fix Australia’s inflation as he promises rising prices will be the main priority of next month’s budget update.

The treasurer has told reporters he stands by promises to find significant savings, as foreshadowed in a grim parliamentary statement earlier this week.

I said on Monday, and I say again today, there’s no amount of responsible cost of living relief or budget savings, which will make this inflation challenge go away overnight, and that’s because it has a number of drivers, primarily, in this case, automotive fuel.

And so we will do what we responsibly can, to hand down a budget update next month, which has substantial savings.

He pushed back at commentary that government spending has caused resurgent inflation, defending past cost-of-living policies such as electricity rebates and fuel excise cuts.

I would encourage people not to make the mistake of concluding that budgets are the primary driver of prices in our economy …

If you look at the last four years, public final demand growth halved while private final demand growth tripled. And that tells us there are other factors at play here.

People shouldn’t pretend, unfortunately, as I have seen in recent weeks and months, that there are no global factors at play here. Of course there are. Budgets are not the primary determiner of prices in our economy.

Chalmers says inflation jump is an unsurprising ‘global phenomena’

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, has said new data showing surging prices was a “unwelcome but unsurprising reminder” of pressure on Australians.

Chalmers said the leap in inflation to 4.9% in October was due to oil and fuel prices, which he said had been widely predicted.

He told reporters in Canberra:

If you look around the world, October saw inflation go up in most major advanced economies, and interest rates are going up around the world as a result.

We did have an inflation challenge in Australia before the war in the Middle East, but the war is making it much worse, and a lot of this inflation is a global phenomena, as a consequence.

Inflation is still much lower than its peak in 2022, but it is still too high.

The Reserve Bank’s preferred trimmed mean measure of inflation – which takes out the most volatile prices, including fuel – lifted to 3.8%.

‘We fixed this law’: Greens claim victory over pensioner carve-out for private health rebates cut

The Greens leader, David Shoebridge, has celebrated after successfully pressuring Labor to carve out pensioners from cuts to private health insurance rebates for over-65s.

Many people over 65 – who are not pensioners – will lose their additional private health insurance rebates, putting them on par with everyone else. All pensioners will be carved out.

Shoebridge said the proposed full cuts would have left people going hungry. He said in a statement:

The Greens have been clear for months now, these changes to the private healthcare rebate cannot go after pensioners. Today we fixed this law to make sure it will not …

I hope this gives some real relief to people, that when people need our support, the Greens were here for them …

Cutting support to people who need it the most will never be supported by the Greens.

NSW government ‘significantly scaling up’ fire ant response

The NSW government is “significantly scaling up” its response to a fire ant infestation detected in Auburn over the weekend, after finding “a larger and more complex infestation than initially identified”.

The first fire ant nest was successfully treated, but authorities have identified more ants and a colony type requiring further treatment.

The Auburn detection involves the polygyne social form of red imported fire ants, the National Fire Ant Eradication Program confirmed on Tuesday night.

The NSW government said in a statement:

Unlike colonies with a single queen, these colonies contain multiple queens and can support a higher concentration of ants and nests within a relatively small area, making them complex to treat.

This type of colony has not previously been detected in NSW and is rare even in south-east Queensland, where fire ants are more widespread.

Tara Moriarty, the NSW agriculture minister, said:

This fire ant type is different to what has occurred in the past and is seemingly the result of infested imported goods.

Our government is boosting our on-ground resources and establishing a taskforce to focus on tracing the supply chains that have led to this occurring …

We’re asking the community to work with us by reporting any suspicious ants or earth mounds and cooperating with our teams as they check businesses, residences and public lands the area.
