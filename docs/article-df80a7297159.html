‘Welsh can tell any story’: Cymraeg dub of Star Wars premieres in Cardiff

2026-11-19 19:33

It has been a memorable few days for Wales.

At the weekend, the nation’s rugby team secured a historic win against New Zealand – their first victory over the All Blacks in more than 70 years.

Then on Thursday evening came another culturally significant moment, when Cardiff rolled out the red carpet to celebrate the first-ever Welsh language dub of the Star Wars franchise.

Stars of stage and screen who voiced the words of Luke Skywalker, Darth Vader and C-3PO et al in Welsh (Cymraeg) for the original 1977 Star Wars film took the plaudits at Cineworld Cardiff for the screening.

Among those on the red carpet was Mark Lewis Jones, who has appeared in shows ranging from The Crown to Game of Thrones and had a role in the 2017 Star Wars film The Last Jedi. In this version of Star Wars, he is a Welsh Darth Vader.

He said he thought Welsh was a good language for Vader. “It has an ancient, poetic sense. It gives him that status, that authority,” said Jones, who hails from the Wrexham area of north-east Wales. “I give all my characters north-east Wales accents.”

Also taking a bow was Osian Morgan – well known in Wales for his appearances in the Welsh language soap opera Pobol y Cwm – who voices Luke Skywalker.

“There’s so much excitement about this project and the cast is unbelievable,” Morgan said. “I think in Wales we’re on an upward trajectory and this instils a lot of hope. The future is exciting for Wales, particularly in the creative arts. The talent is vast.”

Asked if he felt the pressure of being Luke Skywalker, he said he was thrilled rather than nervous. “He’s had such an important impact on my life.” Morgan was also pleased that his 10-year-old brother’s first experience of Star Wars would be in the medium of Welsh. “The fact that Luke tonight in the Welsh language can have that impact on kids here is fantastic.”

Rhys Ifans, who has appeared in Harry Potter and Spider-Man films, has the tough job of standing in for Alec Guinness as Jedi master Obi-Wan Kenobi. Sadly, he did not make it to Cardiff for the event.

His brother, Llŷr Ifans, the Welsh voice of the droid C-3PO, however, was present, which was apposite as C-3PO is “fluent in over 6 million forms of communication” – including, clearly, Welsh.

Ifans said: “When I first heard about it, I thought, wow, what are we doing – Star Wars in Welsh? The idea has settled. C-3PO is a genius who speaks over 6 million languages. Now he speaks 6 million and one. He speaks the best language in the world.”

Asked what accent he used for his C-3PO, Ifans said: “There are no accents in space. But it’s more about the character. He lives on his nerves and speaks staccato. I had to bring in those aspects.”

The Welsh language version of the 1977 film (subsequently retitled Star Wars: Episode IV – A New Hope), has been produced by Lucasfilm and Disney in collaboration with the Welsh language broadcaster S4C and the Welsh government via its Creative Wales agency.

Its Welsh title is Star Wars: Gobaith Newydd (New Hope). Those not lucky enough to be at the screening in Cardiff will be able to hear the immortal line “May the force be with you” in Welsh (“A boed i’r Grym fod gyda chi”) when the version is broadcast on S4C platforms on Christmas Day.

Thursday’s screening wasn’t as glitzy as a London or Los Angeles premiere, but it had charm.

Côr Bechgyn Glantaf, a schoolboys’ choir from Cardiff, performed as the crowds – including many children – arrived and made their way up the escalators of the multiplex to screens eight and nine. Band Pres Llareggub, which bills itself as a Bronx hip-hop-inspired New Orleans-tinged brass band with attitude from north Wales, also provided a musical backdrop.

S4C’s chief executive, Geraint Evans, described the screening as a “hugely proud moment”, adding: “Most importantly, it will give Welsh-speaking children the thrill of hearing some of cinema’s most beloved characters speak their language for the very first time, showing that Welsh can tell any story.”

There are a few connections linking Wales and Star Wars, not least that a version of the Millennium Falcon was built in south-west Wales.

Mark Williams, who oversaw an exhibition about the Millennium Falcon, said he felt the Welsh language version was long overdue. “I’ve been saying for years it should be done, especially with the ties that Wales has with the franchise,” he said.

Also at the screening was the cabinet minister for enterprise, connectivity and energy in the Welsh government, Adam Price, who said it was an excellent opportunity for Welsh actors and production staff to work with a major Hollywood studio.

“Seeing one of the most iconic films of all time in Welsh is a great way to make the language more accessible and introduces it to new audiences beyond our shores,” he said.

The Welsh government is aiming to have a million people speaking Cymraeg by 2050. A spokesperson for the Welsh language commissioner’s office said one of its key aims was to increase the use of the language on a community level.

The spokesperson said: “In order to do this, the language must be seen and heard in all aspects of our daily lives. The use of Welsh in such a high-profile film as Star Wars has the potential to attract new audiences and raise awareness of the language.”
