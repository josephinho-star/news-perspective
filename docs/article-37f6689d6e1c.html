COP31 opens in Antalya with early dispute over climate finance

2026-11-09 09:30

The COP31 climate talks opened Monday in the Turkish city of Antalya with an early dispute over money as UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres urged world leaders to “get serious” about tackling the crisis after a year of devastating weather extremes.

Thousands of participants gathered at a sprawling expo center in the Mediterranean city for the UN’s annual two-week climate conference.

The summit follows a year of punishing heat waves in Europe, major wildfires in Indonesia and catastrophic floods in Nepal, with an exceptionally strong El Nino weather phenomenon fueling further climate disruption.

“We have entered a new age of climate extremes,” Guterres said in a statement.

He urged leaders to “get serious by cutting emissions faster, accelerating a just transition from fossil fuels and delivering the finance and protection vulnerable countries need.”

The opening ceremony began an hour and a half late, with no explanation given for the delay.

Negotiators had been locked in discussions late Sunday over demands by developing nations to include climate finance items on the official agenda.

“This has already been a difficult start for COP31,” said Jasper Inventor, head of Greenpeace’s COP31 delegation.

Under UN climate agreements, developed countries, which are historically responsible for most greenhouse gas emissions, have provided financial support to help developing nations adapt to climate impacts and shift to cleaner energy. Poorer countries say the sums remain insufficient.

Inventor said developed countries had taken a “hard line” over whether to include a platform agreed at COP30 in Brazil last year to discuss how wealthy nations should provide funding to developing countries.

A source familiar with the talks said the “climate work programme” would be included in the agenda.

African countries had also pushed for discussions on tripling adaptation finance, which helps poorer nations pay for measures such as building sea walls and planting drought-resistant crops.

The source said tripling adaptation finance would be discussed under other items rather than as a separate agenda point.

A senior European Union official warned last week that there would be “no political will” for additional funding pledges at COP31.

Observers expect few major breakthroughs as countries grapple with an oil price shock and geopolitical tensions fueled by the US war with Iran and Russia’s war in Ukraine.

As negotiators tussled in Antalya, the UN’s World Meteorological Organization released a report showing that 2026 had continued a streak of exceptional global temperatures, melting ice and rising sea levels.

The long-term outlook is bleak. The UN warned in September that global temperatures were on course to exceed 1.5 degrees Celsius above preindustrial levels, breaching the most ambitious limit set by the Paris Agreement.

The Turkish government holds the COP31 presidency, while Australia is leading negotiations under an unusual arrangement that settled a dispute over who would host the conference.

The partners have branded the summit the “implementation COP,” focusing on turning commitments into concrete action after years of pledges and ambitious announcements.

Ankara is promoting an “action agenda” alongside the formal negotiations, encouraging countries to support nonbinding pledges on issues ranging from artificial intelligence to waste management.

Its centerpiece is an “electrification” drive to raise the share of global energy demand met by electricity from around 20 percent today to 35 percent by 2035.

The initiative could become a source of friction. The EU has called for “clean” electrification, a term that could spark debate over the role of fossil fuels in the Turkish proposal.

Countries committed to transitioning away from fossil fuels in a landmark pact at COP28 in Dubai in 2023 but have struggled to agree on how to put that pledge into practice.

COP30 ended with a deal that made no explicit mention of fossil fuels after oil-producing countries opposed the language.

Instead, Brazil produced a voluntary “roadmap” with proposals for phasing out fossil fuels that countries can tailor to their circumstances.

Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, who was recently re-elected, will attend the leaders’ summit on November 11–12.

A major player remains absent from the talks: US President Donald Trump, a climate change skeptic, has not sent administration officials to COP31.

A US congressional delegation will attend, however, with Democrats buoyed by their historic victory in last week’s midterm elections.
