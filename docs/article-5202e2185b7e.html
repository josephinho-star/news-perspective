Historic 100-plus-year-old California landmark suffers disastrous blow as rain battered the region

2026-11-21 18:00

A massive branch from a 138-year-old California landmark came crashing down just hours before a powerful storm hit, crushing the roof of a beloved local coffee shop.

The towering Wardholme Torrey Pine in Carpinteria lost a major limb around 4:28 p.m. Wednesday, sending it crashing onto the nearby Lucky Llama Coffee House.

Firefighters from the Carpinteria-Summerland Fire Protection District rushed to the scene after hearing the branch fall.

“The crew of Medic Engine 61 heard the branch fall and responded. Santa Barbara County Sheriff’s deputies were already on scene. Crews from Medic Engine 61 and Squad 61 helped the property owner clear debris from the roof so it could be covered with a tarp ahead of tonight’s rain,” the department said.

The tree has stood along Carpinteria Avenue since 1888, when local Judge Thomas Ward planted it from a seedling brought over from Santa Rosa Island.

“In 1968, it became Carpinteria’s first historic landmark,” the fire department added.

Ward planted the rare pine on his estate as part of a contest to see which Torrey pine would grow the tallest.

The tree now stands 126 feet tall, with a 130-foot branch spread and a circumference of more than 20 feet, “making it the largest known example of its kind,” according to Atlas Obscura.

The Torrey pine is considered the rarest pine species in the world, growing naturally only in San Diego County and on Santa Rosa Island. San Diego’s famed Torrey Pines Golf Course also takes its name from the unusual tree.

The falling limb heavily damaged Lucky Llama Coffee House, forcing the business to temporarily close.

“We’ll keep you posted as things progress. Huge thank you to the Carp Fire Department for all the help and for our amazing community who have reached out to check in. We love you all,” the coffee shop said in an Instagram post.

But the shop had some good news for customers Friday, announcing it would reopen Saturday.

Fortunately, no one was inside the coffee house when the massive branch came down.

The incident also came right before a major storm hit Southern California on Wednesday night through Thursday morning. Flood warnings were sent to devices throughout the region, warning of heavy rain and high winds.
