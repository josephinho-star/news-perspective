Piker backs idea to sell Trump on Medicare-for-all by calling it 'Trumpcare'

2026-11-25 02:00

Left-wing influencer Hasan Piker expressed support for Michigan Democratic Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed’s plan to get President Donald Trump to support Medicare-for-all by appealing to his ego and calling it "Trumpcare."

Speaking to liberal tech podcaster Kara Swisher on Monday, Piker lamented enduring the last two years of Trump’s term.

"He's [Trump's] so bad at everything," Piker said. "He's so incompetent, and all he cares about is enriching himself and his family. So, I don't know. I don't know where we go from here."

"We go to Trumpcare," Swisher quipped. "We go to Trumpcare for Medicare-for-all. That's what we're going to call it. That's the situation. Do you object to what El-Sayed said?"

"No, I love it. I've been saying that for years," Piker replied. "Literally, I've been presenting this as a joke for, like, the last decade. I was like, ‘Look, you tell Donald Trump, what does Donald Trump love more than anything else?’ Undermining Obama's legacy. He loves his name.

"He loves putting his name on stuff, and he loves undermining Obama's legacy. You pitch it to him as such. You say, ‘Look, Obamacare is done. This is better. Trumpcare is better. More comprehensive. Everyone will love you. You will be remembered as a legend."

"You're a genius," Swisher replied.

Earlier this month, El-Sayed posted a 16-second video urging the president to embrace Medicare-for-all and call it "Trumpcare."

"Donald. Habibi. Midterms were rough. I get it," El-Sayed said in the video posted Nov. 17, addressing Trump with the Arabic term "habibi," commonly translated as "my dear" or "my beloved."

"Trumpcare-for-all. It's really popular. People really want healthcare. You can put your name all over it."

The video featured a mock "Trumpcare" card and an AI-generated image depicting Trump as Jesus Christ, which El-Sayed, a Muslim, referenced while urging the president to embrace socialized, taxpayer-funded healthcare.

"Donald, this is your chance to heal the sick (like in that one AI image. You know the one)," El-Sayed said.

The White House responded at the time by blasting the pitch, warning that the Medicare-for-all proposal would threaten Americans' existing health coverage.

"The radical Democrats’ socialist proposal for a government takeover of healthcare should really be called ‘Medicare-for-none’ as it destroys access to the life-saving care Americans rely on daily," White House spokeswoman Allison Schuster told Fox News Digital.

Theo Wold, a former White House deputy assistant for domestic policy during Trump's first term, spoke to Fox News Digital about the Piker's comments.

"Democrats just won historic victories, but they understand that they still have two years in which anything that’s going to get done has to go through Trump," he said.

"Their simplistic assessment of Trump isn’t accurate and won’t work, but Republicans should read between the lines and forget stupid ideas like amnesty. Don’t give Democrats anything during these two years unless it’s a policy Republicans (and more importantly, Republican voters) actually want."

Republican consultant Mary Marslender reacted to Piker's commentary as well.

"Hasan Piker and his cronies are attempting to sell a government takeover of healthcare that would destroy the private innovation patients actually need," she wrote.

"Trump has proven he only promotes policies that are good for our country, not policies that appeal to ego. He understands that Medicare-for-all would threaten access to breakthrough treatments, raise prices, limit choices, and deny Americans access to care."

Fox News Digital reached out to the White House and did not receive an immediate reply.

Fox News’ Jasmine Baehr contributed to this report.
