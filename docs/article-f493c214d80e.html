Death toll rises to 5 in İstanbul building collapse

2026-10-09 08:51

The death toll from a building collapse in İstanbul rose to five, including three children, after rescuers recovered the last victim’s body early Friday, according to the İstanbul Governor’s Office.

Two girls aged five and 10, a 12-year-old boy and two other people were killed. Nine people were pulled alive from the rubble, according to the final figures.

“Search and rescue operations in the area under the rubble … ended after the discovery of the last victim’s body at 6:15 am (0315 GMT),” the governor’s office said.

The building in Maltepe’s Cevizli neighborhood, on the city’s Asian side, partially collapsed at about 5:40 a.m. Thursday. Its lower three floors gave way, leaving the upper four resting on the rubble.

The risk of further collapse complicated rescue efforts. Fifty-five people living around the site were evacuated as a precaution.

The cause of the collapse has not been established.

İstanbul Deputy Mayor Nuri Aslan said the building was constructed in 1987 and that its basement, ground floor and first two floors complied with the standards at the time, while the other floors were added later.

Maltepe Mayor Esin Köymen said municipal archive records showed the top two floors had been added without permits after an occupancy permit was issued. She said the municipality had received no previous complaints about the building.

According to Turkish media reports, the manager of a ground-floor shop said she had noticed cracks in the structure since April.

Prosecutors on İstanbul’s Asian side opened an investigation, assigning a deputy chief prosecutor and two prosecutors to the case.

The Interior Ministry also appointed two inspectors to examine whether public officials bore responsibility for negligence or failures during the building’s planning, construction or alterations, according to the DHA news agency.

İstanbul and other parts of Turkey are undergoing an urban redevelopment program intended to replace aging buildings vulnerable to earthquakes with safer housing.
