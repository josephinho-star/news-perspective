Vegas fast-food joint offers front-row seat to F1 race — with insane price tag: ‘Is that even legal?’

2026-11-21 23:47

A Las Vegas Bojangles is drawing heat after allegedly charging Formula 1 fans $100 to sit for just 10 minutes and watch the 2026 Grand Prix, with a manager reportedly threatening to call police on customers who refused to pay.

That’s $10 a minute for a seat at a fried chicken joint.

Video of the confrontation, shared on X by user Jedi Rich, sparked outrage among racing fans and Las Vegas locals, who questioned whether the restaurant’s alleged pricing scheme was even legal.

“$100 to sit at BOJANGLES for TEN MINUTES—and the manager threatened to call Metro unless people paid?! That’s $10 a minute at a chicken joint, with a police threat thrown in. The F1 view is incredible, but this is ridiculous. Is that even legal?” Jedi Rich posted.

The restaurant, located at the LINQ Promenade directly along the Strip circuit, offers a prime view of the race.

Vital Vegas, a popular local watchdog account, also weighed in, writing, “We trust @Bojangles corporate would be appalled by this scam.”

Scott Friedman had his own take, posting, “.@Bojangles – Las Vegas has found a new revenue stream…”

Whether corporate headquarters approved the alleged charge remains unclear.

The location only opened Oct. 7, 2026, and is the chain’s first restaurant to feature a full-service bar.

Businesses generally can impose cover charges and seating fees on private property, provided customers are properly informed beforehand. They can also ask nonpaying customers to leave. However, consumer protection rules could become an issue if fees aren’t clearly disclosed.

The restaurant could also face questions involving franchise agreements and Formula 1 licensing restrictions.

The controversy comes amid longstanding friction between F1 organizers and businesses along the Strip. Race organizers have reportedly demanded licensing fees of up to $1,500 per seat of capacity from trackside venues, with barricades and lighting threatened for businesses that refuse.

Even fans inside the official race gates aren’t escaping the sticker shock, complaining about $36 cocktails, $24 pizza slices, $15 hot dogs and $8 plain fries.
