Robert Kraft, Gillette Stadium boot Macklemore from Ed Sheeran tour over ‘Free Palestine’ rant

2026-09-14 23:41

Macklemore is learning the hard way that concertgoers paying top dollar for stadium shows do not want a lecture on Middle Eastern geopolitics from the guy who wrote "Thrift Shop."

Macklemore was kicked off Ed Sheeran’s U.S. stadium tour after multiple venues refused to host him following his pro-Palestinian speeches and performances at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey. The fallout escalated when New England Patriots owner Robert Kraft barred him from performing at Gillette Stadium, where Sheeran is scheduled to play Sept. 25 and 26.

The rapper opened Sheeran’s shows at MetLife on Sept. 4 and 5. Macklemore wore a keffiyeh, showed footage of destruction in Gaza, performed "Hind’s Hall" and led the crowd in "Free Palestine" chants. The song includes lyrics accusing Israel of genocide.

Macklemore said the tour gave him a massive platform to promote his political message. He also claimed Sheeran told him Robert Kraft had rallied other stadium owners against keeping him on the tour.

Kraft, whose Kraft Group owns Gillette Stadium in Foxborough, Massachusetts, cited the rapper’s stage behavior and what he called a "broader history of anti-Semitic rhetoric and imagery."

"Gillette Stadium has a longstanding commitment to providing a welcoming environment for all guests and ensuring that events held at our venue do not provide a platform for hate speech," Kraft shared in a statement made to ESPN.

"Based on Macklemore's recent actions, material shared from the stage during Ed Sheeran's shows in New Jersey, and a broader history of anti-Semitic rhetoric and imagery that we believe has been deeply offensive and hurtful to the Jewish community, we determined that his participation in the September 25th and 26th shows would not be permitted."

Tour promoter Messina Touring Group confirmed Macklemore’s removal from all remaining U.S. dates after multiple venue operators refused to allow him on the lineup. Eight of the remaining 10 tour stops were scheduled for NFL stadiums.

Macklemore’s controversial history includes a May 2014 performance in Seattle, where he took the stage wearing a bushy black beard, a dark wig and a large prosthetic nose. Civil rights groups, including the Anti-Defamation League, condemned the outfit as resembling historical anti-Jewish caricatures. Macklemore claimed at the time that the costume was a random coincidence.

That controversy resurfaced with the release of "Hind’s Hall," a song referencing student activists who occupied Columbia University’s Hamilton Hall. Critics argue that Macklemore routinely condemns Israel from the stage while ignoring the Oct. 7, 2023, terrorist attacks by Hamas that sparked the current war.

Kraft, who founded the Foundation to Combat Antisemitism in 2019, also addressed what he viewed as Macklemore’s selective presentation of the conflict.

"This decision is not about diminishing the suffering of innocent Palestinians or denying anyone the right to advocate on their behalf," Kraft stated. "But that advocacy should not come at the expense of the Jewish community or obscure the responsibility of Hamas, a designated terrorist organization whose horrific actions have caused immeasurable suffering."

Kraft added that supporting innocent civilians while standing firmly against Hamas and antisemitism should not be mutually exclusive.

"I would welcome the opportunity to sit down with Macklemore and discuss the facts, because ultimately our goal is the same: peace for all people, an end to suffering, and an end to all hate," Kraft said.

Macklemore responded in a social media post blaming venue operators and Kraft for his removal while acknowledging that Sheeran had been placed in a difficult position. He did not accept Kraft’s characterization of his comments as hate speech and insisted that losing his tour spot was a price he was willing to pay.
