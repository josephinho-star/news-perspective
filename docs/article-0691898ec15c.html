Iran imposes electricity rationing, rolling blackouts as regime buckles under US economic pressure

2026-11-26 20:44

Rolling blackouts have become the norm in Iran as the government imposes electricity rationing and other austerity measures — with its president warning of a harsh winter due to mounting US economic pressures.

President Masoud Pezeshkian said the country is due for “difficulties” in the coming months as electricity rationing began this week, with blackouts scheduled for as long as two hours a day across the country’s provinces.

“The attack on the country’s gas infrastructure has aggravated the situation, and we should therefore expect difficulties this winter,” he said, appearing to cast the blame on recent US airstrikes.

The outages, Pezeshkian added, could last even longer once winter settles in, with residents outraged as anxiety grows over how they will keep warm.

“If there’s no electricity and no gas, I guess we can just go out to the car to keep warm,” a teacher in Tehran told The Washington Post. “It’s infuriating.”

The woman added that she and other residents had long feared this outcome as Iran launched other austerity measures in recent months, including cutting back on subsidies for bread and other food.

Residents in at least three provinces are now forced to buy bread at five times the price as normal once their monthly quota runs out, according to state media.

She and her neighbors had since stocked up on emergency supplies, but with no end in sight to the war and the new wave of rationing hitting the streets, the teacher said people have become too exhausted to do anything.

“People are too tired, but they are also kind of numb,” she said of the sentiment in Tehran.

Residents said the rollback on fuel subsidies has hit them the hardest, with people calling the current state of the nation “unbearable” as the war approaches its ninth month.

The US economic pressure campaign, including the naval blockade, is expected to cause Iran’s already struggling economy to shrink by at least 4% this year, Iranian economist Hadi Kahalzadeh told the WaPo.

The blockade alone has deprived Iran of nearly $4 billion every month as the regime’s oil exports, one of its main sources of income, continue to plummet.

President Trump has signaled that he would let the economic pressure campaign play out until Iran signals that it is serious about negotiating a peace deal to end the war.
