Progressives’ Medicare-for-All push is all about the snake oil

2026-11-23 00:09

Emboldened by the midterm election results, progressives are back to pushing Medicare for All — the wonderful-sounding name for an utter monstrosity.

The pretense is that “free” health care is exactly the “affordability” voters want — when Americans overwhelmingly reject this scheme whenever they hear what it actually entails.

Universal health care is “no longer a utopian vision,” cheers Sen. Bernie Sanders, who’ll use his power chairing the Senate Health Committee come January to push his signature issue.

Such a plan is “where the American people are,” he insists; Democrats’ big wins prove it!

No: They voted against Republicans because they felt the GOP wasn’t delivering on its promises to keep costs down, among other gripes.

And putting Uncle Sam in charge of everyone’s health insurance has nothing to do with that.

Any Dem who won in a red state and wants to hold his seat will avoid Medicare for All like the plague.

Sure, you can point to polling that seems to show strong support: One survey shows a majority, 66%, who favor a “national health plan,” vs. 34% opposed.

But that majority flips once you explain that it’s not remotely free: If pollsters say it could mean tax hikes (and it will!), support falls to 35%.

Reminded it could lead to delays in medical tests and treatments (again, dead-certain: that’s how it works everywhere), a bare 24% backed it.

Look: Medicare is already facing deep financial trouble: Its hospital trust fund is set to go bust in just seven years, while its soaring costs are eating ever-more tax dollars, and driving up premiums.

As it is, Medicare depends on cost-shifting from private plans: Doctors and hospitals accept a small loss, making it up by charging everyone else more.

Putting everyone on Medicare, as Sanders wants, could cost as much as $60 trillion over a decade.

And, contrary to the airy assertions of radicals like Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed (Mich.), taxing billionaires more and closing loopholes won’t cover that cost: Every serious analyst acknowledges that middle-class taxpayers will have to pony up — big-time.

One modest estimate: $10,500 a year for middle-class families.

You’d also lose your private insurance (that’s what “Medicare for All” means) and see a greatly limited choice of doctors.

These factors are why every state to look at doing something like this — including Bernie’s Vermont — quickly dropped the idea.

The numbers don’t add up: Medicare for All backers are peddling nothing but lies.

Sanders, El-Sayed & Co. know they have no chance of getting Medicare for All passed, not even with big Democratic majorities — but they’ll use the “vision” of it to pressure moderates into taking steps toward it, one expansion at a time.

Of course, every step toward a disastrous system will actually make the US health care system even more dysfunctional, but they’ll simply keep prescribing the same poisonous snake oil, eventually sticking America with a nightmare it would never accept in one fell swoop.
