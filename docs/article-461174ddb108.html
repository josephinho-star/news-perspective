‘The View’ co-host Sunny Hostin praises Hillary Clinton’s anti-Trump rant after Jensen Huang’s Nvidia return

2026-11-25 02:38

“The View” co-host Sunny Hostin said Tuesday that former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton has “never been wrong about anything,” pointing to her reaction to the reinstatement of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and President Donald Trump’s role.

Hostin read the former Secretary of State’s post to X, which said, “Is it a national security scandal? Yes. Is it a corruption scandal? Yes. Is it an insider trading scandal? Yes. Is it a market manipulation scandal? Yes. If it’s ‘super intelligence,’ call it Supergate.”

“She’s never been wrong, and this is just what it is,” she added.

Hostin claimed Trump made “approximately $15 million” upon Huang being reinstated through the company’s stock rise. A Financial Times report said the president could have made up to $15 million upon Huang’s return.

“The board is not doing its job — the board of this company — and what the board asked the government, which shouldn’t even be a conversation, they asked the government whether or not the Trump administration would make sure or would qualify that Jensen was not a national security risk,” Hostin said. “Trump said he’s not a national security risk, which is not true. And then second, they asked that the Justice Department not be investigating him… This is corruption to the highest level.”

“Sunny Hostin is an extremely unlikeable, talentless hack with a poorly rated TV show who clearly suffers from a severe and incurable disease known as Trump Derangement Syndrome. I lose brain cells every single time I have the displeasure of hearing her speak,” White House spokesman Davis Ingle told Fox News Digital.

Clinton has appeared on “The View” numerous times over the years, with friendly receptions on the liberal-leaning show.

Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Nvidia’s board decided to bring back Huang after he resigned earlier this month following reports of an extramarital relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom U.S. intelligence reportedly identified as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset.

Trump spoke with CNBC on Monday after Huang was reinstated, revealing that he intervened to get him back on as CEO.

“Yeah, the answer is no,” Trump told CNBC in an interview when asked about the development and whether Huang could have been reinstated without his input. “I believe my intervention was very important.”

Here’s the latest on the Nvidia CEO’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy:

A source familiar told Fox News that Trump and Vice President JD Vance worked over the weekend to reinstate Huang after they received security briefings and became convinced that Huang did not present a national security threat in his role as CEO. In recent days, Vance worked with figures in Silicon Valley and higher-ups at Nvidia to urge the board to reinstate him, the source said.

The source also said the administration viewed Huang’s support for open-source AI as a backstop against major AI companies accruing uncontested capabilities behind closed doors — a rare position among AI executives.

Co-host Ana Navarro also called out the president for telling CNBC he intervened.
