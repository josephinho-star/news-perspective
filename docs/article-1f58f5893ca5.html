Obama-era homeless policy fueled crisis it promised to end, expert says as Trump admin makes striking reversal

2026-11-19 01:02

The Trump administration’s decision to permanently cut federal funding to Los Angeles’ scandal-plagued homeless agency marks a stunning reversal of an Obama-era policy that one expert said produced massive failures.

In an interview with Fox News Digital, homeless services veteran Michele Steeb broke down Tuesday’s announcement from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) that it is severing ties with the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority (LAHSA).

Steeb said the Housing First model adopted by LAHSA, which provides housing unconditionally without requiring individuals to pursue sobriety or mental health treatment, was championed by former President Barack Obama.

She argued the model discouraged recovery by allowing people to remain in subsidized housing indefinitely, leaving fewer available units for others seeking permanent housing.

"So, back in 2013, when they instituted this, President Obama promised that this very different approach would end homelessness in a decade. And a decade later, we were at an almost 35% increase with no accountability," Steeb said, referring to the national homeless population.

The program ultimately "eliminates purpose from individuals," Steeb said. "They're given housing for life without any requirements."

Earlier this year, Obama called Los Angeles' homelessness crisis a moral "atrocity," arguing that homelessness programs must pair assistance with accountability.

"We should recognize that the average person doesn’t want to have to navigate around a tent city in the middle of downtown," Obama said. "That’s a losing political strategy."

California, which adopted the model statewide, offers a particularly striking example of what Steeb views as its shortcomings. Despite significant government investment, homelessness has continued to climb rather than move toward the promised goal of ending it.

Steeb said homelessness in California surged 40% between 2016 and 2025, even as federal spending on homelessness increased at least 200% under the Housing First approach instituted in 2013.

The situation has been particularly stark in Los Angeles, where homelessness climbed between 60% and 70% over the past five years despite LAHSA receiving more than $1 billion in federal funding.

Steeb said, despite the mounting evidence of failure, defenders of the system continued to blame the homelessness crisis on insufficient funding.

"It's a shame to me that the system, what I often call the homeless industrial complex, is trying to defend it," she said.

"It just has failed so massively. So, there's no excuses except that it was the wrong approach."

Steeb said HUD's settlement represents an effort by the Trump administration to restore accountability to homeless services after LAHSA became embroiled in allegations of fraud and financial mismanagement.

The agency's funding will soon be transferred to Los Angeles County, according to officials.

Although Steeb acknowledged the county has its own problems, she hopes the transition could improve a system she believes has failed to address the crisis.

"I'm hopeful. … I think it's better than LAHSA, but I don't think it's going to be that much better," Steeb said.
