Turkey says citizenship of 6,134 people canceled or withdrawn over fraud, security concerns

2026-09-21 15:05

Turkey has canceled or withdrawn citizenship decisions covering 6,134 people over irregularities in its citizenship-by-investment program and national security or public order concerns, the Interior Ministry said on Monday.

The ministry did not specify the period covered by the overall figure.

Turkey’s citizenship-by-investment program allows foreign nationals to apply for citizenship after purchasing property worth at least $400,000 and agreeing not to sell it for three years. Applicants must submit an officially recognized appraisal showing that the property meets the required value.

Turkey lowered the minimum property investment from $1 million to $250,000 in 2018 in an effort to attract foreign capital. The threshold was raised to $400,000 in 2022.

The Interior Ministry said investment eligibility certificates belonging to 1,150 investors had been canceled after investigators identified sham or otherwise irregular transactions, including the use of fraudulent property appraisal reports.

The cancellations affected the citizenship decisions of 5,391 people when investors’ family members were included.

The investigations were carried out by the Directorate General of Land Registry and Cadastre, the Tax Inspection Board and the Security Directorate General.

Separately, the ministry withdrew citizenship decisions covering 263 investors who were subsequently deemed a threat to public order or national security. Including their family members, that category covered 743 people.

Together, the two categories comprised 1,413 investors and 6,134 people including family members, the ministry said.

It added that since February 11, citizenship had been canceled for 1,393 people, including 458 investors and their relatives, after their investment eligibility certificates were revoked. Citizenship was withdrawn from another seven people on national security or public order grounds.

The announcement came hours after İstanbul prosecutors detained 72 suspects and put 30 companies under trustee management in a separate investigation into fraudulent property sales used to obtain Turkish citizenship.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek said the operation was the second phase of an İstanbul-based investigation and targeted transactions linked to Gül İnşaat AŞ, Beyaz İnşaat and LİV İnşaat.

Investigators examined property sales to 734 foreign nationals and concluded that 274 transactions were fraudulent, according to Gürlek.

They identified 1,070 people, including investors’ family members, who had acquired Turkish citizenship through the transactions. Another 11 people were initially reported to be in the process of obtaining citizenship.

The Interior Ministry later said one of those 11 was subject to withdrawal proceedings, three applications had been rejected and the seven other people had not applied for citizenship.

The sham transactions were valued at more than TL 3.5 billion ($71.7 million), Gürlek said.

Prosecutors launched proceedings against 88 suspects, 72 of whom were detained in Monday’s operation. Police seized digital material during searches of homes and companies linked to the suspects.

Courts imposed precautionary measures on 2,011 properties, a hotel, 86 vehicles, two yachts and 42 bank accounts. Thirty companies were put under trustee management.

Gürlek said the administrative process to revoke citizenship obtained through the transactions had begun. The 1,070 people identified in Monday’s investigation have therefore not necessarily already lost their citizenship.

Monday’s raids marked the second phase of an investigation into fraudulent use of Turkey’s citizenship-by-investment program.

In the first phase, announced in August, investigators focused on transactions linked to Babacan İnşaat. Prosecutors alleged that low-priced properties had been presented as substantially more valuable through fraudulent appraisal reports and sold to foreign nationals seeking Turkish citizenship.

Investigators identified 687 people who had acquired citizenship through the alleged scheme, and the Interior Ministry began proceedings to revoke it.

Police detained 72 suspects. Courts also imposed measures on seven companies, 1,045 properties, a hotel in the southwestern resort town of Bodrum, 15 vehicles, a yacht and 10 bank accounts.

The investment program has faced criticism from opposition parties over alleged irregularities and national security risks, particularly after several foreign organized crime figures were found to have obtained Turkish citizenship through property investments.

Croatian drug trafficker Nenad Petrak, who was wanted on an INTERPOL Red Notice when he was captured in İstanbul in November 2023, had acquired Turkish citizenship in December 2022 after buying a $250,000 property. He took the Turkish name Nenat Çelik.

A 2025 indictment also revealed that 11 members of an Iranian organized crime network and a Georgian national had acquired Turkish citizenship.

They included Sa’dollah Amirshaghaghi, also known as Saeed Amiri, who took the name Can Diego and was accused of running illegal betting websites, laundering money and leading an organized crime organization.

The Interior Ministry said the irregularities involved fraudulent documents submitted before applications reached the citizenship agencies and that it had found no negligence or wrongdoing by staff at the General Directorate of Population and Citizenship Affairs.
