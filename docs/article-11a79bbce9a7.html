Alleged Mexican drug cartel boss ‘El Chapo Isidro’ arrested in special forces raid

2026-11-21 17:46

The Mexican military captured one of the FBI’s 10 most wanted fugitives during a special forces raid in the Mexican state of Sinaloa on Friday night.

Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro”, was arrested along with two other alleged leaders of his gang after a gun battle that triggered widespread panic in the city of Guasave.

The alleged leader of a major drug-trafficking cartel, the Meza Flores Transnational Criminal Organization (TCO), he had managed to evade arrest for more than a decade, even though there was a reward of up to $5m (£3.8m) on his head.

His FBI “most wanted” poster states his organisation “is allegedly responsible for the possession, distribution and importation of large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States, and has predominately controlled drug production, transportation and distribution in several locations throughout Mexico”.

Mexico’s defence ministry also alleged he is the leader of the Beltrán Leyva criminal organisation, which is allied to the Mayos, one of two rival factions in the powerful Sinaloa cartel.

Meza Flores’s alleged second-in-command, Germán N, whose alias is “Serafín”, and José N, an alleged financial operator for the Beltrán Leyva group, were also arrested.

The operation to capture the men was carried out by “elite military forces” with help from US intelligence, according to Mexico’s defence ministry.

After arriving in the “target area … using stealth and surprise”, the ministry said the arresting force came under attack “with gunfire and explosives” but managed to enter a house and make the arrests.

The men were then evacuated to Mexico City on a Mexican air force plane to be handed over to prosecutors.

Videos and photos show civilians fleeing gunfire at a baseball game at Kuroda Park stadium in Guasave, as well as burnt-out cars.

A barrage of rapid fire from what appeared to be Mexican security force helicopters hit the ground, according to CNN, and players from the Venados de Mazatlán and Algodoneros de Guasave teams abandoned the field mid-game.

Four Mexican army infantry soldiers died in the operation, the defence ministry said in a statement posted on X that extended the institution’s condolences to their families.

It added that security forces killed seven attackers and arrested eight alleged criminals in the operation, including three who were transferred to Mexico City and five who were turned over to the attorney general’s office in Culiacán, Sinaloa.

Overnight on Friday the military operation triggered a wave of violence in the state of Sinaloa, including armed clashes between Mexican security forces and cartel members, who set up flaming roadblocks in some areas.

By Saturday the violence had substantially subsided. Sinaloa state authorities said “the situation is under control and being addressed by all levels of government”.

Meza Flores was originally indicted in 2012, and in 2013 the US Treasury placed sanctions on him under the Kingpin Act, freezing his assets.

In 2017, the US Department of State’s Narcotics Rewards Program began offering up to $5m as a reward for information leading directly to his arrest or conviction.

He has been charged with multiple counts of drug-trafficking violations, and use and possession of a firearm in the US, and was wanted in Mexico for organised crime and offences against public health.
