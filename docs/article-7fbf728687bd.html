German gov’t report highlights continued persecution of Gülen followers, religious discrimination in Turkey

2026-11-27 15:06

Turkey continues to persecute followers of the faith-based Gülen movement and discriminate against religious minorities, according to the German government’s latest report on global religious freedom.

The report, which covers the 2023-2025 period, linked restrictions on religious freedom in Turkey to growing political repression and declining judicial independence, citing barriers to legal recognition and religious practices, discrimination against Alevis and Christians, antisemitism and reports of abductions, ill-treatment and torture of Gülen movement supporters. It was approved by the German cabinet on November 18.

The report said the Turkish government has carried out a harsh crackdown on Gülen movement followers, particularly since a coup attempt in July 2016, which Ankara blamed on the movement.

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has targeted the Gülen movement, inspired by Muslim cleric Fethullah Gülen, who died in 2024, since corruption investigations in December 2013 implicated him as well as some members of his family and inner circle. He dismissed the probes as a Gülenist conspiracy and designated the movement as a terrorist organization in May 2016. The movement denies involvement in the coup attempt or any terrorist activity.

According to figures released by Justice Minister Akın Gürlek in July 2026, Turkish authorities had initiated legal proceedings against 720,338 people over alleged links to the movement since 2016, securing more than 127,000 convictions. Investigations or trials involving 83,404 people were ongoing, while 10,485 people remained in prison.

According to the report, despite constitutional guarantees of religious freedom, the Turkish state maintains control over religious life through the Directorate of Religious Affairs (Diyanet), which reports directly to the presidency and oversees some 90,000 mosques. No non-Sunni religious community has legal personality as a religious community in Turkey.

The report said Alevis, estimated to account for 15 percent of Turkey’s population, are not recognized as a religious minority and that their demands for equal treatment have yet to be fully met. Although their houses of worship, known as cemevis, have received public funding since 2022, they are still not officially recognized as places of worship. Some Alevi associations have criticized the funding over fears of government control.

Christian communities without a historical presence in Turkey continue to face difficulties in obtaining legal recognition, establishing places of worship and training clergy, according to the report. Members of foreign Protestant churches have reportedly been expelled or denied re-entry into Turkey over allegations of missionary activity, which is not illegal under Turkish law.

The report said discrimination against members of religious minorities remains widespread, citing NGO reports that Christians, Jews and Alevis are particularly targeted by hate crimes. Antisemitic views are expressed not only in the tabloid press and on social media but also by senior politicians.

The report also cited credible accounts of discrimination against non-Sunnis seeking public sector employment and said compulsory religious education in public schools remains predominantly Sunni despite revisions following rulings by the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR).

The findings echo those of the US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF), which in March recommended that Turkey be placed on the US State Department’s Special Watch List over systematic and severe violations of religious freedom.

For the first time Germany’s religious freedom report also included a chapter on transnational repression, the practice of governments targeting perceived opponents beyond their borders.

While the chapter does not specifically address Turkish activities in Germany, the country’s domestic intelligence agency previously identified Gülen movement followers as among the primary targets of Turkish intelligence operations on German soil in its 2024 annual report.
