Lisa Cook lawyer signals Fed governor won't attend Trump committee's hearing

2026-11-17 15:38

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook's attorney on Tuesday gave the strongest signal yet that his client will not attend a hearing at the White House with a "committee of inquiry" investigating mortgage fraud allegations against Cook.

The committee established last month by President Donald Trump, who has previously tried to remove Cook from the central bank's board, is pretextual and poses a threat to the Fed, attorney Abbe Lowell wrote in a letter to presidential counsel Will Scharf.

"Governor Cook has no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing — and indeed, has a duty to the Federal Reserve to decline to participate in any proceeding that would undermine the institution's unique status and independence," Lowell wrote in the letter, which is dated Monday and was made public Tuesday.

The latest correspondence came nearly two weeks after the White House committee delayed its proposed hearing with Cook to Dec. 14 from Nov. 5. Lowell said that any hearing with Cook could not take place until at least December because of conflicts with her Fed duties.

While Lowell had raised strong concerns from the outset about the legitimacy of the committee, he initially said Cook "welcomes the opportunity to present the facts so she can clear her name."

But he has continued to cast doubts on both the committee's purpose and its composition of three executive branch officials, including National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett.

Lowell's tone sounded more hostile than ever in this week's letter, the first known to be sent by either party in the matter since the Republican Party's devastating losses in the midterm elections on Nov. 3.

He accused Scharf and the committee of seeking "only to manufacture 'cause' for a predetermined removal outcome."

Lowell highlighted two top concerns. First, he asserted that Hassett "is not competent to serve on any factfinding committee" because his prior comments about Cook "demonstrate that he has prejudged the issue."

Second, Lowell called it "fundamentally unfair" that the committee is demanding Cook hand over documents "immediately," while pledging to provide the governor with its own records just seven days before the hearing.

"Your and the Committee's failure to address these legitimate issues, over multiple weeks of back-and-forth correspondence, reinforces my prior observation: President Trump's decision to fire Governor Cook has already been made," Lowell wrote.

"Consequently, this Committee process appears to be a façade designed to obscure a predetermined decision to fire Governor Cook or to unreasonably manufacture a supposed 'adverse inference' if she does not participate in a process that does not comport with the requirements of a fair hearing," the lawyer wrote.

Lowell told Scharf that Cook would still be prepared to appear for a hearing if the White House "implements fair procedures." But the "ultimatums" Scharf gave in his last reply in early November "do not provide a productive path forward to enabling that participation," Lowell wrote.

The White House did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment on Lowell's letter.

Trump, who frequently urges the Fed to lower interest rates, has long sought to fire Cook, an appointee of former President Joe Biden.

Cook, the first Black woman to serve on the Fed's Board of Governors, last voted to cut rates in December 2025, siding with the majority. The Fed unanimously voted to raise rates after its meeting in September.

Trump officials sent a criminal referral last year alleging Cook may have committed mortgage fraud by falsely claiming different properties as her primary residence in order to get more favorable terms. Cook adamantly denied wrongdoing, and, after Trump tried to fire her in August 2025, filed a lawsuit to block her removal.

The Supreme Court in June prevented Trump from firing Cook, but gave him a path to try again if additional legal steps were taken. In August, the White House told Cook it was reviving its efforts to fire her on mortgage-related grounds. In October, Trump signed a presidential memo establishing the committee of inquiry to investigate allegations that Cook lied on records related to "one or more mortgage instruments."
