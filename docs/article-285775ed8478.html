Veterans Day 2026 brings free meals at popular restaurants — and some deals don't end Nov. 11

2026-11-11 17:02

Veterans and active-duty military members can find free food and other promotions at restaurants across the country on Veterans Day, Nov. 11. Some offers also give customers a reason to return for another deal later.

About 30 restaurant brands are included in Dinova's 2026 Veterans Day Dining Guide, according to Alison Quinn, the company's Florida-based president.

The guide is in its fifth year and includes national and regional chains like Bonefish Grill, Carrabba's Italian Grill, Hooters, Outback Steakhouse and Red Lobster.

"What we're starting to see now is it's going beyond Veterans Day, to try to give back to the vets in a way that is more meaningful," Quinn told Fox News Digital. "Just not about one day but come back, and we'll give you something again."

Quinn pointed to California-based Hofman Hospitality Group, which is offering select meals to veterans and a $10 bonus card for a future visit. Lucille's Smokehouse Bar-B-Que is among the group's brands.

Outback Steakhouse is offering a complimentary Bloomin' Onion and soft drink, along with a meal offer for a return visit, she said.

Other deals listed in the guide include 10 free boneless wings and fries at Buffalo Wild Wings, a complimentary Red's Big Tavern Burger at Red Robin, free shrimp at Red Lobster and a free All-American Smash at Smashburger.

Dinova started the guide to share offers with veterans and, in some cases, active-duty military personnel, Quinn said. The company's business dining network includes about 25,000 restaurants and 600 corporate partners, she said.

Downloads of the guide have increased over the years, Quinn said, and the promotions may be useful for veterans or active-duty personnel who are struggling or "living under a tight budget."

Asked which deal she would choose, Quinn singled out Eddie Merlot's complimentary cheeseburger. Her father is an 85-year-old veteran, she said, and she plans to tell him about it.

Veterans and active-duty military members should bring identification and check with their local restaurant before visiting, Quinn said. Participation and ID requirements can vary by location and offer.
