Mark Ruffalo doubles down on call to oust Schumer over 'entrenched religious beliefs and loyalties'

2026-11-23 00:30

Actor Mark Ruffalo said Sen. Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., was too beholden to "entrenched religious beliefs and loyalties" to lead the Senate in a post on X, advocating for Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., as Senate majority leader.

"Israel is just one issue in a whole host of reasons Van Hollen is the right leader for our times. We need Justice, we need leadership that follows the will of the people not entrenched religious beliefs and loyalties," Ruffalo wrote on X. "Does not follow entrenched fossil fuel and corporate interests, nor throws a blind eye to the hard realties [sic] we face today."

The four-time Academy Award nominee argued that Van Hollen had the integrity, strength and spirit to fight "the social and moral corruption that has set in with age-old presumptions" in the incoming Democratic-led Congress.

"We have absolutely nothing to lose by replacing Sen. Schumer," Ruffalo wrote. "We can only gain more justice and freedom. Put pressure on your senators. Now is the time. Keep the momentum of this election going. Thank you, Senator Schumer, but it’s time to move aside."

Ruffalo faced backlash across both sides of the political aisle for what critics viewed as a slight against Schumer's Jewish background.

"Entrenched religious beliefs? Entrenched loyalties? Please elaborate, @MarkRuffalo," Anti-Defamation League CEO and National Director Jonathan Greenblatt wrote. "Which religion? Loyal to whom?"

"There are many devout Senators & Congressmen with 'entrenched religious beliefs.' Chuck Schumer is not one of them," Rabbi Yaakov Menken, executive vice president of the Coalition for Jewish Values, wrote. "Van Hollen is far more tied to the Episcopal Bishop of Washington than Schumer is to any leading Rabbi. Guess what really bothers Ruffalo."

"Can you clarify what you mean by 'entrenched religious beliefs and loyalties'?" political commentator Meghan McCain asked.

By Sunday, Ruffalo doubled down on his post, citing Schumer's 2018 comments at the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) annual policy conference where he was quoted saying, "Of course, we say it’s our land, the Torah says it, but they don’t believe in the Torah. So that’s the reason there is not peace."

"These are Senator Schumer’s words, not mine," Ruffalo added on X.

"Schumer is entitled to his religious beliefs, but those beliefs cannot determine the rights of another people. And they certainly cannot substitute for international law," Ruffalo continued. "This is a conflict about land, freedom, security, equality and self-determination. Peace requires justice, accountability and respect for the rights of both Palestinians and Israelis."

Fox News Digital reached out to Ruffalo's representative and Schumer's office for comment.

Human rights attorney Brooke Goldstein, executive director of The Lawfare Project and Founder of the #EndJewHatred movement, denounced Ruffalo's posts, calling the actor "obsessively fixated" on the Jewish state to the point of questioning members of the party he supports.

"And now they’re even turning on one of their own, Senator Chuck Schumer, because apparently even being a loyal Democrat isn’t enough when you’re Jewish," Goldstein told Fox News Digital. "They’re saying the quiet part out loud now, no longer afraid to invoke his Jewish identity as grounds for distrust. The message is unmistakable: ‘He’s a Jew. We can’t trust him.'"

"His [Ruffalo's] obsessive fixation on the Jewish state, his selective outrage, and his breathtaking moral relativism raise serious questions about whether his "so called activism" is driven by genuine concern for America’s interests or by hostility toward Jews," she added.

Ruffalo previously faced accusations of antisemitism after he criticized the Paramount merger earlier this year, focusing on CEO David Ellison and his father's history of supporting Israel.
