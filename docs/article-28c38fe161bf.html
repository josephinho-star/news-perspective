Shoebridge says US submission on online safety laws an ‘extraordinary intervention’ – as it happened

2026-09-23 08:00

Shoebridge accuses US of overreach in Australia’s online safety laws

The Greens senator David Shoebridge has accused the United States of interfering in Australia’s domestic politics after the Trump administration criticised the government’s proposed opt-in algorithm reforms.

In a submission overnight, the Trump administration warned the measures risked becoming a mechanism for “viewpoint-based censorship” and called for American platforms to be exempted.

Speaking on ABC’s Afternoon Briefing, Shoebridge said:

It’s an extraordinary intervention in our domestic politics, that’s what we have seen.

The US embassy issuing, basically, a slap down to the Australian government on a piece of domestic legislation that the Australian parliament should be deciding on, not Washington.

It is almost unprecedented in my experience to see a foreign government in this country through their embassy … so overtly try and dissuade the Australian parliament from taking actions to protect the Australian public.

Shoebridge said the US “needs to be brought to heel by the rest of the world” as global discussions continue over artificial intelligence safeguards and the power of major technology companies.

Inner west council to ban AI smartglasses in public facilities

Sydney’s inner west council has moved to ban smartglasses and other wearable recording devices in its facilities, including pools, change rooms, childcare centres and public toilets.

Inner west councillor Kerrie Fergusson said that the technology is likely “just the beginning of many new challenges for local councils”.

For people with vision or hearing impairments, they offer a new level of independence. But they can also discreetly record high-definition video and live-stream, creating genuine privacy concerns …

This isn’t tearing down new technology like a bunch of 19th-century luddites, but ensuring AI technology properly serves our community without compromising its privacy or civil liberties.

Council staff have been asked to report back on how the ban will be implemented, including conditions of entry and signage requirements.

Exemptions will be provided to people who rely on the technology as accessibility devices.

As reported earlier this morning, smartglasses could soon be banned across Sydney’s eastern suburbs beaches Coogee, Clovelly and Maroubra under a motion put forward to Randwick council. It has already banned the technology at the Des Renford leisure centre, citing “growing community concerns” about their potential misuse.

It also follows City of Sydney councillors voting to ban smartglasses at council gyms, childcare centres and pools on Monday.

Parenting advocate Georgie Dent tells National Press Club that Australians should get 52 weeks paid parental leave

More on the national press club address today by Georgie Dent, Parenthood chief executive …

Dent cited Treasury data showing that having children reduces the earnings of Australian women by an average of 55% over the first five years of parenthood.

Dent said families need two incomes to stay afloat in the current economy and even with both parents working, they have less breathing room in their household budget.

For many families it feels like a cruel game of whack-a-mole …

For parents of young children seeking to raise and provide for their family one obstacle towers over the others: early childhood education and care.

Too many face an impossible bind. They cannot access it, they cannot afford it, or they cannot trust what is available. This isn’t perceived: the options families have are largely determined by what the market has delivered.

Dent will call for a policy pathway from government for the provision of 52 weeks of paid parental leave, up from 26 weeks currently paid for by taxpayers, and meaningful parental leave for fathers and partners.

Police offer $1m reward for information about missing boy Gus Lamont

A $1m reward for information about missing four-year-old Gus Lamont is on offer, as police say they have found no evidence to suggest he is still alive.

It has been almost a year since four-year-old Gus went missing from his family’s enormous sheep farm in the South Australian outback.

He was last seen playing outside at 5pm on Saturday 27 September. Half an hour later, he was gone.

His disappearance sparked one of the biggest searches the state has ever undertaken, with new searches launched as new information or theories came to light.

Volunteers, police, emergency service and defence personnel, along with dogs and divers and drones scoured the 60,000 hectare Oak Park Station, about 300km inland from Adelaide.

One of the biggest moments in the saga came in February this year, when the South Australian police declared his disappearance a major crime, and said they had identified a suspect.

Det Supt Darren Fielke said at the time they had effectively ruled out Gus wandering off or being abducted, and that they were now investigating someone in his household.

The suspect was not one of his parents, Fielke said.

The SA premier, Peter Malinauskas, said they stand ready to do “everything they can” to help police. He said it would go to anyone who had information leading to a body or a conviction. He said it was a “crystal clear” message:

If you know something you’ve gotta speak.

The disappearance of Gus Lamont has deeply shocked and saddened all South Australians. I cannot imagine the anguish that Gus’s parents Jess and Josh are going through, and my thoughts remain with them.

Fielke said the police would do everything they could “to locate Gus and reunite him with his family”.

Police arrest Melbourne man who allegedly pledged allegiance to Isis

Counter-terrorism police have arrested a Melbourne man, who they allege acted as an Isis propagandist.

The 65-year-old was arrested during a raid of a home in Carlton this morning.

Police allege he pledged allegiance to Isis and has played a significant role in spreading propaganda for the terrorist group, sending about 10,000 files of violent extremist material.

The man is alleged to have shared information about avoiding detection from authorities and instructions on how to plan and conduct mass casualty attacks.

AFP acting assistant commissioner counter-terrorism, Paula Hudson, spoke to reporters just after midday.

We will allege the videos, photographs, audio files and documents contain messages relating to current content, being executions, mass murder, torture and propaganda ….

Let me be clear, terrorist propaganda is not harmless online content. Terrorist organisations like Isis rely on online platforms to distribute propaganda and recruit supporters.

Hudson added that police launched an investigation after Australian Border Force officers found suspicious material on electronic devices seized as the man flew out of Melbourne.

She said the man was a “principal distributor of Isis propaganda within the group”.

The man lives with his wife and is an Australian citizen, according to police.
