Why this little known Chinese chipmaker has caused a global stock rout

2026-10-13 20:05

Chip stocks sank on Tuesday after a Chinese memory chipmaker said it had made significant progress in developing new and more efficient designs.

ChangXin Memory Technologies, or CXMT, announced over the weekend that it had made headway on new architecture that will increase memory density by packing more data into a smaller space.

CXMT is China's leading rival to global memory makers, including Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung, and the news sent rival names tumbling.

Micron sank 3.62% on Tuesday, while SK Hynix's South Korean-listed shares closed 10% lower and Samsung Electronics fell 7.4%. Other memory-related stocks, including SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital, slipped as well.

Until its summer blockbuster IPO, CXMT was little known outside of its home market.

CXMT leapt onto the global consciousness with its initial public offering in July.

The company soared nearly 466% in its debut on Shanghai's tech-heavy STAR Market, making it the most valuable China-listed company at the time.

Shares have since declined by 4% from their IPO-day price of 49 yuan per share, but the company maintains a market cap of 3.46 trillion Chinese yuan ($520 billion), not far off tech giant Tencent's 3.87 trillion yuan.

Headquartered in Hefei, CXMT's listing came roughly a decade on from its founding in 2016.

One of the biggest questions for CXMT after its IPO was whether it would be able to narrow the capability gap with Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix, particularly in high-bandwidth memory — a key component of AI chip systems.

While huge domestic demand is supporting CXMT, it still faces challenges accessing chipmaking equipment, which has been effectively barred from China by U.S. export controls, including ASML's extreme ultraviolet, or EUV, lithography machines, which are needed to manufacture the most advanced semiconductors.

CXMT reportedly plans to roll out a new server-memory product based on new cell architecture by the end of 2026.

The new design allows higher storage density, based on "4F square" tech. 4F square designs are also being pursued by SK Hynix, which in 2025 presented a roadmap for developing the tech.

CXMT is often seen by analysts as trailing global competitors at the frontier of memory developments, and any news of breakthroughs can have a significant impact on investor perception of global rivals' market advantages.

CXMT has made significant market gains, despite challenges related to export controls.

Its share of the global dynamic random-access memory, or DRAM, market has risen to around 10%, up from about 4% a year ago, according to an HSBC note from Thursday.

"While US government is unlikely to allow made in China memory sales to US, sales to data centers in other regions like Europe could indirectly impact Micron," Citi analysts said in a note from August.

Apple has started testing CXMT's DRAM chips for devices sold within China and was lobbying the U.S. government to permit broader use of the company's products, the Financial Times reported in July, citing people familiar with the matter. Apple has been approached for comment.
