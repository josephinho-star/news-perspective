Far-right ally urges parliamentary vote to clear Erdoğan’s path to another term in 2028

2026-10-06 12:29

Devlet Bahçeli, the leader of Turkey’s far-right Nationalist Movement Party (MHP), on Tuesday called on parliament to call early elections in 2028 to allow his ally, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, to seek another term under an exception to the constitutional two-term limit, the T24 news website reported.

Speaking at his party’s parliamentary group meeting, Bahçeli said Erdoğan should again be the presidential candidate of the People’s Alliance, led by the ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) and the MHP.

The proposal would require support beyond the two parties. The AKP has 280 seats and the MHP 46, leaving them 34 votes short of the 360 needed in the 600-seat parliament to call early elections.

Under Article 116 of the constitution, a president serving a second term can run once more if parliament votes to hold presidential and parliamentary elections before the term ends. A decision by Erdoğan himself to call early elections would not activate that exception.

Bahçeli said parliament should act “at an appropriate time and with the broadest possible consensus,” while keeping the elections in 2028, claiming that doing so would preserve stability and predictability.

“A third term is likely to see many important developments in our country, our region and the world, and resolve and experience, above all, will be needed to manage this difficult process,” Bahçeli said.

He said Erdoğan had the experience needed and described his proposed political formula for the next five-year period as “Erdoğan in the executive, the MHP in the legislature; the People’s Alliance in Turkey, the Turkish century in the world.”

Erdoğan has already been elected president three times — in 2014, 2018 and 2023. Turkey’s Supreme Election Board (YSK), however, treated his 2018 election as the start of his first term under the executive presidential system introduced following a constitutional referendum in 2017.

The board rejected objections to his candidacy in 2023 on that basis. Opposition parties and some legal experts disputed the interpretation, arguing that his presidency from 2014 to 2018 should also have counted toward the two-term limit.

Bahçeli’s reference to a third term follows the ruling alliance’s counting of Erdoğan’s terms under the current system. Another victory would be his fourth presidential election win overall.

His remarks follow repeated calls from ruling alliance figures to use the parliamentary mechanism to enable another Erdoğan candidacy.

Presidential adviser Mehmet Uçum proposed in June that the elections be held on April 16, 2028, several weeks before the expected regular election date. He said a parliamentary decision would activate the constitutional exception. Bahçeli subsequently endorsed the proposal.

MHP Deputy Chairman İsmail Özdemir said in September that Erdoğan would again be his party’s candidate in 2028, without explaining how he would become eligible.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek also said in September that an election would be held in 2028 “to re-elect our president.”

Erdoğan served as prime minister from 2003 to 2014 and has been president since then.
