Cyber security official says there are 'no indications' PHP code flaw has led to government computer sytems being hacked

Ollie Whitehouse, chief technology officer at the UK’s National Cyber Security Centre, told Radio 4’s the World at One that incidents like today’s computer system shutdowns because of a coding problem were likely to re-occur. He explained:

[This] highlights to a lot of organisations that technology is not a one-time purchase now, that it needs that continuous investment throughout its operational lifetime.

We’re in an era now of digital dependency, coupled with heightened cyber threat, coupled with increasing capability, which means all organisations are going to likely have to revisit their risk assumptions and work out how they are able to invest in technology to be able to meet the contemporary era.

Whitehouse said there were “no indications” that the PHP flaw now being fixed had been successfully exploited by hackers.

He also said that the problem that had led to 32 government websites being temporarily shut down (see 12.54pm) was originally identified months ago. He said the NCSC had been conducting a “coordinated disclosure process” to warn organisations of the vulnerability, until a leak meant that the process had to be accelerated.

Starmer granted core participant status in grooming gangs inquiry

Keir Starmer may be summoned to give evidence at the grooming gangs inquiry, the Press Association reports. PA says:

The former prime minister, who ordered the probe last year, has been listed as a core participant for the inquiry’s hearings which are due to begin in December.

This means he is among people, groups and organisations formally granted special status by the chairwoman under public inquiry rules, giving him rights to participate in the investigation.

While this does not automatically mean he will be required to give evidence, many core participants are asked or compelled to do so.

Victims and their families, charities, whistleblowers, police chiefs and government departments are among those also listed as core participants.

The scandal-hit probe is looking at how grooming gangs operated in England and Wales and the actions of police, councils, health and social care services, schools and other public bodies.

An inquiry spokesman said the decision on core participant status “considers whether a person played, or may have played, a direct and significant role in relation to the matters to which the inquiry relates, whether they have a significant interest in an important aspect of the matters to which the inquiry relates, or whether the person may be subject to explicit or significant criticism during the inquiry proceedings or in the report, or in any interim report”, adding: “Nobody is beyond examination or transparency, and that the inquiry is following the evidence, wherever it leads.”

Starmer was prime minister when the grooming gangs inquiry was announced last year. But he was also director of public prosecutions from 2008 to 2013, at a time when the CPS was being accused of being too reluctant to prosecute in many cases of alleged child sexual abuse. As DPP, Starmer set out to change this – ordering prosecutors to be more sympathetic to victims, and less inclined to disbelieve them or to drop cases because victims might not make compelling witnesses.

32 UK government services offline while work carried out to sort out PHP programming flaw, minister says

Some 32 government services are offline and four are partially unavailable while work is carried out to address potential cybersecurity issues, a minister has said.

As the Press Association reports, organisations around the world have been scrambling to fix a critical security flaw in the programming language PHP used by the majority of websites.

Downing Street on Thursday said no large UK public-facing critical services have been affected, but some “limited” government websites have gone dark while issues are resolved. National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) experts are working with OpenAI, whose technology was used to discover the vulnerabilities, and partners around the globe to address potential problems, a No 10 spokesperson said.

In the House of Lords, Liz Lloyd, the tech minister, said:

The information I have is that 32 government services are unavailable and four are partially unavailable.

Lloyd said that “has largely resulted from precautionary measures taken to protect systems while security updates and mitigations are applied”.

The No 10 spokesperson told reporters:

Work has already taken place across government to identify and fix potential exposures to our systems, and our experts are continuing to work closely with both OpenAI and international partners on this issue.

Tories claim crime figures 'spiralling out of control' - and why the data doesn't back this up

The Conservative party seems to have missed the bit in the ONS report on the crime figures where it said “crime against individuals and households has generally decreased over the last 10 years”. (See 9.54am.) Chris Philp, the shadow home secretary, has issued a statement saying:

Crime is spiralling out of control under Labour. Record numbers of sexual offences, knife crime and shop theft show what happens when criminals know they can get away with it. This is a direct consequence of Labour cutting police numbers by 2,000 and letting thousands of prisoners out of jail early.

The Tories justify this claim by looking at the ONS report and picking out all the worst bits (a technique not unfamiliar in the world of journalism). Their briefing highlights four figures in particular. Here is an extract.

Sexual offences have reached the highest level on record under Labour, with the police now logging around 600 of them every single day. Police in England and Wales recorded 218,994 sexual offences in the year to June 2026, the highest annual total since the series began in 2002-03 …

Police are recording more rapes of women than at any point since records began, at a rate of around 142 every day. There were 51,731 recorded rapes of women aged 16 or over in the year to June 2026, the highest figure since the series began in 2004-05 …

More people are being caught carrying knives and blades than at any point since records began. Police recorded 30,216 offences of possessing an article with a blade or point in the year to June 2026, the highest figure since the series began in 2008-09 …

Thefts and robberies from shops have reached a record high under Labour, with more than 1,500 recorded every single day. Police recorded 519,516 shoplifting offences and 31,089 robberies of business property in the year to June 2026, a combined 550,605 and the highest total since the series began in 2002-03.

But these claims are highly selective – to the point of being misleading.

On sexual offences, and rape, the ONS report points out that much of the increase over time is due to more offences being recorded, and not necessarily more of them taking place. It says “there has been a general increase in police recorded sexual offences over the last decade, largely because of improvements in police recording practices”. And it includes a specific warning about drawing the wrong conclusions from the figures.

Police recorded sexual offences are not a good indicator of trends over time. Improvements in police recording practices, the introduction of new offences, and increased reporting by victims have contributed to the level of offences in recent years.

On knives, while more people may be carrying them, the Tories have ignored the figures suggesting fewer criminals are actually using them. The ONS says:

Knife-enabled crime recorded by the police decreased by 6% in year ending (YE) June 2026 (to 48,615 offences), compared with YE June 2025 (51,567 offences). Levels remained 12% lower than YE March 2020 (55,170 offences).

And, on shoplifting, the Tories are again presenting the data in a misleading way. The ONS says:

Following a period of increases in shoplifting, there has been a 2% decrease (to 519,516 offences) in YE June 2026 when compared with the previous year (530,336 offences).

The Tories have justified their “record high” claim by combining the shoplifing figures with the figures for robberies from business properties. They describe this as “shop crime” – even though many business properties are not shops. And the ONS has a caveat about these figures too.

The number of robbery of personal property offences fell by 7% (to 57,193 offences) compared with the previous year, however, overall robbery increased by 10% (to 88,282 offences) mainly because of improvements to recording practices for robbery of business property.

And the ONS says, with shoplifting, more offences are also now being recorded than in the past.

Recent increases in police recorded shoplifting likely reflect both growth in offences and improved reporting resulting from closer engagement between police and retailers. Reporting rates for shoplifting are known to be very low and police are actively encouraging the reporting of all incidents and the submission of supporting evidence, such as CCTV, to strengthen intelligence, identify offenders, and link related offences.

The gender pay gap for full-time employees has continued to decline in the UK. It was 6.8% in April 2026, down from 7.2% in April 2025. This compares with 9.4% a decade ago and 16.4% 25 years ago.

Survey shows 'significant increase' in proportion of adults in England and Wales experiencing domestic abuse, ONS says

There has been a “significant increase” in the proportion of adults in England and Wales who have experienced domestic abuse, the Press Association report. In its report on today’s crime figures (see 9.54am), PA says:

Some 9.6% of people aged 16 and over are estimated to have been the victim of domestic abuse in the year to March 2026, or around 4.7 million people.

This is up from 7.8%, or around 3.8 million people, in the year ending March 2025.

The rise indicates “real change” in the prevalence of domestic abuse and is a “significant increase”, according to the Office for National Statistics (ONS) which carried out the survey.

The jump has been driven by a rise in partner abuse, experienced by 7.9% of people aged 16 and over in the latest figures, compared with 6.1% in the previous year.

Some “minor changes” to the way the survey is carried out are likely to have influenced the size of the increase, the ONS said.

But their best assessment is there has been a genuine rise in the level of domestic abuse.

While there has been a jump in the prevalence of partner abuse, figures for family abuse are broadly similar, at 2.8% of adults in the year to March 2026, compared with 2.7% in the previous 12 months.

Meghan Elkin, ONS head of crime statistics, said: “While minor survey changes and the usual uncertainty around gathering data on this sensitive topic are likely to have affected these estimates, our assessment is that the rise in domestic abuse includes real change.

“More data points are needed to further understand the uncertainty and what this tells us about trends in domestic abuse.”

Healey accepts Labour has put a ‘lot of pressure’ on business and says helping entrepreneurs will be a budget priority

Good morning. A week today John Healey, the chancellor, will be doing a media round defending the budget he is presenting on Wednesday next week. Ahead of that, he has given an interview to Patrick Maguire from the Times. As you would expect, there is no real detail in it about the measures he will be presenting to MPs. But the big picture message is very clear – and quite surprising, coming from a chancellor serving a PM who in some respects is steering left compared to his predecessor. Healey says that he wants to be on the side of bloody-minded entrepreneurs who make a profit, he acknowledges that government has made their life harder over the past two years, and he suggests that cutting the ‘cost of living’ for business is just as important as cutting the real cost of living for families.

In the quote that gives the Times its splash headline, Healey says:

I want to be a chancellor who backs business in Britain making a profit, and those entrepreneurs who, in the end, are the ones who challenge the status quo, come up with innovative ideas and are trying, some of them through sheer bloody-mindedness, to keep going through these sorts of pressures …

If you’re not making a profit, you’ve got nothing to put back into the business. And for the people who start up these businesses, the ones who really thrive and grow, they have to make a profit — and they will be much more inclined to put it back into their own business. But we want it to grow here and stay here.

And this is what he says about the government has recently been putting “a lot of pressure” on business.

If you talk to any of the businesses that have come in and have either had one-to-ones or round tables with me, I have recognised that in recent times there’s been a lot of pressure: employment law, regulation, tax, input costs, supply chains.

And so when I talk about the cost of business, and recognising that in the same way as we’re recognising the cost of living pressures, I’m conscious of that as I go into the budget for both businesses and households.

All chancellors – including all Labour chancellors – say they want to help businesses make a profit. But many people in the business community suspect that, when they face fiscal pressure, Labour government will usually default to treating business as a cash machine.

Healey’s comments about wanting to be seen as “a chancellor who backs business” and about how tax rises and the Employment Rights Act have increased costs sound like an implicit rebuke to Rachel Reeves, his predecessor. In his interview Healey defends her decision to increase employer national insurance, saying she had to deal with the “dereliction of duty” of the last Tory government on the economy. But Healey still gives the impression of wanting to be seen as markedly more pro-business than Reeves.

In his interview Healey does not give details of how he will cut costs for business next week. But, in his write-up, Maguire says:

[Healey] is expected to use the budget to make significant cuts to regulation on businesses that one senior Treasury source described as “the sludge choking firms”. Reporting requirements are likely to be abolished for many smaller enterprises, The Times understands.
