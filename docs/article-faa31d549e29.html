49ers' Kyle Juszczyk has strong message for women as he honors late mother-in-law with special cleats

2026-11-24 19:36

Week 12 in the NFL is officially upon us, and for the players, it’s a fun start to a league initiative many rally behind because they get to showcase their passions and community efforts through their everyday uniforms.

The "My Cause, My Cleats" campaign allows players to not only beef up their style on the gridiron with customized cleats, but more importantly, shed light on charitable causes close to their hearts.

For San Francisco 49ers fullback Kyle Juszczyk, his cause for the last few seasons has been "near and dear" to his heart because of how it’s impacted his family.

Juszczyk’s wife, Kristin, unfortunately saw her mother pass away from breast cancer when she was 18 years old. He was never able to meet his mother-in-law, but stories of her long battle with cancer, where she showed resilience and strength through remission and recurrences throughout his wife’s childhood, all while trying to keep the struggles and pain hidden from her family, has deeply impacted the NFL veteran.

As such, Juszczyk has supported causes aimed at helping through with breast and ovarian cancers, with an emphasis on self exams, early detection and more to make all women aware of how to prevent it if they can.

This year, Juszczyk teamed up with Novartis, an official partner of the NFL, to support the Lynne Cohen Foundation – an organization started by Lynne Cohen’s three daughters to combat ovarian and breast cancer through preventive care, education and community-building programs like their annual "Kickin’ Cancer" 5K and 10K races.

"Really excited and really proud to be partnering with Novartis for ‘My Cause, My Cleats,’ and we’ve chosen to highlight the Lynne Cohen Foundation," Juszczyk told OutKick in a recent interview. "This is just a near and dear topic to me, and one that lays heavy on my heart because my wife’s mother passed away from breast cancer, and my aunt is a breast cancer survivor. So, I know what a touchy subject it is, but I’m excited I have a platform that I can highlight some of the things we can do to get screenings, to get early detection, to encourage the women in our lives to be proactive and get ahead of this thing."

Juszczyk explained how his wife, a fashion designer whose Off Season brand is operated by Fanatics and licensed by the NFL, always has a hand in helping with the cleats.

But when he has them laced up on gameday, and looks down to see his mother-in-law’s name on them just before his usual routine of meeting Kristin on the field prior to kickoff, the emotions run high.

"She always helps me with this, and it always is an emotional one because each year I’ve done it, I’ve put Gini, her [mom’s] name, on my cleats," he said. "Before every game, there’s always these emotions and you’re just feeling everything times 10. In those moments before a game, and I look and I see Gini’s name and I go see my wife and give her a kiss before we start the game, man, what a wave of emotions that is.

"I’m so proud to be able to wear her mom’s name on my feet because I owe so much to Gini and the beautiful, beautiful person and daughter she brought into this world. I’m so thankful for her because she’s given me the best partner in life. It’s a near and dear topic for me."

In many ways, the stories of both Gini and Lynne are similar in that they showed tremendous courage during their cancer battles, while remaining the warm matriarchs of their family even through those hard times.

Juszczyk’s hope is that all women understand they are never alone in those battles, and hopefully they never have to go through it through preventive action.

Only one in two eligible women receive their annual screenings that can catch breast cancer early, when it’s most treatable. And when caught early, breast cancer has a greater than 99% survival rate.

Breast cancer affects one in eight women in their lifetime and is increasingly common in women under 50.

"I think that just highlights how strong and incredible women are," Juszczyk said of Gini and Lynne. "I think our role as men in this whole thing is just to encourage them to go to their doctors, to get these early screenings because they are so important to us. They’re not in this thing alone, and like you said, my mother-in-law and Lynne Cohen herself, so many times it seems these women are handling this burden by themselves. There’s only so much we can do as men, but there is something and every little bit helps."
