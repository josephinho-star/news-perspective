Trump celebrates Thanksgiving with his holiday tradition of hate-posting against his enemies — including far left, sharia backers

2026-11-26 20:37

President Trump carved up his critics on Thanksgiving, dishing out a side of fury with his “Happy Thanksgiving” wishes.

While Americans sat down to turkey and football, the 80-year-old president was busy roasting the “fake” economic numbers that got revised upward after the midterms, a New York Times story that got under his skin, the “Radical Far Left,” and the Somali-American community.

“Happy Thanksgiving to all American patriots who have worked so hard to make our country great!” Trump posted on Truth Social Thursday morning in all caps — before pivoting to his usual passive-aggressive holiday special.

“I would like to wish a Happy Thanksgiving to even those who issued fake Economic Numbers, just prior to the Election, and corrected them yesterday, three weeks late,” Trump wrote. “I would like to wish a very Happy Thanksgiving to all of those people that have worked so hard to bring our Country to the Radical Far Left, those that are looking to change our Court System to that of Sharia.”

He kept going: “[E]ven to those who refuse to work. I want to thank the 2% of our Somalian population that have a job, and are actually willing to work, without receiving Government Assistance.”

It was classic Trump Thanksgiving fare: heavy on the grievance.

Trump also used his Thanksgiving diatribe to credit himself for deporting criminal illegal immigrants and renovating the White House.

The piece by David Sanger noted how the stock market continued sliding after its brief bump following Trump’s intervention to help get Huang back as Nvidia CEO.

“David E. Sanger, the mediocre reporter for the Failing New York Times, didn’t mention in his recent story on me the successful closing of the Border, best crime stats in years (lowest murder rate since 1900 – 126 years!), biggest Tax and Regulation Cuts, EVER,” Trump grumbled shortly after midnight.

“David Sanger is a lowlife that only writes what he is told to write by his bosses.”

The New York Times defended Sanger in response, with spokesperson Nicole Taylor contending that “[n]oisy name-calling is just an attempt to distract from the news.”

Another grievance Trump aired out on Thanksgiving was the Bureau of Economic Analysis’ recent revision of its gross domestic product (GDP) growth figure for the third quarter, which it adjusted up from 3.4% to 4.3% at an annualized rate.

That adjustment means that the third quarter growth rate was the highest in about three years.

“It was just revealed that the REAL Numbers on the Economy, published before the Election, were FAKE, and a correction was issued yesterday by the people in the Government Agency that does these numbers,” Trump whined on Truth Social.

“Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation,” he added. “This is not a small error, this is a “Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the Election?”

“This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error.”, and purposeful, error.”

The president announced an investigation into the matter.

Meanwhile, in sharp contrast to her husband’s angry holiday spiel, Melania Trump directed her Thanksgiving message to American troops.

“May god bless our nation and protect our troops,” she wrote on social media. “Happy Thanksgiving to all!”

Her post included a video of her visiting soldiers, and the voice-over contained remarks from her speech last week, where she paid special tribute to military families.

“While your loved ones are deployed it’s up to you to keep your families strong, keep your children safe and keep your homes full of love,” she said.

The president and first lady were to call service members from Mar-a-Lago on Thursday evening before having their Thanksgiving dinner.

Earlier, Trump called his longtime friend, supermarket king John Catsimatidis, to wish him a Happy Thanksgiving and found himself on Catsimatidis’ radio show.

“Am I on the radio?” Trump asked.

“You’re on the radio,” Catsimatidis confirmed.

“Well, you didn’t tell me that, but that’s okay. I called just to wish you a happy Thanksgiving because you’ve been my friend for a long time, a long, long time,” Trump said.

The two — along with Catsimatidis’ co-hosts — chatted for about five minutes, going on a riff about congestion pricing and how it was resulting in more bicyclists.

“Bicycles are out of control,” Trump said. “They’ve taken over, and it’s a horrible thing. And they’re very dangerous. They hit people all the time.”

New York City had four deaths by traditional bikes last year and 62 by motorized two-wheeled vehicles, such as e-bikes and scooters.

Trump closed the call with some Thanksgiving-related advice for Catsimatidis.

“But I just want to wish you guys a good Thanksgiving. John, you got to — don’t eat so much. I want to make you nice and thin.”
