Fernando Mendoza, Ashton Jeanty give Raiders a reason to believe in loss to Broncos

2026-11-23 00:45

The Las Vegas Raiders aren’t ready to sit at the grown-ups’ table just yet, but they’re getting closer to earning a seat.

After falling behind 17-0 in the first half Sunday at Empower Field at Mile High, the Raiders stormed back to tie the Denver Broncos, only to watch their AFC West rivals pull away for a 38-24 victory, their 17th consecutive win when the outcome is within one score.

For rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza and first-year head coach Klint Kubiak, it was another painful lesson in the difference between competing with a playoff contender and actually beating one.

Here are three takeaways from the Raiders’ loss.

Fernando Mendoza is growing up before our eyes

The reigning Heisman Trophy winner and national champion is starting to look more comfortable running an NFL offense, and that’s a terrific sign for a franchise desperate to solidify its future at quarterback.

Making just his second career start, Mendoza showed considerable improvement from his debut against the defending Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks last Sunday.

His biggest moment came in the second quarter when he connected with Ashton Jeanty for a 68-yard touchdown, his first career NFL passing touchdown. The score breathed life into a Raiders offense that had been practically nonexistent through the opening 20 minutes.

Mendoza followed that with a touchdown pass to tight end Ian Thomas in the third quarter, erasing Denver’s 17-point lead.

His final numbers — 18 completions on 37 attempts, 287 yards and three touchdowns and one interception — only tell part of the story.

The rookie didn’t panic when the game threatened to become a blowout. He kept attacking, made plays and gave Las Vegas a fighting chance. With just over two minutes remaining, Mendoza had an opportunity to tie the game, but he threw a pick-six that sealed the victory for the Broncos, proving Mendoza still has a lot of learning to do.

Feed the beast: Ashton Jeanty is becoming a nightmare for defenses

If Kubiak needs a blueprint for building his offense around Mendoza, he should start by putting the football in Jeanty’s hands.

The second-year running back was an absolute menace Sunday, piling up 153 yards from scrimmage in the first half alone, including 75 rushing and 78 receiving.

According to the Raiders, Jeanty became the first Las Vegas player since Jordy Nelson in 2018 to produce at least 150 scrimmage yards in a half.

His 46-yard run helped wake up a lifeless Raiders offense in the second quarter, and his 68-yard touchdown reception from Mendoza turned what looked like a Denver rout into a legitimate football game.

Jeanty’s final numbers: 79 rushing yards, 90 receiving yards and one touchdown.

The Raiders have a young quarterback finding his footing and a running back capable of turning a routine play into a house call.

The Raiders are improving, but they’re not a playoff team yet

Let’s give the Raiders some credit. They’ve had about as brutal an introduction to the Mendoza era as anyone could imagine.

His first two NFL starts came against the defending Super Bowl champion Seahawks, owners of the league’s top-ranked defense, and an 8-2 Broncos team that was painfully close to reaching last season’s Super Bowl before falling short to the New England Patriots in a snowstorm.

The Raiders didn’t fold against either opponent, but Sunday’s fourth quarter exposed the distance they still have to travel.

With the score tied 17-17 late in the third, Denver reclaimed the lead. Then, after Las Vegas failed to convert a fourth-and-2 near midfield, Broncos running back Jonah Coleman ripped through the Raiders’ defensive front like a passenger strolling through TSA PreCheck to go up two scores and put the game out of reach.

The Raiders have their quarterback in Mendoza, a dynamic running back in Jeanty and an elite young tight end in Brock Bowers.

That’s a foundation worth building around.

But until they can protect leads, execute in critical situations and finish games against quality opponents, they’re still rebuilding rather than contending.
