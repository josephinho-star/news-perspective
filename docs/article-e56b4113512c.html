Container ship sinks in Strait of Hormuz as Iran steps up attacks

2026-10-14 19:22

A container ship sank in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday as Iran escalated its attacks on commercial vessels.

The GFS Pride, registered in Singapore, sank after it was struck by an unidentified projectile and caught fire. The ship's 21 crew members were safely evacuated and no injuries were reported, according to the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore.

"Such attacks are unprovoked, unjustifiable and a breach of international law," the Singapore maritime authority said in a statement Wednesday.

Containers were floating in the water after the vessel sank, Pakistani maritime authorities warned in a navigational alert. It is the third commercial ship sunk during the Iran war, according to the International Maritime Organization, a United Nations' agency.

A second Singapore-registered container ship, the GFS Giselle, sustained damage to its hull after it was hit by an unidentified projectile on Wednesday. The ship has arrived at safe anchorage and none of its crew were hurt, the Singapore maritime authority said.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed early Wednesday that a container ship exploded and sank after hitting a naval mine, according to the state news agency Tasnim. But there is no independent evidence to confirm that a mine is what caused the Pride to sink, according to the maritime security firm Marisks.

Iran's claims are "consistent with an ongoing psychological operations campaign aimed at generating fear, increasing uncertainty and discouraging commercial shipping" in Hormuz, Marisks said in a security alert.

Iran has escalated its strikes on commercial ships transiting Hormuz in recent weeks, as it tries to choke off a rebound in crude oil exports through the strait.

More than 20 vessels, mostly oil tankers, have come under attack this month in Hormuz, the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman, according to incident reports from the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations Centre.

The attacks this month have left two sailors missing and more than a dozen injured, according to the IMO.

Crude oil exports through Hormuz hit prewar levels in September as the U.S. military guided tankers through the strait, but shipments have since pulled back as attacks have increased. Oil prices closed slightly higher Wednesday.

Exports through Hormuz averaged 8.5 million barrels per day (bpd) in the week ended Oct. 12, nearly 40% below normal levels before the Iran war, according to data from Kpler.

Shipments from the Middle East, including pipelines to the Gulf of Oman and Red Sea, stood at 14 million bpd, or nearly 20% below prewar levels, the data showed.

Daily crude flows are very volatile, with only 3 million barrels of crude transiting Hormuz on Monday while 6 million barrels passed through the strait on Friday, according to Kpler.
