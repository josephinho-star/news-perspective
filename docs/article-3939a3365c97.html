Al Gore buys hometown Tennessee newspaper, vows to preserve local outlet's 'independent voice'

2026-11-25 02:30

Former Vice President Al Gore is buying his hometown newspaper in Carthage, Tennessee.

The Carthage Courier announced Monday that the family of Hershel and Geraldine Lake will be handing the paper's ownership to Gore, and that an agreement and ownership transfer will be finalized in the coming weeks.

Carthage is roughly 60 miles east of Nashville.

"I’m so grateful to the Lake family for their decades of leadership in supporting independent news here in Smith County," Gore said in a statement. "Having spent my early years as a military reporter in Vietnam and later at the Tennessean, I am a firm believer that a free, independent local press is irreplaceable for the enduring health of our community. When my hometown paper came up for sale, I saw an opportunity to help preserve the Courier as an independent voice for Carthage and Smith County."

"I look forward to supporting the Carthage Courier team, many of whom have served this community for decades, as they continue to provide vital information and serve this community for generations to come," he added.

The Lake family put the Courier up for sale this fall after 50 years of operating the 113-year-old paper. Geraldine Lake died on Nov. 1 at 84.

The Courier also announced that its longtime publisher, Scott Winfree, will transition into a part-time role, citing health issues, and that newspaper advertising director Roger Wells will step in as the Courier's new publisher going forward.

The paper assured readers that publication will continue during the ownership transition and that all current staff will be retained.

This isn't Gore's first foray into media. The former VP previously launched Current TV in 2005, which featured programs hosted by various media and political figures including Keith Olbermann, Joy Behar, Gavin Newsom, Jennifer Granholm and Eliot Spitzer.

The network was sold to Al Jazeera in 2013.

Before entering politics, Gore worked as a military journalist in the U.S. Army, serving during the Vietnam War, and later worked as an investigative reporter for The Tennessean.
