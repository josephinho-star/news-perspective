Teen attempts to light American flag on fire, shreds it with teeth during LA ICE protest

2026-11-16 21:23

Hundreds of Los Angeles students walked out of class Monday to protest ICE outside City Hall, cheering as a fellow student climbed a traffic light, attempted to set an American flag ablaze, tore it with his teeth and tossed it into the crowd before waving a Mexican flag.

The shocking spectacle unfolded as the students ditched class to protest President Trump’s immigration crackdown, flooding downtown Los Angeles with chants of “F— Trump” and “F— ICE.”

The student dangled high above the street as he burned a hole through the American flag, sank his teeth into the fabric and hurled it toward protesters below, according to video taken by the California Post.

He then hoisted a large Mexican flag above the intersection as students erupted in cheers.

The rowdy demonstration kicked off around 11 a.m. and continued into the afternoon, with students blasting slogans through megaphones and carrying signs saying “STAND WITH STUDENTS ICE OUT NOW!” and “STUDENTS DEMAND: STOP ICE TERROR.”

Pizza deliveries rolled in, barbecue was served, T-shirts were sold and snacks were handed out as students packed the streets outside City Hall.

Monday’s walkout was organized by South Central Community, which called on students from more than 100 schools across Los Angeles and the San Fernando Valley to abandon their classrooms and descend on City Hall.

Organizers had urged students to wear white shirts to symbolize peace, unity and solidarity, promoting the event as a peaceful protest against federal immigration enforcement.

But the flag-burning stunt marked a dramatic scene in the latest wave of student walkouts that have repeatedly emptied Southern California classrooms this year.

Just days later, approximately 4,500 Los Angeles Unified students from two dozen schools ditched class on Feb. 4, according to district figures.

Another coordinated walkout followed Feb. 6 in the San Fernando Valley, with organizers calling on students from more than 40 schools to participate.

The protests have also raised questions about students skipping school.

LAUSD has previously warned that walkouts are not school-sponsored events and students leaving without permission could be marked absent, although California law allows certain excused absences for civic and political activities.
