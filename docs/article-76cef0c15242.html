Iowa's Kirk Ferentz threatens to fire coaches if they mention style points with great old-school statement

2026-11-18 19:10

Kirk Ferentz cares about one thing and one thing only, and that is winning football games. Whether his Iowa Hawkeyes win by one point or by six touchdowns, he just wants to look up at the scoreboard after 60 minutes and see that his team scored one more point than the opposition.

The 71-year-old has accomplished that goal more often than not during his 28 seasons in charge of the Hawkeyes, posting a record of 217-130, but in today's day and age, where a committee selects which teams make the field in the College Football Playoff, the fashion in which you win does matter.

Ferentz, however, could not possibly care less about that fact, and made his opinion quite clear with a perfect, old-school statement about it.

"If that comes up back there (Iowa's offices) I'm not big on firing coaches, but that could be grounds for firing," Ferentz told reporters when asked if he's considering chasing style points in the final two games of the season.

"I mean, we're just trying to win the game. For 28 years, that's been our goal is to put our guys in the best position to win a game. I'm not being judgmental towards anybody else, but hopefully we're going to play smart football and just try to do what's best to win each and every game in front of us. If that's not good enough, then that's not good enough."

With Iowa sitting at No. 15 in the latest CFB Playoff Rankings, the Hawkeyes likely need some help from the teams ahead of them over the final two weeks of the season in order to sneak inside the Top 12 and make their first-ever appearance in the field.

The most glaring issue for Iowa to snag a spot is that its final two games come against Illinois and Nebraska, two teams currently sitting at 6-4, where a close win against them wouldn't necessarily influence the Playoff committee to bump up the Hawkeyes.

Ferentz may not want to hear about running up the score, but getting into the endzone an extra time or two against the Fighting Illini and Cornhuskers wouldn't be the worst strategy imaginable.

Iowa ranks 73rd in the country in scoring (24.8 points per game), but has blown out each of its last three opponents by a combined score of 72-17.

The Hawkeyes stunned Michigan on the road in September in dramatic fashion and also blanked Wisconsin 30-0 in October for their two most impressive wins of the year. Their 13-10 loss at Minnesota is keeping them outside of the Top 12 as the season quickly comes to a close.
