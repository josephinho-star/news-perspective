Russian cargo ship bursts into flames after suspected Ukrainian drone strike in Baltic Sea

2026-11-21 17:38

A sanctioned Russian cargo ship operated by the Russian Defense Ministry was on fire Saturday in the Baltic Sea after being hit by two suspected Ukrainian drones.

“Sparta 2” — which has previously been used to transport weapons — was bound for the Russian port of Kaliningrad when it was struck in international waters shortly before 9 a.m. local time, about three nautical miles off the Russian coast.

Explosions were heard on board before the ship burst into flames and drifted into Finnish waters, the country’s defense forces said.

“It is likely that Ukraine is behind these,” Finnish Defense Forces Communications Director Aki-Mauri Huhtinen said at a press conference Saturday. “Ukraine wants to find Russia’s economic and military targets…there is no precise information as to why this particular ship was struck.”

The burning ship was anchored as crew members evacuated onto life rafts and lifeboats.

There were no immediate reports of casualties, Finnish authorities said, according toYle.

Ukraine has not publicly claimed responsibility for the attack, and there was no immediate denial from Kyiv.

The vessel has been slapped with sanctions by the US and Ukraine for its role in the Kremlin’s shadowy military supply network — including ferrying weapons between Russia and Syria.

“At the moment, there is no confirmed information about what cargo is on board, but it cannot be ruled out that there is military materiel on board,” Finnish Border Guard Commander Ilja Iljin told Reuters.

“Ukraine is seeking to increase the costs of Russia’s war and force it to disperse its defenses. Russia, in turn, will have to strengthen its maritime protection.”

The suspected drone strike comes as Russia and Ukraine have stepped up attacks on each other’s bridges, targeting vital routes used to transport military supplies and sustain civilian life.

Three nights of Ukrainian bombardment beginning Monday severed a critical link between Russia and annexed Crimea, forcing Moscow to reroute military supplies through occupied southern Ukraine.

Opened by Russian President Vladimir Putin in 2018 as his prized project after he illegally annexed the peninsula, the Crimean Bridge has since become a key military supply route, carrying food, fuel and parts to Russian troops in southern Ukraine and bringing damaged equipment back for repairs.

“This is not about liberating Crimea,” Ivan Stupak, a Ukrainian military analyst and former Security Service of Ukraine officer, told TheMoscow Times. “The aim is to undermine Russia’s capabilities in the Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions.”

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky defended Ukraine’s strikes on the Crimean Bridge, accusing Moscow of repeatedly targeting Ukrainian bridges before Kyiv retaliated.

“Before our response on the Crimean Bridge, Russia attacked Ukrainian bridges 109 times,” Zelensky saidon X. “Putin and his people are trying to find reasons to escalate.

“He said the Russians never used this bridge for military logistics, but we all saw it a million times.”

On Friday, Russia — which has been striking bridges in the Ukrainian capital since October — retaliated with ballistic missile strikes on Kyiv’s bridges, killing three people and cutting off traffic across the Dnipro River.

Meanwhile, Russian strikes killed at least two and wounded 83 civilians across Ukraine overnight, with missile and drone attacks battering Zelensky’s hometown of Kryvyi Rih.
