How Rangers are handling roster and lineup decisions as injuries mount

2026-11-28 08:31

CHICAGO — Just as one top-six skater took a step toward returning to the Rangers lineup, the team lost another who could be out for more than a month.

The Blueshirts team has had to adapt all season to injuries that have struck every part of the lineup at one point or another, but the latest slew of players to go down could make for a potential conundrum in regards to the construction of the top two lines.

Though the Rangers recently found out they will still be without Gabe Perreault (4-6 weeks) and Oliver Bjorkstrand (2-3 months) — two recurring top-six skaters — for the foreseeable future.

Though it’s sure to force head coach Mike Sullivan to shuffle his lines even more than he already has, Rangers president and general manager Chris Drury also has some newfound cap space from Igor Shesterkin’s official designation to season-ending long-term injured reserve that he could utilize via trade, the waiver wire or to sign one of the couple of free agents still available.

“We’re going to take the guys that we have healthy that are available to us,” Sullivan said before the Rangers dropped their third game in a row, a 2-1 loss to the Blackhawks. “We’re going to try to put line combinations, defense pairs, power-play units, penalty-kill tandems together that we think give us the best chance to win.”

Sullivan already put usual third-liner Will Cuylle on to the left wing of the top line next to Mika Zibanejad and Alexis Lafrenière, but the veteran head coach opted for Taylor Raddysh over Cuylle in that spot Friday night at United Center.

Zibanejad has been a constant on the first line while Lafrenière has skated all but three games next to the Swedish center. Despite playing most games with Perreault, the two have also logged three contests next to Dorofeyev.

Considering the fact that Raddysh started this season in the AHL, the team’s precarious situation is all the more clear.

“I feel like we just don’t have a lot of zone time that much,” Lafrenière told The Post after practice Thursday. “Off the rush, we’re still getting some chances, but zone time I feel like is not there that much. That’s what we got to work on. Trying to spend more time in their end and trying to make plays from there.”

The line had been growing offensively stale with Perreault, but they started to slip defensively too.

In the five games prior to Friday night’s loss, according to Natural Stat Trick, Zibanejad (22) and Lafrenière (22) were on the ice for the most high-danger chances against during 5-on-5 play on the team.

Asked what would be the ideal complementary skill set to Zibanejad and Lafrenière, Sullivan pointed to speed first and cited how much it helps those two get in on the forecheck, disrupt breakouts and create loose pucks and turnovers.

Their best attribute as a line is their ability to threaten off the rush, but Sullivan has called for the unit to improve their “down-low game.”

“That’s going to give you zone time,” Lafrenière said of the notion. “Moving around the zone, using the D’s, and then that’s when things open up and you can make your plays. So trying to hold on to pucks low and make some plays. I think that’s what we’ve been lacking a little bit lately.”
