Trump says US will ‘hit Iran hard’ as hostilities resume – as it happened

2026-08-31 17:17

The US president, Donald Trump, has told Fox News’s chief foreign correspondent, Trey Yingst, that the US will respond to Iran targeting US military bases in Jordan overnight. “We’re going to hit them hard,” Trump told Fox News. “There will be a response,” he added, after Jordan’s army confirmed it had intercepted eight missiles early this morning.

Trump was sending a message to Iran with his social media post about Kharg Island, vice-president JD Vance said on Monday. “The president likes to switch it up on social media a little bit. He would be the first to say he doesn’t make announcements about military actions on social media, but I think that he’s sending a message to the Iranians,” Vance said when asked about Trump’s post.

Trump’s one-line social media post on Sunday was accompanied by an AI-generated video clip showing fake attacks by the US military. A US official said on Monday the military had not targeted Kharg Island in overnight strikes. An Iranian official dismissed Trump’s post as “laughable”.

US treasury secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC in an interview on Monday that the goal of US sanctions on Iran is “to create the conditions that they will want to come to the table” for negotiations. As Bessent opened meetings of the Group of 20 finance ministers in Asheville on Monday, he said he wants to focus on ways to grow the global economy and rein in mounting debt.

Bessent also briefly touched on the new Iran sanctions earlier this morning, before his opening remarks in Asheville. Speaking to reporters, Bessent predicted that, under the latest round of sanctions, Iran’s economy could collapse within “weeks or months”. But he also suggested that a deal, not collapse, was the administration’s primary goal.

In a brief post to his Truth Social platform, Trump claimed Iran is a “failed nation” with no navy, air force or currency. “IT IS DEAD!” he wrote, saying the only things Tehran have in its favour are a sympathetic US media, a “willingness to kill their protesters” and a “good line of bullshit”. Trump said the Iranian leadership is in “total disarray” and is “incapable” of properly representing the country.

The UAE condemned an Iranian drone attack intercepted over its territorial waters, calling it a “dangerous escalation”. In a statement on X, the UAE’s foreign ministry “strongly condemned the hostile Iranian attack on the country involving a drone that was intercepted by air defence systems over UAE territorial waters”.

US Central Command (Centcom) has denied claims made by Iran state TV and the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps that a ship had struck mines in the strait of Hormuz. In a statement posted to social media, Centcom said “Iran’s claim that a supertanker passing through strait of Hormuz struck two mines and was brought to a complete stop is false.”

The EU issued a statement saying it will work with the US and other allied countries to maintain economic pressure against Iran. “The EU welcomes efforts at ensuring that Iran ceases its destabilising activities and engages in peace negotiations with good faith, also through additional economic pressure, including through the US-led Operation Economic Outcast,” it said in reference to the pressure strategy the White House describes as “an unprecedented campaign to sever every remaining economic lifeline sustaining” the Islamic republic.

Medics said two people were killed and several others injured when an Israeli airstrike hit a crowd of people near the municipal public park in Gaza City on Monday, an area densely populated with families displaced by Israel’s assault. Gaza’s health ministry says at least 1,320 people have been killed by Israeli attacks since a ‘ceasefire’ was signed between Israel and Hamas last October.

Israel signed a multibillion-dollar weapons deal with Greece on Monday, the largest between the two countries, AP reports. Israel’s defence ministry said the $3.5bn (€3bn / £2.6bn) export deal, dubbed Achilles Shield, is one of the country’s largest and includes a multilayered air defence system for Greece using Israeli technology.

For many Iranians, the new US sanctions campaign, called Operation Economic Outcast, seems superfluous given the grim economic situation in their country.

Vegetable oil in Iran in August cost 383% more than a year ago. The price of eggs rose by 294%, chicken by 177% and red meat by 148%.

As purchasing power in Iran collapses, households are buying fewer goods. Diets are changing, clothes are being patched up and repaired rather than replaced, and medicine is in short supply, though it can be found on the hidden market.

According to the Mehr news agency, demand for red meat fell by 50% in April 2026 compared with the same period last year. There has also been a leap in the consumption of potatoes, leading to shortages.

In the interview with Fox News on Monday, Trump said all of the missiles were intercepted by American air defence systems, except one which was allowed to pass through the airspace because it was identified as not on course to hit anything “significant”, according to Yingst.

US denies Iran's claim that oil tanker hit mines in strait of Hormuz

Reuters has a quick snap that US Central Command (Centcom) has denied claims made by Iran state TV and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps that a ship had struck mines in the strait of Hormuz.

In a statement posted to social media, Centcom said “Iran’s claim that a supertanker passing through strait of hormuz struck two mines and was brought to a complete stop is false.”

The denial followed reports in state media earlier today that Iran had said an oil tanker caught fire in the strait after it hit two naval mines.

“A rogue supertanker attempting to pass through the illegal route south of the strait of Hormuz caught fire in massive fires after striking two naval mines and was brought to a complete stop,” Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said in an earlier statement.

Israel signs $3.5bn defence export deal with Greece

Israel signed a multibillion-dollar weapons deal with Greece on Monday, the largest between the two countries, AP reports.

Israel’s defence ministry said the $3.5bn (€3bn / £2.6bn) export deal, dubbed Achilles Shield, is one of the country’s largest and includes a multilayered air defence system for Greece using Israeli technology.

The Greek defence ministry said in was part of “the greatest reform of its armed forces in its history”, as the Nato member looked to ramp up defence spending.

The deal was signed in Tel Aviv by Israeli defence ministry director general, Maj Gen Amir Baram, and Maj Gen Ioannis Bouras of Greece.

Iranian president says he seeks a diplomatic end to the war even though US has 'not fulfilled its commitments'

The Iranian president, Masoud Pezeshkian, is attending a meeting of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation in Kyrgyzstan where he will hold talks with numerous officials and world leaders.

Pezeshkian, seen as a relative moderate in Iran, has already met with Indian prime minister Narendra Modi on the sidelines of the summit and told him that Tehran still seeks a negotiated solution to end the war with the US, despite the flare up in hostilities over the last 24 hours.

According to the Tasnim news agency, the Iranian president told Modi that fighting was in no one’s interest and that dialogue and cooperation were the way to resolve existing problems.

But, according to Fars news agency, Pezeshkian said the US has “not fulfilled its commitments”, which he presumably means the US continuing a blockade of Iranian ports and holding on to frozen Iranian assets. He also said “there are currents within America that see their interests in the continuation of war”, without eleaborating.

There are sharp disagreements within Iran on how to proceed in the conflict, with the new hardline leadership reportedly favouring confrontation with the US, wanting to squeeze reparations out of Washington and a right to toll the strategic strait of Hormuz.

Death toll from Israeli attacks on Gaza reaches 73,456, says health ministry

Medics said two people were killed and several others injured when an Israeli airstrike hit a crowd of people near the municipal public park in Gaza City on Monday, an area densely populated with families displaced by Israel’s assault.

Gaza’s health ministry says at least 1,320 people have been killed by Israeli attacks since a ‘ceasefire’ was signed between Israel and Hamas last October.

Hamas accuses Israel of violating the ceasefire and undermining efforts to implement a broader plan by the US to end the war on Gaza.

Israeli prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, said earlier this month that there can be no progress on the stalled Gaza ceasefire deal or an Israeli withdrawal from the territory until Hamas first disarms.

In its latest update, Gaza’s health ministry said that 73,456 people, mostly women and children, have been killed in Israeli attacks across Gaza since October 2023, when Isreal launched its assault on the territory following the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, in which about 1,200 people were killed.

A number of legal and rights experts have concluded that Israel has committed genocide – considered the most serious international crime – against the Palestinian population of Gaza. Israel denies the charge, despite the widespread evidence that supports the conclusion.
