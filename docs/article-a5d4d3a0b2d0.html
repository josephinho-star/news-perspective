Trump, Xi, Putin issue 4-point ‘declaration toward peace’ after summit

2026-11-19 12:31

President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin issued a four-point “peace declaration” following their unprecedented summit in China on Thursday.

The world leaders addressed United Nations reform, oil production and supply, the International Criminal Court and nuclear conflict, according to text released by all three countries.

The declaration states that as permanent members of the U.N. Security Council, all three countries are “committed to promoting UN reform so that it may respond more effectively to global challenges” and intend to plan to “increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations.”

They also agreed “that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction” and reaffirmed that a nuclear war must never be fought

“The three countries will conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons,” it reads.

Trump met Putin and Xi in Shenzhen, China, early Thursday for a historic 90-minute trilateral meeting.

“We have a special responsibility to maintain open, respectful, and productive dialogue on issues impacting not just our people but the entire planet,” Trump said in opening remarks, before the meeting was closed to the press.

“Too often, past leaders avoided talking to nations with whom they had disagreements, and we feel the opposite,” he continued. “I think that we are sure about that. No problems were ever solved under the old school, refusing to talk about it. No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement and speech.”

The president indicated in the lead-up to the summit that he would like to discuss the Iran conflict, the Russia-Ukraine war and denuclearization.

Washington has backed Ukraine in its effort to prevent Russia from taking over the country, while China has helped support Russia. Meanwhile, Iran has also received some aid from Moscow and Beijing in its war with the U.S.

On nuclear nonproliferation, China has resisted checks on its buildup of nuclear weapons.

However, during Thursday’s bilateral meeting, Xi said, “Nuclear weapons should be comprehensively banned and thoroughly destroyed, building a nuclear-free world,” according to China’s Xinhua News Agency.
