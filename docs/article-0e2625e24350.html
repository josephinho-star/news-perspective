Victorian election 2026 live results: Jess Wilson vows to ‘bring back hope’ to Victoria after historic victory over Labor

2026-11-28 11:03

The Labor party are finally getting some more runs on the board with the latest round of seat calls. We’re projecting a Labor win in the following seats:

Ivanhoe - ALP Mill Park - ALP Thomastown - ALP Williamstown - ALP Greenvale - ALP

Wilson is coming to the denouement of her speech (she has been speaking for more than 15 minutes now). After numerous thank-yous, she comes back to the party’s drubbing of Labor:

Friends, I have said many times that we can’t fix Victoria overnight. It will take years of hard work and responsible governing to get Victoria back to the place it used to be. A place where people felt hopeful and optimistic, where they felt safe and content.

But I want to make a commitment tonight. Labor have failed many times over 12 years, but their chief failure was that they stopped trusting the Victorian people with the truth.

Victorians have placed their trust in me tonight and I will always return that trust with the truth. That’s my commitment. That’s the kind of premier I want to be. The kind of premier who is honest from day one.

Wilson: Liberal party’s ‘last rites were premature’

Jess Wilson, still addressing the party faithful, alludes to the fact that for a long time and until not very long ago, the Liberal Party’s prospects of winning back government in Victoria seemed out of the question.

A lot has been said about the future of the Liberal Party. Tonight, we showed that those last rites were premature.

We showed that when you put in the work, you listen to the Victorian people, and you put forward a plan that will deliver hope for this state, you can win, and we did win.

Wilson thanks ‘decent man’ Ben Carroll after outgoing premier called to concede

Jess Wilson says she received a call from outgoing premier Ben Carroll earlier this evening.

He graciously congratulated me and the Liberal and Nationals team. He has served Victoria at the highest levels – as a minister, as deputy premier and as premier. Taking on the leadership of the state in difficult circumstances. Ben is a decent man, and I wish his family well for what lies next.

But today, Victorians have drawn a line in the sand after 12 long years. Victorians have sent a clear message that they cannot allow the state to continue on the direction it has been going in under Labor. Victorians want a fresh start.

I’m incredibly proud of the positive plan we have put to the Victorian people at this election. And now we have the mandate that we need to get started.

Earlier this year I travelled the length and breadth of this great state. Everywhere I went people told me that they needed a fresh start. And as I stand here tonight, I am deeply grateful to have earned the trust of the Victorian people to deliver that fresh start.

I am very conscious of the great privilege pledge and the weight of responsibility that has been placed upon me and my entire team tonight. To those who placed their faith in the Liberal and National parties tonight, I will not let you down. We will not let you down.

To those who did not, rest assured, I will govern for all Victorians. I will govern for all Victorians and in the best interests of Victoria every day.

Jess Wilson claims historic victory for the Liberal party

Jess Wilson, Victoria’s first elected female premier and, at 36, one of the youngest premiers in Victorian and Australian history, is speaking to party faithful at Melbourne’s Park Hyatt, taking the stage to chants of “Jess we can”.

She entered the room to huge cheers and resounding applause, swamped by ecstatic supporters as she made her way to the stage, while the upbeat dance classic Sunchyme by the late British artist Dario G played loudly.

Thank you, my friends, and thank you, Victoria. I first want to address the people of Victoria. Thank you for the trust that you have placed in me and my team.

I know that for many of you this is the first time that you have ever voted Liberal. And for others it was the first time you have ever given us your preference. And I realise that you have lent us your vote. We don’t own it. And we will work every single day to demonstrate that you were right to place your trust in us.

I know that you have elected me to bring back hope to this state. And that is what I pledge to do, starting from tomorrow.

The Coalition’s primary vote is actually down compared with 2022, so how are they doing so well in winning seats?

The Liberal party’s primary vote is down by about 1 percentage point, but they’ve managed to secure enough seats to form government.

Meanwhile, Labor’s primary vote is down by 19 percentage points, and One Nation has gained 21 percentage points, albeit off a base of almost zero votes in 2022.

The Coalition has produced an incredibly efficient geographic distribution of votes, managing to stay just ahead of enough candidates to be the beneficiary of the preferential system.

Greens and Victorian Socialist voters prefer the Liberals over One Nation, and with Labor being knocked out quite early in the count, the Liberal party is mopping up preferences from the left of politics.

We think that there is a possibility that Bendigo West could end up as a competition between the Greens and One Nation, making this an interesting one to watch.

The reason is that after the Animal Justice party and Victorian Socialists are excluded in the fifth and sixth positions, that allows the Greens to leapfrog Labor. One Nation may benefit from Family First voter preferences, allowing them to get ahead of the Nationals.

However, it’s also possible that when early votes are counted, the Nationals stay ahead. All of this makes for a complicated race to call.

Pauline Hanson addresses One Nation election gathering

One Nation federal leader Pauline Hanson has been speaking in Pakenham, opening with: “Farewell Labor!”

Her aim, she said, in running candidates in all 88 seats in Victoria, was “to get rid of Labor and the toxic Greens out of this state.”

“And we did it,” Hanson says. (Note: the Greens are currently projected to have actually increased their representation in the Legislative Assembly from three seats to seven.)

They used to ignore us but they can’t any more. Because One Nation won’t be slowing down. I’m proud of what we put to Victorians this election. The major parties spent this campaign chasing focus groups. One Nation spent it saying what we believe.

The crowd gathered at the Greens event is chanting the name of the candidate for Northcote, Campbell Gome, as his victory there seems likely. (The Guardian is predicting a Greens win in Northcote.)

“Were not taking anything for granted,” Anasina Gray-Barberio, upper house candidate for northern metropolitan, tells the crowd at Docklands before leading the room in a Samoan chant of rubbing hands and exclamations of: “Greens! Opposition! Greens! Opposition!”

Past and present state and federal Liberal leaders at election party

The federal opposition leader, Angus Taylor, is at the Park Hyatt. His deputy, Jane Hume was spotted in the room earlier too, as was former federal treasurer Josh Frydenberg. The function room is feeling full, and the mood in here is feeling jubilant.

The former Liberal premier, Ted Baillieu, is also in the room. Baillieu held the state’s top job from 2010 until 2013, when he stepped down and was replaced by Denis Napthine. The Liberals lost the Victorian election the following year, beginning Labor’s 12 years in government.

Everyone’s waiting for Jess Wilson to arrive to give her victory speech.

Labor figures are already beginning to call for an end to the factional games that have plagued the party in recent years.

Every Labor MP in the caucus belongs to either a “right” or “left” faction and also a sub-faction. A subfaction of the left was key to right-aligned Ben Carroll beingsuccessful in toppling of Jacinta Allan for the leadership in July.

The party had attempted a clean-up of the factions following allegations of branch stacking in Adem Somyurek’s right-aligned moderate faction but Julijana Todorovic, who has been a convenor on Labor’s left, says the same patterns have repeated themselves:

We had a factional reckoning with Somyurek but the winners - including my own faction - then chose to behave the same way. This has to end.

It seemed inevitable that Ben Carroll would make reference to his first job at Kmart Airport West, given he joined the powerful SDA union while working at Kmart in high school and university. His press conference on his first full day as premier was inside a Melbourne Kmart.

As premier, I fought for working people. Today I thank working people and the Labor movement who represents them. I’ve been a proud trade unionist since I was 14, when I started work at Kmart Airport West. And I’ve been a member of the Labor party for over 30 years.

He finishes his speech with a quote from a concession speech from Ted Kennedy:

The work goes on. The cause endures. The hope still lives. And the dream shall never die.

Ben Carroll says the Labor party will need to do a lot of listening from here on if it is to win back the trust of the public.

There’ll be a lot of talking after this result. But right now Labor needs to listen. And we need to listen as a united team, not a divided one. We have a long journey ahead, back to government. The only way we’ll get there is together. People can reflect on where we went wrong. But let us always feel proud about what we did right.

He lists things that Labor has championed as its successes over the past 12 years in government: new hospitals, schools, nurses, free kinder, free Tafe, free public transport for children, the Metro tunnel, the West Gate Tunnel, myriad level crossing removals, and treaty.

Carroll: ‘We ran a strong campaign, but you can’t outrun time’

Despite the absolute thumping Labor has received tonight, Carroll’s speech is receiving serious applause and cheers.

Serving as the member for Niddrie, minister and premier has been the honour of my life. As premier, I never shied away from the hard decisions. And I always put Victoria first. From my first day in office, calling a royal commission into construction, establishing a special prosecutor. To our last day in the Victorian parliament, strengthening and passing laws for the anti-corruption commission. Integrity is not optional for me or for Labor.

I didn’t run away from the problems facing the Victorian people. I ran toward them. That’s the Labor way. Protecting our health system, more police, more nurses, acting with integrity and making every dollar count. All around us a city and a state transformed. With new schools, hospitals, transport and renewable energy.

We are part of the longest-serving Labor government in Victoria’s history. We ran a strong campaign, but you can’t outrun time. To the Victorians who stuck with Labor, we’ll spend the next four years working to repay your belief.

Ben Carroll tells party faithful that he’ll always love Victoria, “our great state, no matter who runs it”.

This is a significant loss for Labor. It’s not the result we wanted, but it is a result we accept. As leader of the party, I take full responsibility. Democracy is both beautiful and brutal. Beautiful, because we get to witness the will of the people. And brutal, because it is so difficult and painful to lose.

But Victorians have spoken. And their message is clear. They want change.
