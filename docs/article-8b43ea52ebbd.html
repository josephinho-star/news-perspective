Hundreds complain after Mariella Frostrup calls AJ Odudu ‘aggressive’ on I’m a Celebrity

2026-11-25 16:00

Almost 900 people have complained to the UK’s media regulator about an episode of ITV’s I’m A Celebrity … Get Me Out of Here! after the broadcaster Mariella Frostrup called her fellow campmate and presenter AJ Odudu “aggressive”.

Ofcom said 887 people had filed complaints in relation to “alleged bullying behaviour” by Frostrup, who has been accused by other black media figures of using a “microaggression” against Odudu.

The complaints relate to an exchange broadcast on Sunday which stemmed from an attempt by Odudu, who presents Big Brother, to help Frostrup cook dinner, shown on Saturday’s episode.

The issue of cooking came up again on Sunday, as campmates Wayne Rooney and Claire Sweeney attempted to improve relations between the two.

Frostrup, 64, accused Odudu, 38, of “making a drama out of a crisis” over the cooking arrangements, saying she was delighted to have some help. Odudu replied: “You have a funny way of showing it, that’s all.”

Frostrup responded: “You see, that’s just aggressive and that’s what keeps happening.”

Odudu said being called aggressive was “very harsh”. She later told campmates that describing her as “aggressive” was an “insane word” to use. Frostrup later said the pressure of being in the camp was “too much”.

An Ofcom spokesperson said: “We are assessing the complaints against our rules, but are yet to decide whether or not to investigate.”

ITV producers are reported to have intervened and spoken to Frostrup in the camp’s bush telegraph cabin. Frostrup later apologised to Odudu on Tuesday’s episode.

A host of TV personalities spoke out in defence of Odudu, including the Strictly Come Dancing judge Motsi Mabuse, the comedian Judi Love, and Will Best, who co-hosts Big Brother with Odudu.

Clara Amfo, the former BBC Radio 1 DJ, said: “Calling a black woman ‘aggressive’ when she is being anything else but is a classic microaggression.”

The singer Beverley Knight wrote online: “The word ‘aggressive’ feeds directly into the ‘angry Black woman’ trope. It IS hurtful and offensive.”

Others have urged viewers to consider the pressures of being on reality TV. The dancer Jordan Banjo, a former campmate, said while Frostrup had not handled the situation well, viewers should “give a bit of grace to Mariella, and everyone in the camp”.

He said: “[It is] a really weird situation that every single person’s in, and I’m not sure if people go at stuff with the clearest head. You get these weird breakdowns and I think that’s what we’re seeing.”

An ITV statement said: “The production monitors everyone in camp 24/7 and if at any time we feel someone has overstepped the line by using inappropriate language or displaying unacceptable behaviour, we would take steps to intervene and speak to everyone involved.

“The duty of care for our contestants is of the utmost importance to us. We take the welfare of the celebrities very seriously and have measures in place to ensure they are supported at every stage of the process: before, during and after filming.”
