Jaxson Dart takes brutal hit early in Giants vs Cowboys opener but what happened next was kind of surprising

2026-09-14 01:22

Jaxson Dart got absolutely rocked in the first quarter of Sunday night's regular-season opener between the New York Giants and Dallas Cowboys.

It happened along the Giants sideline. Indeed, it happened on the white (out of bounds) area along the Giants sideline.

And then some interesting things happened:

Dart went flying into some equipment along that sideline and immediately raised up to make sure everyone understood he was fine.

His teammates, obviously not appreciating that Cowboys linebacker DeMarvion Overshown knocked their starting quarterback into hard sideline stuff while he was clearly out of bounds, went after the linebacker.

Then Cowboys players rallied to defend Overshown.

And we had beef among the opposing players, NFL style. You know, guys pushing and shoving while wearing helmets and more padding than an ancient gladiator.

Officials stepped in a restored order by throwing a flag on Overshown and the Cowboys for unnecessary roughness. That cost the Cowboys 15 yards above the 22 yards Dart had already gained on his run.

Dart didn't get summoned into the blue medical tent.

He clearly took a significant hit that could have easily caused the Unaffiliated Neurotrauma Consultant to force Dart into the tent. But it didn't happen.

And, you may recall, that's something Dart lobbied for in a manner of speaking during the preseason.

Dart expressed frustration after being summoned to the Giants’ blue medical tent during an Aug. 9 preseason game against the Vikings, saying he respected the NFL’s safety rules but was "puzzled" he always seemed to wind up in the tent every time he was hit.

Dart said he believed the hit in that particular game had merely knocked the wind out of him. He was cleared to return after the evaluation. But the QB wasn't happy about having to leave the game.

His history, by the way, explains the heightened scrutiny.

During his 2025 rookie season, Dart was sent for concussion evaluations five times and was diagnosed with one concussion. That caused him to miss two games.

He also exited a November game against the Bears after suffering that concussion, although he returned later in the season and finished the year.

Sunday night's hit was no less aggressive and severe as any of the ones Dart endured last year.

But, again, no trip to the tent. So here's a conspiracy theory:

Maybe Dart helped himself by complaining in August.

No one is saying independent doctors would fail to do their jobs in protecting vulnerable players. But perhaps Dart earned himself the benefit of the doubt.

It looked that way in the first quarter of Sunday night's game.
