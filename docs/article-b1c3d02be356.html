Look out — Democrats’ ‘affordability’ fix adds up to more inflation

2026-11-23 00:48

Democrats plan to spike spending in the name of “affordability” — but that’s sure to explode inflation all over again.

The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget just put the tab for Dems’ “first 100 days” proposals at a staggering $4.1 trillion through 2037.

Republicans from President Donald Trump on down need to hold the line — to head off new price spikes and keep mushrooming debt from tanking the economy.

Democrats are open about their plans: Soon-to-be Speaker Hakeem Jeffries says they’ll move “to reverse Medicaid cuts, restore SNAP funding” and “extend the Affordable Care Act tax credits,” for starters.

Democrats insist the Medicaid and food-stamp reforms passed in Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act were heartless, yet they mainly sought to cut waste and fraud and to ensure able-bodied Americans collecting benefits make an effort to work or study.

And the Obamacare “tax credits” Dems mean to restore are the “temporary” COVID-era expansion of already-generous subsidies, a giveaway that led to huge numbers of phantom enrollees: Unscrupulous brokers signed up people without their knowledge for plans that (thanks to the credits) the feds paid for in full.

So insurers got the taxpayer cash but never had to pay any claims, while the “beneficiaries” got nothing.

The real problem is that Obamacare is collapsing under its own weight, but Dems prefer spending these hundreds of billions in added subsidies rather than admit it needs fixing.

Yes, voters are worried by high health-care costs, but Democrats’ plans don’t actually lower them, just borrow more cash to cover them.

In all, Dems’ agenda would add at least $4 trillion in spending, repeating their Biden-era splurge that sent prices soaring 21% from 2021 to ’25.

Without new revenue or offsetting spending cuts, the CRFB notes, the added outlays would drive up Uncle Sam’s red ink to an economy-wrecking 135% of gross domestic product.

Dems might defray the costs with tax hikes, including rolling back Trump’s 2017 rate cuts, but that’s sure to slow the economy — while still failing to tame debt growth anywhere close to enough.

America’s debt is already unhealthily high, and growing way too fast. Spending in the 2026 fiscal year alone added $2 trillion to America’s IOUs.

Prices, too, are already too high; voters made that clear in the midterm elections.

Yet Democrats’ plans mean bigger trouble for both the debt and inflation.

It’s the exact opposite of the affordability they promised the voters.
