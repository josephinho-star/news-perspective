Scientists just found a bizarre cosmic object ‘hiding in plain sight’

2026-09-25 18:00

An invisible stream of superfast plasma is heading straight for Earth, and scientists are thrilled.

The reason why is that these jets offer the first evidence of a microblazar. This is a tiny version of a blazar—an active galactic nucleus that contains a supermassive black hole that throws out high-speed jets of plasma at Earth. Scientists have long believed small versions of blazars exist in our own galaxy, but nobody had ever seen one—until now.

In a study, researchers describe how they discovered one “hiding in plain sight,” said Josep Martí, an astronomer at the University of Jaén in Spain and lead author on the new study, in a statement.

The system is around 12,000 light-years from Earth and consists of a star and black hole orbiting one another. Because they’re so close together, the black hole draws matter from the star, absorbing and shooting some of it toward Earth. The plasma is ejects is traveling at around three-quarters the speed of light.

Astronomers previously knew about this object, Martí said, but it was clouded by dust and never thoroughly analyzed.

“It is hidden behind so much dust that it is practically invisible in standard optical images,” he said. “It was catalogued decades ago and then was more or less forgotten.”

Now that they’ve found the microblazar, astronomers want to learn all they can, including how it evolved, how the jets interact with interstellar medium and whether more systems like this exist in our galaxy.

The study was released as a preprint on September 1 and has been accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics.
