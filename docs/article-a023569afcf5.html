Both blocs claim upper hand in crucial Israeli election | First Thing

2026-10-28 11:33

Good morning. Israel could be in for weeks of political haggling after exit polls and partial official results showed neither Benjamin Netanyahu’s coalition nor the opposition bloc led by the former general Gadi Eisenkot had won enough seats to form a government.

With 70% of the vote, or 4m ballots, counted so far, the Netanyahu bloc had 54 seats, the Eisenkot-led Change bloc 47 seats, and the Palestinian parties 19 seats. Sixty-one seats are needed for a majority.

What have the two leaders said so far? Israel’s longest-serving prime minister vowed to assemble a coalition through agreements between his Likud party, far-right factions and Jewish religious parties. Eisenkot had said earlier in the evening that “people decided in favour of change” and Netanyahu should have “announced today his retirement from politics”.

What does the increase in the Palestinian bloc mean? The election was marked by a record participation by Israeli citizens of Palestinian origin, a discriminated minority that makes up about 20% of the country’s population. Julian Borger explains from Nazareth that opposition leader Eisenkot will have to work with Arab-Israeli leaders to have a hope of governing. Millions of other Palestinians living under Israeli rule in the occupied West Bank and Gaza cannot vote.

Trump says government to ‘terminate’ Biden-era methane emissions rules

Donald Trump has announced his administration is “terminating” Biden-era regulations holding oil and gas companies accountable for large leaks of planet-warming methane. According to the Environmental Protection Agency, the proposal is expected to save $45bn in costs to consumers and businesses. Last year, the US emitted more methane from its oil and gas sector than any other country in the world. Methane is responsible for at least a quarter of today’s warming.

What else has Trump been saying about fossil fuels? Trump suggested on Tuesday that Venezuela would become the 51st US state as the president showed off a tanker he claimed had “1m barrels of oil on it” from the country, nine months after deposing its president.

Flávio Bolsonaro flees Brazil after presidential election loss, reports say

The far-right senator Flávio Bolsonaro has reportedly fled Brazil and is on his way to the United States after losing Sunday’s presidential election to the leftwing incumbent, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Bolsonaro allies told the news website Metrópoles that he would land in Florida and planned to request political asylum. “The Trump administration has been informed,” the website added. Brazilian federal police are investigating him for possible involvement in a money-laundering scheme worth about £680m ($905m) involving betting companies.

What has re-elected president Lula had to say about Bolsonaro’s exit? He condemned Bolsonaro’s apparent decision to flee. “This is what the family Bolsonaro are like. Brazil is their second homeland. Their first homeland is licking the boots of the US, as he’s become accustomed to doing. The act of leaving Brazil is shameful.”
