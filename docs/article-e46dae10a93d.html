Julianne Moore Screaming Obscenities at Paul Giamatti in Brilliant Scene From ‘The Debut’ Earns NYFF Applause Break

2026-10-01 19:30

Warning: This article contains mild spoilers for The Debut, coming to movie theaters on December 11.

It’s not often that New York Film Festival press audiences—overworked and underpaid film critics who have way too many movies to watch—burst into spontaneous applause, mid-movie. But, to my delight, that’s exactly what happened at the Thursday morning NYFF press and industry screening of The Debut, a lively, laugh-out-loud comedy coming to U.S. theaters in December. And it’s all thanks to a jaw-dropping, expletive-laden, Oscar-worthy outburst from Julianne Moore, in which she absolutely rips Paul Giamatti a new one.

Written and directed by Jesse Eisenberg—who is on a roll as a filmmaker after last year’s Oscar-nominated A Real Pain—The Debut takes place vaguely in the ’90s, some time before cell phones, and vaguely in the Midwest, in some small town near St. Louis. Moore stars as Mona, a meek, stay-at-home wife who is not respected by her husband, her kids, or even the shopper who brazenly cuts her in the grocery line.

Mona auditions for a regional production of Nosy Neighbors, a hysterically cliché fake musical about a New York City apartment building, with original songs written by Eisenberg (one particular-groan worthy lyric: “One bedroom, two hearts”) Though initially laughed off by the musical’s cutthroat director, Jerry (Giamatti), a last-minute drop-out means Mona lands the part of Miss Danielle, the building super. And Mona’s life is completely transformed.

Mona is not a good actor. She doesn’t project. She doesn’t emote. Worst of all, she doesn’t transform into her character. But with no one else available to play Miss Danielle, Jerry is determined to get Mona to where she needs to be. About an hour into the film, Jerry holds Mona back after rehearsal—mortifying her by announcing everyone in the cast can go home early, except for Mona—and instructs her convince him, Jerry, to pay his rent.

“Am I me, or am I Miss Danielle?” asks Mona, flustered.

“You’re Miss Danielle. You’re always Miss Danielle,” Jerry bites back, frustration in every word.

At first, Mona timidly asks for the rent. When Jerry asks why he should pay, she responds, simply, that the rent is due. Why, Jerry presses. Because she needs to pay the building owner. But why. Because it’s her job. Why. Because she needs money for her husband. Because she needs money for herself. Because she needs to eat.

And still, Jerry is not satisfied.

Why why why.

Because because because.

Their voices start to crescendo. They climb to a scream. And then it’s Oscar-nominee Paul Giamatti and Oscar-winner Julianne Moore going toe-to-toe. Full volume, spit flying. Two of our best living actors, just absolutely unleashed.

Mona screams that she cant go back to living the street. She screams that she’ll be living under a bridge, eating disease-riddled rats. She screams that Jerry is nothing more than a pathetic egomaniac, and she’ll fucking gut him if he doesn’t cough up that fucking check.

Why should Jerry pay his rent, he asks?

“Because the motherfucking rent is motherfucking due!” Moore screams at the top of her lungs.

Silently, Jerry hands over his rent check.

It’s here that the NYFF press audience burst into applause. Can you blame us? It’s the perfect climax—a perfect, cathartic line—to a brilliant build of a scene. It’s a moment of pure victory. Of triumph. Mona did it. She got Jerry to pay his motherfucking rent. What else is there to do but clap and cheer?

The Debut will open in U.S. theaters on December 11, 2026.
