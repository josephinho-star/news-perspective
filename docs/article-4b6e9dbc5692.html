‘The Voice’ winner’s house, belongings destroyed in ‘devastating’ fire: ‘Starting over’

2026-11-22 00:04

Alexia Jayy, the winner of Season 29 of “The Voice,” has suffered a catastrophic loss as her family’s possessions were destroyed in a tragic house fire.

The soul singer was blindsided when her Mobile, Alabama, home became engulfed in flames on Friday.

A link to a GoFundMe fundraiser has since been shared by Jayy, 33, in which she explains that she and her family — which includes her two children — were in the process of moving out of the house when the “devastating” fire occurred.

“I had been moving things out of the house and decided to take a quick nap. My son called me from my grandmother’s house because he wanted to show me what he had learned to cook, so I got up and went to see him,” she explained.

Jayy revealed that she and her family were notified that the house was on fire “less than six minutes” after she left.

“In such a short moment, so much changed,” she continued. “We lost many of our belongings, and while we are incredibly thankful that everyone survived and no one was physically injured, we are now facing the heartbreaking reality of starting over after losing so much.”

She shared that the fundraiser aims to help her and her family replace the “essentials” they lost in the fire, including clothing, shoes, furniture, appliances, electronics and “other everyday necessities [they] need to move forward.”

“This has been an unexpected hardship, and we are doing our best to rebuild one step at a time,” Jayy wrote. “We are beyond grateful that our family is alive and safe, and we are trusting God to carry us through the days ahead.”

Photos from the scene shared by TMZ show the inside of the house completely destroyed, with its contents reduced to rubble while the structure of the house itself remains standing.

Jayy competed on Adam Levine’s team during Season 29 of “The Voice” and ultimately won the competition in April, scoring a $100,000 grand prize and a recording contract with Universal Music Group.

She became the first black woman to win the NBC singing competition series since its 2011 premiere.

Jayy released a song titled “Break Your Heart” earlier this month.

She described the track in an Instagram post as “me basically saying don’t push me too far. I’ve been through enough to know better now, and I’m not making the same mistakes twice. Please don’t make me break your heart … because I will.”
