Five people hospitalised after forklift fire in Brisbane

Five people have been hospitalised after a forklift fire at a commercial site in Brisbane.

Emergency services responded to reports of a fire just before 11am this morning on Anton Road in Hemmant.

Seven trucks were deployed to the scene where fire fighters battled a blaze that had spread to neighbouring containers in the immediate area. The fire was extinguished at 2.15pm.

An official with Queensland’s fire service told reporters earlier:

An industrial forklift contains a lot of lubricants … and oils.

The containers that were on fire were refrigerated shipping containers so they are insulated with insulation foam. That would have attributed to a lot of the smoke we saw.

Queensland Ambulance Service said two women were taken to hospital in life-threatening conditions and three men were transported for smoke inhalation.

Victoria’s premier says public official found to have engaged in ‘serious and concerning conduct’ no longer serving in his role

The Victorian premier, Ben Carroll, has confirmed that a public servant who was found last month to have engaged in “serious and concerning conduct” after a corruption investigation has left his role.

Carroll told reporters earlier today that Tony Bates was no longer secretary of the Department of Education and would be leaving the public service. A search for his replacement is under way.

Bates took a leave of absence after an investigation by the Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission (Ibac), released last month, found he was part of a group of government officials who engaged in “serious and concerning” conduct while negotiating firefighter pay deals with Peter Marshall – the secretary of the United Firefighters Union (UFU).

The inquiry found Bates was involved in the negotiations, which occurred without the knowledge of the emergency services minister.

I read the Ibac Operation Richmond back to front, and I made it very clear that integrity is non-negotiable and a deal essentially was done behind a minister’s back.

Bates is the third public servant head to depart since Carroll took over from Jacinta Allan in late July. The head of the Department of Premier and Cabinet, Jeremi Moule was replaced by Treasury secretary, Chris Barrett, days after Carroll became premier and last week transport head Jeroen Weimar announced he would also leave his position.

Ex-rail boss in Victoria jailed over corrupt contracts

The masterminds behind a major corruption scheme over cleaning contracts for a state rail network have been jailed over their “corrosive” offending, AAP reports.

Former V/Line chief executive James Pinder was jailed for two years and eight months with a non-parole period of one year and nine months for misconduct of public office in the Victorian county court on Thursday.

Transclean co-director George Haritos was handed a term of two years and three months with a minimum of one year and five months for conspiracy to give a secret commission and two counts of giving secret commissions.

The company’s other co-director Alex Kyritsis and employees Steven Kyritsis and Maria Tsakopoulos were each given community corrections orders and ordered to do unpaid community service.

All five pleaded guilty to charges brought by the Victorian corruption watchdog following its Operation Esperance investigation into serious corrupt conduct in the state’s V/Line and Metro’s tendering and procurement processes.

The offending occurred between April 2018 to August 2020, when Pinder, as V/Line boss, participated in awarding a large-scale cleaning contract to Transclean worth more than $54m.

Queensland ambulance bug in triple-zero system resolved

A computer bug which forced the Queensland ambulance service to use a backup dispatch system to answer triple-zero calls has been resolved.

The service switched on a new computer aided dispatch system on Wednesday. The Guardian understands users had latency issues – computer lag – on Thursday, forcing the ambulance service to use a planned manual backup.

That meant some calls were completed over the radio with information entered manually, rather than using a computer aided dispatch system.

The problem has been resolved and the new computer aided system turned back on.

A spokesperson for the Queensland ambulance service said “all triple-zero calls are being answered and resources are being dispatched in a timely manner, with the most urgent cases prioritised”.

The Guardian understands that the service believes no high-priority patient was delayed treatment, though some less urgent cases may have had delays. The ambulance service has a service staffed by paramedics, doctors, nurses and allied health professionals which keeps in touch with low-triage patients waiting for an ambulance.

“As is best practice, business continuity processes were enacted during the transition to the new CAD system to maintain operational oversight and call-taking and dispatch services,” the spokesperson said.

Victoria records state’s first H5 bird flu cases in mammals

Victoria has recorded its first cases of deadly H5 bird flu in mammals, including a confirmed case in red fox at Torquay, and a suspected positive case in a dolphin at Lorne, the state’s acting chief veterinary officer, Dr Cameron Bell, said.

Since 20 June, the state has confirmed 212 positive cases from 892 investigations.

The chief biodiversity officer, James Todd, said heading into the spring breeding season surveillance efforts would be focused on high-risk areas, including the Mornington Peninsula, Port Phillip Bay, Western Port Bay and the Gippsland lakes, places that were important breeding grounds for waterbirds and marine mammals and near poultry facilities. He said further mortality events and spread into other species was expected. He said:

We’re at the beginning of a virus, which will continue to impact on Victoria’s wildlife.

We know that people care deeply about wildlife, and the impacts that the community may be observing, in terms of dying and dead wildlife, is really confronting and distressing.

A renowned forensic scientist on Nepal’s ‘extreme challenges’

The bodies of some of those killed in the Nepal-Tibet flood disaster may never be found, and some of those that are may never be identified, a world-renowned forensic scientist says.

Prof Richard Bassed, the head of forensic medicine at Melbourne’s Monash University, spent three months in Thailand after the 2004 Boxing Day tsunami and also helped to identify victims of Victoria’s catastrophic Black Saturday bushfires in 2009.

Now he has watched as authorities in Nepal and Tibet struggle to find and identify the dead amid the agonies of the living after last week’s devastating flash flood. More than 1,250 people have been confirmed dead, with more than 5,000 missing.
