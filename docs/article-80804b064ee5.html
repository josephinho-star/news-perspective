Turkey to join Saudi, Pakistani officials for Mecca defense pact talks in Riyadh

2026-10-04 20:36

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Defense Minister Yaşar Güler and Chief of General Staff Gen. Selçuk Bayraktaroğlu will travel to Riyadh on Monday for talks with Saudi and Pakistani officials under a three-way defense pact signed in August, according to Turkey’s Foreign Ministry.

The meeting of the pact’s Strategic Political and Defense Committee will focus on regional developments and efforts to turn the agreement into a functioning security framework, the ministry said, according to the state-run Anadolu news agency.

The Mecca Joint Defense Agreement was signed on August 7 by President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. It says an armed attack on one member will be regarded as an attack on all three and calls for closer defense cooperation and collective deterrence.

Fidan is expected to present Turkey’s views on the alliance’s next steps and discuss threats to peace and stability in the Middle East, Anadolu reported.

The talks come as the regional security environment grows more tense. Saudi Arabia has faced renewed missile and drone attacks by Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthis, while Pakistan has publicly pledged military support for the kingdom under the agreement.

Pakistani Defense Minister Khawaja Muhammad Asif said last week that Islamabad would make “whatever means are available” available to defend Saudi Arabia.

Turkey has so far taken a more cautious position. Fidan said last month that Ankara was ready to meet Saudi military needs, particularly in technical areas, but did not commit Turkey to direct military action.

Monday’s gathering will be the second meeting of the Strategic Political and Defense Committee. The first was held in İstanbul on August 31, when the three countries agreed to establish a secretariat in Saudi Arabia, initially led by a Pakistani secretary general, and to deepen cooperation in defense production, joint technology development and military coordination.

Foreign ministers from the three countries also met in New York on September 25 to review technical work on the pact and prepare for the next committee meeting.

The Riyadh talks are expected to take place against the backdrop of renewed fighting in Yemen. The head of Yemen’s Saudi-backed government announced Sunday the start of a major military operation against the Houthis, adding urgency to discussions over how the pact’s members would respond to threats facing Saudi Arabia.

The alliance is still being developed. Fidan said after the first committee meeting that it was designed to admit additional countries and described it as the beginning of a new regional security framework
