Train collision in Spain kills at least 30 people

2026-10-28 09:26

A high-speed train has crashed into the rear of another train that had stopped in central Spain, derailing two carriages and killing at least 30 people, the regional government said.

The crash occurred early on Wednesday near Urda, in Toledo province, south of Madrid, on a high-speed railway line connecting the capital with the southern city of Seville.

Esther Padilla, a spokeswoman for the regional government of Castilla-La Mancha, told Spanish public broadcaster TVE that the trains had been travelling in the same direction when one struck the rear of the other, which was stationary, causing two carriages to derail.

She said there were injuries, without providing a toll.

The collision involved an AVE high-speed train operated by Spain’s state railway company Renfe, travelling from Madrid to Seville, and another operated by private rail company Iryo.

The cause of the collision was not immediately clear.

Footage circulating on social media showed the wreckage from one of the carriages scattered along a slope after it derailed, with members of the public and rescue crews gathered around it.

Padilla said 397 passengers were on the Renfe train and 147 on the Iryo train.

Two carriages of the Iryo train fell down an embankment, according to emergency service sources cited by Spanish newspaper El Pais.

Spanish Health Minister Monica Garcia told TVE that emergency workers were focusing on rescuing survivors and bodies from the two most damaged train cars. She said that the injured are being sent to different hospitals in the area.

“It was a high-speed impact, so we are expecting this to be a tragedy,” García said.

Madrid’s mayor, Jose Luis Martínez-Almeida, said on X that the capital was also sending emergency medical teams and firefighters to assist with rescue efforts.

Spain’s railway infrastructure operator, ADIF, suspended high-speed rail services between Madrid and the southern region of Andalusia, while the Civil Guard sent a specialist disaster investigation team to the site, El Pais reported.

Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said on X on Wednesday that he was shocked by the incident and remained in contact with Transport Minister Oscar Puente and Castilla-La Mancha’s regional president, Emiliano Garcia-Page, as emergency crews worked at the scene.

“Shocked by the serious train accident in Toledo. My affection, solidarity, and support to all the victims and their families,” Sanchez said.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said she was devastated by the news and extended condolences to the victims’ families and loved ones.

“Today, Europe mourns with you,” she said in a post on X.

French President Emmanuel Macron expressed solidarity with Spain, saying his thoughts were with the victims, their families, the injured and rescue workers.

The disaster comes nine months after a train derailed and struck an oncoming service in southern Spain, killing 45 people.

The crash near the town of Adamuz was Spain’s deadliest railway accident since 2013, when 80 people were killed.
