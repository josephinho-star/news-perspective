Trump defends actions in Iran and Venezuela in UN General Assembly address amid rising tensions with allies – live

2026-09-22 17:53

Trump threatens to ‘annihilate’ Iran and ‘drive them into Hell’ if there isn’t a deal, while also underscoring US policy on Cuba, Mexico, Greenland and AI

Trump opened his speech to the UN by commemorating the nation’s 250th anniversary and focusing not on international issues, but touting his domestic record. Tax cuts, stock market highs, crime rates, and immigration enforcement, to name a few.

He once againframed the war he launched on Iran earlier this year with Israel the same way he has to his domestic audience — as a necessary conflict to addressing a long-ignored threat. The United States, Trump said, had acted to prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, while other countries ignored the issue.

Trump then re-issued his threat to “annihilate” Iran and “drive them into Hell” if the regime didn’t capitulate and make a deal.” He touted his version of the war: one in which the US had defanged Iran militarily and driven the country to the brink of economic collapse. And hedged by saying he was confident Iran would make a deal after the US midterm elections – an argument he’s made to US voters.

Trump said he was “working closely with the leaders of Russia and Ukraine” to end the disastrous war. “It’s going to happen, I think, more quickly than people understand,” he said. The US president said he had signed into law “a powerful new law” on sanctions, and “if necessary, I will have to use them.” He added: “It’s time to stop the killing.”

Trump defended his administration’s actions in Latin America – including the midnight raid to arrest former Venezuelan president Nicólas Maduro, and the oil deal he struck with his more US-friendly successor, Delcy Rodriguez. “To the victor belong the spoils,” Trump declared, justifying the deal that will funnel a share of the nation’s immense oil wealth into the US treasury.

Defending his drone strikes on boats in the Caribbean and the Pacific, Trump equated the cartels he claims to be fighting with Islamist terrorist organizations. “The cartels are the ISIS of the western Hemisphere – not good people,” he said. “Like ISIS, they should be killed, exiled or detained as enemy combatants without the possibility of release, which is what we’re doing.”

He accused Mexico of being effectively controlled by the cartels, and said it was “unacceptable” for the US to share a border with a nation under such conditions. “So Mexico must reclaim control of its soil from the enemies of humanity,” Trump said.

Trump also pointed to regime change in Cuba. “My administration is also seeking a fundamental change in the situation in Cuba, where the communist regime is under great pressure, the biggest pressure they’ve ever been under,” he said. “It’s an absolutely failed state. It’s failing.”

Trump said the US has reached “an unprecedented agreement” on Greenland, with the Kingdom of Denmark. He said the deal – to be formally signed shortly after his speech – “gives the US permanent control over security and all of the needs on that territory” and that the US will build two “very major” military bases in Greenland.

Trump said he would reject any calls for a “globalist scheme” to control artificial intelligence, and made an impromptu pitch to world leaders to call the technology “super intelligence” or “SI”. Amid mounting fears over the dangers of AI as some, including leaders in the industry, call for international regulation on the technology, Trump said he would not hinder its growth. He said he believes will be “bigger than the industrial revolution,” and vowed the US would lead on AI. “We’re only going to encourage superintelligence.”

Trump endorses a US embargo on diesel exports in conversation with Zelenskyy

President Trump appeared to support the increasingly loud calls from some members of his party to halt the export of US diesel fuel, whose skyrocketing price has begun to ripple out through the American economy and stung Republicans ahead of the upcoming midterm elections.

“I’ve called for that too, I’ve said, ‘let’s not send out the diesel’,” Trump said, when asked about the outcry from his fellow Republicans.

He then turned to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, sitting at his side, who said the Trump administration is evaluating the feasibility of a full or partial plan. Trump declined to share a timeline for when such a ban could come down.

The subject was raised after a reporter mentioned Ukraine’s attacks on Russian oil infrastructure, which Trump was quick to point out had damaged global diesel supplies. (The conflict between the US and Iran, which Trump launched in February alongside Israel, has also strangled global energy markets.)

“It is a serious hit on the Russians,” Trump said. “It’s also a very serious hit on the price of diesel.”

Trump, Zelenskyy speak to reporters at the United Nations

Trump is now fielding questions from reporters alongside Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the president of Ukraine.

“I think they’re going to make a deal,” Trump said, when asked what, if anything, Ukraine would have to give up to end the war. “I think’s somethings’ going to happen.”

The Ukrainians, Trump said, were “very capable people” with – and a nod here to the man sitting beside him – “very capable leadership.”

Vance, Oz announced removal of 760,000 Obamacare enrollees, alleging fraud

At a joint press conference on Tuesday, Vice President JD Vance and Mehmet Oz, administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, announced that the federal government was removing 760,000 enrollees from Affordable Care Act insurance marketplaces.

Those 760,000 marketplace enrollees – representing 4 percent of all Americans who obtain coverage there – were allegedly receiving benefits they weren’t qualified to get, or were not real people at all, per Vance. The vice president said the action will save $2.2 billion “for the essential services for which is meant to go.”

The removals, first reported by the Wall Street Journal, were carried out by Vance’s White House task force aimed at eliminating fraud. Critics have accused that group of primarily targeting blue states.

“This is what the fraud task force is all about,” he said.

Vance said that an additional 419,000 enrollees suspected of similar violations were undergoing further review. He accused the previous Biden administration of incentivizing healthcare brokers to enroll more people in the program while inadequately vetting for fraud.

Donald Trump to CNN at UN general assembly: 'You shouldn't be here'

Donald Trump told a CNN reporter at the UN general assembly on Tuesday that he was “surprised” the outlet was covering him, saying it ‘shouldn’t be here’ after he banned the network alongside Politico and MS Now from the White House on Friday.

“The United Nations allowed us in, sir,” a CNN reporter replied, when Trump began to protest the outlet’s presence.

“You should not be here covering me,” Trump shot back. “You said you weren’t gonna cover me, you shouldn’t be covering me.”

CNN, alongside MS Now and Politico, have sued the Trump administration for the White House ban, and other outlets in the president’s standard TV pool have paused their duties in solidarity.

Trump praises Delcy Rodríguez, Venezuela's interim president, before their planned meeting

In his remarks to the UN earlier, Donald Trump hailed his new relationship with Nicolás Maduro’s successors in Venezuela, after the capture of the South American dictator during the US raid on 3 January.

“In January, at my direction, the United States military conducted a massive military operation penetrating deep into the Venezuelan capital, Caracas .... and arrested the outlaw dictator - a real criminal, Nicolás Maduro,” Trump said, as Venezuela’s interim leader, Delcy Rodríguez, sat listening in the audience.

Rodríguez, formerly Maduro’s vice president, has been a more pliant partner to the US in Venezuela, helping broker a lucrative deal granting the Trump administration access to the country’s immense oil wealth.

“We’re working closely with the leaders of Venezuela to forge a much better future for the Venezuelan people. Last month we signed the largest oil deal in history - covering 65 billion barrels of oil which will benefit both Venezuela and the United States enormously,” Trump added.

Trump is slated to meet personally with Rodríguez later today.

Trump hails 'tremendous' relationship with Denmark, Greenland as he signs Greenland deal

At the Greenland deal signing ceremony, Trump said it “has been an amazing journey with tremendous people” with Danish prime minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenland’s PM Jens-Frederick Nielsen.

“We’re going to have a tremendous relationship, a secure relationship,” he said.

He thanked them for the deal, and says they will be working “very hard” on its implementation.

He said the deal has been “long in the making” – “many decades, probably centuries” – and it will be “tremendous for Europe and for the US and for everybody.”

Speaking next, Denmark’s Frederiksen said “This deal is good for the United States. It’s good for Greenland, for Denmark, for the Nato alliance, and therefore also good for Europe.”

Frederiksen said any adversaries “will not be allowed to get any control or significant influence in Greenland,” adding that the agreement “underlines the importance of the Nato alliance.”

In tricky, carefully worded paragraphs, she said the agreement “recognises the US defining historical and ongoing contributions to the security and defence of Greenland,” but it also “respects the sovereignty and the territorial integrity of the Kingdom of Denmark, as well as Greenland’s right to self-determination.”

“I think everybody knows that we live in difficult times where we have to show our strength. If we let ourselves be divided as allies, then we do our votes a favour. We will do everything we can to prevent that from happening.”

Then, Greenland’s Nielsen said it is “a very important day for our security” that “builds on 85 years of security cooperation” since the second world war.

He stressed that “this is not only an agreement about us, but an agreement with us” and called it “a win-win”.

“It takes into account the people of Greenland, our rights, our livelihoods and the development of our country. This has been a key priority for us throughout this process,” he said, saying this is why Greenland “insisted on engaging in a constructive dialogue.”

“Greenland matters for North American security and for transatlantic security. And this agreement reflects that we understand our responsibility and act accordingly,” he said.

“Nato matters. We believe that it is vital to keep Nato strong, also in the Arctic, where the alliance has a key role to play.

This agreement should put to rest any doubt today and in the future about where Greenland’s loyalty lies.

Our commitment to this alliance and to the security we build together is not in question. We are, have been, and will remain a steadfast friend of the United States and a part of the western alliance. Your security is our security, and our security is your security.”
