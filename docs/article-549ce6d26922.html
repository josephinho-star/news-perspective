Ex-Yankee Tyler Austin looking for stateside return — and both New York clubs are interested

2026-11-23 22:39

The Mets and Yankees are among a group of teams that have expressed interest in former Yankee Tyler Austin, The Post has learned.

Austin is almost certain to receive a minor league contract, and the Blue Jays, Cubs and Rays are the other teams that have expressed interest, a source said.

Austin, a 13th-round pick of the Yankees in 2010, debuted on Aug. 13, 2016, the same game as Aaron Judge. They hit seventh and eighth, respectively, and both homered in their first career at-bat. Austin was part of a July 2018 trade to the Twins for Lance Lynn and also played in the majors for the Giants and Brewers.

For six seasons (2020–2025), Austin played for the Yokohama DeNA BayStars in Japan, appearing in 403 games with 85 homers and a .945 OPS. Austin signed a one-year, $1.25 million contract with the Cubs last year but needed a debridement of his right knee in late February and never played in a 2026 game. Austin, 35, has been hitting and is healthy.

For the New York teams, Paul Goldschmidt impacts a potential signing of Austin to a deal that would include an invite to spring training. The Mets reached agreement with Goldschmidt on Monday as the right-handed part of a platoon in conjunction with lefty Jared Young. The Mets must open a 40-man roster spot for Goldschmidt and it is possible that would involve removing Mark Vientos from the roster since he no longer has an obvious place to get even part-time at-bats. Vientos has no minor league options.

So, the Mets can designate Vientos for assignment and try to trade him and failing that could seek outright waivers and see if he accepts a non-40-man minor league role. If Vientos leaves the organization, there would be a more obvious fit for Austin to go to Triple-A and be available should injury hit notably Goldschmidt or Jorge Polanco.

Goldschmidt had been with the Yankees the past two years, serving largely in 2025 as the right-hand-hitting side of a first-base platoon. The Yanks have not replaced that on their major league roster and might not be able to because of a logjam of two lefties — Luis García Jr. and Ben Rice — at first, and Giancarlo Stanton at DH (not to mention probably a desire to get Aaron Judge more DH time) leaves a less obvious place for a righty first base bat.

But Stanton is oft-injured, and García could become involved in trade talks because, in part, he is entering his walk year. Thus, the Yankees would want to stash a righty hitter at Triple-A who can potentially play first and/or DH. And there remain many people in the Yankees organization from when Austin was with the team.
