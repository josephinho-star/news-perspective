Zohran Mamdani unveils plan to close Rikers Island jail complex

2026-11-20 10:00

Zohran Mamdani, New York City’s mayor, on Friday unveiled a plan to close the Rikers Island jail complex, with a goal to end all incarceration at the current facility and relocate its remaining jail population by the end of 2032.

“It is time to finally close Rikers Island,” Mamdani said in a statement. “For decades, New Yorkers have demanded a correctional system that is safe and humane. This plan puts us on a path to deliver just that: closing Rikers, reducing pretrial detention, tackling the case delays that deprive people of their right to a speedy trial and investing $146m in the evidence-based supports that helps break the cycles of incarceration.”

Described by Mamdani’s administration as “the city’s first comprehensive plan to close Rikers Island”,it comes as the city faces a legally mandated deadline to close the facility by August 2027.

That deadline was established under legislation passed by the city council in 2019 and signed into law by then mayor Bill de Blasio. The legislation called for Rikers to be replaced by four borough-based smaller, modern jail facilities located in Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens and Manhattan.

However, the city is not on track to meet the August 2027 deadline, with construction behind schedule, and an increase in the city’s jail population since 2020, which has raised concerns about the city’s ability to reduce its population enough to accommodate the capacity of the four new facilities. The four facilities are expected to have about 4,500 beds, while roughly 6,600 people remain held in jails on Rikers Island as of early November.

Mamdani’s plan states that the Brooklyn facility is on track to open in 2029, followed by the Bronx and Queens facilities in 2031, and the Manhattan facility in 2032. It also states that his administration hopes to bring the 6,600 number down to no more than 4,100 people by the end of 2032, with a population of no more than 5,500 at the time of the Brooklyn Facility’s opening in 2029.

In a news release on Friday, the mayor’s office said the plan also includes the creation of hundreds of therapeutic beds “for New Yorkers with serious mental health conditions and complex medical needs”; and an investment of $146m from the city next fiscal year toward “mental health care, substance use treatment, educational and vocational programming both inside jails and in communities as well as victim services”.

As part of the proposal, the city will also work to reduce pretrial detention and case delays, modernize the department of correction, “and plan for the future reuse of the island”, per the release.

In the plan document, it states that Mamdani’s administration will “work closely with City Council on legislation to bring this plan into law, as well as the Remediation manager, federal monitor and the Board of Correction”.

The Mamdani administration cannot unilaterally decree a new legal closure date, it will require the city council to pass a new law extending the deadline, per Politico,

“We have a legal and moral obligation to close Rikers,” Mamdani said in a statement. “Working alongside the City Council, Courts and nonprofit partners, we will meet it – for the people in our custody, the staff who serve them, victims of crime, and every New Yorker.”

The Rikers Island complex has, for years, faced criticism over the conditions of the jails, the infrastructure, staffing challenges and reports of violence and deaths. The conditions have drawn sustained scrutiny from civil rights advocates, local lawmakers and more.

Last year, under former mayor Eric Adams, a federal judge moved Rikers Island out of the city’s direct control, and placed it under the authority of an outside official who was tasked with making the decisions affecting the jail facility. That official was appointed this January. In the plan on Friday, Mamdani’s administration said it would work with the federal monitor, as well as councillors and more, to “bring this plan into law”.

In a Friday statement, city council speaker Julie Menin said “delays under previous administrations have made it clear that more time is needed to fulfill the commitment the City Council made to New Yorkers in 2019 when we legislated closing Rikers Island”.

“With Rikers now under federal court-ordered oversight, we must bring this troubling chapter in our city’s history to an end once and for all” she said.

The proposal shared on Friday includes plans to restructure and modernize the department of correction, aimed at ensuring that “the operational challenges faced at Rikers Island are not transferred to the new facilities” per the report shared with press. It includes plans to “optimize jail staffing”, modernize data collection systems, improve processes for investigating, addressing and disciplining misconduct, and more.

The plan also proposes the administration invest $50m next fiscal year to “expand access to vocational training, educational programming, mental health counseling, substance use services and other supports”.

The news release states that the proposal includes plans for the city to restore its online bail payment portal in 2027, which it says has been unavailable since 2023. According to the news release, the city will also invest an additional $76m next fiscal year in “community-based services for New Yorkers” such as expanding access to “reentry services and community diversion programs, such as supportive housing”.

The plan also includes investment of $16m in support services for crime victims and survivors, to “expand access to mental health care and legal services”.

The lengthy proposal also discusses the planning “for the Future Reuse of Rikers Island” and said that the administration is “exploring near-term renewable energy and sustainability projects for Rikers Island”.

Since taking office, Mamdani has emphasized his administration’s commitment to closing the complex. He appointed a “Close Rikers Czar” and selected Stanley Richards to lead the city’s department of correction, making Richards the first formerly incarcerated person to head the department.

In June, Mamdani also announced the closure of North Infirmary Command, a facility at Rikers that first opened in 1932 and was described by the administration as a “vacant jail”. Officials said the facility was not housing people in custody, and it was transferred to the department of citywide administrative services, along with large portions of two other centers.

“This report is only the beginning,” the proposal unveiled on Friday states. “We are committed to demonstrating progress transparently and frequently, through quarterly updates.

“Together, these steps will result in the closure of Rikers Island, once and for all.”
