Australia news live: new regulations planned for smartglasses will include ‘right to erasure’, Rowland says

2026-08-31 02:44

Attorney general outlines planned privacy reforms including 'right to erasure'

The attorney general, Michelle Rowland, is announcing planned new privacy reforms at a Canberra press conference. She has told reporters:

We know that Australians are increasingly concerned about how their data is being collected, used, and shared, particularly as AI and other emerging technologies such as smart glasses become more accessible. People should not have to choose between protecting their privacy and participating in the digital economy.

She said the government was planning to introduce:

A “fair and reasonable” test to limit companies to collecting and using only the personal information they need;

A right to erasure, so people can ask digital platforms to destroy their stores of personal information;

Stronger standards for consent and clearer information when people agree to share personal details;

Limits on businesses buying and selling personal information without clear permission;

And a new ID Lock service through MyGov, designed to let Australians block, unblock, and monitor the use of their eligible identity documents for verification.

Government not planning to ban import of smartglasses, says Rowland

Michelle Rowland says the government is not immediately planning to ban imports of smartglasses.

The Greens and others have called for the camera-enabled wearable technology to locked out of the country.

The attorney general has told reporters the calls to limit the technology were “valid”. She said:

Firstly, I want to acknowledge the very deep concerns. We are not specifically in this consultation consulting on an import ban. … that would have consequences across government, including across trade, foreign affairs, and other potential legal obligations that can apply internationally.

She said the government’s reforms today would still impact on the smartglasses, with digital platforms prevented from storing recordings and data from the glasses if it was not “fair and reasonable” to do so.

Rowland said she commended local governments who were blocking the glasses’ use, for example at public pools, and said the federal government needed to use the powers that it had.

Let’s be clear, we’re talking about the harmful behaviours and regulating the harms. That’s what governments seek to do. In terms of an import ban, as I said, we are not ruling anything out, but we are utilising what we have at our disposal right now.

Victorian prisoners to be sent out to repair roads under Labor’s pothole plan

Victorian prisoners will be put to work on road maintenance as part of Labor’s plan to fix the state’s potholes if re-elected.

The state’s premier, Ben Carroll, will outline the plan on Monday off the back of an announcement on Sunday that the government would be increasing the road maintenance budget by $352m to fix 500,000 potholes by the end of January.

Carroll will declare that if Labor wins the November election, it would work with Corrections Victoria to develop a pilot program to put supervised, minimum-security prisoners to work performing roadside maintenance.

The plan would include having inmates take over “basic” duties such as mowing lawns, controlling weeds, removing graffiti and picking up rubbish, allowing more skilled crews to focus on fixing potholes.

The government said the work of prisoners “will not replace paid jobs” but instead “supplement the workforce where there are skill and capacity shortages”.

Home lending to owner-occupiers growing faster than investors

Owner-occupier home lending grew faster than property investor lending in July, for the first time since 2024.

Statistics from the Reserve Bank show investor credit grew by 0.462% in the month, its slowest growth rate since October 2024. It had risen by at least 0.7% every month in the year prior.

The significant slowdown appears to reflect the drop in new loan commitments to investors since the budget’s tax reforms.

Owner-occupier lending data shows no such slowdown. It rose by 0.463% in July, very slightly faster than the investor measure. It has grown by an average of 0.48% a month in 2026 so far.

Albanese heading to Palau for Pacific Islands Forum leaders’ meeting

Anthony Albanese will travel to Palau today and stay on the island nation until 3 September for the 55th Pacific Islands Forum leaders’ meeting.

NSW opposition leader says ‘let’s not wait’ to take action on violent pornography

The NSW opposition leader, Kellie Sloane, says she has written to Anthony Albanese after a 16-year-old girl died from a suspected sexual strangulation incident.

Sloane said the reports were shocking and had “rocked everyone to their core”, saying she had written to the prime minister about the need to tackle violent pornography.

I’m saying, you know, after the shock of the last week, that let’s act now, let’s not wait any longer.

The opposition leader said certain types of pornography should be placed into the most restricted category and pressure should be applied on porn companies and social media giants to “just make sure wherever we’re at, we can block it”.

It has the ability to program the young, vulnerable minds of a generation of people whose first sexual experiences are being distorted because of this. Kids at 16, this is shocking. And it’s becoming normalised.

Sloane went on to say education about consent was also a critical prong, even if it’s a “very tough” conversation between parents and teenagers.

It’s a hard conversation to have with your kids. But around consent, we need to model that behaviour as well.

Some Nepal flood victims had minutes of notice. Others had seconds

Guardian Australia’s Cait Kelly has a dispatch from the recovery effort in Kathmandu, Nepal. She writes:

On Sunday the combined toll in Nepal and Tibet climbed to 781 dead and more than 3,000 people missing. Forty-three of them are Australians. The humanitarian crisis on the ground is huge – but aid, a large amount of it from Australia, has started to arrive.

An additional $3m, on top of $5m already announced, was confirmed on Monday morning.

The army barracks have become the heartbeat of the crisis – bringing survivors in, taking supplies out and trying to place the dead with their families, so they can be laid properly to rest.

“With every helicopter there are mixed feelings,” says Plan International’s Unni Krishnan, who is on the ground in the Nuwkot district to oversee the response.

Australian boxing great Tony Mundine dies aged 75

Indigenous Australian boxing great Tony Mundine has died, aged 75.

AAP reports the father of former boxer and NRL star Anthony Mundine had a heart attack on Sunday.

Born in Baryulgil, near Grafton in northern New South Wales, Tony Mundine was the first Australian to fight in four different weight divisions, challenging for the world middleweight title against Argentina’s Carlos Monzón in 1974, in a bout stopped in the seventh round.

Mundine leaves a significant boxing legacy. Anthony was an IBO middleweight and WBA super-middleweight world champion, whose son Rahim won his fourth career fight from as many professional bouts on the Gold Coast only last week.
