Australia news live: NSW confirms new HSC assessment rules to crack down on AI use; Sydney man killed in home invasion

2026-08-31 23:45

NSW HSC students limited to one take-home assessment per subject

Year 12 students in New South Wales will be limited to just one take-home assessment worth no more than 15% of the school-based assessment mark for each HSC course, under new rules to combat the use of AI announced this morning.

School-based assessment is worth 50% of a student’s HSC result, meaning the sole take-home assessment would only be worth 7.5% of the total HSC mark under the changes. The plan comes after the state’s minister for education, Prue Car, asked the NSW Education Standards Authority (NESA) to look at measures to protect student learning.

Right now, schools can determine how much of that 50% school assessment is given through take-home tasks. Schools will still be able to use a range of assessment types for each course, including exams, oral tasks and process diaries, but the take-home component will be dramatically reduced in value.

HSC major works are not included in the restrictions, but schools will still be required to authenticate the work.

The limit will apply to students in the class of 2027 beginning HSC studies in term 4 this year, and to students beginning year 11 in term 1, 2027.

The message this sends to schools and communities more broadly is that we are committed to protecting the teaching and learning happening in schools.

When students outsource their learning – whether that is to tutors, classmates or artificial intelligence, they are missing out on the rigorous, high-quality education they need to set themselves up for the future.

China pledges ‘consequences’ over Taiwan’s presence at Pacific Islands Forum

China has promised “consequences” for Taiwan’s presence at the Pacific Islands Forum opening ceremony, which put the two rivals in the same room, AAP reports.

Palau’s National Gymnasium in Koror was the unlikely venue for the geopolitical flashpoint on Monday night. On the stage were traditional dancers, Pacific leaders and speeches pleading for unity and climate action.

Palau is one of just 12 nations that recognise Taiwan and Taipei has used the Micronesian nation’s hosting rights to engage in diplomatic efforts. That outreach extended to the foreign affairs minister, Lin Chia-lung,attending the opening ceremony on Monday night, prompting a stern reaction from Chinese officials.

China’s Pacific envoy, Qian Bo, said he had lodged diplomatic objections at Taiwan’s presence at the summit.

We certainly have serious concern. We have made our position very clear to the government of Palau and the (Pacific Islands Forum) secretariat.

Asked if there would be consequences, Qian said “there will always be consequences”.

It should not have happened. We think it’s very inappropriate.

The Nationals criticise Albanese government’s ‘go-slow’ visa approach

The Nationals have criticised the Albanese government’s decision to hit the brakes on visa processing, warning it could drive up grocery prices if farmers can’t get enough workers to harvest crops.

In a press release this morning, the shadow agriculture minister, Darren Chester, said the quietly announced “go-slow” approach – which aims to address political pressure over higher immigration levels in recent years – was causing farmers “enormous uncertainty”.

Working holidaymakers are a critical part of the seasonal agricultural workforce, particularly during peak harvest periods, so the timing of Labor’s delays is critical. This is not just an inconvenience for farmers.

If growers cannot access the workers they need when crops are ready, it affects food supply, supermarket shelves and the prices Australian families pay at the checkout … we need practical migration settings that support agriculture, strengthen regional communities and help keep food on Australian tables.

The government is considering ways to bring down net overseas migration levels after it became a key political attacking point by One Nation and the Coalition. It’s understood a number of different levers are being considered, including tightening conditions for British backpackers exempt from regional working requirements and stopping those enrolled in higher education from making visa category.

AI will likely have ‘profound’ impact on jobs in Australia

Treasury officials believe the adoption of artificial intelligence will likely have a “profound” impact on Australian jobs.

Jim Chalmers requested a departmental briefing paper on what AI means for productivity, investment and employment, ahead of the release of the government’s latest intergenerational report later this month.

To what degree AI replaces or augments Australian jobs remains an open question, and the answer depends on how transformational you believe the general purpose technology (or GPT) will prove to be.

The Treasury briefing note says:

Because AI has the potential to automate a significant share of cognitive, non-routine tasks currently performed by high-skilled workers, and accelerate productivity and economic growth, labour market impacts could be materially different to past waves of technology.

In either case, the effects are likely to be profound.

Jobs and Skill Australia has found that most occupations in Australia have more potential to be augmented rather than simply replaced by AI automation.

Separate research from the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations found no evidence that AI was having a big impact on jobs – so far.

Early uptake of AI in Australia is promising but task automation is still at an early stage and greater reorganisation within firms is needed to capture AI’s productivity benefits.

NSW police hold press conference after man shot and killed inside Sydney home

NSW police are holding a press conference after a man was shot and killed during a home invasion in Sydney’s south-west early this morning.

Acting Supt Gretchin Atkins said it was still very early stages and she couldn’t say much about the incident, which is being investigated as a homicide. She said:

We believe at this point in time it is a targeted attack, that is all the information that we’ve been able to gather at this time … Everything is certainly subject to our inquiries.

Atkins said police believe there were multiple offenders but there remains “a lot we don’t know”.

The victim was only known to police for a traffic offence but no other activity. There were family home at the time of the shooting and they are cooperating with police.

Atkins urged anyone in the community with information or footage to contact police.

Victoria opposition says Labor has ‘twice promised and twice failed’ on transport upgrades

The former Labor premier Daniel Andrews first promised to electrify the Melton and Wyndham Vale lines, which still operate regional V/Line trains, in 2018. He made the same commitment at the 2022 election. In May, state and federal governments announced $152.7m for design and preparation works for the extension of the Metro rail network to Melton.

Wilson said Labor had “twice promised and twice failed to deliver” the services commuters in the growing western suburbs deserved. She said:

Labor says one thing before an election then does another afterwards. Our plan will deliver metro-quality train services to Melbourne’s west sooner – meaning more trains, more seats and greater capacity.

Our hybrid suburban trains will deliver metro-quality services now and are able to run seamlessly across both electrified and non-electrified train lines.

Victoria opposition touting more trains during peak hour in election pitch

Commuters in Melbourne’s west would be able to get more trains during peak hours under a proposal the Coalition opposition is taking to the November state election.

Under the plan to be announced today by the opposition leader, Jess Wilson, and the public transport spokesperson, Matthew Guy, a Liberals Nationals government would spend $1.7bn to buy 25 new hybrid trains for the Melton and Wyndham Vale lines.

The trains – which would run on the overhead electric suburban network and then switch to battery power on V/Line, non-electrified corridors – would run during peak hours and increase passenger capacity.

The opposition said the proposal would deliver electrified train services to the west quicker than the government’s plan, which requires waiting until the Sunshine station upgrades are complete.

Attorney general says any action on smartglasses needs to strike ‘balance’ without ‘stifling innovation’

Michelle Rowland, the attorney general, said the spread of smartglasses has become an important issue and concern to many Australians, but the government needs to fully understand the pros and cons to new technology before banning or limiting its use.

Rowland spoke to RN Breakfast this morning after Australia’s online safety regulator urged smartglasses companies to automatically blur people’s faces among other changes as the government faces pressure to ban the technology.

The attorney general told RN Breakfast this morning there were legitimate reasons to use the glasses, including for those with vision impairment, but there were legitimate concerns as well:

One of the key points here when we talk about addressing digital harms is that we identify the harms and we need to address those harms without stifling innovation … and we need to understand that Australians have always been early adopters. So it is about striking that balance.

I do want to stress that whilst I completely understand and there are very legitimate concerns that people have about nefarious use, the government doesn’t want to see that nefarious use. But we do want to make sure that we get this right.

AI will help boost productivity performance back up to average, Treasury says

Treasury says artificial intelligence will help drag Australia’s productivity performance back to its historical average over coming years, according to departmental briefing papers.

With the government set to release its latest intergenerational report this month, Jim Chalmers said “AI is shaping up to be the biggest economic transformation in our lifetime”.

This is a gamechanging technology which will impact every part of our economy and society, in every corner of our country. I’m confident we can maximise the economic upside and minimise the risks.

Australia’s productivity growth has stalled, with the nation no more productive now than it was at the beginning of the decade.

Amid a national and sometimes heated debate over the regulation of AI and its related technologies, such as datacentres, the Treasury briefing paper said “AI is the first substantive, credible global productivity growth accelerant in almost two decades”.

But Treasury officials advised that, for now, the adoption of AI across the economy was not expected to usher in a new era of turbocharged growth, even as they flagged the unusually wide range of forecasts.

Instead, the “baseline” forecast was that “AI will support the achievement of the long-term productivity assumption of 1.2%” – a forecast outlined in the lead up to this year’s budget.

Most capital city home prices fall below $1m in August

The typical capital city home price has slipped back below $1m as buyers fail to show up in a “buyers’ market”, property data firm Cotality has reported.

The median price in the capitals surpassed $1m for the first time back in October but has steadily fallen this year with demand suppressed by rising interest rates and weak confidence in the economy. By the end of August, it fell to $990,394, Cotality found.

The typical home price in Perth passed $1m in March, but slipped to $999,987 by the end of August.

Sydney and Brisbane are back to being the only capitals with median prices above $1m. Sydney’s is at $1.22m, $93,000 below its peak earlier this year, while Brisbane is at $1.08m, down $30,000 from its peak.

Cotality’s research director, Tim Lawless, said nearly every capital city suburb had now seen prices fall, yet far fewer homes were being sold compared with this time last year, meaning listings on the market were building up. He said:

Longer selling times, larger vendor discounting and persistently low auction clearance rates all point to a buyer’s market, yet buyers are lacking the confidence to transact at the moment.

Members of Melbourne’s Nepali and Tibetan communities gathered in the city’s Federation Square last night to remember the victims of the Nepal-Tibet flood.

More than 40 Australians are among thousands of people who remain missing after the natural disaster last week. The overall death toll is at least 810 people.

At the vigil, volunteers unfurled a sign on the ground – encircled by candlelight – that read “Vic united for Nepal”.

The event, organised by the Non-Resident Nepali Association’s Victorian branch, was attended by the Victorian premier, Ben Carroll, and Labor and opposition MPs.

Speaking to the crowd of about 400 people, Carroll said:

I know so many of your hearts are breaking right now. Look around you, all of Victoria stands behind you and every candle in Federation Square is lit up for you. You are not alone.

Carroll said the Victoria government had pledged to donate $100,000 to Australia’s immediate efforts in Nepal.

Aid on way to Nepal amid grave concerns for missing Australians, Wong says

An RAAF flight containing hygiene kits, shelter and solar lights will leave for Nepal this morning, as the foreign affairs minister and acting prime minister, Penny Wong, says grave concerns are held over the 43 missing Australians after the Nepal-Tibet flash flood.

More than 4,400 people are still missing on both sides of the border, with more than 930 people confirmed dead. Wong told the ABC’s 7.30 program last night that the federal government is urgently seeking to find the whereabouts of the 43 missing Australians.

She said the Australian government has provided $8m in aid so far and noted that Australian citizens had donated $2m to a public appeal – something that the federal government is matching.

More aid will leave today, she said:

We are deploying an RAAF aircraft to deliver these kits which are designed to support people in circumstances of natural disasters that destroy infrastructure and their shelter.

Whether it’s shelter kits, lights, hygiene kits, these are the things needed for those people who are facing the devastation of their homes and their communities.

On the 43 missing Australians, Wong said Australia has been engaging with the Nepali and Chinese governments but the recovery and search effort is “very difficult”.

Nine of the 43 missing were on the Chinese side of the border at the time of the floods, but no more information was available on the 43 missing, she said.

I first want to again express what I’ve been expressing to the families and friends of the 43 Australians who are not accounted for, that this is such a difficult time, it’s an incredibly distressing, traumatic time. I think all Australians are with you. And certainly we are with you. We are working to do whatever we can and to provide any information we obtain.
