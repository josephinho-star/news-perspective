DHS chief tells Americans to 'breathe' amid tech outages, insists homeland is 'secure'

2026-11-17 03:55

Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Secretary Markwayne Mullin had a direct message for concerned Americans after the U.S. experienced a slew of technology disruptions that halted flights, email services and online banking platforms across the country.

"Well, breathe. The homeland is secure. Our banking system is secure and when something like this happens, we’re able to react and react fast," he said Monday on "Hannity."

Mullin’s remarks came as customers Monday reported trouble accessing online services of major U.S. banking platforms, including Wells Fargo, Chase, U.S. Bank and Bank of America. United Airlines also faced a widespread system outage on Monday that briefly affected departing flights.

The latest disruptions follow a nationwide ground stop issued by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) on Friday, which caused widespread flight delays and cancellations, as well as a separate Microsoft outage last week that affected Outlook email services.

Mullin maintained that the incidents were not a result of cyberattacks and described the disruptions as "individually unrelated."

"I can tell you with 98% certainty we’re not seeing the cyberattack," the DHS secretary said.

"We’re not seeing the footprints going in. We’re not seeing the footprints going out," he continued. "And so, nothing points to it being connected."

Four banks reportedly faced outages on Monday, according to Downdetector.com.

JPMorgan Chase said the problems were not related to cybersecurity issues in a statement to FOX Business, adding that customers still have access to their accounts and funds and that the company was working to fix the issue.

Mullin suggested the broader technology disruptions highlight vulnerabilities in aging infrastructure as newer technologies place demands on legacy systems.

"We just have a lot of technology thrown at us and our world runs on technology now," Mullin told host Sean Hannity. "We used to have backup systems and the backup systems are overwhelmed by the new systems now."

Mullin also said a prominent technology expert briefed him earlier Monday and suggested to him that "super intelligence" could have contributed to the disruptions. The DHS secretary believed the theory "kind of makes sense."

"He said, because these programs aren’t designed to work as fast as SI is. And so when they put in a program that’s able to write codes, and it goes in and it self-fixes itself, as it starts self-fixing itself, it’s like doing a change order on a house. When you change one thing in a house, everything else starts changing. That’s a ripple effect," he explained.

"It causes these systems to be overrun that weren’t designed to run as fast as the SI technology is today," he added.

Mullin defended the Trump administration’s response to recent technology disruptions and pledged to deploy federal resources to help companies experiencing technology problems.

"We are very secure, and I feel very comfortable about where we’re at," he said.
