LA bar famed for oysters to close permanently after 2-year landlord battle

2026-11-19 01:41

Oy Bar — the buzzy, divey Studio City hotspot beloved for its elevated and inventive Jewish-inspired comfort food — is shutting its doors for good on Jan. 16, 2027, to make way for a 101-unit apartment complex.

The Moorpark Street staple, which opened as a liquor-licensed follow-up to the popular Highland Park sandwich shop Jeff’s Table, is being cleared out to make way for a new affordable housing project.

The closure comes after a nearly two-year battle by owners Jeff Strauss and Peter Jarjour to keep their business alive after the property changed hands in September 2024, according to the Los Angeles Times.

The redevelopment will displace jobs at the popular valley establishment, which employs 21 people. The building’s buyer, HVN Development, specializes in affordable housing, and the proposed apartment complex is reportedly in the final stages of approval.

Before Oy Bar opened in 2022, the space housed the Oyster House Restaurant and Saloon, a longtime institution that dated back to 1972.

Chef Strauss embraced the vintage charm of the space, and paired the dimly lit, wood-paneled interior with a creative menu that drew inspiration from LA’s melting pot of cultures.

Unconventional mashups like matzo ball ramen and pastrami quesadillas quickly became fan favorites, along with the signature Oy burger featuring house-made hoisin ketchup, Persian cucumbers, cilantro, Toma cheese, gem lettuce and Dijon mustard.

The strip mall that houses Oy Bar, built in 1955, has already lost several small businesses. The owners reportedly turned down a buyout offer and enlisted lawyers in an effort to stay in the building.

The forced closure is the latest gut punch to LA’s beleaguered hospitality industry, which has seen a staggering number of beloved neighborhood establishments throw in the towel this year. Nearby in Sherman Oaks, Augustine Wine Bar recently announced it was closing after nearly 12 years in business, citing mounting financial pressures.

For fans of Oy Bar, however, there is a glimmer of hope. Strauss is reportedly scouting potential new locations in the Valley and exploring pop-ups to keep the concept alive.
