White House pulls nomination of Heidi Overton to lead FDA

2026-11-18 01:08

The White House withdrew the nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) amid opposition from Senate Republicans, it confirmed to The Hill.

Overton was set to face a key vote Wednesday in the Senate health committee, where Republicans hold only a one-vote majority — meaning losing one member would be enough to sink the nomination if all Democrats oppose it.

GOP leaders could have attempted to take her nomination directly to the Senate floor without a committee vote, but such a move would have likely faced significant obstacles.

Overton serves as the deputy director of the White House’s Domestic Policy Council and has been closely involved in President Donald Trump’s health agenda.

Overton had an uphill battle since she was first tapped as the nominee to replace Marty Makary, who resigned in May. She faced questions from some Republicans as well as many prominent supporters of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr’s Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) movement.

Health committee chairman Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.) has expressed concern over Overton’s role in Trump and Kennedy’s changes to vaccine policy. Last summer, Overton stood beside Trump when he announced an executive order to reduce and separate the childhood vaccine schedule. She called the action “historic,” spurring criticism from Cassidy.

During her confirmation hearing in September, Overton wouldn’t directly answer whether Trump was wrong and declined to say if she would stand up to the president or Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

Overton was also questioned by Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-Alaska) about abortion-related matters including the abortion pill mifepristone.

Overton is openly anti-abortion. She described the 2022 Supreme Court ruling overturning Roe v. Wade as “a huge victory for life.” During her hearing, she wouldn’t say directly whether she believes mifepristone is safe.

Former Kennedy senior adviser Calley Means, a prominent figure in the MAHA movement, said this month that Overton wrote a policy relaxing standards for fruit-flavored vapes.

He also alleged Overton helped the Justice Department “construct a legal argument” for why the Supreme Court should take up a case that involved shielding major chemical company Bayer from lawsuits connected to its pesticide products.

FDA has been roiled by an exodus of top staff and is viewed by many in the healthcare industry as less trustworthy and more politicized than it has been in any previous administration.

Some health industry analysts said the agency instability would likely continue under Overton. But it was not immediately clear who the administration is picking to replace her.

Senate HELP Committee chairman Bill Cassidy (R-La.) in a post on X took issue with the White House’s characterization that Overton did not have the votes.

Cassidy said he was having “productive conversations” with committee members and believed Overton would have cleared his committee. The final hangup, he said, was anti-abortion senators seeking assurances from the administration about its commitment to “life issues.”

“My conversations with her convinced me she would follow the science and push back against decisions that put Americans’ health at risk,” Cassidy wrote. “The Administration withdrew Dr. Overton rather than make such commitments. Unfortunately, with the limited time left, confirming a permanent FDA Commissioner will fall to Senate Democrats.”
