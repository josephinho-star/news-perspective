Burnham says it would be ‘unthinkable’ for the UK to rejoin the EU without a referendum – as it happened

2026-11-23 18:31

Andy Burnham has criticised former prime ministers for being “stuck in the past” amid warnings over re-opening the Brexit debate, with informal talks now set to take place with Brussels about how the UK could forge even closer ties with the EU.He spoke out in an interview before he gave a statement to MPs, which has only just finished after two an a half hours, defending the deal with the EU announced last Friday. Burnham was confident, restating claims that the deal would be good for consumers (based on the endorsements the deal has had from many leading UK retailers), and he dismissed criticism from the Fresh Produce Consortium, which said consumers would have to pay more because EU regulations would add at least £300m a year to the cost of importing fresh produce from outside the EU. (See 2.12pm.) That was a minority view, Burnham claimed. He was also scathing about the Conservatives and Reform UK for defending a status quo that disadvantaged British food companies exporting to the EU. The Conservatives had gone from being red wall Tories to “red tape Tories”, he said.

Burnham has said it would be “unthinkable” for the UK to rejoin the EU without a referendum. (See 4.30pm.)

In the Commons Andy Burnham is still taking questions from MPs about his statement. In response to a question from Calum Miller (Lib Dem), Burnham said he was amazed the Conservatives were saying they would vote against the legislation implementing this legislation when they had not even read the bill.

The latest episode of the Guardian’s Politics Weekly UK podcast is out. Pippa Crerar has been speaking to the PM and she discussed with Kiran Stacey how Andy Burnham will get the public to accept an EU reset.

Greens' Ellie Chowns says Burnham should set out pathway for rejoining EU

Ellie Chowns, the parliamentary leader of the Greens, told Burnham she welcomed the “shift in tone from the Labour benches” in recognising that “Brexit was disastrous”. She went on:

So can I ask him where will he set out the pathway to the truly brightest future for Britain, ie. to rejoin the EU?

Burnham said he would set out a bold plan for Britain next month.

Richard Tice, the Reform UK deputy leader, said this was a terrible deal. He said the youth mobility scheme would make it harder for Neets to find jobs. And he asked what Burnham would say to Jonathan Hinder, the Labour MP who told Newsnight last week that Labour’s strategy was in danger of making red wall voters think the government was treating them like thick, northern racists.

Burnham said he was confused by Reform Uk’s position because Nigel Farage was on record as saying Brexit had failed. He suggested he could not understand why Reform UK was not in favour of making the situation better.

Burnham reprimands Labour MP who accuses him of breaking Makerfield byelection promise over Brexit

Graham Stringer (Lab) said Burnham fought extremism when he was mayor of Greater Manchester. He said Burnham, during the Makerfield byelection campaign, said “it would be wrong to reopen the debate on Europe because it would be divisive”. But Burnham did exactly that in Brussels, he said. He went on:

That leads to cynicism and a loss of trust in parliamentary democracy and where there is a loss of trust in parliamentary democracy, you get extremists.

Burnham rejected the claim he had gone back on what he told voters in Makerfield.

It was around the 10-year anniversary of the referendum, and I was talking about rerunning the referendum. I am not proposing at this moment to rerun the referendum …

I am somebody who does take care about what I say and stick to it …

I inherited that situation [the plans for the deal with the EU], and it was entirely in keeping with the manifesto [Stringer] was elected on, I was elected on, to develop that deal because we promised the public of Makerfield and the country to deliver closer ties with Europe. That is what this deal does.

And I don’t think it helps anybody for him to stand up and say that somehow I’ve somehow either broken that trust because I haven’t.

Hilary Benn, who was Northern Ireland secretary when Keir Starmer was PM, said he thought “working more closely together with our European friends and neighbours is absolutely in our country’s national interest”.

Esther McVey (Con) said the government wanted to allow 50,000 young people from the EU to come to the UK under the youth mobility scheme, but ended up accepting 100,000. In a reference to this mini-row, she said Burnham’s time would be better spent “polishing up his negotiating skills rather than concentrating on the choreography of a dance routine of a TikTok influencer”.

Burnham said McVey’s question probably sounded better when she practised it in the mirror in her office.

Burnham said deal should offer 'some reassurance' to UK car sector on Made in Europe, but final deal on this not yet agreed

Layla Moran (Lib Dem) asked for an assurance that the proposed 10% tariff on electric vehicle (EV) exports to the UK would not be going ahead. And would the UK have to impose tariffs on Chinese EVs?

Burnham said he realised this was a vital issue for Moran’s constituents working at the Mini factory in Cowley. He went on:

We are doing all we can … And there is language in the agreement which I were pointed towards which should give her some reassurance.

But we have not reached a final conclusion yet.

Later Anneliese Dodds (Lab) also asked about EV tariffs.

Burnham said he should have said the Mini plant was in Dodds’ Oxford East constituency, not Moran’s Oxford West and Abingdon constituency.

To repeat, Made in Europe [the EU law that could penalise British EVs without the concessions the government is seeking] was not on the table as part of this negotiation. I put it on the table and we got it in the agreement. And that is good negotiation. And I hope it will turn into something meaningful for her constituents.
