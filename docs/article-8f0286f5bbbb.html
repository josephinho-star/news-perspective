Turkish vessels locate wreckage of northern Cyprus ferry as 20 remain missing

2026-09-02 11:35

Two Turkish naval vessels equipped with remotely operated underwater vehicles have located the wreckage of a passenger ferry that sank off northern Cyprus, as search teams continued efforts Wednesday to find 20 people still missing.

At least eight people died when the Filo Jet capsized and sank shortly after leaving Kyrenia, known in Turkish as Girne, on Sunday. All eight victims were Turkish nationals, according to officials in the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus.

Eighteen of those still missing are Turkish nationals, while the remaining two are Turkish Cypriots.

“The vessel has been reached,” Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhürman said Wednesday.

“Imaging and search operations are continuing around the clock and will continue without interruption,” he added.

Erhürman said the Turkish navy vessel TCG Alemdar had joined the operation Wednesday. It is equipped with technology similar to that aboard the TCG Işın, which had been searching the waters off Kyrenia since Tuesday.

Turkish Cypriot Prime Minister Ünal Üstel said the ferry was lying at a depth of approximately 550 meters (1,800 feet) and that all 20 missing people were believed to be inside the wreck.

“Search operations continue for the 20 missing individuals who we believe are on the ship,” Üstel told reporters at Kyrenia port, adding that he hoped more information would be available by Wednesday evening.

Remotely operated vehicles were surveying the wreckage and surrounding seabed, officials said. Divers cannot operate at such a depth, making the operation dependent on deep-sea robotic equipment.

The TCG Işın is equipped with an underwater vehicle capable of descending to 1,000 meters, transmitting images of the seabed and retrieving objects with robotic arms, according to The Associated Press.

Two more victims were reportedly buried Wednesday in the southern Turkish port city of Mersin as northern Cyprus observed a third day of national mourning.

The Filo Jet began taking on water through its bow shortly after leaving Kyrenia for Taşucu in Turkey’s Mersin province on Sunday afternoon. It capsized while attempting to return to port and later sank.

Captain among 16 detained

Police have detained the ferry’s captain and 15 others as part of an investigation into the disaster. Nine appeared in court in Kyrenia on Monday on charges of causing death by negligence, while proceedings were also initiated against seven others.

The captain told the court that the front of the vessel had been torn open by the force of the waves, according to Turkey’s state-run Anadolu news agency.

Survivors have questioned the crew’s response, saying requests to turn back were ignored after the vessel began moving erratically and taking on water.

The Filo Jet is a French-built catamaran first registered in 2001 under the name IrisJet.

International Maritime Organization data reviewed by Agence France-Presse show that the IrisJet sailed under a Spanish flag between 2011 and 2017. However, the Equasis merchant shipping database lists the vessel under a Spanish flag until 2011, followed by a six-year gap before it reappeared under a Greek flag in 2017.

It has operated under the name Filo Jet and flown a Cyprus flag since 2024, according to AFP.

Cyprus has been divided since 1974, when Turkish forces seized the northern third of the island in response to a Greek-backed coup seeking union with Greece.

The Turkish Cypriot administration declared independence in 1983 but is recognized only by Turkey. The internationally recognized Republic of Cyprus, an EU member, controls the southern part of the island.
