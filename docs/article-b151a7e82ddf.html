Ukraine hits oil facility in Russia's Samara region despite pressure from Trump

Back to Ukraine, Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the Ukrainian army struck a Russian oil facility in the Samara region, days after US president Donald Trump sought to put pressure on Kyiv to stop hitting energy targets in Russia.

“I thank all our defenders who are working to send Russia’s sick war back to where it came from,” Zelenskyy said.

“Today, we also have important results from our deep strikes. A target in the Black Sea was hit, as was a site in the Oryol region used to launch and store attack drones.

Sanctions were also applied to an oil facility in the Samara region and a supply depot for the occupiers in the Bryansk region.

These are exactly the kind of just responses we need right now. We will continue to deprive Russia of the ability to keep this war going.”

Russian drone hits grounds of nuclear research facility in Kyiv

Meanwhile, a Russian drone hit the grounds of a nuclear research facility in Kyiv, causing a fire but leaving the facility’s test reactor undamaged, Ukraine’s nuclear safety watchdog said, and AFP reported.

The regulator did not say where the drone landed or provide imagery of the attack, which happened on Wednesday.

Several of Ukraine’s nuclear sites have come under fire since Russia invaded in February 2022, raising fears of a nuclear accident.

The research reactor at the site has the capacity to generate around 10 Megawatts, around a hundredth of the generating capacity of a conventional nuclear reactor. There are four nuclear power plants in Ukraine, the largest of which was seized by Russian forces in 2022.

Nato’s Mark Rutte, Germany’s Friedrich Merz, and the Netherlands’s Rob Jetten are about to speak on a visit to see the 1st German-Netherlands Corps in Münster, Germany.

Germany's Jürgen Klopp disgusted by claims national team are ‘not white enough’

In other news, Jürgen Klopp has reacted with disgust to a social media debate questioning whether the Germany team he is in the process of forming are “white enough”.

Klopp said he believed there was no point in giving room to “such people and their utterances”, dismissing them as “complete nonsense”.

Asked to respond to the debate as to whether the national team were “not German enough” or “not white enough”, he said: “There is absolutely no reason to pay attention to these people. You won’t get my thoughts on this. I have little time in everyday life for idiocy – and even less on social media.”

“The great crux of our lives is that everyone is allowed to express their opinion … it’s insane when something like that exists.”

Klopp said watching sportspeople interact with each other was proof to him that mutual respect was still possible. “The world could learn a thing or two from a sports changing room … There, everyone is respected based on the facts and not discredited based on false opinions.”

Klopp, whose plans for Germany since he took over as coach in the summer have attracted huge attention, said the players he had gathered at the national team camp, including several players with a migrant background, “could not be better united than they are”.

The barrage of racist and generally offensive commentaries on social media followed Germany’s 1-0 defeat by Greece last Sunday, in the Nations League, marking the first time Greece had beaten Germany.

It is also reflective of plenty of utterances from MPs of the far-right Alternative für Deutschland (AfD), the largest opposition party in the Bundestag, who have repeatedly and consistently criticised the diversity and ethnic makeup of the national team, often referring to the squad in derogatory terms such as a “politically correct mercenary force”.

EU countries, Britain hold call on possible release of reserve diesel stacks

The EU Commission, France, Italy, Ireland and Britain are holding a call on the possible need to release diesel stocks, an EU official said, as reported by Reuters.

A Commission spokesperson had earlier declined direct comment on reports that the Trump administration has told Germany and France to draw down emergency diesel inventories to help to ease global fuel prices or face a potential US diesel export ban.

Kyiv Independent editor: ‘Our readers want Russia to suffer but we can’t be propagandists’

Readers of Ukraine’s leading English news site “really want Russia to suffer” but that should not lead the title to downplay the military and political problems facing Kyiv, its editor has said.

Speaking to the Guardian before an event to London, Olga Rudenko said many of the Kyiv Independent’s most-read stories described Ukrainian deep strike attacks into Russia, potentially distorting its coverage.

The Kyiv Independent, however, cannot afford to become propagandistic, she argued, and marginalise Russian military successes or reduce reporting on Ukrainian corruption because the subjects are not so well read.

“We had a conversation in the newsroom when somebody raised that our website, in the mornings, is all about Ukrainian attacks on Russian Wildberries warehouses and so on,” Rudenko said.

“But it’s misrepresenting the full picture when you don’t balance it out with enough stories about attacks on Ukraine, which are, by the way, far less popular with our readers.”

The challenge is that “people really want Russia to suffer”, and read about “something going boom” but it was the Kyiv Independent’s duty to “not just to feed them more of what they like”.

The Kyiv metro and public transport services across the Dnipro river have been suspended this morning after two Russian drones hit the support structure of the Pivdennyi Bridge in the Ukrainian capital.

“Metro and surface public transport traffic across the bridge have been suspended. Specialists … are inspecting the structure’s condition following the strikes,” Kyiv mayor Vitaly Klitschko said.

Russia expels Hungary's diplomats in retaliation to Budapest's move earlier this month

Meanwhile, Russia has summoned the top Hungarian diplomat in the country and told him that “a group of employees” of the Hungarian embassy in Moscow and its consulates in St. Petersburg and Kazan must leave within two weeks.

The Russian foreign ministry’s move seeks to retaliate for a recent Hungarian decision to expel 10 Russian diplomats “for conducting activities incompatible with their status under the Vienna Convention” (Europe Live, 8 September).

Hungary’s prime minister Péter Magyar said at the time that the 10 Russian diplomats “carried out activities in Hungary that are unacceptable for diplomats.” “We will defend our country’s sovereignty,” he added.

UK adopts new sanctions on Russia over Ukraine war

The UK has adopted 31 new measures sanctioning LNG shadow fleet vessels, Kremlin disinformation networks and alleged perpetrators of civilian torture and child indoctrination, the foreign ministry said.

“Following sham elections in temporarily occupied territories of Ukraine earlier this month, the UK is now taking firm action against people and entities found to have undermined Ukraine’s territorial integrity through human rights abuses,” the FCDO said.

The new listings include eight people alleged to be involved in the arbitrary detention, torture and ill-treatment of Ukrainian civilians as part of a broader system of repression.

Seven people are targeted “for their role in the indoctrination and militarisation od Ukrainian children.”

The individuals will be subjected to asset freezes, trust services sanctions, and travel bans.

The package also hits new vessels involved in Russia’s shadow fleet.

“We are targeting both the revenues that help sustain Russia’s aggression and perpetrators of horrendous crimes against Ukrainian civilians, including children. The UK will not flinch in our support for Ukraine,” Europe minister Steward Wood said.

“While the UK does not seek confrontation with Russia, until Putin chooses to end his senseless and barbaric war, the UK’s support for Ukraine will remain unshakeable,” the ministry added in the statement.

A Russian drone hit a school in Ukraine’s capital, authorities said, the latest in a spate of worsening air attacks on Kyiv, Reuters reported.

Mayor Vitali Klitschko said children had been in the shelter at the moment of the strike, which sparked a fire. He reported no injuries.

The State Emergency Service released images of rescue workers picking through debris and dousing the charred building, whose windows were shattered.

Reuters noted that the morning attack came amid intensifying, around-the-clock strikes on Kyiv and other cities with jet-powered drones which Ukraine’s military has struggled to shoot down.

Italy, Poland extend suspensions of Schengen rules to fight irregular migration

In other news, Italy has decided to extend the suspension of the European Union’s border-free arrangement with Spain for another 15 days.

The decision was taken due to “the current situation in the Spanish exclave of Ceuta and, in particular, on the persistence of the critical issues already identified,” the ministry said.

The move – made in the immediate aftermath of a mass influx of migrants into Ceuta in late July – remains a source of tension between Rome and Madrid.

Separately, Poland also extended its border controls with Germany and Lithuania by six months until 30 March, saying it was needed to curb irregular migration.

Since the reintroduction of border controls, entry into the country has been denied to nearly 1,300 people at the Lithuanian border and nearly 1,200 at the German border, Reuters reported.

Estonia bans transit of Russian, Belarusian grain through its territory

Meanwhile, Estonia has banned the transit of Russian and Belarusian grain through its territory, Estonian foreign minister Margus Tsahkna said.

Sources last month said Russian companies are repurposing fertiliser, coal, and other cargo terminals at Ukraine’s Baltic and Arctic ports to handle grain exports after Ukrainian drone attacks disrupted shipments through the Black Sea.

“I warned already in September: do not make plans to transport Russian grain through Estonia. Today, we turned that warning into law.

Together with Latvia and Lithuania, we are working to ensure that Baltic ports do not become a transit route for Russia.

Russia cannot wage a war of aggression and expect us to help keep its trade flowing. We will not help finance its war or make its aggression easier to sustain."

Morning opening: Russia continues strikes on Ukraine as Zelenskyy makes a frontline visit

Russia has continued its attacks on Ukraine overnight, with infrastructure damage reported in Kyiv and Odesa, among others.

The latest attacks come as part of an air campaign against Ukraine’s energy grid, hitting power facilities and infrastructure around Kyiv and other regions in the heaviest combined attack on energy sites in months.

Menawhile, president Volodymyr Zelenskyy visited a post near the frontline.

“This is where independence is won. And the path to peace runs through these very labyrinths, these very corridors,” he said in a video published on his social media channels, walking in the trenches.

“When we say, ‘Ukraine is holding on,’ this resilience begins right here at the front, in command posts like this and at every one of our positions because Ukraine has defenders,” he said.

Elsewhere, Nato’s Mark Rutte will travel to Münster in Germany today to meet with the 1st German-Netherlands Corps and meet with chancellor Friedrich Merz and prime minister Rob Jetten.

The trio will speak at a joint press conference early afternoon, and Merz is later expected to deliver a major speech on Ukraine as he presents the 2026 International Award Ceremony of the Peace of Westphalia to Rutte in early evening.
