Putin's grip on Crimea tested amid major blackout as Zelenskyy warns of looming Russian strike

2026-11-27 02:25

Russian President Vladimir Putin's control over Crimea faced renewed pressure Thursday as a massive blackout crippled the Russian-occupied peninsula, disrupting public transport and leaving people without heat, according to reports.

The blackout came after Ukrainian strikes had damaged the Kerch Strait Bridge last week, disrupting a key Russian military supply route, and as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Moscow was preparing another large-scale attack on Ukraine.

Russian-controlled utilities blamed the Crimea blackout on an accident at energy facilities outside the region, after reports of explosions and air raid sirens sounded across the Black Sea peninsula, according to the Kyiv Post.

Meanwhile, Zelenskyy said Ukrainian intelligence suggested that Russia was preparing to target the country's energy infrastructure, logistics networks and essential services.

"Unfortunately, the Russians are already completing preparations for a new massive strike," Zelenskyy wrote on X Thursday following a coordination meeting. "According to intelligence, energy facilities and logistics infrastructure, as well as essential services facilities in cities and communities, will once again be the targets."

The Ukrainian leader also urged residents to remain vigilant, warning of renewed missile attacks.

"In the coming days, please pay attention to the alarms of missile threats," Zelenskyy said in his nightly video address Thursday, according to Reuters. "And in Russia, they should know that we will rebuild what is ours and retaliate against every Russian strike."

In Crimea, Russian-controlled electricity provider Sevastopolenergo said that Sevastopol had lost power, while electricity restrictions affected Simferopol, Kerch, Alushta and Yevpatoria, according to Ukrainian news outlet Ukrainska Pravda.

Streetcar services were also suspended in Yevpatoria and buses replaced trolleybuses on some routes in Simferopol. Parts of Kerch were also reportedly left without heating.

Russian-installed authorities had issued air raid alerts Thursday morning amid reports of explosions.

The blackout is the latest in a series of disruptions that have hit Crimea since late October, with Russian authorities blaming damage to power infrastructure.

The disruptions come amid mounting pressure on Crimea, which Moscow annexed in 2014 and has since used as a military and logistics hub for its operations against Ukraine.

The Kerch Strait Bridge, which connects the peninsula to mainland Russia, serves as a critical transportation link for Russian forces and has been targeted by Ukraine since Putin launched his invasion in 2022.

The bridge was built after the Russian annexation and opened in 2018, serving as a symbol of Moscow's control over the peninsula.

Meanwhile in Kyiv, Mayor Vitali Klitschko also said seven people were wounded in an overnight Russian attack on the Ukrainian capital that damaged a shopping center and two other buildings, Reuters reported.

Zelenskyy also called for additional air defense assistance Thursday, emphasizing the need for interceptors for Patriot air defense systems and National Advanced Surface-to-Air Missile Systems (NASAMS).

"I expect a report from Ukraine’s Foreign Ministry on the results of its communication with our partners regarding the supply of air defenses — interceptors for Patriots and NASAMS are the key priority," Zelenskyy wrote.

It was the second time this week Zelenskyy had warned of preparations for another major Russian attack, according to Reuters.
