Sweden election live: results too close to call, with projections narrowing as more votes are counted

2026-09-13 20:43

SVT projection: centre-left opposition bloc leads by one seat

Drama.

The SVT’s much-awaited projection at 9.45pm local time, using the actual results from 10% of districts in their model, shows the two blocs effectively tied, with the opposition leading by just ONE SEAT in the new parliament.

Here are the revised figures:

Social Democratic Party (S) 27.6%

Moderates (M) 20.0% Sweden Democrats (SD) 17.5%

Christian Democrats (KD) 6.3%

If confirmed, it would give a truly extraordinary result: the opposition bloc at 175 seats in the new parliament, and the incumbent government at 174.

Amazingly, if the projection came true, it would not be the first time Sweden has seen a nail-biter of this scale.

In the 1973 general election,the two main political blocs literally tied at 175 seats each. That stalemate prompted parliament to change the number of available seats to an odd 349 ahead of the next election.

That proved to be a smart move, coming in handy just six years later, in 1979, when the centre-right bloc won by, erm, a single seat: 175 to 174.

With 57% of votes now counted, the opposition bloc leads by 0.3pp, 46.2% to 45.9% – and still 175 to 174 when it comes to seats.

The latest figures are as follows:

Social Democratic Party (S) 27.6%

Moderates (M) 19.8% Sweden Democrats (SD) 17.7%

Christian Democrats (KD) 6.2.%

Projection shows Kristersson's Moderates broaden their advantage over far-right Sweden Democrats - snap analysis

It’s worth noting the Moderates gaining a lot in the SVT’s projection, moving up from 18.1% to 20.0%, well ahead of the far-right Sweden Democrats in third, at 17.5%.
