Good morning and welcome to our live coverage of US politics. Donald Trump is travelling to Florida today to speak at another rally as midterm election day races nearer.

The president will stump for gubernatorial candidate Byron Donalds. Whether Donalds regards this as helpful remains to be seen after he removed mentions of the president from his website and has barely uttered his name during the race for governor.

Last night, Trump further stoked the controversy over an attack ad by Vivek Ramaswamy, featuring the mother of his Democratic rival, Dr Amy Acton – telling supporters at a rally that he personally told the Republican candidate for Ohio governor to keep running the spot.

Acton’s mother lost custody of her daughter at age 12 because her husband was charged with raping the young girl. Trump told the rally in Iowa: “I said, ‘You’ve got to ride with it.’”

Trump looks increasingly out of step with his own party over the ad after yet another Republican denounced the ad on Thursday. Iowa Republican Senate candidate Ashley Hinson broke with the president by denouncing the attack ad, saying: “I think it’s absolutely terrible, and I think they should take the ad down.”

A data leak from Germany’s far-right German Alternative für Deutschland (AfD) has revealed the extent of efforts by the party to court Donald Trump and other leading figures in the Maga movement, stretching back more than a decade.

The trove reveals the pains the AfD took in wooing the president, whom they saw from the beginning as a potential ally, with senior party officials agonising over the wording of an obsequious congratulatory telegram when he was elected in 2016, which included the wish that his “hopes as a person be fulfilled”.

Trump hailed the win in Saxony-Anhalt in a post on his Truth Social, concluding his message with a German version of his Make America Great Again acronym, “MGGA!!!”

The leaked documents show AfD officials quickly moving to requests for support from the White House on policy issues when he first won the 2016 election.

Others show a burgeoning political courtship between senior figures including the AfD’s now leader Alice Weidel and key Trump ideologue Steve Bannon.

The data, provided to reporters by the transparency organisation Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets), covers more than 420,000 emails, along with documents and personnel records, from the party’s foundation in 2013 to the present, with most coming before 2019.

The ongoing slowdown to Ukraine’s access to Starshield – the modified military-grade version of the Elon Musk’s Starlink satellite network – reached fever pitch this week.

The Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday that the Donald Trump had asked Musk to restore the service, with Trump later told reporters the system was “back”.

However, the president refused to comment on whether he had spoken to Musk, but appeared to suggest the ultimate power to limit the service rested with the SpaceX founder and CEO. “I guess the bottom line, he put it back,” Trump said, referring to Musk.

This comes after a protracted series of disruptions for Ukraine accessing the network – a key defense tool against Russian strikes.

Despite Trump’s insistence, Ukrainian officials said on Friday morning that speeds remained capped, with no indication that the restrictions had been lifted.

Trump will begin his day in Washington, before his midterms blitz continues in Florida. A reminder that today is his fifth day of campaign rallies this week. He’ll deliver remarks at 2pm ET in Fort Myers.

We mentioned the key gubernatorial race in the Sunshine state earlier – where Republican congressman Byron Donalds will face off against Democrat David Jolly. But there is also a key contest that we’re watching in Florida too.

This is the special Senate election, where the Republican incumbent, Ashley Moody, is facing a challenge from progressive Democrat Angie Nixon – who defied odds and won her primary earlier this year.

Americans heading to the polls for next month’s midterm elections are not just choosing whom to send to Washington, or their state capitols. There are also 145 statewide measures on ballots across 39 states, polling voters on actions from a novel wealth tax on California’s billionaires to requiring Alabama’s public schools to broadcast or perform the first stanza of the national anthem each week.

Voters in Idaho, Nevada and Virginia are asked to protect abortion rights; voters in Missouri are asked to give those rights back. Property tax issues appear on dozens of state ballots. Several state referendums ask voters to bar trans athletes from competing in women’s sports, or bar gender-affirming medical care for minors.

Voters like referendums, in principle, as a tool of direct democracy. “Polls in every state show at least 2-to-1 support for having an initiative process,” said John Matsusaka, a business professor at the University of Southern California (USC) and executive director of the Initiative and Referendum Institute.
