Aidan Hutchinson breaks silence after season-ending injury and announces decision about continuing celebration

2026-11-20 01:11

Aidan Hutchinson is making two promises following his season-ending right knee injury that came when he celebrated a sack by jumping into the air and landing on a leg that buckled under him.

Hutchinson on Sunday suffered a torn ACL, which requires surgery and has ended his 2026 season.

Well, the Detroit Lions defensive broke his silence about the injury on his Instagram account on Thursday evening.

And the news that comes out of Hutchinson's message is that he is done with celebrations that require him to take flight.

"I honestly can’t believe it," Hutchinson wrote. "I’ve jumped hundreds of times on a football field and never in a million years did I think this would happen to me.

"But even something like this won’t hold me back.

"I can ask why over and over again, but it’ll never make sense. All I can do is surrender to the path. Only God knows why and I trust in Him.

"Very grateful for all of your prayers and well wishes."

There's wisdom and faith here. But perhaps the smartest thing Hutchinson wrote was next.

"Two things I know for sure," he added.

"1. I always come back stronger.

"2. That’s the last time I go airborne 😁👍

"Hold down the fort 'til I’m back, boys."

And there it is. Hutchinson's learned lesson is that the celebration he's done so many times will not be repeated because the chance of an injury repeat will never go away.

Hutchinson is not going to risk another seemingly preventable injury by continuing to do the thing that got him hurt in the first place.

Hutchinson hasn't been alone, by the way.

New York Giants running back Cam Skattebo said Wednesday he is putting his signature backflip touchdown celebration on the back burner.

There have been no NFL coaches who have forced these changes.

Lions coach Dan Campbell, whose team lost its best pass rusher, said this week he wasn't preventing his players from celebrating in whatever fashion they wished.

And Denver Broncos coach Sean Payton said there are a lot of teachable moments from games every week that he puts on tape and shows his team in a meeting.

But that Hutchinson's injury wouldn't be one of those because he's not going to orchestrate celebrations that are acceptable or not.

But there's apparently no need for coaches to police the situation.

The players are doing it for themselves.
