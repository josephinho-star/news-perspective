Four separate flights from London, Copenhagen, Manchester and Israel affected by bomb threats – Europe live

2026-11-03 13:06

Flights from London, Copenhagen, Manchester and Israel affected by bomb threats

If you are joining us now, we are following reports about at least four planes affected by bomb threats across Europe this morning: two in Poland (from the UK 10:33, 10:58, 12:00 and Denmark 11:33, 12:59), one in Bulgaria (from Israel 12:50), and one in Spain (from the UK 12:44).

All four of them landed safely and are being checked by security services. It may be nothing, but is highly unusual (particularly after another alert yesterday 10:44), so we are keeping a close eye on that.

Separately, we are keeping an eye on Berlin where Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy is just arriving to meet German chancellor Friedrich Merz on his previously unannounced high-security trip to Berlin.

Polish authorities call off bomb alert on London flight

Similarly, Warsaw-Modlin Airport has also given the all-clear after concluding security sweeps on a LOT Polish Airlines flight from London Heathrow following an earlier bomb alert (10:33).

“The bomb threat was called off … Following the completion of operations by the relevant services, Warsaw/Modlin Airport has returned to normal functioning.”

Spanish police rules out presence of explosives on Ryanair flight to Murcia

We have just got an update from Spain’s Guardia Civil on the Ryanair flight from Manchester to Murcia (12:44).

The authorities “ruled out the presence of explosives on the plane,” the Spanish government’s delegate in the region of Murcia, Francisco Lucas, confirmed.

“The International Airport of the Region of Murcia resumes its activity normally. Thanks to the emergency teams that have participated in the operation,” he said.

Swedish centrist Thand Ringqvist rejected as prime minister as search for new government continues

Over in Sweden, centrist leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist has just lost a vote on her proposed government – which is hardly surprising given that even she as very clear she had no chance of winning.

324 votes against, 25 in favour.

It’s been 51 days since the Swedish parliamentary election, and it’s the second failed attempt to form the government. Two more and we get a snap election. Gulp.

The next attempt is expected to be led by the interim centre-right prime minister, Ulf Kristersson, but his outgoing coalition is also unlikely to secure enough support in the parliament, further complicating the process.

The Scandinavian SAS airline has issued a brief statement confirming its earlier incident in Warsaw (11:33).

Note, that’s two separate incidents involving two separate flights in Warsaw – a PLL LOT flight from London Heathrow to Warsaw-Okęcie, diverted to Warsaw-Modlin (10:33, 10:58, 12:00), and a SAS flight from Copenhagen to Warsaw-Okęcie.

“We can confirm that a threat has been made against flight SK751 between Copenhagen and Warsaw. The aircraft landed normally in Warsaw and the passengers have disembarked.

We are following established security procedures while taking care of our passengers and crew. The matter is now being handled by the police in Warsaw, and we are following their instructions.

We refer any further questions regarding the threat to the relevant authorities and the police in Warsaw.”

And Reuters is reporting a fourth plane affected by a bomb threat this morning, this time in Bulgaria.

A plane flying from Israel landed at Sofia airport after a bomb threat was received by email, Reuters reported after local media, with counter-terrorism units deployed to check for possible explosive devices.

The airport’s website shows only one plane from Israel landing there this morning: an El Al service from Tel Aviv.

Bulgarian public broadcaster BNT reported that it was a part of a broader bomb threat alert, affecting “various institutions.”

Meanwhile, we are getting news about a third bomb threat issued against a plane in Europe this morning: this time it’s a Ryanair flight FR4116 from Manchester to Murcia in Spain.

The plane landed safely at Murcia airport. 114 passengers and 7 crew members safely disembarked the plane which is now being checked by security services.

The report was confirmed by the Spanish government’s delegate in the region of Murcia, Francisco Lucas, and reported by local media.

EU to host talks on Ukraine with EU, US, Nato, World Bank on Wednesday

The European Commission will host a meeting regarding Ukraine with representatives from the United States, Ukraine and its key European allies on Wednesday, Commission spokesperson Paula Pinho said.

“So tomorrow the European Commission will host a meeting with representatives from Ukraine, from the United States, France, Germany, the UK, Nato and the World Bank and this meeting will build on earlier talks that took place in Miami,” Pinho told reporters in Brussels.

United States’ envoy Steve Witkoff had said earlier that this meeting would take place in Brussels, Reuters added.

Warsaw Modlin airport resumes operations after bomb threat reported on London flight

Back to Poland, Warsaw Modlin airport says it has resumed operations after an earlier bomb threat – but a part of the airport where the London flight was parked remain closed and is being checked by security forces.

Berlin talks with Merz to focus on diplomacy, air defence, energy, Zelenskyy says

Ukraine’sZelenskyy has said his talks with Merz and Germany’s president Steinmeier will focus on “diplomatic coordination” on air defence and energy sector.

“These will be the priorities of today’s talks. We all have an interest in security, stability, and protecting the rules-based international order. And we must work together to turn these principles into concrete decisions and actions.”

The Ukrainian president will also take part in today’s Berlin Security Conference.

Back to Poland briefly, Danish media are reporting that a separate threat was also reported in relation to an SAS flight from Copenhagen to Warsaw Okęcie, which also landed in the Polish capital this morning.

The Guardian understands the airport received a report about a potentially dangerous item on board of the SAS aircraft, and it is currently being assessed by security services.

Kremlin confirms Abu Dhabi talks with US, Ukraine next week

Back to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, the Kremlin confirmed that US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks to end more than four years of war will take place in Abu Dhabi next week.

“The Russian delegation will arrive on 10-11 November in Abu Dhabi to take part in trilateral talks,” Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said, quoted by AFP.

He added that presidential aide and former culture minister Vladimir Medinsky will represent Moscow at the meeting.

AFP noted that Medinsky, who is 56 and previously served as culture minister, is known for his nationalistic views on Russian history and took part in previous failed talks.

In the latest update from Warsaw Modlin airport, passengers are now allowed to disembark from the plane, and will be asked to stay in the main terminal.

The airport said in a press statement:

“Further actions are being carried out in accordance with safety procedures. At this time, we are not providing any additional information regarding the operations of the emergency services.”

The bomb alert comes just a day after a WizzAir flight from London Luton to Bulgaria’s Sofia was redirected to Budapest after a reported mid-air bomb threat.

After careful inspection, it proved to be a false alarm.

Warsaw Modlin airport closed after bomb threat reported on London flight

Meanwhile, I am also following reports from Poland after a Polish LOT airline flight from London Heathrow made an unplanned landing at Warsaw Modlin airport following a bomb alert.

The airport – the Polish capital’s secondary port, located just north of Warsaw – has just confirmed that L0286 flight from Heathrow to Warsaw Okęcie, with 181 people on board of a Boeing 737 MAX aircraft, landed safely in Modlin.

The plane will be inspected by security services, and the airport remains closed until further notice.

Russian attacks on Ukraine on US election day 'telling,' Zelenskyy says

Before arriving in Berlin, Ukraine’s Zelenskyy condemned Russia’s overnight attacks on critical infrastructure across the country, which he said were causing “emergency and unscheduled power outages”.

He said the attacks – which involved 289 drones, 59 cruise missiles and 17 ballistic missiles – also hit civilian buildings, including a city hospital in Kharkiv, where 73 people were injured.

“A rescue operation has also been underway since yesterday in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region. Tragically, the death toll has risen. Russia has killed 29 people.”

“It is very telling that all of this is happening on the day of the US congressional elections. The Russians believe that America will not notice this signal from them. But such terror must be noticed and stopped. Ukraine must be helped to defend its skies, otherwise Russia will never stop its missile strikes. It is essential to help build peace honestly and reliably, and America can do that.”

Morning opening: Zelenskyy to make unexpected visit to Berlin for talks with Merz

Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy is set to make an unexpected visit to Berlin for talks with German chancellor Friedrich Merz ahead of next week’s trilateral talks with Russia and the US in Abu Dhabi.

The rumours about Zelenskyy’s potential visit started swirling last night, with Merz confirming the visit during an industry speech this morning. The Ukrainian president’s trips are usually kept secret until the last moment for security reasons.

Recalling the infamous showdown between Zelenskyy and Trump at the White House last year, Merz said:

“He’ll be here with me just after midday, and things will be a bit different then.”

In his speech, Merz said the German push to rapidly increase its defence spending last year was triggered by the scenes in the Oval Office.

“What we experienced last year … led me to conclude that we must ensure, on our own, that we are capable of defending ourselves in Europe,” he said.

He added that this was not meant to question Nato, but strengthen it. “We are making Nato more European so it can remain transatlantic,” he said.

Elsewhere, we are expecting updates from Sweden, with a second parliamentary vote on the new government, which is expected to fail once again (51 days on from elections).

We should also get some lines from Spain, where transport minister Óscar Puente is will present new ideas on reforming high-speed rail after last week’s tragic crash in Toledo before facing a parliamentary committee.
