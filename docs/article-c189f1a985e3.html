Fierce storm lashes southern California after record rain in San Francisco

2026-11-19 19:08

An atmospheric river pounded southern California with heavy rain and winds, one day after the fierce storm set records for rainfall in the north of the state.

Los Angeles authorities had urged residents in the Palisades, Hurst and Sunset burn scar areas to be prepared to leave before the storm’s arrival on Thursday, and warned of a risk of debris flows and flooding. Flash flood warnings were issued from Eastern Malibu to Beverly Hills to Hollywood, with heightened risk until noon on Thursday, while flood warnings were issued across the region through Thursday afternoon.

Heavy rainfall started hitting the region on Wednesday night and through Thursday morning. Multiple drivers had to be rescued from submerged vehicles in flooded city streets near the Burbank airport, KTLA5 News reported.

Karen Bass, Los Angeles’s mayor, said in a press release that city crews had been deployed to address localized flooding on roadways and other storm-related impacts.

The downfall slowed by midmorning in central Los Angeles, and the National Weather Service predicted lighter rain and less intense flooding risk in the afternoon.

The atmospheric river first hit the San Francisco region on Wednesday, bringing heavy rain and fierce winds.

San Francisco broke a rain record previously set in 1917. The city’s fire department said it responded to hundreds of calls, many of which related to flooding, along with downed trees and electrical wires. Flights at San Francisco airport were delayed hours because of wind. The self-driving car service Waymo temporarily halted operations in San Francisco on Wednesday, amid reports and video of Waymo cars stuck in floodwaters and blocking traffic.

Areas along the city’s Embarcadero were flooded as waves crashed over barricades recently installed to prevent flooding near the Ferry Building, a waterfront landmark. Officials canceled several ferries leading from the city due to the storm.

Tens of thousands of households lost power across the region.

Several highways were blocked by flooding or debris in nearby Napa, Marin, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties.

As the storm moved south, a segment of Highway 1 near Big Sur was forced to close.

Several roads in San Luis Obispo and Santa Barbara counties were inundated overnight.

In the mountains, heavy snows forced the closure of highways in the Tahoe area. Snow accumulations of up to 4ft (1.2 meters) were forecast through Friday in the highest portions of the Sierra Nevada.

Atmospheric rivers, huge currents of water vapor that can carry as much or even more water than an actual river, have been growing stronger and deadlier in recent years. In the relatively dry western US, atmospheric river storms provide both essential precipitation and flood risk.

The Golden state is bracing for more weather challenges in the week ahead, when fresh rain is set to coincide with high tides.
