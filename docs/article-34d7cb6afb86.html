Senior AKP lawmaker says parliamentary vote could enable another Erdoğan candidacy

2026-09-15 14:35

A senior lawmaker from Turkey’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) said on Tuesday that a parliamentary vote to call early elections could allow President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan to run again.

Turkey’s constitution limits presidents to two five-year terms. However, Article 116 provides an exception: If parliament calls early presidential and parliamentary elections during a president’s second term, the incumbent may seek another term.

Such a decision requires the support of at least three-fifths, or 360, of Turkey’s 600 lawmakers. The exception applies only when parliament calls early elections; if Erdoğan calls them himself, he would not be eligible to run again.

AKP parliamentary group deputy chair Abdulhamit Gül made the remarks at a news conference in the northwestern province of Sakarya during the party’s “Century of Turkey Meetings,” the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

Gül claimed there was broad public support for Erdoğan to remain in office, including among voters who had not previously backed him.

“There is broad agreement among the public that our president should remain in office and continue to provide strong leadership for Turkey,” he said.

Gül then pointed to a parliamentary decision as the mechanism that could enable another candidacy.

“We fully believe that with a decision to be taken by our parliament, this expectation of our people for our president to run again and be elected president will be fulfilled,” he said.

He separately reiterated the AKP’s call for a new, civilian constitution, saying he believed the proposal would find support in parliament. He did not explicitly say that a new constitution should remove or alter the presidential term limit.

Erdoğan was elected president under Turkey’s current executive system in 2018 and secured a second term in 2023. He had also been elected president in 2014, when Turkey still operated under a parliamentary system.

The AKP has argued that Erdoğan’s 2018 victory constituted his first term under the executive presidency introduced following a 2017 constitutional referendum. Opposition parties and some legal experts contend that his 2014–2018 presidency should also have counted toward the two-term limit.

Turkey’s Supreme Election Board accepted the ruling party’s interpretation in 2023 and allowed Erdoğan to seek re-election.

For Erdoğan to run again under the exception in Article 116, parliament would have to vote to move forward both the presidential and parliamentary elections before the end of his current term. The AKP and its main ally, the far-right Nationalist Movement Party (MHP), do not have the 360 votes required and would need support from other parties.

The issue has gained renewed attention as senior figures in Erdoğan’s ruling alliance have increasingly discussed another presidential bid.

Erdoğan’s chief legal adviser, Mehmet Uçum, proposed in June that the next presidential and parliamentary elections be held on April 16, 2028, several weeks before they would otherwise take place.

He said a parliamentary decision to call early elections would activate the constitutional exception and allow Erdoğan to run “for the last time.”

MHP Deputy Chairman İsmail Özdemir also said earlier this month that Erdoğan would again be the party’s presidential candidate in 2028.

Erdoğan last week described Turkey’s next elections as “approaching,” raising speculation that they could be held before their regular date in 2028.

Erdoğan has governed Turkey since 2003, first as prime minister and since 2014 as president.
