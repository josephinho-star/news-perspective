Shocking details emerge as survivor of Spirit Halloween mass shooting speaks out

2026-11-21 01:52

A father’s Halloween shopping trip with his young son turned into a bloodbath when gunfire erupted inside a crowded Spirit Halloween store, leaving both wounded and a pregnant woman dead.

Anthony Barlow was struck by a bullet that shattered his elbow during the Oct. 30 shooting at the Fairfield store.

But the most devastating moment came after the gunfire stopped, when he discovered his son had also been hit.

“This pain that I got right here is nothing compared to seeing my son on a gurney,” Barlow told CBS News Sacramento.

The father now faces roughly six weeks with his arm in a sling. A .45-caliber bullet remains lodged in his elbow, according to medical scans he shared with the outlet.

Barlow recalled having no time to react when the shooting began.

“I knew I was shot when it first hit, and then the adrenaline took over,” he said.

The store quickly descended into chaos as shoppers scrambled for safety and Barlow lost sight of his son.

“It was like slow motion as everybody was running,” Barlow said. “You know, you try to decide what’s going on in the moment.”

The father and son were transported to separate hospitals, where Barlow learned the boy had suffered a gunshot wound to the stomach.

His son remains hospitalized but is expected to be released within the next few days.

The shooting killed 31-year-old Constance Hages, who was 13 weeks pregnant, and injured six other people, including Barlow and his son. Prosecutors also brought charges against the accused gunman in connection with the death of Hages’ unborn child.

The teenager accused of opening fire, 17-year-old Monte Smith, has pleaded not guilty to two counts of murder, three counts of attempted murder and three counts of assault with a semi-automatic weapon.

Smith was arrested Nov. 2. Prosecutors have filed a motion seeking to have him prosecuted as an adult, a request that remains pending.

The allegations against Smith also come amid questions about his previous encounters with the juvenile justice system.

Authorities said he had been on probation following a 2025 shooting at Fairfield High School, where he allegedly fired into a vehicle occupied by other teenagers in the school parking lot.

He spent several months in juvenile detention before being released.
