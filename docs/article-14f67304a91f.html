Heart-pounding moment killer whales converge on paddleboarder off California coast

2026-11-25 03:29

A California paddleboarder got the wildlife encounter of a lifetime when two curious killer whales repeatedly circled his board in the open Pacific, coming close enough to leave his legs shaking.

Bill Clements was paddling in Monterey Bay on Nov. 8 when a pair of orcas swam up to investigate, putting on an astonishing underwater show captured on video and shared by We Love Animals on YouTube.

In the clip, each whale circles Clements over and over again, diving into the water and coming back out, putting on quite the show.

One whale repeatedly approached the board, but neither appeared aggressive or made any attempt to knock him into the water.

“I’ve always dreamed of paddling with orcas but I was not prepared for this encounter,” Clements wrote in his post on social media.

“My legs were literally trembling with excitement as they repeatedly swam by me. The younger one was particularly curious and swam up to me several times!”

It is clear from the video, the paddleboarder was more than excited, as he can be overheard giggling with joy and saying “Oh my God,” repeatedly.

“These orcas were supremely curious and began their interaction with me by presenting a gift – a fresh Sealion carcass!” Clements wrote.

“This pod is known as the CA51As or Aurora’s pod. The pair that came up to me at the beginning of the clip are Aurora’s daughter Andi and her 2-year-old calf, Astrid.”

“Astrid was such a curious calf and repeatedly swam by me, at times on her side to make eye contact with me. Unforgettable experience! I hope our paths cross again!” he added.

Many people reacted to the video and talked about Clements’s breathtaking experience.

“As much as I’ve always dreamed of this… it still seems like it would be initially… terrifying. Beautiful and incredible… and still terrifying. What an amazing experience!” one person wrote.

“Absolutely incredible and look how careful they were not to bump your board!” another person added.

Aurora’s pod is a well-known multigenerational orca family that frequently visits California’s Central Coast, according to Monterey Bay Whale Watch.

The adult female Aurora is often seen with three members of her pod, including offspring Andi and Astrid, in the Monterey Bay, according to Sanctuary Cruises.

A full-grown orca can weigh up to 11 tons and get as long as 32 feet, with the females lighter and smaller, according to the NOAA.

While a younger calf whale is typically 7 to 8 feet in length and weighs up to 400 pounds.

The adult orcas can travel up to 35 miles per hour and are considered to be among the fastest of marine mammals.
