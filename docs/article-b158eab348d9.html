Las Vegas Grand Prix betting guide: How to bet Saturday night's race on the strip

2026-11-21 23:25

Vegas doesn't do anything quietly, and the Las Vegas Grand Prix is no exception. The lights are on, the casinos are packed and Formula One cars will be screaming down the Strip at more than 200 miles per hour Saturday night. It's the one race on the calendar that feels like a show as much as a sporting event.

The betting board fits the setting. You can lay heavy juice on the favorite, or you can do what Vegas is built for and take a few swings. The race also has one more ingredient that makes it great to bet: chaos. Las Vegas averages 48.7 overtakes per race, more than Monza, Baku or Jeddah. The starting grid is a suggestion here, not a guarantee.

Here's how to bet the Las Vegas Grand Prix, from the race winner to a couple of fun swings further down the board.

Race: Saturday, Nov. 21, 11 p.m. ET (8 p.m. PT), Las Vegas Strip Circuit

Circuit: 3.8 miles, 17 turns, about 80% of the lap at full throttle

The grid (after penalties)

- Max Verstappen (Red Bull)

- George Russell (Mercedes)

- Isack Hadjar (Red Bull)

- Arvid Lindblad (Racing Bulls)

- Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes)

- Liam Lawson (Racing Bulls)

- Franco Colapinto (Alpine)

- Charles Leclerc (Ferrari)

Antonelli was the fastest driver in qualifying, 0.258 seconds clear of Verstappen. A five-place penalty for an extra engine component dropped him to sixth. Oscar Piastri, Carlos Sainz and Lance Stroll took 10-place penalties, and Piastri starts 17th after qualifying seventh.

Kimi Antonelli to win (+470)

This is the bet. The fastest car on the track is starting sixth, and you're getting almost 5-1 on it.

Antonelli has already won this year's drivers' championship, and he's chasing his 10th win of the season. He out-qualified everyone by a quarter of a second, and Mercedes brought a turbocharger upgrade this weekend that only he is running. Russell is on the older spec.

Starting from behind hasn't slowed him down this year. He won at Monza from 19th on the grid and finished fifth in Baku from 16th. Both are high-speed, low-downforce tracks, just like Vegas. Throw in a circuit that produces almost 50 overtakes per race, and five spots isn't much to make up.

Lando Norris to finish on the podium (-125)

Norris is the steady play. He starts third, he has four straight top-five finishes, and he took pole here last year before finishing second, a result later wiped out by a post-race disqualification.

He's also been quick all weekend. Norris set the fastest second sector in qualifying and finished just 0.093 seconds behind Verstappen's lap. In Brazil, he beat Verstappen to the line by 14.6 seconds. Starting on the second row of a track where passing is easy, a top-three finish is a reasonable ask.

Charles Leclerc to finish on the podium (+350)

This is the Vegas swing.

Ferrari had a rough qualifying session, with both cars knocked out in Q2. After the penalties, Leclerc starts 10th. That's exactly why the price is +350, and exactly why I'm interested.

Leclerc has finished in the top four in all three of his Las Vegas starts. He's led 13 laps here, the third-most of anyone since the race began. He has six podiums this season, finished second at the U.S. Grand Prix and has finished in the top five in seven of his last eight races in North America.

Some drivers just have a feel for a track. Leclerc has one for this one. At 7-2, it's worth a bet.

Oscar Piastri to be the biggest mover (+152)

Here's a fun one. Piastri qualified seventh, then a 10-place engine penalty dropped him to 17th on the grid.

The pace is there. He was within about a second of pole in qualifying, and he's running the same McLaren that Norris has on the second row. A fast car starting 17th on the highest-overtaking track of the season is a natural fit for this market. If he finishes anywhere in the top 10, he'll likely be among the biggest movers on the night.

The fade: Max Verstappen at -130

Verstappen has won here twice, in 2023 and 2025, and he starts on pole. I understand why he's the favorite.

But -130 means risking $130 to win $100 on a single race, at a track where the polesitter has less protection than almost anywhere else, with the fastest car in the field coming up behind him. Under the Vegas lights, I'd rather bet on chaos than lay juice on the chalk.

- Kimi Antonelli to win (+470)

- Lando Norris to finish on the podium (-125)

- Charles Leclerc to finish on the podium (+350)

- Oscar Piastri to be the biggest mover (+152)

Odds subject to change.
