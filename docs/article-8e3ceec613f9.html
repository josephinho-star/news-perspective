Trump threatens ‘tremendous penalties’ for US tech firms that build datacenters abroad

2026-11-20 21:47

Donald Trump threatened on Friday to impose “tremendous penalties” on American tech companies that build datacenters outside the US, though he gave no details and told reporters he had not yet informed his own officials about the plan.

In a discursive press conference from the White House briefing room, Trump also said he would “look into” the report that Iran had been planning to possibly detonate a “dirty bomb” in Washington DC, and backed JD Vance over comments the vice-president made blaming Republican midterm losses on the Iran war.

Trump also expounded on his friendship with the Russian president Vladimir Putin, whom he met alongside China’s Xi Jinping at the Apec summit on Thursday, adding he had “fantastic relationships” with both autocratic leaders.

At the White House, Trump told reporters he wanted datacenters built by US companies to stay on US soil.

“We’re not allowing datacenters to go into other countries, because China will take all of them, and we’re not allowing that to happen. And we’re going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try to do it,” he said.

He said Americans should welcome datacenters, and urged communities to extract concessions from developers, repeating a point he made before the midterms.

“You ought to take advantage of the datacenters. Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don’t shut them off,” he said.

“We want them to build it here.”

With many Americans turning against the datacenters being built near their homes, the US tech industry is making a show of conciliation by promising to take fewer government handouts, to be more transparent, and to give more money to towns and cities willing to allow server warehouses to be built.

The Trump administration on Monday issued new rules to restrict remote access to advanced US computing chips and servers, in a move it said was designed to limit China’s ability to use US chips to train its artificial intelligence models – though a House committee said on Wednesday the US had signed off on sales of nearly 900,000 AI chips to China in six months.

China’s ability to obtain advanced AI chips made by the US firm Nvidia, in which Trump is an investor, has been spotlighted in recent weeks after its CEO, Jensen Huang, was fired over his affair with a woman alleged to be spying for China.

In the same press conference, Trump was asked about reports Iran was planning to potentially detonate an explosive laced with nuclear material – a so-called “dirty bomb” – in Washington DC. The president called it a “rumour” and said: “If they do, we’re going to fight.”

He added: “We know a lot about what’s happening in Iran. We know everything they say. We know who they are, where they are. We’ll find out about it.”

Responding to a question about reports of a rift with Vance, Trump said the vice-president’s remark blaming the Republican collapse on the Iran war was “a very accurate statement”.

“I didn’t say I was unhappy with it,” the president said. “I would have added one other thing if it were asked … would you do it again? And absolutely, the answer is yes, because we cannot allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon.”

Trump was asked whether Vladimir Putin could really be considered a friend to America since Russia is a close ally of Iran.

“I can’t speak to America,” he said, adding: “I consider [that] I have fantastic relationships with Putin. That’s a very good thing. I have fantastic relationships with President Xi. That’s a good thing, and our country is safe because of that.”

He praised Putin as he repeated his grievances over the Department of Justice’s investigation into the Russian attempts to influence US voters ahead of the 2016 presidential election.

“I’ve been dealing with Putin … [since] the fake Russia, Russia, Russia hoax,” he said.

Trump also spoke about two prominent Democrats. He said Gina Hinojosa, who won the race for Texas governor over Trump’s Republican ally Greg Abbott, called him. “She was really nice,” Trump said. “It was a very productive call. And she wants to have a strong border, and I think we’ll be able to work something out.”

And he said Hakeem Jeffries – who Trump previously called a “thug” but is now in line to be the next speaker of the House, and the first Black speaker – is “a smart guy. He’s a streetwise guy.”
