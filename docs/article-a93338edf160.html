Zoo shares autopsy findings following Fiona the hippo’s heartbreaking death

2026-11-20 23:54

The Cincinnati Zoo on Friday released new autopsy findings after the death of Fiona, the hippo whose battle after swallowing a visitor's hat left people across the nation heartbroken.

A postmortem examination determined that a foreign object, confirmed to be a hat zoo officials said was accidentally thrown into her enclosure, was lodged deep inside the hippo's massive digestive tract, according to the zoo's director of animal health, Dr. Mike Wenninger.

The hat was located halfway through the small intestine, about 25 feet from the beginning of her intestinal tract and 50 feet from where it would have exited her body as waste.

The findings settle lingering questions about the hat's exact location after care teams spent days trying to locate it using various methods, including ultrasounds and upper and lower gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopies.

Care teams even obtained 9-foot-long endoscopy tools but were still unable to reach her intestines through her mouth.

"The upper GI endoscopy couldn't even reach out of her large sacculated stomach," Wenninger said.

"Given her anatomy and the attachments from her intestinal tract to body wall, there's no place, no way to get in there with specialized endoscopes," he added.

The hat's location and Fiona's anatomy also made surgical removal impossible, Wenninger said, explaining that even laparoscopic procedures, in which a scope is inserted into the abdomen through a small incision, were not feasible.

"Laparoscopic surgery wasn't possible given the location and a laparotomy, so (an) open incision in a hippo that size with an incision that's that long would have been a death sentence for her," he said.

Zoo officials added that the findings confirm the care team did everything possible, and the complications left no viable options for additional operations.

"No treatment aside from supportive care had any chance for success, and the necropsy confirmed that no amount of supportive care would have worked," Wenninger said.

Following Fiona's death, the facility said the zoo remains open, but the area around the Hippo Cove enclosure will be closed through the weekend to give the animal care team the space it needs during this time.

"Keepers, vets, staff and volunteers are shaken and trying to navigate this loss. She meant a lot to a lot of people," the zoo said in a statement.

While most hippos in captivity live up to 50 years, Fiona was just 9 years old when her condition rapidly deteriorated in November.

She first made headlines in 2017 after defying the odds as a premature hippo. She was born six weeks early, weighing just 29 pounds. By comparison, healthy newborn hippos typically weigh between 50 and 120 pounds at birth.

Zoo officials said Fiona became ill after a visitor tossed a hat into the air near her enclosure. The hat became caught in a ceiling fan, which propelled it over the viewing windows.
