Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor dies at 65; bandmates pay emotional tribute

2026-11-25 21:58

Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor has died of prostate cancer at the age of 65, Fox News Digital confirmed.

Taylor died peacefully at home surrounded by family on Monday, according to a joint statement from his family and former bandmates.

"To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather," his wife, Tracey, said on behalf of the family.

"He was the rock of our family. For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first. Nothing made him happier than having us all together, and no matter how far music took him, his heart was always at home."

His family added that the musician "faced the last few years with so much courage and determination," noting "he never gave up" and kept "fighting until the very end, never losing his love of life."

"The kindness and support shown to him by fans from all over the world throughout his illness brought him immense comfort and strength, and for that we will always be grateful," they added. "Andy knew just how deeply he was loved, and we take great comfort in that. We will miss him every single day."

Members of the band each wrote their own tribute to Taylor on the band's website, with lead singer Simon Le Bon writing, "There’s a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart."

He added that when he thinks of Taylor he remembers "the blond bombshell, firebrand teenager I met in 1980" who had "spikey hair and a spiky personality" and was an extremely talented guitar player.

"I have so many wonderful, hilarious, tragic memories of our time together: on tour, in the studio, in our little flat on Caroline Road, Balsall Heath at 6am," he added. "And through it, & above it all he was in love with his family - most important thing in his life."

John Taylor said, "What he brought down to Birmingham on that train from Whitley Bay can never be quantified," while Nick Rhodes wrote him a poem, in which he called him "An adventurer who found his new home."

"The 'Geordie Genius', 'the live wire from Newcastle': how fortunate we were to have found him," Roger Taylor said.

"The incredible, talented musician and gifted songsmith arrived on a train from Cullercoats to knock us into shape and help us grow into the burgeoning version of Duran Duran that took on the world within a year or two of his arrival.

"The sadness cuts extremely deep, but admiration and respect run even deeper for the Taylor that wasn't related but will forever be my brother. Rest in peace now, Andy."

Duran Duran formed in the late 1970s, releasing their self-titled debut album in 1981 and quickly making a name for themselves as a group ready to push boundaries. They became household names with the release of their second album, "Rio," in 1982, which featured hits like "Hungry Like the Wolf."

Despite their success, the band went on hiatus in the mid-1980s, with two members, including Taylor, pursuing solo projects. He reunited with the band in 2001 but left again in 2006. As a band, they have won two Grammy Awards, have a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and were inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.

Andy Taylor released his first solo project in over 33 years in 2023 with the album "Man's a Wolf to Man." Also in 2023, he reunited with Duran Duran one more time to play guitar on their album, "Danse Macabre."

"Andy was there at the very beginning of our story. From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music," the band said in a statement.

"His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.

"It was great to make music with him again when the original five reunited in the early 2000s, and that time together, both on stage and in the studio, meant a tremendous amount to all of us. When we were inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, Andy was too unwell to join us, but his words that night reminded everyone of his strength and the bond we shared. Andy faced his illness with remarkable courage, honesty and grace."

Taylor is survived by his wife Tracey, his children Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew and his grandson, Albie.
