Ex-White House press secretary Katie Zacharia criticizes GOP over midterm losses, says party isn’t ‘good at winning’

2026-11-22 08:43

Published Nov. 22, 2026, 3:43 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Former White House press secretary Katie Zacharia offered a blunt assessment of her party’s midterm collapse, saying Republicans lack the offensive instincts needed to win elections.

“There’s nobody persuasive in the Democrats, but they’ve always banked on Trump Derangement Syndrome,” Zacharia told the outlet.

She then took aim at her own party as she looked to cast blame for Republicans losing nine Senate seats and at least 50 seats in the House of Representatives to Democratic challengers.

“The Republicans are terrible at the offense,” she added. “I mean, we can’t win. We’re not good at winning.”

However, Zacharia appeared to not harbor any ill-will toward her former boss, telling The Free Press that she does not intend to spill any secrets about the White House because “there’s nothing terrible to write.”

“President Trump made me famous,” Zacharia said, boasting about her relationship with Trump. “I can’t even keep up. I had to hire someone to go track all my opportunities.”

Zacharia departed her position after Trump revealed to reporters that he told her, “You don’t have to do this if you don’t want to. I think you should reconsider.”

Despite her short tenure in the White House, Zacharia reportedly remains in lockstep with Trump on certain topics — both social and political.

Earlier this month, the president sat down for an interview on CBS’s “60 Minutes,” where correspondent Ross Douthat asked whether he would consider forgiving his political enemies if faced with the decision while standing at the gates of Heaven.

“I probably cannot do that, no, because I do hate some of my enemies and I always will,” Trump replied.

Zacharia appeared to agree with the president on the topic of forgiveness, telling The Free Press that she has found it difficult to pray for her own enemies.

“I respect him for saying it,” Zacharia said. “That’s how we all feel. I had friends telling me after everything that happened that I needed to be praying for my enemies, and I was like, ‘No, justice is the Lord’s, and sometimes you want to see retribution for those who have harmed you.’”

Zacharia’s comments regarding religion and forgiveness sparked criticism from Democratic strategist Kaivan Shroff, who told Fox News Digital that her stance is surprisingly in conflict with her Christian faith.

“What jumped out to me was how casually she agreed with Trump that you shouldn’t forgive or pray for your enemies,” Shroff said. “Republicans talk constantly about Christian values, but apparently one of Christianity’s clearest teachings becomes negotiable when Trump has a grievance.”

Shroff went on to point to Democratic Sen.-elect James Talarico’s use of religion in his successful campaign in Texas — a move that marked a significant shift surrounding the party’s appetite for religious candidates.

“We saw James Talarico succeed at pointing out a lot of those double standards when it comes to faith on the right,” Shroff told Fox News Digital. “So it does seem that Republicans have ceded ground on the topic of faith with all of the Trump administration and MAGA hypocrisy around it.”
