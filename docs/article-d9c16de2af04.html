Rights group calls on Turkish government to take ‘urgent steps’ for lasting Kurdish peace

2026-11-10 13:58

Turkey’s Human Rights Association (İHD) on Tuesday called for urgent steps to turn the Turkish government’s efforts to settle its conflict with the outlawed Kurdistan Workers’ Party (PKK) into lasting peace, the Bianet news website reported.

The current peace initiative, launched in late 2024, aims to end a conflict spanning four decades that has killed more than 40,000 people.

The initiative led to the PKK’s decision to lay down arms and disband. In turn, the Turkish Parliament approved a law that took effect on August 18 that enables the conditional suspension of investigations, trials and prison sentences for certain PKK-related offenses, as well as relief dependent on verification that the PKK has ended its activities and surrendered its weapons.

The İHD urged transparency, calling on the government to make public the criteria for verifying that the PKK has laid down its arms, and regularly provide updates on the state of progress in the peace initiative, as well as parliamentary scrutiny of implementation.

The rights group also called for clear rules identifying who qualifies for relief, equal treatment of people in comparable circumstances, housing, health care and support for PKK members who abandoned the armed conflict to rebuild their lives.

The İHD argued that democratic reforms must proceed in parallel with the laying down of arms and that restrictions on rights as well as prosecutions against government opponents deepened public mistrust over the peace initiative.

The rights group called on the government to end the practice of replacing elected mayors with government appointees and to restore removed mayors to office.

The association also demanded compliance with European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) and Constitutional Court rulings, along with the release of sick prisoners.

Turkish courts have refused to implement ECtHR judgments requiring the release of Kurdish politician Selahattin Demirtaş and businessman Osman Kavala, while criminal courts have also defied the Constitutional Court rulings ordering the release of Can Atalay, a lawyer elected to parliament while in prison.

The İHD also sought prison reforms ensuring equal treatment and the “right to hope,” a principle requiring sentence review and a prospect of release for prisoners such as jailed PKK founder Abdullah Öcalan and other PKK prisoners serving life sentences.

The group also urged guarantees for freedom of expression, association and peaceful protest, along with political dialogue on Kurdish identity, language, culture and equal citizenship.

The İHD called for independent monitoring involving human rights groups, bar associations and academics, along with parliamentary reports allowing the public to assess progress in the peace process.

The association demanded investigations into enforced disappearances and unsolved killings, the prosecution of those responsible and programs to help communities recover from the conflict.

Legislation alone would not secure lasting peace, the İHD argued, calling for parallel programs to strengthen democratic rights and pursue truth, justice and support for victims.
