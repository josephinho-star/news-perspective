Week 10 winners and losers: Gibbs dominates, Mahomes has biggest game in 5 years

2026-11-15 23:57

Week 10 of the 2026 fantasy football season featured huge games by Jahmyr Gibbs, Patrick Mahomes and rookie Emmett Johnson. Meanwhile, Jonathan Taylor and the Green Bay and Carolina passing games underperformed in a big way Sunday.

So, who were the big winners and losers around the league? Matt Bowen and Tristan H. Cockcroft offer their analysis.

Ranking winners | Ranking losers

Biggest injuries and what's next

Ranking fantasy winners of Week 10

1. Jahmyr Gibbs, RB, Detroit Lions

After a quiet Week 9, Gibbs roared back with a 44.2-point performance in Munich, reminding us why he's the No. 1 player in our game. He tore up a New England Patriots defense that has been reeling the past two weeks; the Patriots surrendered a combined 80 points and 972 total yards to the Green Bay Packers and Lions.

Gibbs has exceeded 40 fantasy points in three of his past 16 regular-season games and 35-plus in seven of his past 20. His 253.4 points for the season is the eighth-most ever by a running back through his team's first nine games. Gibbs' Week 11 matchup against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers might seem scary -- they entered the week the No. 1 defense against running backs -- but he's on too historic of a run to quibble about his clear overall RB1 fantasy stock. -- Cockcroft

2. Patrick Mahomes, QB, Kansas City Chiefs

The deadline acquisition of WR Josh Downs paid immediate dividends for Mahomes and the Chiefs as the veteran quarterback scored 35.42 fantasy points (his most in a game in five years) and the team totaled 461 yards of offense. Kenneth Walker III's absence shifted the Chiefs' offensive philosophy more toward the pass, as fill-in running back Emmett Johnson has been excellent as a receiver.

Should Walker miss more time -- he's tentatively expected to play in Week 11 -- Mahomes could put up another big score in an extremely favorable matchup against the Arizona Cardinals. -- Cockcroft

3. Emmett Johnson, RB, Kansas City Chiefs

Johnson, making his first pro start while filling in for an injured Walker, scored a career-best 35.1 points in the win over Atlanta. Johnson rushed for 101 yards on 22 carries as he showed that elusive running style from his college tape, but coach Andy Reid has unlocked his receiving ability in this offense. Johnson caught six passes for 70 yards and two touchdowns Sunday. If Walker doesn't return for the Week 11 game versus Arizona, Johnson should be played as an RB1 with dual-threat upside. -- Bowen

4. Chuba Hubbard, RB, Carolina Panthers

In a prime matchup versus the Saints' defense, Hubbard had a season-best 34.2 points. Hubbard rushed for 92 yards and two touchdowns on 27 carries, plus he had a season-high 80 receiving yards on five catches. The Week 11 tilt versus the Ravens' defense creates a tougher matchup for Hubbard, but with 20 or more touches in three straight games (and at least one touchdown in two), he'll be a fringe RB1 in my rankings. -- Bowen

5. Parker Washington, WR, and Trevor Lawrence, QB, Jacksonville Jaguars

Washington scored 28.2 points in Sunday's win over the Titans, catching seven of nine targets for a season-high 152 yards and a touchdown. Washington has now caught a touchdown in back-to-back games, with at least seven targets in each. Lawrence (26.78 points) threw for a season-high 352 yards with three touchdowns, and he added 27 rushing yards. Lawrence will be in my QB1 ranks for the Week 11 game versus the New York Giants, while Washington can be trusted as a WR2. -- Bowen

6. Nico Collins, WR, Houston Texans

His 24.4 points surely pleased his fantasy managers as they represented his second-best total in 2026 and his 16 targets were easily his season high. Collins' day had its rough spots, as he collided with a pair of Cleveland Browns defenders en route to C.J. Stroud's only interception late in the second quarter, he committed an unnecessary roughness penalty late in the fourth quarter and he failed to catch five of his final seven targets on the day in a game his Texans lost in the final minutes. Still, Collins' heavy usage continues to bolster his back-end fantasy WR1 value. -- Cockcroft

7. Tony Pollard, RB, Tennessee Titans

Tyjae Spears' healthy return and the opposing Jacksonville Jaguars' handy lead for much of the day suppressed Pollard's rushing workload, but the scorching-hot running back rewarded his fantasy managers nonetheless. Pollard's 23.9 fantasy points were buoyed by an 8-66-1 receiving line, easily his best in any of his three seasons with the Titans. He now has 89.1 points in his past three games ahead of what should be a high-scoring matchup with the Dallas Cowboys. Pollard is a fantasy RB1 for that game. -- Cockcroft

Ranking fantasy losers of Week 10

1. Jonathan Taylor, RB, Indianapolis Colts

Taylor had 12.3 points in the Week 10 win over the Dolphins, finishing with 103 total yards on 18 carries. He failed to score a touchdown for the third straight game, and he's averaging only 9.5 points per game over that stretch. After the Colts traded away Quenton Nelson and multiple wide receivers at the deadline, we are now watching a very limited offense, which will continue to impact Taylor's production. He'll drop down into my ranks for the Week 11 matchup versus Houston. -- Bowen

2. Green Bay Packers' passing game

Over the five weeks preceding this game, QB Jordan Love and WRs Matthew Golden and Christian Watson ranked among the top eight in fantasy points scored. In Week 10, they managed 10.8, 12.0 and 6.7 points, with Love's and Watson's numbers representing season lows. Chalk much of this up to the matchup against the tough-as-nails Minnesota Vikings defense, which sent a relentless pass rush (week-high 68% blitz and 82% pass rush win rates) leading to Love being sacked eight times. The bye comes at a good time for the Packers, who can lick their wounds following this disappointing showing and go right back to being solid fantasy options over the season's final seven weeks. -- Cockcroft

3. Carolina Panthers' passing game

Considering the matchup as the New Orleans Saints entered the week the eighth best for quarterbacks and the best for wide receivers (when schedule-adjusted), QB Bryce Young and WRs Tetairoa McMillan and Jalen Coker were huge disappointments. Young scored 8.1 fantasy points in his third poor performance in his past four, calling back into question his lineup utility as we head into "Bye-mageddon" Week 11. McMillan scored 6.5 and Coker 0.3 points on seven and four targets, as Young's funk has had a noticeably adverse impact upon their production. Consider Young a superflex/QB2 at best against the Baltimore Ravens, while McMillan should be a midrange WR2 and Coker more of a WR3/flex. -- Cockcroft

4. Tyler Shough, QB, and Chris Olave, WR, New Orleans Saints

Shough completed 71% of his throws in Sunday's loss to the Panthers but he finished with only 187 yards passing and a touchdown, plus he turned the ball over twice. Shough's 25 rush yards pushed his total to 9.98 points, but I expected more here versus Carolina. Olave had season lows across the board, scoring 6.0 points on three of four targets for 30 yards receiving. It was a rough day for the Saints' pass game. With the Bears up next, Shough will remain in the QB2 range while Olave will be a fringe WR1 in my rankings. -- Bowen

5. Rhamondre Stevenson, RB, New England Patriots

Cleared to play despite suffering what seemed like a serious knee injury in Week 9, Stevenson -- and the Patriots as a whole -- had a forgettable game in Germany on Sunday. Stevenson's 1.5 fantasy points were the third-fewest he has scored in any game in which he played at least 20 offensive snaps, though he was hardly needed (and didn't have a rushing attempt in the game's final 18 minutes) in what was a 35-10 blowout loss. He has a Week 11 bye and he'll be a matchups-oriented flex play against the Los Angeles Chargers in Week 12, though much of that depending upon Drake Maye's (ankle) status. -- Cockcroft

Drake Maye, QB, New England Patriots

What we know: Maye injured an ankle when he was tackled by Lions DE Aidan Hutchinson -- who, incidentally, was also lost for the day due to injury on the same play -- and was ruled out for the game midway through the second quarter.

What's next: The Patriots have their bye in Week 11, affording Maye additional time to rest but the swift decision to rule him out raises concerns that he might have a high ankle sprain, which usually requires multiple weeks of recovery. Tommy DeVito, who had a 54.3 passer rating and 7.14 fantasy points, will be the starter if Maye isn't ready for Week 12, but that would significantly set the offense back. No Patriots player scored as many as 10 fantasy points against the Lions in Munich. -- Cockcroft

A.J. Brown, WR, New England Patriots

What we know: Brown, who has been playing through a knee injury for much of the season, left Sunday's game in the fourth quarter with a hamstring issue. He didn't return and ended his day with 4.7 fantasy points.

What's next: Brown has now been limited to four games due to his various ailments, and he has yet to exceed 7.8 fantasy points (Week 7, @CHI) in any of them. He has the bye week's rest to potentially get healthy, but with all of his setbacks and Maye's uncertain status, Brown should be treated as a risk/reward fantasy WR3 going forward, until he proves otherwise. The Patriots could lean more on Kyle Williams and tight end Eli Raridon if Brown is forced to miss Week 12. -- Cockcroft

Woody Marks, RB, Houston Texans

What we know: Marks suffered a concussion in the third quarter versus the Browns, and he did not return. Before exiting, Marks had 65 total yards on 13 touches (7.5 points).

What's next: With Marks expected to enter the concussion protocol, we'll monitor updates on his condition throughout the week. David Montgomery (groin) hasn't played since Week 5, but he could return for the Week 11 matchup versus the Colts, where he will have RB2 value if Marks is out. -- Bowen

Geno Smith, QB, New York Jets

What we know: Smith left the game twice in Sunday's loss to the Bills. After taking a hit at the end of the first half, Smith was evaluated for a concussion but returned to the game midway through the third quarter. However, after putting together a drive that resulted in a field goal, Smith exited again with an ankle injury, and he did not return.

What's next: Rookie Cade Klubnik relieved Smith twice, completing 11 of 20 passes for 137 yards and an interception, plus he was sacked three times. Klubnik will be in line to start for the Week 11 matchup versus the Los Angeles Chargers if Smith can't go, which would lower fantasy expectations for the Jets' receiving options. -- Bowen
