Turkey imposes travel ban on former interior minister in probe into religious group

2026-09-02 13:20

A Turkish court on Tuesday barred former interior minister İdris Naim Şahin from leaving the country after prosecutors questioned him over allegations that a car linked to him was used to help a member of the Süleymanlılar religious community wanted by the police to flee abroad.

The car was owned by a company, but Şahin said he rented it for his use and had its license plate put in his name under a privilege available to former ministers.

Şahin, who served as interior minister from 2011 to 2013 under then-prime minister Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, was released under judicial supervision after some three-and-a-half hours of questioning on suspicion of aiding a criminal organization and harboring an offender.

His link to the case emerged after police on August 21 caught Hilmi Tuna Kuriş in the car in the Marmaris district of Muğla.

Kuriş, the brother of jailed Süleymanlılar leader Alihan Kuriş, was allegedly preparing to leave Turkey when police stopped the vehicle.

The Süleymanlılar are a Sunni Muslim community rooted in the teachings of Turkish cleric Süleyman Hilmi Tunahan and centered around a network of Quran courses and student dormitories.

Şahin said he rented the car on December 25, 2025, under a contract listing an annual price of 1,000 lira ($21).

He said he paid only for the days he used the car and sent the money in cash through a former aide, with his lawyer describing the price in the contract as symbolic.

Şahin said he last drove the car in June and did not know who used it afterward, adding that he filed a criminal complaint and had his name removed from the registrations of cars allegedly used without his permission.

Turkish courts have so far jailed 81 people pending trial and released 10 under judicial supervision in the investigation.
