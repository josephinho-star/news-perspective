EPA reports 'zero detection of airborne lead' day after Iowa lead azide detonation

2026-11-26 19:20

The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) said Thursday that its air monitors continue to report "zero detection of airborne lead throughout the region" after the detonation of a stockpile of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant.

The statement comes as the EPA said it collected more than 1,000 samples following Wednesday's controlled detonation of the bomb-making chemical in Middletown, Iowa. Nearly 1,600 air, water, soil and dust samples were taken beforehand to establish a baseline for measuring any potential environmental changes.

Around five tons of degraded lead azide were destroyed after being deemed unsafe and unstable, and there were concerns that a plume of potentially toxic dust could drift towards Missouri in the wake of the blast.

"Following the controlled detonation, EPA sampled homes, public parks with playgrounds, and every drinking water intake in the pre-detonation plume model. Today, EPA personnel and contractors will be specifically sampling schools, playgrounds, daycares, churches, and more in the region," the EPA said Thursday.

"Partnering with the U.S. Department of Agriculture, EPA has collected samples from nearby farms both before and after the controlled detonation," it also said. "Extensive sampling of water in the Mississippi River watershed has been completed both before and after the controlled detonation."

The EPA added that "air cassettes that sample for lead will be replaced to continue sampling today," and that "soil and dust sampling is expected to conclude today."

"The Trump EPA will continue its presence on the ground for as long as needed to ensure the proper environmental monitoring is taking place following the successful controlled detonation," it concluded.

EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin said late Wednesday that the controlled detonation "exceeded expectations in a big way."
