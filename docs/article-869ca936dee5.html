Germany, US sign 'letter of intent' on close defence cooperation, despite previous clashes

Following months during which the German government has sought to convince the public that militarily (and otherwise) Germany must stop relying on its erstwhile trustworthy partner, the United States, due to the erratic, rude and unruly behaviour of its president, the defence ministers of both countries signed a “letter of intent” this week, signaling close cooperation after all.

Germany’s defence minister, Boris Pistorius, visibly surprised at the warm reception he received in Washington earlier this week, said after his first meeting with US war minister Pete Hegseth he had only heard warm words of praise for Berlin over its contribution to joint defence goals.

This was in sharp contrast to the bitter tirade Hegseth had delivered to European defence ministers just weeks ago, accusing them of abandoning the US in the Iran war, or to the message disseminated by president Trump earlier this year when he announced the withdrawal of thousands of US troops who are stationed in Germany, in what was interpreted as a clear snub to Berlin.

At a time of dwindling military stocks – the US looking at its war against Iran, Germany, focused mainly on helping to defend Ukraine - the counterparts’ intention, so the letter states, is for “enhanced bilateral cooperation in the defence sector to support transatlantic industrial production and maintenance capacities” and although using slightly wishy-washy terminology, it suggests quite a political U-turn.

From accepting that the Trump administration had mentally withdrawn itself from Nato, and Berlin should become more independent from Washington as a result, the government of Friedrich Merz has now signed up to the statement “Germany and the United States agree that the current political challenges require a robust and mutually beneficial, closely integrated defence industry base on both sides of the Atlantic,” as the declaration reads.

The powerful industries of both countries “can and should” complement each other more closely in the future in the production of “state of the art” weapons systems, components and complex munitions.

Pistorius has sought to play down accusations that this is a contradiction, painting it instead as a pragmatic and direct reaction to current tensions. (What we sometimes refer to as Realpolitik.)

Today he will take delivery of F-35 fighter jets manufactured for Germany’s armed forces in Texas, before heading home tomorrow.

Romanian EU lawmaker Siegfried Muresan proposed as Romania's next prime minister

Sweden is not the only European country working to change its prime minister.

Following a day of consultations with political leaders, Romania’s president, Nicușor Dan, has nominated EU lawmaker Siegfried Mureșan to become the next prime minister.

Romania has had a caretaker government since early May, when prime minister Ilie Bolojan lost a vote of confidence in parliament.

“Romania needs a government,” Dan said, adding that “people’s lives depend on it.”

However, he acknowledged that none of the prospective candidates can currently command a clear majority in parliament, as political parties remain cannot agree on the way forward.

Dan said he had seen “a great deal of concern amongst the parties about the elections and far less concern for national interest.”

He added that Mureșan, 44, had the necessary experience to take on the role and attempt to break the political deadlock.

The MEP has been a member of the European parliament since 2014. Born to German and Romanian parents, he is also a vice-president of the centre-right European People’s Party, the European parliament’s largest grouping.

Elsewhere, the EU and China are continuing efforts to avert a trade war after an hour long call between EU trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič and his Chinese counterpart Wang Wengtao.

The EU reiterated the need to move from “rhetoric to results” with a “credible” solution that will curb China’s growing €1bn a day surplus in trade with the bloc.

Extensive work mapping the potential safeguards including quotas and price floors and possible retaliatory measures by the Chines have already been carried out in advance of Sefcovic’s planned meeting with Wang on 8 October.

A spokesperson said the call lasted more than an hour and took stock of the work carried out by officials so far covering the management of Chinese exports to the EU, the EU’s access to the Chinese market and export controls on rare earths.

Restrictions on exports of rare earths were imposed by Beijing in April 2025 but were suspended for a year last November after Donald Trump’s meeting with Xi Xingping in Korea last October.

Experts expect that suspension to continue after the Trump Xi summit next week in Washington but the EU will be pushing for a crave out for the bloc.

“While genuine engagement remains a priority, it is equally important that first concrete outcomes are delivered at the second session of the Trade and Investment Council in Beijing in Cotober which the commissioner will co-chair – a signal that we are moving from rhetoric to results,” said the EU’s deputy chief spokesperson Olof Gill.

Centre party leader thanks outgoing PM, says will be 'a constructive force' to keep government 'rooted in the centre'

Meanwhile, Centre party leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist also thanked the outgoing prime minister Ulf Kristersson praising his “efforts and leadership during the past term.”

“He has led Sweden through a tumultuous time with a changed security situation and Sweden’s historic entry into Nato. Being prime minister is Sweden’s most important role, and it deserves respect regardless of political divides,” she said.

Speaking about what’s next, she added:

“Now Sweden is entering a new political phase. The Centre party will be a constructive force to ensure Sweden gets a functioning government with a policy rooted in the centre.”

What that will actually mean in practice, we will have to wait and see.

SVT reported that the leaders of the four opposition parties are planning first meetings already.

The current parliament speaker Andreas Norlén is also hoping to individually speak with party leaders tomorrow, SVT said, getting ahead of the formal schedule (14:10).

Right-leaning bloc 'ready to continue governing Sweden if they fail,' liberal leader says

Liberal party leader Simona Mohamsson praised Kristersson’s time in government, saying he led “Sweden’s most liberal government ever,” and hailing what she sees as its successes on education and crime.

Her Liberal party, part of the outgoing government led by Ulf Kristersson, cleared the 4% threshold into the new parliament with 5.3% of the vote last Sunday.

Mohamsson thanked Kristersson for “four important years,” and giving Sweden “a fresh start when it needed it the most.”

“Now it is up to Magdalena Andersson to show whether she can put together any government foundation from the opposition’s mixed bag of left socialists, cultural relativists, and Centre Party members. My teammates to the right and I stand ready to continue governing Sweden if they fail,” she said, echoing the sentiment expressed by Kristersson earlier (13:57).

Christian Democrat leader Ebba Busch also posted a message in a similar tone, conceding the result, but praising the outgoing government’s track record.

“At present, there is no one who commands a majority in the parliament. The red-greens have not automatically won the government power. Various parties’ red lines before election day can lead to a complicated government formation,” she said.

Similar to Kristersson earlier, she also hinted at possible alternative majorities formed along the economic policy lines.

Sweden's election winner Andersson vows to 'move Sweden forward'

Meanwhile, the leader of the left-wing bloc that won Sweden’s election, Magdalena Andersson, vowed Thursday to lead the country in a “new direction” after four years of right-wing rule backed by the far right.

“The Swedish people have voted for a new direction for our country. Voted for unity and against division. The Swedish people have voted to move Sweden forward, and I want to realise the new direction that you have voted for,” she said in a speech posted on social media.

In the address, recorded in a prime ministerial aesthetics and with a Swedish flag in the background, she said she wanted to lead Sweden “away from conflicts from the sake of conflicts, and in a direction based on unity” between key parties as political talks about the new government are set to begin.

“It is up to all political parties to put the party politics aside and put Sweden and the Swedish people first,” she said.

She also talked about priorities on climate, schools, health care, and social care as examples of areas where she wanted to change the government’s policy.

Kristersson's language suggests he still hopes left-leaning bloc will fail to form government - snap analysis

It’s worth noting that the language used by Kristersson in his resignation letter – particularly a reference to irreconcilable differences in the various parties’ stated positions (13:57) – is not accidental.

The left-leaning bloc – led by former prime minister Magdalena Andersson and her Social Democrats - consists of four parties, and there are significant friction points between them, particularly between the Left Party and the Centre Party.

The Centre Party had previously suggested it would not accept ministers from the Left; the Left, particularly now emboldened by its strong election result, is unlikely to agree to no top jobs in a government it is expected to support.

If either party drops out of the arrangement, the bloc will struggle to form a government, as there are no viable alternatives.

But their voters generally expect them to work together.

And a leading figure from the Centre Party implied on Monday (Europe Live, Monday) that they could probably find a way of working with the Left, confirming the party would take part in exploratory talks led by the Social Democrats.

Still, the discussions are likely to be tricky and could take weeks before they agree on a path forward.

Outgoing PM Kristersson is clearly hoping those discussions fail.

His supporters repeatedly pointed out before the election that you could often see the four leaders of the four right-leaning parties in his bloc together – in fact, they also had a dinner together Monday night – and they marketed themselves a “stable” arrangement that was sort of ready to go.

But same didn’t really apply for the red bloc amid some apparent internal divisions between the leaders, leaving lots of questions mark to solve only now.

Kristersson appears to be keen to exploit some of these differences.

On election night, he suggested forming an alternative coalition against socialist economic policies – widely interpreted as an attempt to woo the Centre Party back to his side.

Even then, huge questions remain over how he could secure a majority without the far-right Sweden Democrats, an option that remains unacceptable to centrists.

Expect these talks to pick up speed now that we have the official results – and a looming vacancy at the top.

Swedish PM Kristersson resigns after election defeat

And in the last few minute, Sweden’s prime minister Ulf Kristersson has resigned from the post after official results confirmed that his right-leaning bloc lost last Sunday’s parliamentary election.

The four left-leaning parties, with former prime minister Magdalena Andersson’s Social Democrats as the largest party, won by just 50,359 votes, the final count showed.

The four left-leaning bloc will have 176 seats in the new parliament to 173 for Kristersson’s three-party coalition government and the far-right Sweden Democrats who were expected to join the administration after the vote, the Tidö bloc.

In a letter posted on X, Kristersson said that following the tight election results “may have complicated the conditions for the new government to be formed” with contradicting statements from different parties on their positions.

“Constructive openness from the parties in parliament will now be needed for a prime minister to be accepted, and for a new government to be able to receive support for its state budget.”

“Discussions will need to be held with all parties to find ways around these deadlocks and to create the conditions for a stable and effective government.

“It is the speaker who will lead the next steps towards forming a new government. In order for that work to begin immediately, I hereby request to be relieved of my duties as prime minister.”
