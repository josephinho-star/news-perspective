TPUSA mourns Florida high school chapter president killed with family in devastating plane crash

2026-11-27 01:42

Turning Point USA's student program mourned the death of a Florida high school chapter president this week after the teen and five members of his family died Tuesday in a plane crash off the Mississippi coast.

The organization said it was "heartbroken" over the loss of Hunter Stokes, 16, who led the Fort Myers High School Club America chapter.

"Hunter was a young patriot who loved his country and led by example," TPUSA Students said in a Wednesday post on X.

"From attending our Chapter Leadership Summit this summer to helping lead his chapter’s 9/11 Flag Run in honor of the victims of the September 11 attacks, Hunter was dedicated to making a difference in his community," the statement continued. "We are grateful to have had Hunter as part of our TPUSA family. Please join us in lifting his family, friends, and fellow chapter members up in prayer."

Hunter was killed in the crash alongside his father, Brent Stokes, 50; his aunt and uncle, Arlyn and Michael Knox, both 43; and his cousins, Charles Holden Knox, 12, and Truett Knox, 10, according to Jackson County Coroner Bruce Lynd Jr.

The group was returning to Florida after a family fishing trip.

"They died doing what they loved: celebrating the start of a wonderful holiday season with a fishing trip together, before heading home to be with the rest of our family," both families said in a joint statement to The News-Press.

The six were aboard a Cessna 210 piloted by Brent Stokes when the aircraft went down two miles off Horn Island, Mississippi, according to the Federal Aviation Administration.

Officials said the plane entered a descent of 10,000 feet per minute prior to the crash.

Air traffic controllers advised Stokes of precipitation and adverse weather shortly before losing contact with the aircraft at about 3:45 p.m. local time, the New York Post and the FAA reported.

Brent Stokes was the president and CEO of Stokes Marine Inc., while Arlyn and Michael Knox led Knox Technologies, an IT company based in Fort Myers.

St. Cecilia Catholic Community on Wednesday said Brent and Hunter Stokes were former parishioners.

"Please keep the Stokes and Knox families in your prayers during this time of loss," the community wrote in a Facebook post. "May the Lord grant eternal rest and the promise of the Resurrection. Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them. May they rest in peace. Amen."

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
