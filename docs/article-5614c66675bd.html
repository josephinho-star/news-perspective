Camden Green councillor says he will fight Polanski to run in Starmer’s seat – UK politics live

2026-09-07 14:51

Polanski faces challenge to be Greens' candidate for Holborn and St Pancras, as most Greens on local council back rival

Peter Walker is a senior Guardian political correspondent.

Zack Polanski is facing unexpected internal opposition in his bid to fight the Holborn and St Pancras byelection for the Greens, with a local councillor saying he is also seeking the nomination and that the seat needs a local MP, not “a politician you’ll only see on a leaflet”.

Hamza Chowdhury, a Green councillor on Camden council in north London, said in a post that he had received nomination from six other Greens, meaning seven of the 10 Greens on the council oppose Polanski being selected.

Among those backing him is Lorna Russell, the group leader in Camden for the Greens, who are the official opposition to Labour.

Polanski, the leader of the Greens in England and Wales, announced on Thursday that he would seek the nomination to fight the seat vacated by Keir Starmer, setting up a high-stakes byelection against Labour.

Local party members will vote later this week to select a candidate, and the Guardian has been told that they are broadly supportive of Polanski standing.

But such high-profile opposition is notable, especially given Chowdhury argued that Polanski, who currently lives in east London, would be unable to properly represent the area if he won.

I know Camden because I live it every day. I know the challenges facing our communities, our estates, our broken lifts, our unpicked bins, our unclean roads and endless housing register waiting list because these are the places I have grown up and continue to call home.

We don’t need another MP who arrives during an election campaign, puts out a few leaflets, makes a few promises and disappears once the votes are counted. We need an MP who is present, accessible and visibly part of the community all year round.

I am asking Green party members for the opportunity to represent your home truly and properly. I’m not asking you to vote for a politician you’ll only see on a leaflet. I’m asking you to choose someone who is already here.

In the Commons, Angela Rayner, the communities secretary, has just given a statement about her plans to cancel some local government reorganisation plans, and to delay others. (See 2.20pm.) Lindsay Hoyle, the speaker, said it was relief to have an announcement that had not been briefed to the media in advance.

On the subject of Liverpool, the Department for Transport has confirmed that Liverpool city region is getting £86m to bring its bus services under public control. In a news release, the DfT says:

Millions of passengers across the Liverpool city region are set to benefit from better, more reliable bus services as the city becomes the first to bring its network under public control under this government.

In a huge boost for local growth, the government will provide £86.3m of funding until 2029 to deliver better services for passengers, as well as a share of a new one-off £3m pot to help mayors take control of their services.

This funding will give the combined authority the power to plan bus networks to run services where they are needed most. Like in London and Greater Manchester where bus services are franchised, this also means local leaders in Liverpool will have more say over routes, branding and fares, which could mean cheaper journeys across the city.

No 10 refuses to deny report saying the Sun won't be getting any favours from Burnham while he's PM

Downing Street had refused to deny an Observer story saying that Andy Burnham intends to ensure that the Sun newspaper does not get any special editorial favours from him as PM.

That suggests Rupert Murdoch’s favourite tabloid won’t be getting exclusive interviews, or special articles for publication carrying Burnham’s byline.

The Observer report was written by Tom Baldwin, a former Labour communications director, who explained:

Although neither Burnham nor his official spokespeople have said anything explicit since he entered No 10 about ending cooperation with the newspaper, three separate sources close to him have privately said he intends to maintain a stance of granting the tabloid “no special favours”.

His position is understood to be causing “consternation” within the headquarters of the newspaper’s owner, Rupert Murdoch’s News UK, where executives have long since been used to the Sun getting access and securing influence at the very highest level of government.

Burnham’s decision is understood to relate to the Hillsborough tragedy. He grew up near Liverpool and had friends who were at the Hillsborough match, and campaigning for justice for the families of victims became one of the big causes of his political career. As a young culture secretary, Burnham gave a speech at the Hillsborough 20th anniversary memorial in 2009 which was instrumental in persuading him that the government had to redouble its efforts to ensure the full truth came out.

No paper did more to smear the Hillsborough fans than the Sun, which falsely claimed that their behaviour was to blame for the crush that led to 97 people being killed. Although the paper has apologised, and all those involved in that reporting have long since moved on, the paper is still reviled on Merseyside.

Asked about the Observer story at the Downing Street lobby briefing, the PM’s spokesperson did not deny the claim that the Sun would not be getting special favours from the new PM. Asked repeatedly about the story, the spokesperson just said that Burnham would be engaging “with a wide range of media, including new forms of media”, as he has been doing already.

Lucy Powell, the deputy Labour leader, has issued this statement following the moving of the writ for the Holborn and St Pancras byelection. (See 2.41pm.)

Labour has hit the ground running in Holborn and St Pancras. We’ve been out every day speaking to local residents about their priorities and it’s clear that Andy Burnham’s message of hope is really cutting through. Since this byelection was announced we’ve had thousands of conversations on the doorstep right across the constituency and Labour activists and supporters will be speaking to thousands more in the coming days.

While others talk, Labour is delivering the progressive change people in Holborn and St Pancras want to see. We’ve taken early action to give people vital breathing space on the cost of living, and we’re taking on the long term challenges people really care about, like ending rough sleeping, tackling youth unemployment and making sure we finally fix our social care system.

Rayner cancels some local government reorganisation plans for England, and delays others, after Burnham requested rethink

Decisions on flagship local government reorganisation have been withdrawn as the government launches a review of the plans following new legal advice, the Press Association reports. PA says:

The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government (MHCLG) said the review is to ensure the process is robust and aligns with the new government’s aim to “rewire the state”, and follows “updated and privileged legal advice”.

At least eight councils have launched legal challenges or were considering to do so due to opposition to the government’s choice of footprints for new councils.

Reform UK-led Kent county council is set to consider a judicial review at a meeting on Tuesday after it did not receive a response from the government to a letter setting out concerns.

Plans for reorganisation in Essex, Hampshire, Norfolk and Suffolk have been withdrawn in the light of new legal advice.

Decisions on 14 further areas will be reviewed and paused alongside the cases of Cambridgeshire and Peterborough, and West Sussex, which are yet to have a decision confirmed.

The government will also confirm that local elections in May 2027 will go ahead on existing council boundaries, saying it remains committed to effective and sustainable local government and simplifying these structures.

Communities secretary Angela Rayner is expected to tell parliament on later: “As we are now more determined than ever to devolve power closer to the people we represent, it’s only right that as a new secretary of state I make sure that everything we do is working towards that plan to change Britain, without delay.

“The prime minister told the House last week he was ‘prepared to look at local government reorganisation’ and he asked me as his communities secretary to ‘do that’.

“In that context, in light of legal advice, I want to satisfy myself firstly that the right process is in place, that it is robust, and of course complies with the law.

“And, secondly, I want to fully test whether our proposals for local government reorganisation meet the priorities of a new administration and a new prime minister, and any additional considerations.

“I do not take this lightly. I recognise the huge amount of work that has already gone into progressing reorganisation in each of these areas and I am very grateful for it.”

MHCLG said reorganisation in Surrey will not be affected by the review and the two new unitary authorities, East and West Surrey, will go live in April 2027.

MP calls for circumcisions by non-health professionals to be banned, as Commons debates amendments to health bill

Chris Osuh is a Guardian community affairs correspondent.

A proposal to ban anyone who is not a healthcare professional from performing circumcisions has been launched in parliament after the deaths of baby boys in the UK.

Sam Carling, Labour MP for North West Cambridgeshire, has tabled an amendment to the government’s health bill.

At present, anyone can legally circumcise in the UK. The amendment calls for non-therapeutic male circumcision to become a “regulated activity,” and follows coroner’s inquests and criminal prosecutions drawing attention to the risk of unregulated circumcisers.

Circumcision is routinely performed for religious and cultural reasons in some Black, Jewish and Muslim communities in Britain. Community organisations have been warning of the risks of the lack of regulation.

In a prevention of future deaths report published in January, the government was urged by a coroner to take action to avoid similar tragedies to the death of Mohamed Abdisamad, a baby who died of a streptococcus infection in February 2023, a week after being circumcised.

The need to strengthen the regulation of non-therapeutic male circumcision could not be clearer. The tragic death of Mohamed Abdisamad in 2023, and other tragic deaths and serious injuries in recent years, have shown that the legal framework in this space is simply not good enough.

My amendment would enact the recommendations of the coroner in Mohamed’s case, who was clear that the status quo, in which anyone can perform a circumcision with no requirements on infection control measures or aftercare, would lead to future deaths.

Carling’s amendment would require circumcision to come under the inspection regime of the Care Quality Commission, and for integrated care boards to produce annual data on prevalence and complications of non- therapeutic male circumcision.

MPs will be debating report stage amendments to the health bill this evening, and it will get its third reading tomorrow.

Yvette Cooper, the new health secretary, is tabling a series of government amendments to the bill intended to strengthen local accountability and increase support for maternity services. Explaining the changes, a government source said:

Mandatory local government representation will be restored on the integrated care boards which manage health services around the country, reflecting Andy Burnham’s devolution agenda.

And a new role of maternity and neonatal commissioner will be created by the bill, reflecting Cooper’s personal prioritisation of maternity services and developed alongside the government’s maternity advisor Michelle Welsh (MP for Sherwood Forest).

No 10 hits back at US ambassador to Israel, Mike Huckabee, after he accuses UK government of 'Jew hate'

At the weekend Mike Huckabee, the US ambassador to Israel, posted this on social media about the UK.

The Brits have lost it. The Jew hate of their government knows no boundaries and knows no facts

Huckabee was commenting on a tweet from the Israeli government criticising Ed Miliband, the foreign secretary, for comments about Israel’s record in Gaza.

Asked about Huckabee’s comment at the Downing Street lobby briefing, the PM’s spokesperson said:

We completely reject that assertion. We’re committed to protecting Jewish communities in the UK and continue to engage with community leaders and groups.

The government is investing almost £380m over three years, to protect Jewish communities, crack down on antisemitic extremism and hostile state activity, and tackle antisemitism in schools, universities and local communities.

The spokesperson would not say if the government had formally complained to the US authorities about Huckabee’s remark.

Jaguar Land Rover has announced thousands of job cuts in a “body blow for workers” as it grapples with tough trading conditions, Donald Trump’s tariff wars and the fallout from a cyber-attack. Rob Davies and Alex Daniel have the story.

Asked about this at the No 10 lobby briefing, the PM’s spokesperson said:

We understand that this will be an uncertain and concerning time for affected workers, their families and the wider communities.

And we know that current market conditions are challenging for the automotive sector globally. That’s why we’ve taken significant action to back the UK’s automotive industry by lowering electricity bills for manufacturers, providing £4bn of capital R and D funding to manufacture zero-emission vehicles and launching a £2bn electric car grant to encourage people to buy Evs.

The business secretary [Jonathan Reynolds] is in close contact with JLR and he will be meeting them early this week.

The spokesperson also confirmed that Reynolds has been “clear on his ruling out of any form of bailout”.
