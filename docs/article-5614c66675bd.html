Being ‘true to Labour’s values’ means controlling spending, says Healey – UK politics live

2026-09-07 08:58

Healey says there's 'nothing progressive' about paying debt interest, and being 'true to our values' means controlling spending

Healey stresses the government’s commitment to fiscal discipline.

On my first day in the Treasury, I said that fiscal discipline was my first priority of chancellor. It underwrites every promise this government makes.

And the prime minister and I are in lockstep in our commitment to meeting the fiscal rules at the upcoming budget, to balancing the books with a buffer to protect against uncertainty, to controlling borrowing to bear down on inflation, and reducing long term pressures on our public finances.

And this isn’t about lines on a graph or numbers on a spreadsheet. It’s a matter of values.

And there’s nothing progressive about the government spending £1 in every ten on debt interest.

You know, if debt interest were a government department, it would be the second biggest in Whitehall after health, bigger than defence, the Home Office and justice put together.

That’s why staying true to our values means being honest about the need to control government spending.

Healey says he wants to 'draw the line' under ever-rising costs for business

Healey says he wants to take decisions that improve business confidence.

Let me be clear, I recognise that the costs of business, its energy bills, regulation, burdens, planning constraints, labour costs, have grown since Covid and I want to draw the line.

And whether it is the cost of business or the cost of living, the only way we deal with these in the long term is growth.

I want to see this country as a country of wealth creation. I want our government decisions and policies to raise the levels of confidence and investment and profit in British businesses.

Healey says making 'Great Britain growth Britain again' is 'central' to his mission as chancellor

Healey says he wants to make ‘Great Britain growth Britain again”.

Fiscal credibility is indivisible from growth.

It was the heart of those G20 discussions just the other day in North Carolina, because growth is the sustainable pathway out of indebtedness into prosperity.

And while Britain’s growth trajectory has been weak, this now must change. This is essential. It is possible. It is central to my purpose as chancellor.

Ask an economist what growth is and you’ll get a number. But ask the country and you’ll get a different answer. A young person getting a new job in a company that’s on the up. A sole trader winning a new contract that pays for a holiday with their kids. A business winning a new export order, giving the community around it a new lease of life.

And I dedicate myself to this mission as chancellor to make Great Britain growth Britain again, with more investment, more innovation and more jobs.

Healey starts by stressing the government’s commitment to growth. And he strikes a positive tone, stressing “our belief in Britain’s future”.

Because the UK is home to nearly half the most innovative companies in Europe. We’re world leaders in life sciences, in defence, technology and creative industries, financial services, our science and research base is world beating, our capital markets are some of the largest and most vibrant anywhere in the world, and the UK stock market is hitting all-time highs.

He says he is building on the work done by Keir Starmer. and Rachel Reeves restoring Britain’s fiscal discipline.

He blames Liz Truss for setting that back.

UK debt costs were historically around the G7 average until Liz Truss’s mini budget crashed the economy and collapsed confidence in Britain’s fiscal strength.

So since 2022, we have been paying that trust penalty as we battle to re-establish belief in Britain and the UK’s fiscal sustainability.

The Labour government is now telling “a new story” about Britain, he says. (See 9.29am.)

John Healey is just starting his speech. He is in the West Midlands, and he says he is pleased Richard Parker, the Labour West Midlands mayor, is in the audience. Jonathan Reynolds, the business secretary, is there too, along with Healey’s fellow Treasury ministers.

Healey says he wants growth story 'written in more places'

John Healey is about to deliver his speech on the economy. Here are extracts that were released overnight by the Treasury.

On the UK turning the corner

A new story about Britain. An optimistic story. One of resilience. Pride. Huge latent potential. A country turning a corner. A country whose people, businesses and communities are ready to seize the opportunity of new technologies and new ideas.

On what makes good growth

Ask an economist what growth is and you’ll get a number. But ask the country and you’ll get a different answer. A young person getting a new job in a company that’s on the up. A sole trader winning a new contract that pays for a holiday with their kids. A business winning a new export order, giving the community around it a new lease of life.

The prime minister laid out a clear diagnosis of what has gone wrong in the past. The solution is a fundamental shift that starts with putting power in the right places.

The next chapter of Britain’s growth story will be written in more places.

On the importance of wealth creation

Instead of getting in the way with complexity and red tape, we need a strong, strategic centre wired to enable local leaders and their ambitions.

That’s why we created No 10 North, working on a dual mission with the Treasury, removing obstacles so that local leaders and businesses can pull together to drive growth and productivity.

I want to see wealth creation in this country. I want to see businesses make a profit. And to create the conditions for that we need an active, accountable state at all levels to remove blockages and create the conditions for more investment.

Minister condemns ‘thuggish’ far-right, anti-migrant protesters in Portsmouth

Good morning. John Healey is giving his first major speech on economic policy since he became chancellor seven weeks ago. It is meant to be the main government news of the morning and, as Pippa Crerar writes in her preview story, Healey will argue the government’s plans to boost growth across the whole country will enable the British economy to “turn a corner”.

Healey’s deputy, Emma Reynolds, the chief secretary to the Treasury, has been giving interviews as part of the build-up.

But, as well as taking questions about growth and tax, Reynolds has also found herself taking questions about the latest far-right protest about immigration, triggered by a large boat carrying migrants arriving for the first time in Portsmouth.

Commenting on the behaviour of the protesters, who blocked roads and some of whom were masked, Reynolds said:

This is thuggish and intimidating behaviour. This is not peaceful protest.

Obviously, as a politician, I absolutely support people’s right to peaceful protest. But this crosses a line and I just want to take the opportunity to thank the police and other emergency services who have brought this under control.

We do not want to see this kind of behaviour anywhere, frankly.
