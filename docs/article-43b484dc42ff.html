Asia-Pacific markets rally as Huang’s return to Nvidia reignites AI trade, despite US political backlash – business live

2026-11-24 07:28

Introduction: Asia-Pacific markets rally on Huang's return, despite backlash

Good morning, and welcome to our rolling coverage of business, the financial markets and the world economy.

JensenHuang’s surprising, and controversial, return to run Nvidia has sparked a strong rally in Asia-Pacific stock markets today,

Stocks have surged in Taiwan, South Korea, Japan and Australia, as the AI trade lurches back into life.

The Taiwanese TW50 index has surged by 9.5%, away from the seven-month high touched yesterday. In Seoul, the Kospi index has jumped by 6.5%, led by chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics.

Tokyo’s Nikkei gained almost 4%, and Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 has risen by 1.8%.

That followed a surge on Wall Street last night, where Nvidia’s shares rocketed by 31.5% – adding around $950bn to the company’s value as Huang returned to the company’s HQ.

Huang’s return “removes an important source of uncertainty,” said CharuChanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo in Singapore, adding:

“His return restores strategic continuity at Nvidia and reduces the immediate risk of disruptions to its supply chain and technology roadmap.”

But while Wall Street recorded its best day since April 2025 (when Donald Trump paused his Liberation Day tariffs), doubts over the high valuations of AI companies, and questions over whether the industry will generate revenues to sustain their high spending on data centres, remain.

As does the political backlash against Huang’s return, and the role played by Donald Trump’s White House in pressing Nvidia to take its former CEO back.

Democrat senator ElizabethWarren has called Huang’s restoration “reckless,” and warned that the banking committee she will head next year will be digging into whether Nvidia has complied with export controls.

She also said the Trump administration “needs to explain to Congress what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials in the United States government to support his reinstatement.”

Republican lawmakers also roundly criticized the Nvidia board’s decision to reinstate its chief executive, Jensen Huang, even after Donald Trump said he spoke “directly and indirectly” with board members and “convinced them” to bring him back.

Pete Ricketts, a US senator from Nebraska, wrote on X that Huang’s “behavior does not support reinstatement”, a reminder of his alleged affair with a Chinese spy.

The surge in stock markets since JensenHuang’s return to Nvidia was announced shows that investors are “willing to re-engage with the AI theme when uncertainty recedes”, says ChristianStocker, lead equity strategist for UniCreditMunich.

But… Stocker argues that the rally reflected relief that the AI supply chain and investment cycle are unlikely to be abruptly disrupted, rather than renewed conviction that all concerns have disappeared.

He told clients this morning:

Questions about financing structures, profitability and the ultimate returns on AI spending remain largely unresolved.

In other words, the debate has shifted. Investors are no longer asking whether AI investment will continue. They are increasingly asking whether the path from investment to monetisation will be slower, more uneven and more dependent on favourable financing conditions than previously assumed. Recent market volatility suggests that the AI narrative remains intact, but the market is becoming less willing to assume a straight-line path to future earnings growth.

Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia’s CEO could, perhaps surprisingly, be painful for US borrowers.

That’s because America’s central bank is more likely to raise interest rates if they’re not facing a bursting AI bubble.

JimReid of DeutscheBank explains:

With fears of an AI slowdown receding, it also led markets to price back in a more hawkish path for the Federal Reserve over the months ahead.

So for the next meeting in December, markets are once again fully pricing in a 25bp hike, with 26.1bps priced in for that decision (+3.1bps on the day). Then looking further out, 70bps of hikes are now priced in by the September 2027 meeting, up +6.6bps relative to Friday

US adds Megaspeed, AI firm at center of Nvidia scandal, to trade blacklist

In another significant development in the Nvidia story, Megaspeed International has been placed on a US trade blacklist that bars it from buying advanced AI chips.

In a filing on Monday, the Commerce Department moved to formally add the company to its entity list. This means Megaspeed will now need approval from the commerce department to obtain US exports.

This move revelations that Jensen Huang had a relationship with Megaspeed’s Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman alleged to be a spy for Beijing – a claim China has said is “groundless”.

Jensen Huang made his return to Nvidia’s headquarters with a hero’s welcome yesterday.

Hours earlier he’d been reinstated as the CEO of the world’s most valuable chip company, following a whiplash series of events that involved a romantic affair with a suspected Chinese spy, a drastic plummet in the stock market, and an intervention from Donald Trump.

As Huang entered the company’s Silicon Valley office, wearing his signature dark-colored uniform, crowds of employees rushed to greet him clapping, hollering and chanting: “Jensen, Jensen, Jensen!”

“I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around,” Huang told employees, according to several verified videos posted to social media. The crowd went wild – it was Huang’s second coming.
