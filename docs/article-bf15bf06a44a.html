Iran rejects French claim it was behind supermarket fridge cyber-attack

Iran has responded to the accusation by France that it was responsible for the recent cyber-attacks on supermarket fridges in the European country, declaring them “baseless”.

AFP reports that in a statement, Tehran said “Iran’s foreign ministry categorically rejects the French government’s baseless and one-sided accusations against Iran regarding alleged cyber activities in France”, adding that it “strongly condemns this hackneyed policy of levelling accusations against the Islamic republic”.

The EU has said it stands ready “to come with a swift and united response” after France blamed Iran for a spate of cyber-attacks on supermarket freezers and chillers that caused millions of euros of damage.

Speaking on Wednesday, a European Commission spokesperson acknowledged the French attribution, without endorsing it. The spokesperson said:

We acknowledge that France has attributed these (cyber-attacks) to Iran. Pending discussions with member states, we as (the) European Union, we stand ready to come with a swift and united response.

We as the EU, we have consistently called on all states to refrain from malicious behaviour in the cyberspace. And we will continue to address such malicious behavior, making use of all the tools that we have also under the EU cyber diplomacy toolbox, including sanctions.

In a major decision earlier this year, the EU designated Iran’s IRGC a terrorist organisation, ending years of division over the issue. The designation became possible after France dropped its long-held opposition, having previously feared it would damage diplomatic channels and the interests of French nationals in Iran.

A spokesperson for Belgium’s foreign ministry said they took the information provided by France “very seriously”. An investigation into incidents that took place in early November in Belgium was still ongoing, the spokesperson said, adding: “we have no evidence that would allow us to attribute these incidents to Iran or any other actor, nor can we establish a link with the earlier attacks in France”.

Belgium has “repeatedly condemned Iranian cyber-attacks and other hostile activities” and was open to examining the French proposal for sanctions, “based on the available evidence,” the person added.

Portugal put on high alert as it braces for the impacts of a new storm

In other news, Portugal will shut schools and non-esesential public services on Wednesday, with Storm Charles expected to bring torrential rain and heavy winds to parts of the country.

“All those who can should stay at home,” the country’s interior minister Luis Neves told a press conference on Tuesday, warning people to stay away from coastal areas and to work from home where possible.

“It’s a tough, difficult time, and the country will be put to the test, especially tomorrow, given what has been presented as the scenario,”he was quoted by the Portugese news outlet Diario de Noticias as having told journalists. He said a state of alert will be in effect from between 00:00 and 23:59 on Wednesday.

Eight of the country’s districts, including the capital Lisbon, are on the highest red alert for extreme weather, with the storm expected to reach mainland Portugal on Wednesday morning after passing the island of Madeira this evening.

An official from the Portugal’s environment agency said 101 municipalities are at risk of flooding, noting the peak of the tide is expected to be recorded tomorrow at 3:20pm local time.

Mainland Portugal is still under the influence of Storm Bruna, centred north of the Azores, which has brought heavy rain and caused travel disruption.

Viktor Orbán’s brother detained in people-smuggling investigation, Hungarian PM says

Hungarian authorities have detained the brother of Viktor Orbán as part of a people-smuggling investigation, the prime minister, Péter Magyar, has said.

The Hungarian anti-graft body, the national asset protection and recovery office (NVVH), said in a statement it and police had detained four people and searched homes in Budapest and Pest county.

“Three ex-ministers of Orbán are already in custody,” Magyar said in Berlin, where he was giving a speech. “Already his brother today went to custody,” he added, referring to the former prime minister’s sibling, Aron, 49.

Writing on Facebook, Orbán dismissed his brother’s detention as a case of political revenge. “In this dirty game, the aim is the elimination of the political opponent and the establishment of a one-party system. I am the target, my younger brother is just a tool,” the former prime minister wrote.

The NVVH was set up by Magyar to investigate allegations of corruption during Orbán’s 16-year rule. The latter has denied the claims.

Russia launches deadly attacks on Ukraine ahead of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi

Russia attacked 10 Ukrainian regions overnight, hitting five major energy substations that transmit electricity between regions, Ukrainian prime minister, Sergii Koretskyi, said.

The substations that were hit were in the Poltava, Dnipropetrovsk, Vinnytsia and Kirovohrad regions, Koretskyi said. Russian attacks killed at least nine people and injured 39 others across Ukraine over the past day, according to a report in the Kyiv Independent.

The attacks came after Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who has urged the US to increase its flagging aid to Ukraine, accused Russia of intensifying strikes across the country to put pressure on Kyiv at the Abu Dhabi talks (see this post for more details).

Police officer dies after backpack explosion in Spanish city of Oviedo, authorities say

A police officer from Spain’s canine unit was killed and an expert from the explosives disposal unit was seriously injured in a backpack explosion in the northern Spanish city of Oviedo, according to authorities.

The officers had been deployed to an address in the Villafría neighbourhood after a previous alert about a shooting in which a 17-year-old girl was injured, according to the Spanish outlet El Mundo.

When the officers arrived, they saw a partially opened backpack with a flashlight, prompting them to call the bomb squad (Tedax) for assistance, El Mundo reports.

An officer from the canine unit approached the bag to inspect it, but it reportedly then exploded, killing him, and injuring the bomb disposal expert that was with him. The bomb disposal expert has been taken for treatment at the Central university hospital of Asturias in a serious condition, according to the report.

At least ten people die after traffic accident in Bulgaria, governor says

At least 10 people died in a traffic accident involving about 50 vehicles on both sides of a foggy stretch of highway in southeastern Bulgaria this morning, the BTA news agency reported, citing the Yambol regional governor, Georgi Chalakov.

Ch Insp Todor Mishinev from the Yambol regional directorate of the ministry of internal affairs told Nova TV that some people were still trapped in their cars.

“Right now, the priority is rescue operations. Once they are completed, the procedural and investigative actions will be carried out,” he said.

The incident occurred at about 7am local time around the Zimnitsa road junction in both directions of traffic on the Trakia motorway, according to the Dnevnik news site, which reported that at least 31 people have been injured.

The US special envoy Steve Witkoff, who is leading the American delegation alongside Jared Kushner - Donald Trump’s son-in-law – has just posted on X with details of their meeting with European and Ukrainian officials ahead of the trilateral talks between the US, Ukraine and Russia.

Witkoff said the meeting helped to coordinate their efforts to end the war and “establish the conditions for a durable peace”, including support for “robust security guarantees” for Kyiv. He said officials from Europe, the US and Ukraine shared the same objectives and all held a commitment to “Euro-Atlantic security”.

Participants emphasized that any future arrangements must respect sovereignty, territorial integrity, independent decision-making, and provide credible security guarantees for Ukraine.

The shared objective is to develop a practical and mutually reinforcing security framework that strengthens Ukraine’s long-term security and builds the confidence necessary to reach a negotiated settlement.

“Participants further recognized that addressing postwar security concerns and reducing the risk of conflict are essential. They discussed how to ensure strong mechanisms for managing risk and avoiding dangerous escalation,” Witkoff added.

Witkoff, a property developer and a longtime friend of Donald Trump, took a leading role in Ukraine-Russia negotiations with little diplomatic experience, effectively doing the role usually reserved for the secretary of state.

Donald Trump has said that Ukraine would like be expected to give up territory in a final deal, and would end with a “little bit different shape“.

After meeting with Kremlin advisor Kirill Dmitriev last week, Trump said he would seek a “big, beautiful agreement” that “might involve all of Europe”. Critics are concerned that Trump and Putin could try to go over the heads of European leaders in order to negotiate a peace that gives serious concessions to Russia.

“There’s a lot of baggage that people in Europe have about this idea,” said a US administration official, pointing specifically to Russia’s 2021 proposal that was viewed as an ultimatum to Nato to get out of eastern Europe.

In 2021, Russia issued a list of demands to Nato that included a ban on expansion to new countries including Ukraine, a rollback of troops to its pre-1997 borders, and limits on where it could place missiles. Nato rejected that proposal and Russia invaded Ukraine less than a month later.

“We have to build something new that works today… Because if all the old architecture was working well, then we wouldn’t be in this long war. And so obviously something’s not working, and we need to come up with a way that when we end this war, it really is a final end to the war,” said the US administration official.

The content of those proposals are not clear yet, although the secretary of state, Marco Rubio, claimed on Monday “there’s been some advances made in what that architecture should look”. “We obviously have to get past the conflict to get to that point,” he added.

Zelenskyy says Kyiv in strengthened position for talks as officials seek end to Russia-Ukraine war

We are restarting our Europe live blog ahead of the US-led trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi that seek an end to the war between Russia and Ukraine.

The Ukrainian president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, said on the eve of the talks that Ukrainian battlefield successes were strengthening Kyiv’s position in negotiations.

The three-sided talks between Ukrainian, Russian and US negotiators are to open on Tuesday and will be the first substantive talks since February aimed at ending the war, which has raged on for more than four-and-a-half years.

“Ukrainian forces are currently genuinely strengthening Ukraine’s negotiating position,” Zelenskyy said in his nightly video address on Monday. “Every task that is fully accomplished – every defensive operation that increases Ukraine’s strength and delivers results – strengthens Ukraine and, above all, strengthens us in negotiations.”

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner (who is also Donald Trump’s son-in-law) are to lead the American delegation at the talks, which are scheduled for two days. In Washington, Trump said on Monday that the US was seeing a “very strong inclination” towards ending Russia’s war, and that both sides were “tired of fighting”.

A US official said security guarantees for Ukraine would be a central topic for the discussions, including the potential for Kyiv to be granted a Nato-style article 5 defence, which protects member states in case of an attack. Any territorial agreement would probably come at the end of negotiations once other items were agreed upon, including a postwar security architecture, the official said.

But Ukraine and Russia have both been adamant about territory, with neither side willing to make any major concessions during previous rounds of diplomacy. Ukraine wants Russia pushed back to the original border between the two countries, while the Russian president, Vladimir Putin, wants to obtain all of eastern Ukraine’s Donbas region.

European officials, including from France, Germany and the European Commission, will not directly take part in negotiations, as per Russia’s insistence. But instead the Ukrainian delegation, led by foreign intelligence chief Rustem Umerov, will meet separately to brief their European allies on progress.

The two US envoys met Ukrainian and European officials in Abu Dhabi ahead of the trilateral talks this morning. “Our key priorities are ending the fighting, securing reliable postwar security guarantees, ensuring Ukraine’s economic recovery and development, and taking humanitarian steps,” Umerov wrote in a social media post.

European officials, who have been largely excluded from peace talks, wanted to be involved directly in the talks amid concerns the US administration could try to force through a deal favourable to Russia.
