US Federal Election Commission’s website target5ed by hackers - report

Reuters reports that the US Federal Election Commission’s website has been targeted by a hacker or hackers, disrupting the agency’s electronic filings system to disclose campaign spending a week before the midterm elections.

In a statement to the news agency, the FEC said, “at this time, the FEC’s electronic filing system is currently offline and our IT staff are actively working to restore service.” The FEC, which tracks the spending of candidates and political committees, said users could still search the website for past reports. It declined further comment on the outage.

The FEC does not play a role in administering US elections and the outage should have no effect on voters’ ability to cast ballots, although it will keep information on how much political groups are spending on specific races from the public as they cast ballots.

“We have a bad actor attempting to breach FEC systems. As a result, we’ve had to temporarily shut down our web facing services,” an FEC electronic filings officer said in an email seen by Reuters.

In Texas, Trump repeats a stream of false claims and screens misleading edit of CNN polling data segment

Near the end of his speech to a Republican campaign rally in Corpus Christi, Texas, Donald Trump just screened two short clips, edited from longer CNN segments first broadcast in August and September, highlighting polling data that flatters him.

As we have reported previously, screening these brief clips has become a routine part of Trump’s argument as he tries to convince supporters, and perhaps himself, that he is far more popular than other polling suggests.

The clips, however, were created by editing out negative information the president does not want his supporters to hear.

The first clip is 43 seconds of CNN’s chief data analyst, Harry Enten, showing viewers in September that Trump remains very popular with self-described “Maga Republicans,” which is to say people who describe themselves as loyal to his Make America Great Again movement.

“Maga GOP on Trump. In the 2024 election versus Kamala Harris, he got 99% of the Maga GOP vote—perhaps surprisingly, I might have thought it’d be 100%, but it was 99% percent,” Enten said in the first part of the clip. “Compare that to his approval rating now with the Maga GOP. It’s 98%; that is, there’s been no real decline in Maga support.”

“Look, now in terms of the congressional vote, 98% of them say they’re going to be voting for House Republican candidates,” Enten added. “So, that support is actually really holding up strong. It’s really, really strong when it comes to Maga GOP and Republicans holding on to that vote.”

However, a look at the full 14-minute segment broadcast in late September shows that what the White House edited out was Enten carefully explaining that Trump’s approval rating among Republicans who do not describe themselves as Maga loyalists had suffered a sharp drop, plunging from +56 points in the 2024 election to just +7 points in September 2026.

“You know, when it comes to non-Maga, this is where we’ve absolutely seen Donald Trump’s numbers plummet into the ocean,” Enten said, in a part of the report Trump did not screen at the rally.

The president also screened for his supporters a portion of a prior CNN segment, from August of this year, in which Enten showed viewers that according to polling conducted in May, Trump was, at that time, called the president Republicans most admire by 53% of Republicans, ahead of Ronald Reagan at 18% and Abraham Lincoln at 8%.

Trump is so fond of these two cherry-picked pieces of data, from months ago, that he included both in his 1,300-word social-media tirade against the comedian Bill Maher on Sunday.

Trump cited the data as supposed proof that Maher was wrong to say that the president is unpopular. “I have High Ratings and, more importantly, he said that I’m losing the support and approval of the Republican Party when, in fact, CNN just came out with a Poll showing that I’m at 98 and 99% support within the Republican Party, an All Time Record High,” Trump wrote.

Given that the president has repeatedly screened the clip of Enten clearly saying these were his ratings with Maga Republicans, not the party as a whole, this is not the evidence that he seems to think it is.

Trump flaunts Venezuelan oil shipments at campaign event in Corpus Christi, talks of making Venezuela "51st state"

Trump has taken the stage in Corpus Christi, Texas, the first of two campaign events in the state he’ll be speaking at today.

He started off by gesturing to an oil tanker docked just outside the campaign event, filled, he said, with Venezuelan crude – a product of the deal his administration inked with Venezuela’s new, US-pliant leadership.

“The oil in the ships outside is being provided to the United States at zero cost to the American taxpayer,” Trump said.

US forces launched a surprise incursion into Venezuela to seize its former leader, Nicólas Maduro, in January, dragging him to the US to face criminal charges. Since his ouster, his former Vice President, Delcy Rodriguez, ruling with the Trump administration’s blessing, has agreed to deals that would funnel vast shares of the nation’s oil wealth to US coffers.

Trump spoke of his relationship with Venezuela in unsparing terms, as a conqueror extracting resources from a defeated nation.

He described the oil as being the byproduct of “our big victory in our recent war with Venezuela,” and repeated a phrase he used while addressing the United Nations last month about his toppling of Maduro: “To the victor belong the spoils.”

Trump continued: “We’re bringing out a lot of oil for ourselves, a lot.”

He went on to say, repeatedly, that the US should make Venezuela the 51st US state.

“It will become a prosperous country, and who knows, maybe someday soon it will become a prosperous 51st state. That’s possible. Let’s make it the 51st state, and instead of a president, we’ll have an election for a governor.”

Trump’s remarks about Venezuela’s oil shipments come against the backdrop of surging oil prices downstream of the war in Iran. At several point in his remarks, Trump has vowed that gas prices are set to drop precipitously any day now.

DoJ asks Washington state not to count ballots from possible noncitizens

The US justice department has asked Washington statenot to count any mail ballots received from more than 400 residents who it says may not be US citizens, Reuters reports, opening a new front in the Trump administration’s push to expand control of election administration ahead of next Tuesday’s midterms.

The letter, which resembled a request earlier this month that California not count any ballots received from around 20,000 alleged noncitizens, marked an escalation in the Trump administration’s pressure on state election officials over alleged noncitizen voting.

Multiple state audits and independent studies have found that voter fraud, including voting by noncitizens, is extremely rare and has not affected election outcomes.

The pressure campaign is a rare intervention by the Justice Department in the voting process in the weeks before an election. Donald Trump’s Republican party is locked in a tight battle to retain slim majorities in both chambers of the US Congress, and centrist Democrat Marie Gluesenkamp Perez’s campaign to hold onto her southwest Washington seat is one of 10 key races that could decide control of the US House of Representatives.

In the letter, dated 26 October, the top federal prosecutors in Seattle and Spokane asked Washington secretary of state Steve Hobbs, the state’s top elections official, to determine whether the more than 400 people were in fact US citizens and to not count their ballots until their citizenship was determined. The letter said the justice department would seek “emergency relief” if the state could not take those actions by noon local time tomorrow.

There were 5 million registered voters in Washington state as of 2024, according to the secretary of state’s office.

In a statement, Hobbs said his office would only respond to requests that follow due process.

“The timing of this request — one week before the General Election — is no doubt intended to cast unfounded suspicion on the results,” Hobbs said.

A justice department spokesperson said: “With his [Hobbs’] help, we can ensure that only American citizens vote in Washington’s elections.“

In its letter to Washington state officials, the justice department acknowledged that some of the individuals flagged as possible noncitizens may have since naturalized, but it did not say how many.

Trump’s cuts have exposed US to ‘cascade’ of El Niño dangers, experts warn

Sweeping cuts to the premier US climate agency under Donald Trump have severely hampered the country’s capacity to forecast,research and respond to extreme weather events, former government officials and scientists have warned, as the US faces a cascade of hazards fueled by a potentially historic El Niño this winter.

From the start of his second term, the president has taken aim at the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (Noaa), slashing staff levels, canceling contracts and grants, and shutting down databases and research laboratories. The agency that has long been considered a global crown jewel in climate science and forecasting, has lost roughly 20% of its staff since January 2025.

The National Weather Service, which is housed within Noaa, has also taken a hit. Weather stations have had to delay data collection and cut operating hours, with hundreds of meteorological and other technical positions still vacant across its 122 forecast offices.

The effects have already been felt at Noaa, where a chaotic and volatile environment has sent morale plummeting, increased burnout and hampered vital work. While capacity has quietly unravelled, a large-scale disaster will quickly expose the most dangerous gaps.

“The system has not really been tested since all the cuts happened,” said Tom Di Liberto, a former Noaa climate scientist who now works as the media director of Climate Central.

That test could soon be coming.

This year’s super El Niño, a climate phenomenon characterized by warmer than average equatorial sea surface temperatures, has the ability to fuel extreme weather events around the world. It’s already climbing off the charts and is expected to strengthen through the northern hemisphere’s autumn and winter.

Along with dangerous spikes in temperature, El Niño will supercharge storms in parts of the American west, raise sea levels in California by up to a foot, and set the stage for debris flows, landslides and flash floods.

“Whether we as a country can handle the kind of coast-to-coast extremes that could potentially happen this winter is a big question,” Di Liberto said.

Trump-appointed CDC deputy director to resign after six months on the job

The deputy director and chief medical officer of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) will resign after six months on the job, according to an agency email obtained by the Guardian.

Dr Jennifer Shuford was appointed by Donald Trump in April as part of a four-person senior leadership team.

The appointments were meant to stabilize the agency after the previous CDC director, Susan Monarez, left less than a month into the job – publicly describing the directives of the health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, as “unscientific”.

“I am leaving to spend more time with my family,” Shuford said in a statement, sent via email by the CDC director, Dr Erica Schwartz. “I am so thankful to President Trump and Secretary Kennedy for giving me the opportunity to help lead CDC. It has been an amazing experience, and I will be forever grateful for it.”

Shuford is expected to leave her post on 12 November, after the midterm elections.

The deputy director was appointed as part of a four-person team in April, alongside Schwartz and two other top leaders. Shuford’s appointment was cheered by public health leaders, such as Association of State and Territorial Health Officials (ASTHO), who would have known her work as Texas health commissioner.

In the time since Shuford has worked at the agency, Kennedy has made significant changes that undermine vaccine trust and access. In just a few recent examples: Kennedy fired all 17 of the CDC’s expert vaccine advisory panel members and replaced them with ideologically aligned appointees, slow-walked death updates to the agency’s measles dashboard, and forced the CDC to publicly contradict scientific consensus on vaccines and autism – a change the Autism Science Foundation said left it “appalled”.

At the same time, the agency has lost one-third of its workforce and 48 distinct measles outbreaks have roiled the US, particularly in Pennsylvania. The overwhelming majority of Americans – including Maha supporters – trust vaccines.

Tom Phillips, chief justice of Texas supreme court, endorses James Talarico in Senate race

Tom Phillips, a longtime chief justice of the Texas Supreme Court and a staunch Republican, endorsed Democrat James Talarico in the Texas Senate race on Monday.

In an op-ed published in the Austin American-Statesman, Phillips, a Republican who served as the state’s chief justice from 1988 to 2004, said that he believed that Talarico would be a better representative of Texas in the Senate, and more willing to engage in productive bipartisanship.

By contrast, he said, Paxton would be a Trump acolyte who would enable the president’s excesses and self-dealing, steering the Republican Party away from the ideals Phillips believes it should embody, such as limited government and free enterprise.

He also felt that Paxton’s history of scandal would cast a shadow over his Senate tenure, regardless of his future conduct.

“However exemplary his future conduct might be, Paxton’s longstanding reputation will embarrass Texas in ways our state simply cannot afford,” Phillips wrote. “While I, like many Republicans, do not agree with all of Talarico’s past statements or current positions, I am confident that he would serve this great state with dignity and honor.”

Phillips said he believed there were still Republican candidates on the ticket who were worthy of serving. Paxton just wasn’t one of them.

“Thus, while I enthusiastically support the statewide Republican judicial candidates and many Republican candidates for other statewide offices, my choice for United States Senator must be James Talarico,” Phillips wrote.
