Stream It Or Skip It: ‘Moana’ on Disney+, a Disney ‘Live Action’ Remake Boasting an Even Bigger and Buffer Dwayne Johnson

2026-10-21 19:00

The trademark Late Capitalism Disney Live Action Remake Machine churns out another widget in Moana (now on Disney+), arriving 10 years after the OG cartoon Moana, considered a new Disney classic, and less than two years after cartoon Moana 2, considered considerably less than a classic. And it sure looks like the new movie falls under the Too Soon category, since it eked out a relatively meager $323 million at the worldwide box office on a $250 million-plus production budget — and without a shred of evidence, I theorize that two-thirds of that was spent on Dwayne Johnson’s wig and seemingly unnecessary extramuscular prosthetic bodysuit, which symbolizes the seeming unnecessary-ness of the entire project.

MOANA: STREAM IT OR SKIP IT?

The Gist: Do we have to go over this again? I mean, Moana 2 just tweaked a few details of OG Moana and called it a new movie, and this one, Live Action Moana With “Live Action” In Scare Quotes, is pretty much exactly the same. Guess OG Moana was so delicious, we’ll just keep plucking the last shreds of meat from those bones. So here we are, being introduced to Moana (Catherine Laga’aia) again, who dwells on an island of scared isolationists who’ve managed to feel less scared and isolated thanks to the near-paradise their lovely collectivist society has built on the island of Te Fiti. Moana’s chieftain father, Tui (John Tui), says, “Nay, I say, NAY!” to venturing beyond the lagoon — it’s too dangerous for anyone out there. But Moana yearns. She sings her yearnings, her desires to see what’s out there, in the world outside of Yonkers, Barnaby.

But fate, especially in Disney fables, has a way of satiating such yearnings. As Moana learns from her self-described “crazy lady” grandmother Tala (Rena Owen), their people were wayfinders, not landlubbers. And there’s a whole lotta myth and lore underscoring that cultural history — myth and lore that comes into play when the island’s vegetation begins dying and the lagoon ceases to produce seafood. It seems a demigod named Maui (Dwayne Johnson) stole the Heart of Te Fiti, a special gem, then promptly lost it and got lost himself. And the Heart and Maui aren’t about to find themselves, are they?

So off goes Moana, assisted by the living anthropomorphic ocean — a hand of fate, it seems — on her sailboat, her brainless Werner Herzogian chicken sidekick Heihei stowing away in the comic-relief hatch. She tracks down Maui, who sings about his egomania in an amusing fashion, but it’s not nearly as charming as when he was a funny cartoon and not The Rock in a wig and fake giant muscles, because his real giant muscles apparently aren’t giant enough? This is rather distracting, folks. But the quest must go on, and the obstacles they face are exactly the same as in OG Moana, including the li’l coconut guys, the giant crab maniac and the CGI lava beast. And you get three guesses as to whether Moana succeeds, and the first three don’t count.

What Movies Will It Remind You Of? This Moana is the latest dud in a run of on-again/off-again run of remakes in the Disney+ era: The Little Mermaid, Pinocchio, and Snow White ranged from underwhelming to awful; Mufasa and Lilo and Stitch were popular and Just Fine artistically; Mulan and the terribly underrated Peter Pan and Wendy best justified their existences.

Performance Worth Watching: Laga’aia delivers a warm and upbeat performance that deserves a better movie.

Our Take: We’ve reached peak why-bother with this Moana — it’s supposed to be a crass exploitation of long-established intellectual property, but like Snow White and The Little Mermaid before it, the cash-in was underwhelming. Curious, considering even the most rudimentary analysis seems to predict that result. Which leads us to the artistic reasoning behind the remake: there is none. Live Action Moana With “Live Action” In Scare Quotes is a business decision, and a bad one (until Disney spins it by calling it one piece of the uberglobal endeavor to promote theme parks, exploit merchandising opportunities, etc.).

I scrape the mildew off the bottom of a sinking boat to find one entertaining potentiality from this endeavor, and that’s seeing Johnson bring Maui to flesh-and-blood life, even if it’s in front of ugly and obvious green-screen graphics. But the result is underwhelming, the superstar looking uninspired throughout, not even mugging amusingly like he tends to do when anchoring family films. Laga’aia’s shrugworthy chemistry with Johnson makes one wonder if Jason Momoa could’ve played Maui with a little more verve, and maybe a different flavor, and yes, you may read some desperate yearning of our own within that wondering, underscored by the desire to see something, anything, besides a near shot-for-shot regurgitation of the original film.

Director Thomas Kail — a veteran theater director who helmed Broadway versions of Hamilton and In the Heights — shows little innovation in adapting the cartoon to “live action.” He struggles to make the emotionally broad Broadwayisms of OG Moana translate to real humans, and run-throughs of indelible earwormers like “You’re Welcome” and “How Far I’ll Go” feel lifeless and mechanical, the house remaining disappointingly un-brought-down. Such failure reflects the film’s cynical origins and creative bankruptcy. The apparent need (and yes, “need” also needs scare quotes) to artificially bulk up Johnson is emblematic of the pointlessness of remaking this film at this time – it’s a hat on a hat on top of an empty head.

Our Call: Thankfully Moana is an enduring story, strong enough to survive the many failures of this listless remake. So SKIP IT and stick to the original.

John Serba is a freelance film critic from Grand Rapids, Michigan. Werner Herzog hugged him once.
