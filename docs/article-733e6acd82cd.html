RFK Jr tries to blame measles outbreak on failure to vaccinate children during Covid-19 shutdowns

Speaking to reporters in Virginia on Thursday, the US health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, claimed that the measles outbreak that has killed at least six people in Pennsylvania was caused by the suspension of vaccination against the disease during the Covid-19 pandemic.

After an event focused on efforts to address a shortage of health care providers in the US, a reporter asked Kennedy, the former leader of an anti-vaccination group, if he believes that the number of measles deaths in Pennsylvania “is acceptable”.

When a press aide interrupted to say the question was off-topic, the reporter said, “he’s the highest-ranking health official in the country; he can answer that question. He’s a cabinet secretary.”

“We’ve done better than- we’re having a global measles outbreak right now,” Kennedy replied. “It’s all over the world, and the measles outbreak is occurring because so many people went unvaccinated during the Covid pandemic.

“And this is something that Anthony Fauci, in fact, predicted,” Kennedy added, before referring, imprecisely, to an entry in a private journal kept by Fauci, the former director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, on 6 May 2020, about the “unintended consequences” of shutdowns to stop the spread of the coronavirus. “He said: ‘We’re gonna kill more people from measles than we save from Covid ‘cause we’re shutting down measles vaccination programs,’” Kennedy said.

What Fauci wrote at that early stage of the shutdowns, as discussions about when to end the restrictions in the US were underway, was that more than “1 million children worldwide have missed their measles vaccinations because of the shutdown. This is terrible and needs to be addressed since on that basis alone, there would be far, far more excess deaths from measles among children than coronavirus deaths among children.”

On Thursday, Kennedy claimed that that pause in childhood vaccination, not fears about vaccines spread by anti-vaccine activists like him, was the cause of the current outbreak.

“So now, six years later”, Kennedy said, “we have a global outbreak.

“We have done better, and particularly my administration has done better, at controlling the outbreak than any other country in the world,” Kennedy claimed. “We’ve had 4,000 measles cases this year. Mexico, which is a third of our population, had 20,000. Canada has 7,000, has a third, or one-eighth of our population. In Europe, they’ve had 135,000 cases. So we’re doing better at controlling it than any country in the world.”

He then pivoted to blaming the severity of the Pennsylvania outbreak on the state’s Democratic governor, Josh Shapiro.

“Unfortunately, in Pennsylvania”, Kennedy said, “the governor- the only state where the governor would not accept CDC Epi-Aid,” referring to the shorthand for a CDC investigation of an epidemic.

In August, Shapiro said that he blamed Kennedy for spreading anti-vaccine misinformation.

Trump administration sues California to block counting of mail ballots from 20,000 registered voters

The Trump administration sued the state of California and its election officials on Thursday, seeking to compel the state to not count ballots cast by 20,000 registered California voters whom the Department of Homeland Security suspects might not be citizens.

Officials in California named in the suit rejected the demand, which the state’s governor, Gavin Newsom, called “a sad, desperate attempt by Trump to use his politicized Justice Department to try to suppress California voters just three weeks before Election Day.

“The courts have made clear that the federal government has no role in how a state carries out its election process, and now, in the middle of Californians voting, they have asked local officials – who do a damn good job defending democracy – to violate federal law,” the governor added.

In a letter to the Department of Justice, California’s secretary of state, Shirley Weber, noted that the federal government did not explain the methodology it used to compile the list of 20,000 voters who had been sent mail ballots for the midterm elections. Setting ballots cast by those voters aside, Weber said, would violate federal and state law.

Weber also pointed out that a similar list of suspected noncitizen voters in Nevada had been shown to be entirely wrong. “DHS initially alleged that Nevada’s voter roll included nearly 16,000 potential non-citizens,” Weber wrote. “However, after working with the State of Nevada, DHS officials failed to establish that a single non-citizen was on the voter rolls.”

Dean Logan, the registrar of the state’s largest county, Los Angeles, with 5.8 million registered voters, said in a statement that his office received a thumb drive from the federal government two days ago, said to include a list of 20,000 alleged noncitizens on the voter rolls, with a “hand written note providing a password”.

Logan said that his office did not open the thumb drive, since “we do not consider the device a secure medium.” The secretary of state’s office said the password was written “on a sticky note”.

“Federal and state laws are clear on how voter eligibility is established – and any action to remove a voter from eligibility must be based on verifiable information, not assertions of unconfirmed data matching,” Logan added.

Experts have warned for years that a DHS database that flags people who were once immigrants to the US is not a reliable method of finding current noncitizens, since it is not updated when people become naturalized citizens.

According to a 2024 data analysis from the USC Dornsife Equity Research Institute, 5.7 million Californians are naturalized US citizens, including 1.3 million who became citizens since 2015.

The FBI and the US Postal Inspection Service are investigating letters sent to state officials and election offices across the country containing an “unknown substance”.

Just weeks before November’s US midterm elections, election officers in Louisiana and Oklahoma received envelopes containing a powdery substance, leading to evacuations. A similar package containing white powder was also sent to the Ohio secretary of state, but the US Postal Service intercepted the package.

The US Senate sergeant at arms, who serves as chief law enforcement for the Senate, informed members of the upper chamber on Thursday of the investigation in a message, obtained by the Associated Press.

The notice said law enforcement officials were working to intercept additional mail, but it is possible some could reach their destination. Federal authorities have urged recipients to treat the mail as “potentially hazardous”.

Brendan Donahue, a senior official with the US Postal Inspection Service, confirmed the investigation into the suspicious mailings during a press conference on Thursday. “We are committed to protecting our nation’s election officials from threatening and dangerous mail. Any threat or attack on an election official is unacceptable,” Donahue said.

The US Postal Inspection Service referred the Guardian to the FBI.

In a statement, the FBI said it would not confirm specific instances but said that it was “investigating a series of suspicious mailings sent to various addresses nationwide, including Secretary of State office and their related election officers” alongside the US Postal Inspection Service.

“Some of the letters contained an unknown substance and we are working closely with our law enforcement partners to respond to each incident,” the FBI said. “We are also working with our partners to identify the individual or individuals responsible for the letters.”

The FBI declined to comment on what the purported substance may have been.

UK defence secretary Wes Streeting emphasizes importance of standing with Ukraine as he speaks in Washington DC

Britain’s defence secretary Wes Streeting has stressed the importance of standing with Ukraine in its defence against Russia amid mounting fears over the United States’ commitment to Kyiv, the Press Association reports.

During his first visit to Washington in the role, Streeting warned that if Vladimir Putin’s aggression was rewarded, “the consequences will not remain confined to Ukraine or to my continent.”

Streeting is expected to meet his US counterpart PeteHegseth in a bid to strengthen ties that have been strained by issues including DonaldTrump’s stance on Kyiv and the widening US-Nato rift.

Streeting said today that the UK and US must strengthen their defence partnership to confront growing threats at a time when “it is not yet clear if this century will be defined by freedom or tyranny”.

Speaking at an event held by the Centre for Strategic and International Studies and the Royal United Services Institute, he said:

Britain and America have never been content to simply watch history unfold. At defining moments, we have helped determine its course.

The front line for freedom today can be found in Ukraine.

Ukraine may be thousands of miles from American shores, but if aggression is rewarded, if borders can be redrawn by force, if the sovereignty of nations becomes negotiable, the consequences will not remain confined to Ukraine or to my continent.

So standing with Ukraine is not a choice between our values and our self-interest. It is a recognition that ultimately they are inseparable.
