Wisconsin police officer who fatally shot ex-con will not be charged, prosecutor says

2026-11-20 01:13

Published Nov. 19, 2026, 8:13 p.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

The Wisconsin police officer who shot and killed a 38-year-old Black man in Madison in July will not face criminal charges, a prosecutor announced Thursday, sparking calls for justice from the victim’s family members and community advocates.

The shooting of Corey Ruiz sparked racial justice protests, and city leaders were bracing for more protests in the wake of the decision not to charge the officer, who is white.

The victim’s brother, Adam Ruiz, told reporters the prosecutor has “mistreated” his family.

“We’re destroyed,” he said. “We won’t accept this.”

Kiel Baitinger-Peterson, an officer with more than a decade of experience in law enforcement, fired the shots that killed Ruiz on July 22. Baitinger-Peterson was one of four officers who responded to calls about someone checking out parked cars and an alleged burglary attempt, police said. They surrounded Ruiz, who was on a bicycle.

“I conclude it was reasonable for the officer to use deadly force when this person resisted officers, produced a knife, and was not affected by multiple attempts to use Tasers while officers attempted to take him into custody,” Dane County District Attorney Ismael Ozanne said in a statement announcing his decision.

The Wisconsin Department of Justice said immediately after the shooting that two officers attempted to use a Taser on Ruiz but weren’t successful. Ruiz “brandished a knife and injured” Baitinger-Peterson, the department said.

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who represents the Ruiz family and was surrounded by them at a news conference Thursday, said there was nothing in the videos that the district attorney showed them to justify the shooting.

“In the video you see they tackle him, they tase him, they kick him and then the police officer executes him,” Crump said. “The video is the objective unbiased witness here.”

Madison Police Chief John Patterson said he has trust in the city’s police officers, but he realizes the decision not to bring charges “will bring various emotions.” He urged protesters, however, to remain peaceful.

Patterson said the police will now conduct an internal review of the shooting, and all of the officers involved will remain on administrative leave.

Prosecutor shows the Ruiz family video of the shooting

Ozanne said he reached his decision after reviewing evidence that included police reports, diagrams, surveillance videos, police squad car videos and citizen videos. Madison police officers do not wear body cameras.

Crump said it is one of the worst cases of excessive force that he has worked on.

“When you are shooting a man who is on the ground, three (times). It hits him in his chest and the kill shot hits him in his head,” he said.

Crump said the medical examiner told the Ruiz family he would have survived had the third shot not been fired and hit him in the head.

When asked if the family would be filing a lawsuit, Crump said his team will “explore every possible avenue.”

Ozanne said his decision was a correct interpretation of the law.

“A person does not have to like and/or agree with the law, however I am sworn to apply the law to the facts at hand,” the prosecutor said. “The law applies equally to members of law enforcement and to those who are not.”

The Wisconsin Department of Justice released its investigative files concerning the shooting Thursday, including photo and video evidence.

One police dash-camera video captures the moment three officers tried to wrestle Ruiz into submission. They repeatedly yell, “He’s got a knife! He’s got a knife!” The weapon is visible in his right hand, the blade pointed downward, as a fourth officer arrives and deploys a Taser. Ruiz stumbles and falls onto his right side before Baitinger-Peterson fires three shots at close range.

Days of protests followed the shooting

Baitinger-Peterson has not replied to an email sent Thursday seeking comment.

Baitinger-Peterson told investigators during an interview days after the shooting that he got on his knees and grabbed Ruiz’s right arm before realizing Ruiz was holding a knife, according to records released Thursday. He said he was struggling to control the arm and feared that Ruiz would stab the officers. He said he fired a third time because he didn’t think the first two shots had struck Ruiz because Ruiz’s facial expression and body did not change.

Jim Palmer, executive director of the police union Wisconsin Professional Police Association, said in a statement the decision that the officer’s use of deadly force was legally justified “is supported by the evidence and consistent with the legal standards governing the use of force by law enforcement officers.”

It was the third time since 2012 that Madison police had shot and killed someone in the same neighborhood. The killing sparked days of protests and forced the city to reckon with calls for police reform to address racial justice issues.

Baitinger-Peterson and the other three officers who interacted with Ruiz that day have been on paid administrative leave since the shooting.
