Turkey revokes Iranian Bank Mellat’s operating license

2026-09-19 10:19

Turkey has revoked the operating license of Iran’s Bank Mellat, which has faced Western sanctions for years, according to a decision published in the Official Gazette early Saturday.

The decision came about two weeks after Washington imposed sanctions on a Turkish bank over its alleged ties to Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). It was unclear whether the two actions were linked.

Turkey’s Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK) cited Article 71(b) of the banking law, which applies when a bank’s continued operation threatens depositors’ rights or the security and stability of the financial system.

Tehran-based Bank Mellat provides financial support to the Iranian government and is subject to US, European Union and UK sanctions, according to OpenSanctions, an open-source database of sanctioned entities.

The Iranian government is the bank’s largest shareholder, the database shows.

Bank Mellat has faced Western sanctions over accusations that Tehran pursued nuclear weapons under the cover of a civilian nuclear program.

The sanctions were lifted under a 2015 agreement between Iran and world powers aimed at limiting Tehran’s nuclear activities.

The United States withdrew from the agreement in May 2018 and reimposed economic sanctions on Iran.

The United States and Gulf countries sanctioned Bank Mellat again in 2019 after identifying it as one of 25 entities linked to the IRGC.

The US Treasury Department imposed sanctions on Turkey’s Golden Global Bank on September 4 over its alleged ties to the IRGC as Washington sought to increase financial pressure on Iran.

The Turkish investment bank denied the accusations.

Washington imposed sanctions on 36 more entities several days later, among them three Turkish ground services companies accused of helping Iran’s Mahan Air, a civilian carrier that the United States says supports the IRGC.

Mahan Air said this week that it would suspend all flights to Turkey beginning September 21 at the request of Turkish aviation officials, Iran’s ISNA news agency reported.

Turkey’s aviation authority did not respond to a request for comment.

Iran has been at war since late February, when the United States and Israel launched a bombing campaign that killed the country’s supreme leader.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has described Washington’s campaign as an “economic D-Day” against Iran and warned of consequences for countries that refuse to join it.

The Turkish regulator’s decision came days before President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan was expected to meet with US President Donald Trump on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly, which begins in New York on Tuesday.
