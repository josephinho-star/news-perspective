Mom sues driver for wrong-way NYC crash that killed daughter months before her wedding

2026-11-19 19:40

The grieving family of a young woman killed by a wrong-way driver in Greenwich Village last year is suing the driver who pleaded guilty — and also blames the city for missing signs and unsafe conditions that led to her death, according to the filing.

Mom Gudrun Schoeck filed the wrongful death lawsuit Wednesday in Manhattan Supreme Court over the tragedy that claimed the life of her daughter last November.

A “One-Way” sign was missing on the one-way street where Massachusetts native Valerie Bettina Schoeck, 27, was killed, the lawsuit states.

“We will keep seeking the truth, accountability, and justice she deserves,” Schoeck said. “We believe every party responsible must answer for the loss of our Valerie.”

Valerie Schoeck was crossing Morton Street at Seventh Avenue South on Nov. 6, 2025, when Levinson was driving the wrong way down Morton Street and struck her with a 2020 GMC van, officials said at the time.

Levinson could not be reached for comment.

The city was “careless and negligent” for failing to install or maintain “adequate, visible, clear, and properly positioned traffic signs” as they repaved and redesigned the street, the filing states.

That left Valerie Schoeck at “unreasonable risk of harm,” despite having prior notice of the need to warn pedestrians and drivers alike of the “dangerous conditions,” the suit claims.

Her mother’s suit also makes similar claims against Courier Car Rental, who employed Levinson and owned the van he was driving at the time.

“The simplest of acts by each Defendant could have prevented this unspeakable tragedy,” said the mom’s attorney, Imran Ansari. “Their failure to do so requires accountability and each Defendant must answer to the law.”

Levinson was arrested and charged with criminally negligent homicide and reckless driving last February.

In May, he pleaded guilty to one count of criminally negligent homicide, and was sentenced to a five-year suspension of his professional license and a three-year conditional discharge.

Valerie Schoeck grew up in Boston’s South End with her mother and sister, according to an obituary shared with The Post.

She attended The College of Charleston, moved to the West Village and worked as a vice president at PR firm, Argot Partners, at the time of her death.

Her wedding was planned for the following June, the obituary noted.

“Valerie’s death has left our family wrapped in silence and emptiness that no words can ever fill. We know, with aching hearts, that nothing can bring her back to us,” her mother said in a statement.

“But, we will carry her memory with tenderness and strength, refusing to let the light she brought into our lives grow dim.”
