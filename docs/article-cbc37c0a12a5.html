Iranian airline plane seized at İstanbul Airport over €3 million debt

2026-09-29 14:01

An aircraft belonging to Iran-based Caspian Airlines was seized at İstanbul Airport over an alleged debt of about €3 million ($3.4 million), with passengers ordered off the plane shortly before departure, Turkish and Iranian media reported.

The Boeing 737 had completed boarding and was preparing to depart for Tehran on Monday when enforcement officials prevented it from taking off on the basis of a court-issued seizure order, according to the state-run Anadolu news agency.

The seizure followed a legal dispute over approximately €3 million allegedly owed by Caspian Airlines to ACM Temsil Gözetim, a company providing representation and supervision services to the aviation industry.

Lawyers representing the company and enforcement officers went to İstanbul Airport to execute the seizure order.

Passengers were subsequently removed from the aircraft and taken back to the terminal.

In a statement carried by Iranian state television, Caspian Airlines said the İstanbul-Tehran flight had faced “temporary operational restrictions regarding one of its Boeing aircraft due to an administrative issue in Turkey.”

Iranian state television confirmed that passengers had been required to disembark and said the airline had taken “administrative” measures concerning a €3 million debt for “Istanbul airport services.”

The aircraft was expected to return to Iran on Tuesday, according to the report.

Turkey’s Foreign Ministry did not respond to Agence France-Presse requests for comment.

Caspian Airlines is also subject to US sanctions. The US Treasury designated the carrier in 2014, accusing it of supporting elements of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps by transporting personnel and material, including weapons, from Iran to Syria.

Washington expanded sanctions targeting Iran’s aviation sector earlier this month, designating 27 previously unsanctioned Iranian airlines and warning that companies doing business with sanctioned carriers could themselves face penalties.

There is no indication that the seizure of the Caspian Airlines aircraft in İstanbul was connected to the US sanctions.

Turkey has so far not followed the latest US measures by halting Iranian airline operations, and carriers including Iran Air continue to operate regular flights between the two countries, AFP reported.

The measures have nonetheless affected some Iranian carriers’ operations in Turkey. Mahan Air, another Iranian airline under US sanctions, suspended all flights to Turkey beginning September 21. AFP reported that the airline said the suspension came at Ankara’s request.

Agence France-Presse, with reporting from Turkish Minute
