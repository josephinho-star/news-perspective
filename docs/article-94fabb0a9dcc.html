Patrick Clancy in his own words: Top takeaways from grieving father's first interview

2026-09-21 00:44

Patrick Clancy begged for just one of his children to survive, held baby Callan as he died in his arms and revealed why he forgave Lindsay after she killed their three children.

Speaking with correspondent Ross Douthat on CBS News' "60 Minutes," the grieving father shared new details about his children's final moments, what Lindsay told him about the killings and the online attacks he says are hurting their legacy.

The interview aired after Lindsay's triple-murder trial ended in a mistrial. Her attorneys argued she was suffering from postpartum psychosis and was not criminally responsible, while prosecutors maintained she knew what she was doing.

From his desperate plea at the hospital to the online claims that followed the mistrial, Patrick described how the tragedy has shaped every part of his life.

Patrick said he clung to hope that Callan might survive after his siblings, 5-year-old Cora and 3-year-old Dawson, were found dead inside the family's Duxbury, Massachusetts, home.

Doctors had restored the baby's heartbeat, and Patrick told Douthat he began imagining the life he could still give his youngest child.

"I remember that night, I really thought there was a chance that Callan would live," Patrick said.

"And I remember just thinking, ‘Just give me one. Just give me one kid,’" he continued. "And I would raise him as best I could, and I would give him the best life I possibly could. And I'd tell him about his brother and sister."

Patrick said that hope faded when he reached Boston Children's Hospital and saw the look on a doctor's face.

Callan's heart continued beating for four days. Patrick became emotional as he recalled apologizing to his son and spending his final moments beside him.

"I said, ‘I'm so sorry,’" he told Douthat, his voice breaking. "And I got to hold him. I got to lay down next to him. And he died in my arms."

While Callan remained in the hospital, Patrick said he held his son's hand and used his free hand to write about all three children. He told Douthat he feared their memories might fade if he did not put them into words.

Lindsay never asked for forgiveness

The tributes Patrick wrote also included words of forgiveness for Lindsay. He said she has never asked him to forgive her, but he chose to give that forgiveness anyway.

"I saw headlines that said, you know, ‘killer mom’ and ‘baby murderer,’ ‘monster,’ and none of them were accurate," Patrick said during the interview. "I believe that it was her mental illness that caused that."

Patrick said he also feared anger would consume him if he could not forgive her.

"I felt that if I didn't get there, I would be eaten up inside," he said.

When Patrick asked Lindsay whether she remembered killing their children, he said she described the memory as dreamlike.

"She said it was like a dream, and she remembered it much in the way you would remember a dream after waking up," he told Douthat.

Patrick said he did the best he could as Lindsay's condition worsened but must live with what happened.

"I think I did the best I could with what I had at the time, and I live with the outcome," he said. "I live with it every day."

Grief left him unsure he would survive

Patrick told Douthat his grief became so overwhelming that he questioned whether he would live to see another birthday.

"There was a time where I didn't think I would live to see my 35th birthday, and I was completely fine with it," he said.

Patrick later left Duxbury for New York City, where he could grieve without being recognized. Five weeks after the move, on what would have been Callan's first birthday, he went to St. Patrick's Cathedral, lit a candle and told his son, "I love you, buddy."

"And I walk out on Fifth Avenue and I start crying my eyes out, and nobody cared," Patrick recalled.

For Patrick, being able to cry without drawing attention was exactly what he needed.

"I thought, this is where I need to be," he said. "I can grieve."

His Manhattan apartment remains filled with reminders of the children, including a baby sloth Cora carried everywhere and a photograph of Callan wearing an oversized bow tie, "60 Minutes" showed.

Patrick said they also return to him in vivid dreams. In one, Cora walked into the kitchen of the family's former home and hugged him.

"I just looked at her and it was like I basically just said, ‘Where have you been?’" he recalled. "Those dreams are so real."

‘Trial by TikTok’ fueled painful attacks

The mistrial unleashed what Douthat described as a "trial by TikTok," with online sleuths and influencers spreading baseless theories portraying Patrick as uncaring, unfaithful and even responsible for the children's deaths.

"It's deeply harmful to the legacy of my children," Patrick said. "It is a huge distraction from what really matters here, which is perinatal mental health, and it's done real harm to real people."

He said the repeated accusations have made it painful to be in public and warned that people may start believing false claims if they see them often enough.

"After seeing lie after lie after lie, they start to believe it," Patrick said. "And that's what we're left with."

Patrick said he sought answers from Postpartum Support International, psychiatrists and other experts. Those conversations helped him begin processing what happened.

A new marriage and keeping his children’s memory alive

Patrick's wife, Dr. Rachel Danis, joined him for the "60 Minutes" interview and recalled first learning about his children after noticing their photograph on his phone during a coffee outing.

"I just asked, ‘Who are those cuties?’" Danis told Douthat. "And Pat had said, ‘Those are my kids.’"

When she asked whether they lived in Massachusetts, Patrick told her they had died.

Patrick and Danis met through a New York running club in January 2024. Their relationship deepened as they continued running together, and they married in New York City this April.

The couple also discussed starting a family.

Asked what he would tell a future child about Cora, Dawson and Callan, Patrick said all of his children would remain part of the same family.

"You have different relationships with your kids, but they're all your kids," he said. "And I can't wait to tell them about their brothers and sister."

Judge William Sullivan declared a mistrial Sept. 4 after jurors split 11-1, with 11 favoring a finding that Lindsay was not criminally responsible and one holding out for a conviction. Prosecutors have not announced whether they will try her again.
