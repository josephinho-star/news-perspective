U.S. military repelled drones at UK airbase this month, FT reports

2026-11-28 07:40

The U.S. military repelled a half dozen drones swarming Britain's RAF Lakenheath base earlier this month, the Financial Times reported on Saturday, saying Iran was suspected of being linked to the incident.

The newspaper, citing people familiar with the matter, said the U.S. deployed counter-drone technology to repel the unmanned aerial systems but that the drones were not retrieved by British or American personnel.

Asked for comment on the report, a spokesperson for the UK's Ministry of Defence told Reuters in an emailed statement: "Ministry of Defence Police and Suffolk Police worked closely with USAF Security Forces to respond to reports of unauthorised drone activity at RAF Lakenheath on 13 November and the situation was quickly resolved, with RAF Lakenheath returning to normal security and force protection measures."

The White House and Pentagon did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

In the incident, U.S. Air Force personnel at the base responded to "suspected unauthorised drone activity", and an Air Force spokesperson said at the time that no personnel, aircraft or infrastructure were affected.

In a September incident at RAF Fairford, another British base used by the U.S. Air Force, a threat linked to Iran prompted the removal of American B-1 bombers, President Donald Trump said last month.

Tehran has rejected accusations of involvement in the Fairford incident or other malign activity in Britain, where the authorities have for years accused Iran of being involved in plots and attempted assassinations against dissidents and Israeli and Jewish targets.
