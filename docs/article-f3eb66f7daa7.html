We tried Alix Earle’s Reale Actives skin care line — here’s what’s worth your money

2026-11-13 20:38

Alix Earle has built an empire out of letting the internet into her bathroom.

From candid “Get Ready With Me” videos to famously unfiltered conversations about acne, the TikTok star has made talking about skin struggles part of her brand.

So, when she announced Reale Actives, her own skin care line, it felt like a natural next step. The University of Miami graduate has struggled with cystic acne, previously telling followers about her experience with Accutane and the frustrating cycle of trying products that didn’t deliver.

With that in mind, her collection was developed alongside her own dermatologist, Dr. Kiran Mian, giving the celebrity-led venture some additional credibility.

The launch arrives at an interesting moment for celebrity beauty. Rhode, Rare Beauty and other founder-led brands have shown that a recognizable face can turn a relatively straightforward beauty product into a must-have.

But there’s a difference between wanting to buy something because a celebrity uses it and wanting to keep using it because it actually works. That’s the question behind this Reale Actives review: Does the brand deserve a spot on our bathroom shelves?

What is Reale Actives?

Reale Actives officially launched March 31, 2026. The original collection centered on four products: a makeup-removing cleansing balm, an exfoliating gel cleanser, a mandelic acid serum and a barrier-supporting moisturizer. The brand subsequently expanded its lineup with an azelaic acid serum.

The premise is straightforward: skin care for acne-prone skin that doesn’t feel overly clinical or intimidating. The brand emphasizes dermatologist-developed formulas, active ingredients and products designed to address breakouts while supporting the skin barrier.

“Hot and Acne can be used in the same sentence,” Earle wrote on Instagram before the launch. “My dream for this brand is to not only provide you with AMAZING products for your skin, but to change the way people think about skin ‘imperfections.’”

Our credentials: Why we tested Reale Actives

I follow all things beauty and celebrity as a commerce reporter, so I’m familiar with the influencer-to-founder pipeline — but, as someone who grew up in Earle’s corner of New Jersey, there’s something especially fun about watching a hometown name build a beauty business.

That said, my own skin is dry and sensitive, which means I’m far more interested in whether these formulas are comfortable, hydrating and gentle than whether they look good in an Alix-approved GRWM.

A product line designed around acne-prone skin can include exfoliating ingredients that aren’t necessarily ideal for everyone. My priority was finding out whether the brand balances its blemish-focused actives with enough barrier support to make the products approachable for skin that gets tight, flaky or irritated.

For a fuller picture, Senior Commerce Social Media Marketing Manager Michelle Moezam tried the line along with me. As someone who’s been studying influencer trends for years, she was eager to test out the latest and greatest skin care setting the internet abuzz.

The Reale Actives cleansing balm is arguably one of the easiest products in the collection to understand. Get Bare is designed as the first step in an evening double-cleansing routine, for melting away makeup, sunscreen and excess oil before a second cleanser does the rest.

The formula features squalane, green tea extract, centella asiatica, vitamin E and a blend of lightweight oils. That’s an appealing ingredient combination for dry skin: Squalane helps replenish moisture, while centella and green tea are commonly used in soothing skin care formulas.

I generally prefer a balm or oil cleanser over an aggressive foaming wash when removing makeup, because the goal is to dissolve buildup without leaving my skin feeling stripped. The formula’s moisturizing and soothing ingredients are a promising starting point.” — Victoria McDonnell, Commerce Reporter & Content Strategist

Pore Power is the more targeted cleanser in the collection. Unlike a basic face wash, it incorporates exfoliating ingredients, including LHA and BHA, to help refine the look of pores and address buildup. LHA is a salicylic-acid derivative used for exfoliation, while BHA is commonly associated with helping clear excess oil and dead skin cells from pores. Those ingredients make sense for an acne-focused brand, particularly one founded by someone whose personal skin journey has been so public.

Here’s where the Reale Actives lineup gets interesting: Go Deep contains 8% mandelic acid, an alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) used to exfoliate the skin’s surface and improve the appearance of uneven texture. Mandelic acid is often considered a gentler AHA option because its larger molecular structure allows it to penetrate skin more slowly than glycolic acid. That can make it an appealing alternative for people who want exfoliation without immediately reaching for a stronger treatment.

“The 8% concentration is worth noting, especially for someone like me with dry, sensitive skin. I appreciate that the brand has chosen mandelic acid rather than building its entire exfoliating story around a more aggressive acid. Still, gentler doesn’t mean irritation-proof. I’d introduce this serum gradually, avoid layering it with several other exfoliants and make sunscreen a nonnegotiable part of my morning routine.” — Victoria

The Go Calm serum is an interesting addition because azelaic acid has a reputation for being a versatile skin care ingredient. It can help improve the appearance of redness, uneven tone and blemish-related discoloration. Unlike exfoliating acids, azelaic acid is not an AHA or BHA. It’s a dicarboxylic acid with anti-inflammatory properties, which helps explain why it’s often included in routines for redness-prone and acne-prone skin.

Is Reale Actives worth it?

Reale Actives just might be one of the most promising celebrity skin care launches for acne-conscious beauty shoppers.

The reason? It has a clear identity. It isn’t simply selling the promise of perfect skin or another viral glow product. It’s built around a recognizable founder story, a specific skin care concern and ingredients that have established roles in dermatology.

I’d give it extra credit for making barrier support part of the conversation rather than treating exfoliation as the entire solution.

The individual products range from $28 to $39, putting the collection in the accessible prestige skin care category. That’s not drugstore pricing, but it’s also not the kind of luxury skin care investment that requires a second mortgage.

At $29, the Get Bare Cleansing Balm is a reasonable starting point. The $39 Go Deep and Go Calm serums are more specialized purchases, so I’d choose based on your actual skin concerns rather than buying both simply because they’re part of the collection.

The $28 Pore Power Cleanser is another targeted option, while the $38 Dew More Moisturizer is the step I’d consider most important for maintaining hydration alongside active treatments.

Is Reale Actives worth the hype? I think the brand has a compelling premise, especially for Earle’s existing audience and people who want acne-conscious skin care. But celebrity appeal shouldn’t replace ingredient research or an understanding of your own skin.

For someone with dry, sensitive skin like mine, I’d approach the collection selectively rather than automatically buying the entire routine. A celebrity founder can get a product onto my radar, but a comfortable formula is what gets it to stay in my bathroom.

The bottom line: Reale Actives is more than an Alix Earle merch moment. It has a defined skin care philosophy, interesting active ingredients and a recognizable point of view. Whether it earns a permanent place in your routine will depend on your skin type, your concerns and how well the individual formulas work for you.

Who should buy Reale Actives?

I’d put Reale Actives on the radar of people who:

- Have acne-prone skin and want a streamlined routine

- Are interested in ingredients like mandelic acid and azelaic acid

- Want to address the appearance of post-acne marks and uneven texture

- Prefer a celebrity-founded brand with dermatologist involvement

- Enjoy skin care that combines treatment-focused ingredients with attractive packaging

For very dry or sensitive skin, I’d be more selective. The cleansing balm and moisturizer are the categories I’d prioritize, while the exfoliating cleanser and mandelic acid serum would require a slower introduction.

Anyone with a compromised skin barrier should focus on restoring comfort before adding multiple active ingredients. And if you’re dealing with persistent or painful acne, a dermatologist can help determine whether over-the-counter products are enough.

How we tested Reale Actives

For a comprehensive review, we evaluated the collection using the following criteria:

- Formula and ingredients: We examined each product’s published ingredient list, paying particular attention to exfoliating acids, soothing ingredients and barrier-supporting components.

- Texture and application: We considered each product’s intended texture, application method and place within a practical morning or evening skin care routine.

- Skin-type suitability: We assessed the formulas through the lens of dry, sensitive skin, considering potential irritation and the importance of maintaining hydration.

- Price and value: We compared individual product prices, sizes and intended uses against other prestige and celebrity-founded skin care brands, including Rhode.

- Brand credibility and claims: We reviewed the brand’s dermatologist involvement, published product information, clinical-testing claims and customer-facing transparency.
