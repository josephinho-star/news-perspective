Jamie Dimon shakes up JPMorgan leadership, taps de Best as CFO

2026-11-17 15:29

Dutch national de Best, just 43, will step into the top finance job after first-quarter results next year, taking the keys to a $5.1 trillion balance sheet.

Barnum leaves the firmwide desk after six years once the first-quarter books close, and will exit the bank in early 2028, the Securities and Exchange Commission filing said.

“Bregje is an extraordinary leader with outstanding experience both in finance and our businesses,” Dimon said. “I’m very excited she will become our company’s next CFO.”

But the Tuesday filing left out the numbers Wall Street watches closest: the moolah.

A JPMorgan spokesman declined to comment on the new pay packages for Barnum and de Best’s new roles.

The Dimon-led company also left de Best’s old seat open, saying it will name a new commercial and investment bank CFO in early 2027.

For 54-year-old Barnum, the vice chairman title marks a familiar Park Avenue off-ramp. The bank ran the exact same playbook for former President Daniel Pinto, who shifted into a vice chairmanship to hand off his duties before retiring from the firm.

Barnum is slated to exit JPMorgan in early 2028, giving de Best nearly a year of overlap with her predecessor. But Tuesday’s filing did not set a firm departure date or spell out what Barnum will do day to day.

“Regarding Jeremy’s duties, it will be whatever Jamie and his operating committee colleagues want him to focus on,” said a source close to the bank’s top brass. “I wouldn’t call it a retirement. He’s still young and has plenty of interests. We’d like him to stay forever.”

Dimon, who has run the bank for two decades, has spent this year remaking the ranks of his top lieutenants.

De Best worked alongside Rohrbaugh and Petno managing the finances of the division before winning Tuesday’s promotion.

She now takes charge of regulatory capital, earnings calls and investor relations for the bank’s global operations.

Wall Street investors track every move at JPMorgan’s Park Avenue headquarters in midtown Manhattan, searching for clues in Dimon’s succession race and possible intel on who will eventually run the global banking giant.
