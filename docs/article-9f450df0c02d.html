Erdoğan signs off on defense pact with Saudi Arabia, Pakistan ahead of Yemen talks

2026-11-27 13:36

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has given final approval to a mutual defense pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported on Friday, citing the Official Gazette.

The ratification decision was published in Turkey’s Official Gazette on Friday.

The Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, signed on August 7, treats an armed attack from outside the pact on one member as an attack on all three. It calls for immediate assistance and leaves the countries to determine what support to provide, including whether or not to use military resources.

The pact takes effect when the last instrument of ratification is deposited with the secretariat of the three countries’ Strategic, Political and Defense Committee. It remains in force indefinitely, with members able to withdraw after 10 years by giving the others at least three months’ written notice through diplomatic channels.

Turkey’s parliament approved the pact on November 5 by 277 votes to 118. Opposition lawmaker Hasan Karal of the New Path parliamentary group questioned what support Ankara might be called on to provide if Saudi Arabia came under attack from Yemen and called for a clear role for parliament in decisions involving combat missions.

The Iran-backed Houthis, who control Yemen’s capital, Sanaa, have targeted Saudi cities, oil facilities and shipping since fighting in Yemen’s civil war intensified in July.

Turkish officials have said Ankara is considering military assistance to Saudi Arabia and that Turkish troops would not be sent to fight in Yemen or attack the Houthis.

Pakistani Defense Minister Khawaja Asif told Agence France-Presse that the three countries’ foreign and defense ministers would discuss Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and how to put the pact into practice at Friday’s meeting in Islamabad, the third under the agreement. Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan was expected to attend.

At its first meeting in İstanbul on August 31, the committee agreed to establish a permanent secretariat in Saudi Arabia.

At an emergency meeting in Riyadh on October 5 following Houthi attacks on the kingdom, the three countries agreed to prepare for the rapid deployment of military forces to Saudi Arabia and named retired Pakistani Lt. Gen. Nauman Mahmood the pact’s first secretary-general.

The joint statement gave no troop numbers or deployment timetable.
