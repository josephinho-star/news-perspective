More questions than answers: Arch Manning likes the disrespect, Big 12's disaster and a CFP race in disarray

This time it was No. 2 BYU falling victim to the hangover, losing to UCF in double-overtime that turned into pure chaos over the final few minutes of regulation, after defeating Notre Dame last week.

And, maybe Steve Sarkisian was right this past week in the buildup to the Ole Miss showdown that everyone involved in the talk around Arch Manning, even if warranted, created a monster in the Texas quarterback.

It was also the Big 12 Conference that was at the forefront of madness this week, taking a page out of the SEC's playbook after last weekend, while both No. 15 Iowa and No. 16 USC lost in Big Ten play.

Did we expect Texas Tech to go on the road and lose to unranked Cincinnati?

No, but we also didn’t think the Red Raiders would look so lethargic in the process behind their expensive roster that is partially funded by mega-booster Cody Campbell.

One minute it's Arch Manning putting on a show for college football fans, and then it's the Longhorns going cold in the second half that has us all wondering why there is this tale of two halves with this Steve Sarkisian offense.

What we witnessed on Saturday night in Austin was essentially just another night in the SEC.

A back-and-forth slugfest between two teams that are capable of making a college football playoff run if everything is clicking. Somebody had to lose, and we still have plenty of questions lingering around both teams coming out of this one.

Trinidad Chambliss is still that dude, as he proved in the second half of this matchup, and Arch Manning is still trying to figure out how to play consistent football for four quarters.

Each team is still in the running for the SEC title game, while every conference team besides Georgia and Kentucky has two losses.

And yes, that list now includes Tennessee after a fourth quarter frenzy by South Carolina.

But, when the Vols couldn’t get out of their own way, South Carolina quarterback LaNorris Sellers and defensive end Dylan Stewart kicked the door down.

Another one of those hangovers, right? Maybe, but after a dominating performance against Alabama last week, one would’ve expected a better outing from the No. 11 ranked team in the country during the most important times of the game.

But, it was the Gamecocks who reeked havoc on the Vols once again at home, with head coach Shane Beamer even taking shots at those floating out conspiracy theories about the win over Tennessee in 2022 that was absorbed in talk of his team using signals stolen by Connor Stallions to win that game.

Yea, it got pretty weird on Saturday night in Columbia. But the only certain thing in the SEC is that nothing is for certain.

Big 12 Chaos: No. 2 BYU, No. 8 Texas Tech, No. 14 Houston lose

If the Red Raiders are expecting to still have a shot at making the college football playoff after losing on the road to Cincinnati, you can thank BYU for that glimmer of hope.

Judging by what we all witnessed, this Texas Tech team is obviously not in the same stratosphere as the 2025 roster, with Will Hammond struggling to complete passes against the Bearcats defense.

Maybe we can chalk that up to 'karma' for how things went down with former Cincinnati quarterback Brendan Sorsby in his transfer to Texas Tech before being ruled ineligible due to his sports gambling.

Who knows, but something is clearly wrong with this Joey McGuire football team on offense.

As for BYU, this squad is providing opportunities to others within the Big 12 to keep pace within the conference after UCF shocked the Cougars 39-31 in double-overtime.

Going on the road this season is clearly a trap for ranked teams, but coming off the win against Notre Dame last week in Provo, I don't imagine many folks thought the Cougars would fall into this category behind an offensive unit that had proven to be a problem through the first half of this season.

Now, we are in store for all sorts of craziness out of the Big 12, and we still have the 'Holy War' in the coming weeks between Utah and BYU.

Just when you thought the SEC and Big Ten were getting a little wild, it's the Big 12 that decided to devour itself on Saturday.

As for the Utah, it was the defense that stepped up in a major way on Saturday night, leading to the win over No. 14 Houston that has left the Big 12 conference in a very weird state.

What happens now when BYU and Utah meet in a few weeks in the 'Holy War'?

Houston suffered its second loss of the season, and it comes at a time when the Big 12 is still in its chaos mode.

Now that Texas Tech has also suffered a conference loss, we are headed towards a wild final week of October, which will lead to a chaotic month of November as teams fight for a spot in the college football playoff.

USC and Iowa also lose. Where are we headed on this wild 2026 ride?

I think the obvious answer is that we are living in a time where we don't know who the best teams in college football are.

We might think it's teams like Georgia, Ohio State, Notre Dame, Miami, Indiana and one or two others, but there are so many questions right now that it's hard to pin down just a few teams.

Next week could clear up a few questions about what Ohio State will look like without quarterback Julian Sayin, or how Georgia looks against another tough opponent after having the week off following the loss to Auburn.
