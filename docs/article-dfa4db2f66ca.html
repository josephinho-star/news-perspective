Democrats reveal details for 2028 presidential primary following midterm victories – US politics live

2026-11-23 11:08

Democrats are planning 12 presidential primary debates for what’s expected to be a crowded field of 2028 candidates, with the first scheduled for June, the Democratic National Committee has said.

The DNC’s announcement on Monday comes before major candidates have launched campaigns to succeed term-limited Republican president Donald Trump and follows the party’s convincing midterm performance, AP reported.

DNC Chairman Ken Martin said in a statement the party is aimed at staying united and helping the eventual nominee win the White House. “The DNC is keeping our foot on the gas as we head into the 2028 cycle,” he said.

Anticipating a large number of presidential hopefuls, the DNC said it will cap participation in each debate to 20 qualifying candidates over two nights, if needed.

The criteria for making the debate stage will be similar to 2020, when there was also a big field and a dozen debates, the DNC said, and will include meeting polling and other criteria such as grassroots fundraising.

More details are set to be announced in January, including specific times and locations for the debates, the DNC said, though the party confirmed that five debates will be in 2027 and seven in 2028. The final primary debate is planned for March 2028.

For the first time, candidates must pledge to reject dark money as a condition of making the debate stage. The new requirement is part of a DNC effort to curb the influence of outside groups that can spend unlimited amounts to support or oppose candidates without having to reveal funding sources.

The debate schedule is one of the most closely watched decisions the DNC makes, along with selecting the city for the 2028 convention. Earlier this year, the DNC narrowed the list of cities to Boston, Denver and Philadelphia.

Donald Trump has headlined his third cryptocurrency gala at his country club in Virginia, rewarding top holders of his own meme coin, as Democrats accuse the president of improperly profiting from his crypto ventures. The dinner, which took place at the Trump National golf club in Sterling, Virginia, included meet-and-greets with Trump supporters including the boxing champion Floyd Mayweather, the Shark Tank investor Kevin O’Leary, the NBA champion Tristan Thompson and the magician Lior Suchard, according to the event’s website.

Michigan US senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed on Sunday doubled down on naming healthcare reform legislation after Donald Trump as long as it meant the president would not impede its passage. “If he’s open to the conversation, I am open to it, too,” El-Sayed said of Trump on Sunday. “If the president is opening to signing that bill, let’s get it done.”

Jay Clayton, the US director of national intelligence, said he trusts Trump “completely” after the US president called Vladimir Putin “a friend” during their recent meeting in China. Clayton made the remarks on Sunday, where he was pressed during media appearances on Trump’s warm characterization of the Russian president – who is viewed as a chief US adversary – following his trilateral summit with Putin and China’s Xi Jinping.

Invoking Greek mythology, US senator John Kennedy has characterized former Nvidia chief executive officer Jensen Huang’s reported affair with a suspected Chinese spy as “the most expensive sex since Helen of Troy”. The Louisiana Republican’s allusion to the legendary figure whose desirability was such that it caused the mythic Trojan war came during an interview on NBC’s Meet the Press on Sunday.

Democrats have won their largest majority in the House in more than three decades after Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina on Sunday, extending the blue wave that swept the US midterms and increasing pressure on the party to deliver its promises to US voters. House Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in a midterm election sweep unseen since 1992, gaining 44 House seats so far, four more than the 40 they picked up in Donald Trump’s first midterm in 2018.

Trump administration deported US citizen to Mexico, DoJ admits in court

The Trump administration recently admitted in federal court that it deported a US citizen to Mexico after American authorities incorrectly concluded he was an undocumented immigrant.

Attorneys for the US justice department made that admission on Friday while requesting the dismissal of a lawsuit for damages that the deported man – Brian José Morales García of Austin, Texas – has been pursuing against the federal government.

Morales García’s case took that turn as the White House has faced bipartisan criticism for the aggressive mass deportation campaign pursued by Donald Trump throughout his second presidency – widely seen as a factor in the electoral battering his Republican party suffered during the 3 November midterms, costing it control of Congress.

The Denver-born, Mexico-raised Morales García was riding with his boss to his job as an air-conditioning installation technician on 3 April when they were pulled over by state and local officers near Fredericksburg, Texas.

Morales García was his boss’s passenger and did not have his identification on him. The 25-year-old also told the officers that he had his birth certificate and social security card at home, but they didn’t believe him – so they called immigration agents, who detained Morales García.

Court filings recount how Robert Gil Jr, a US Customs and Border Protection agent, consulted records from Mexico that listed a Mexican birthplace for Morales García. Gil Jr also accused Morales García of saying he was born in Mexico and observed that he only spoke in Spanish, according to court records.

Morales García was deported on 7 April, with the Trump administration later accusing him in a media statement of being in the country illegally.
