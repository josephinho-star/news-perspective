Top Democrats raise corruption concerns after Trump involvement in Jensen Huang’s Nvidia reinstatement – live

2026-11-23 22:37

Robert Garcia, top Democrat on House oversight, and Chris Van Hollen, vying for Senate leadership, join Republicans in expressing concern at return of CEO felled by affair scandal and Trump administration’s involvement

Van Hollen says Huang’s reinstatement reveals 'political corruption and rot at the heart of the Trump administration’s big tech and AI policy'

Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat vying to become the next Senate majority leader, said Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia chief executive is “exhibit A” of the “political corruption and rot at the heart of the Trump administration’s big tech and AI policy”.

On a call with reporters on Monday, Van Hollen said he plans to focus on Huang’s “efforts to increase and accelerate the export of high-end chips to China, how he’s been accomplishing that, how he’s been getting his way with the administration” when the Nvidia chief appears before the Senate banking committee next week.

“It’s completely hypocritical for the Trump administration, on the one hand, to say the reason we have to take all the brakes off AI development here at home is because China will leap ahead if we don’t,” Van Hollen said. “When in fact, it’s the Trump administration that’s helping China leap ahead.”

The senator pointed to Nvidia’s $25m donation to Trump’s White House ballroom project, the president’s personal holdings of Nvidia stock, Huang’s contribution to Trump’s inauguration fund and his attendance at a $1m-a-plate dinner at Mar-a-Lago as evidence of the executive’s influence over the administration’s advanced-chip policy.

Van Hollen told the Guardian on Monday it was a “good sign” that some Republican lawmakers had expressed alarm over Huang’s reinstatement.

“Frankly, it’s hard not to,” he said. “But given the last couple of years, when Republican senators buried their heads in the sand regardless of what the Trump administration was doing, it does seem to be a good sign that they recognize this as a threat to our national security.”

Van Hollen added: “The fact that this individual was able to use the president to secure his reinstatement at Nvidia is a problem that I think Republican senators can see as well. So hopefully they will join us in moving forward to close the loopholes in our export-control laws and take other action on an urgent basis.”

If elected majority leader in next week’s closed-door Democratic leadership contest, Van Hollen said that addressing the expansion of AI should be a priority during the new Congress’ first 100 days. He is also backing a Senate rules reform proposal that would allow “expedited consideration” of bipartisan AI legislation emerging from committees.

The senator also said he supports penalties for technology companies that “develop agents that can endanger the public” and creating a joint congressional committee to examine “all of the different elements of how AI will impact our society and our world”.

Newsom responds to Trump's attack on his commutations, noting his demand for wrongfully convicted Central Park Five to be executed

Shortly before Donald Trump attacked him for changing every California death sentence to a sentence of life in prison without parole, California’s governor, Gavin Newsom, released a scathing video statement in which he criticized Trump for his use of the pardon power, and for demanding the execution of five wrongfully convicted young New Yorkers of color in 1989 for a brutal rape they did not commit.

“Today, Donald Trump is once again standing inside the Department of Justice next to his former personal defense lawyer who is now the attorney general giving a speech about protecting American families. So, let’s talk about protecting families,” Newsom said. “Donald, in 1989, you took out full page ads screaming for the death penalty for five black and Latino teenagers before a trial, before a shred of evidence. They were innocent. DNA proved it. You never apologized.”

“To the families standing with him today, your grief is real. That’s why every person, every person on California’s former death row will die in prison. No one walks out,” the governor continued.

“Donald Trump’s criminals, they walk out,” he went on. “A billionaire charged with bribery; his family hands Trump’s lawyer a $50 million deal while she’s working in that very building; weeks later, pardoned. A man who stole $10 million from nursing home workers’ paychecks; his mother buys a seat at a million-dollar Mar-a-Lago dinner; less than three weeks later, pardoned. He never served a day. Contractors convicted of killing 14 Iraqi civilians, including two children, pardoned. A man who attacked police on January 6th, pardoned. Then he pleaded guilty to soliciting what he thought was a 15-year-old girl for sex and walked free.”

Newsom also mentioned Trump’s pardoning of the former president of Honduras, Juan Orlando Hernández, and noted that he had “moved 400 tons of cocaine into American communities”.

“Donald Trump doesn’t oppose mercy; he sells it,” Newsom added. “If you’re poor, Black or Brown, you get the needle. If you’re rich and know Donald Trump, you get a pardon and a personal phone call.”

“A justice department that prosecutes his enemies and pardons himself and his friends, that’s not law enforcement, that’s a protection racket,” Newsom also said of the president who criticized his clemency decisions. “We need foundational federal reform starting with this: no president can ever again turn the power of mercy into a business.”

Trump brings families of violent crime victims to Department of Justice to rail against Newsom's commutations

Donald Trump just finished speaking at the Department of Justice in Washington DC.

In his remarks, the president, whose first intervention in public policy was a 1989 open letter calling for five young men of color who were wrongly accused of the brutal rape of a white female jogger in New York City’s Central Park to be executed, devoted his remarks to railing against California governor Gavin Newsom’s decision to commute the death sentences of hundreds of California inmates this month.

As he did during all three of his campaigns for the presidency, Trump spoke in graphic terms about violent crime, dwelling on gruesome details of the murders carried out by the California inmates – who will remain in prison without the possibility of parole.

Trump also invited the families of some victims to share their outrage at what seemed more like a campaign event than a governing one, given that the federal government has no power to force states to carry out death sentences for state crimes.

Rather than announce some specific action that his administration planned to take in response to Newsom’s clemency decisions, Trump instead said that he would direct the Department of Justice to act more quickly to execute federal inmates.

“I will sign a presidential directive ordering the Department of Justice to seek the death penalty for child murderers, as well as rapists and human traffickers who kill their victims. My order also directs the attorney general to expedite the execution of federal death sentences. Once juries have spoken, capital punishment will be swift and certain,” Trump said.

The event seemed to underscore what a White House directed by Stephen Miller, the new chief of staff, is likely to look like for the next two years. Miller, who wrote many of Trump’s speeches during his campaigns, is, like his boss, intensely focused on the idea of using violent crime as a political issue, by suggesting that Democrats are soft on crime and will fail to keep Americans safe.

Trump says Newsom's commutations for death row inmates 'should not be allowed to stand'

Speaking at the US Department of Justice on Monday, Donald Trump just said that the commutations of death row inmates by California’s governor, Gavin Newsom, “should not be allowed to stand”.

As he has throughout his time in public life, Trump focused his remarks on the most graphic details of violent crimes.

“Just days ago, the governor of California, Gavin Newsom, granted clemency in mercy to 548 of the most evil monsters on the face of the Earth,” Trump said.

“Among the serial killers he spared is Charles Eng, who tortured, enslaved and slaughtered at least 11 people, including babies, in a torture chamber and a remote cabin, as well as the South Side Slayer, who killed 14 women and enjoyed every moment of it”, the president added.

“To every parent, son, daughter, sibling or spouse here today whose loved one was brutally killed by those monsters, we love you, we support you, and we are with you and your quest for us. Really the the ultimate of final justice. We have to do this. We can’t let this happen. What happened to you, what happened to all of us, should not be allowed to stand”, Trump said.

He then invited some of the victims’ family members to speak about the gruesome crimes that were committed against their loved ones and criticize Newsom for commuting the death sentences in their cases.

Families of victims whose sentences were commuted by Newsom gather for Trump's remarks

Donald Trump is about to speak at the US Department of Justice. He was introduced by Todd Blanche, his former criminal defense lawyer now serving as US attorney general.

Trump is expected to criticize California’s governor, Gavin Newsom, who announced this month that he had decided to commute every California death sentence to a sentence of life in prison without parole. The offenders will remain in prison and are not eligible for release.

Blanche said that the audience assembled for Trump’s remarks include “some of the families of the victims whose lives were forever changed by the vicious behavior of the death row inmates, whose sentences have now been commuted by Governor Newsom.”

Senator Jack Reed, the top Democrat on the Senate armed services committee and a member of the Senate banking committee, said in a statement that Jensen Huang’s “abrupt reinstatement is troubling on many levels”.

The Rhode Island senator continued:

This case raises serious issues about ethics, misconduct, corruption, national security, the integrity of our technology and military supply chains under the Trump Administration’s watch, and whether NVIDIA itself is ‘too big to fail’ or was Mr. Huang too powerful to fire?

The American people deserve answers from Mr. Huang, NVIDIA, and the Trump Administration. Congress must act on a bipartisan basis to get to the bottom of this about-face. Mr. Huang should disclose all communications between himself and President Trump. The NVIDIA board should release a transcript of its call with President Trump.

Public reporting suggests President Trump chose not to act when our intelligence services – led by National Security Adviser Marco Rubio – first briefed him on Mr. Huang’s involvement with a suspected Chinese operative. But he was willing to twist arms and exert his influence when it came to bringing Mr. Huang back to power. Why?

Reed also called on US treasury secretary Scott Bessent and commerce secretary Howard Lutnick to appear before the Senate banking committee, which is scheduled to hear testimony from Huang next Thursday.

Congressman Jimmy Gomez, a California Democrat who lost his Los Angeles area seat to democratic socialist Angela Gonzales-Torres in a landslide earlier this month, reportedly plans to resign from Congress next week, after the release of a House ethics committee report that found he had “engaged in sexual misconduct … including making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer”.

“I recognize the seriousness of the Ethics Committee’s report and as I previously stated I take full responsibility for the personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage,” Gomez told Politico. He also said that he plans to resign on 1 December.

The US commerce department plans to add Singapore-based chip firm Megaspeed, which is closely tied to an espionage investigation involving Nvidia, to its trade blacklist on Monday, Reuters and Semafor report.

The expected addition of the company follows revelations that off-again, on-again Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and Megaspeed’s controlling shareholder who is reportedly alleged to be a spy for Beijing.

US companies cannot ship goods, software and technology to companies on the list without a license, which is likely to be denied.

The New York Times reported on Monday that Alice Huang “kept in contact with an official of China’s top spy agency for more than a year while she was carrying out a secret affair with Jensen Huang, according to multiple people with knowledge of U.S. intelligence reports that describe the encounters”.

The US justice department is investigating whether Megaspeed violated US export controls by shipping Nvidia chips to China.

As Semafor notes, the US commerce secretary, Howard Lutnick, has been under pressure to act since the news of Jensen Huang’s relationship with the former reproter for Chinese state television who became a director at Singapore-based Megaspeed in 2023 became public.

The US treasury secretary, Scott Bessent, said earlier this month that his agency along with the Pentagon, the state department and the US trade representative’s office had all wanted Megaspeed on the blacklist “for months” but that “Commerce holds the pen”.

'I’m terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this,' Jensen Huang tells Nvidia employees

As our colleagues on the business desk report, Jensen Huang returned to an enthusiastic welcome at Nvidia following his reinstatement as CEO on Monday.

Video shared on Instagram showed that Huang’s brief remarks to employees, who cheered and chanted his name, were recorded by dozens of onlookers.

“I want to thank all of you. I’m terribly sorry for, for putting you through all of this,” Huang said. “I’m terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this. I’ve made some very terrible mistakes. And I’m going to work on coming back.”

“Thank you all for the second chance,” he added. “I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around.”

The House ethics committee said in a 19-page report released on Monday that there is “substantial evidence” that Democratic congressman Jimmy Gomez of California “engaged in sexual misconduct … including making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer”.

The report said what Gomez described as “one sole occasion” of “consensual kissing” was described as “forceful” sexual contact by the former congressional aide.

“Representative Gomez’s conduct caused a traumatic and ongoing chain of interactions for” the woman, the committee reported, “which, combined with a broader culture of sexual misconduct on the Hill, drove her from government service”.

“Representative Gomez’s conduct also reflects a broader environment of sexual misconduct and toxic working conditions in some Capitol Hill offices which the Committee is determined to address,” the report stated.

The committee did not recommend any particular sanction for Gomez, who has served in the House since 2017. It said the Constitution charges the House with policing the behavior of its own members.

“The House should take whatever action it deems appropriate in light of the Committee’s findings,” the ethics panel said.

Top oversight Democrat says Trump intervention 'raises serious national security and corruption questions'

Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House oversight committee, said in a statement on Monday that Donald Trump’s “direct involvement” with Nvidia reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO “raises serious national security and corruption questions”.

“President Trump’s direct involvement with Nvidia’s decision to reinstate CEO Jensen Huang raises serious national security and corruption questions,” Garcia said.

“We must investigate these actions. No president who has invested in a private company should be involved in their board decisions. Congress must come together in swift bipartisan action and demand answers immediately.”

Hakeem Jeffries, the incoming House majority leader, denounced Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO after his alleged affair with a Chinese spy, calling it “indefensible” and “irresponsible”.

“The decision by the NVIDIA board to reinstate Jensen Huang is indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible. Congress will not be deterred from conducting a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation into business malfeasance by NVIDIA and corrupt abuse of power by Howard Lutnick and the Department of Commerce,” Jeffries said in a statement.

“We will not allow America’s national security to be sold out by the Trump Administration for personal financial gain. The National Defense Authorization Act must include ironclad export control legislation to prevent powerful AI technology from being exploited by the Chinese Communist Party and other adversaries,” he added.

Jeffries called on his Republican colleagues to join Democrats in “reining in this corruption”.

Donald Trump and JD Vance played a central role in helping Jensen Huang, the Nvidia CEO, be reinstated to his position after Huang briefly stepped down following allegations of having an affair with a Chinese spy, the New York Times reported.

Over the weekend, Trump asked Vance, the vice-president, to speak directly with Huang and work with Nvidia’s board to assure them Huang was not a national security risk, those familiar with the matter told reporters Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan.

Vance has close ties to several Silicon Valley executives, including David Sacks, a close ally to Huang who also co-chairs the President’s Council of Advisers on Science and Technology.

However, only a small number of top US officials knew of the conversations that took place over the weekend, according to the report.

Pete Ricketts, a senator from Nebraska, wrote on X that Huang’s “behavior does not support reinstatement”.

Ricketts, who sits on the Senate foreign relations committee, said Huang still needed to testify before Congress and Nvidia still needed to answer questions about national security concerns.

John Curtis, a senator from Utah who sits on the Senate commerce committee, noted that Nvidia’s board relied on information from a government official to reinstate Huang.

“Nvidia’s board says it relied on input from senior U.S. officials to conclude that bringing Jensen Huang back posed no national security risk … Who in our government weighed in? What did they tell the board? And on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?” Curtis asked.

John Cornyn, an outgoing senator from Texas, posted several times on social media, questioning whether the FBI did a defensive briefing on Huang, whether that information was ignored, Nvidia’s corporate governance and more.

A former top aide to Pete Hegseth, the defense secretary, said he should resign, accusing the secretary of being more concerned with his own image than leading the Department of Defense.

“Pete’s ability to be effective has been constrained and very soon he’ll have lost any ability to be effective,” Joe Kasper, the former chief of staff to Hegseth, told Politico in an interview published on Monday.

“For that reason and others, it’s time for Pete to call it quits,” he added.

Kasper told Politico that Hegseth was “all about Pete rather than the institution and the men and women who support it”.

“There’s quite an emphasis on the showmanship,” Kasper said. “The virtues of humility and basically service before self and those things have disappeared. I didn’t see those at the start, and see them ever less so now.”

The Guardian has asked the defense department for comment.

Huang to testify before Senate banking committee on 3 December

Jensen Huang, the recently reinstated Nvidia CEO, will testify before the Senate banking committee on 3 December, the committee announced on Monday.

The open hearing is called “Protecting America’s AI Advantage: Oversight of the US Export Controls”.

It’s one of several congressional hearings that lawmakers launched after reports accused Huang of having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, leading Huang to briefly step down from Nvidia and roiling US stock markets.

Though Huang has been reinstated, with the help of the US government, lawmakers still have questions about the situation.

Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the committee, wrote on X that the “reckless decision to bring back Jensen Huang does not change the fact that Huang and NVIDIA still owe Congress answers”.

Warren wrote that the administration “needs to explain what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials to support Huang’s reinstatement”.

Andy Kim, a New Jersey senator who serves on the committee, wrote on X: “Americans deserve answers about what NVIDIA and the Trump administration knew, when they knew it, and whether sufficient steps were taken to prevent sensitive technology from falling into the wrong hands.”
