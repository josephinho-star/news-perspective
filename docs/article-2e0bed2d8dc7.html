Lesley Stahl ‘would never quit’ ‘60 Minutes’ despite reported tiff with CBS News’ Bari Weiss: sources

2026-10-13 20:10

She’s “addicted to the limelight,” in the words of Jay-Z.

Lesley Stahl would “never quit” CBS News’ “60 Minutes” — despite a report that the star correspondent mulled leaving the show after a flap with the network’s Editor-in-Chief Bari Weiss, sources told The Post on Tuesday.

Weiss and her deputy Charles Forelle became “very, very hands on” editing Stah’s segment examining Israeli settlers in the West Bank, prompting Stahl to privately consider quitting the show, the Status newsletter reported Monday night.

Status reported that Forelle suggested tabling the segment, but Stahl strongly objected and got her way with the piece, which aired Sunday.

CBS News called Status’ report “ludicrous,” adding that Stahl’s West Bank segment went through a “rigorous editorial process.”

“She’s not going anywhere,” said one CBS insider, who said that Stahl is “addicted” to being on the newsmagazine.

The person explained that Stahl — like most other TV anchors — craves the spotlight.

“It’s her life,” the source said. “What’s she going to do, give lectures like [former correspondents] Scott Pelley and Sharyn Alfonsi?”

The source was referring to a Columbia Journalism School lecture series by Stahl’s former colleagues, who are now journalists in residence there — noting that the duo have become “irrelevant.”

“If she didn’t leave then, she ain’t gonna leave now,” one insider told The Post.

Regardless of whether Stahl privately sounded off potentially quitting, her dustup with Weiss, a self-described “Zionist fanatic” — appeared to reveal a shift in the power dynamic at CBS News, sources opined.

“Bari is caught between in a rock and a hard place, and Lesley knows that,” one insider said, pointing to Weiss’ row last year with Alfonsi over a story about President Trump’s controversial deportation of illegal immigrants to the high-security El Salvadorian prison CECOT.

“I assume she got the message that she needs to tone things down,” said a former CBS exec. “I don’t believe Ellison wants to be dragged deeper into every little upset at the network.”
