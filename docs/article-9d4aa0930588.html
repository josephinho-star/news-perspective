Turkey jails 2 journalists, puts 2 under house arrest over fund crisis ‘misinformation’

2026-10-10 11:35

Turkish courts jailed two journalists pending trial on Friday and ordered house arrest for two others on Saturday over allegations of spreading misinformation about the country’s investment fund scandal.

Turkey has been grappling with an investment fund crisis that erupted in mid-September, prompting an investigation into allegations of market manipulation.

The scandal has put pressure on President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP), with one senior party official resigning after allegations about her investment activities.

The main opposition New Party on Thursday questioned whether two ministers had profited from investing in the funds under investigation.

Trade Minister Ömer Bolat and Family and Social Services Minister Mahinur Özdemir Göktaş have denied wrongdoing.

The four journalists were questioned over reporting and social media posts that raised questions about the involvement of AKP figures.

A court on Friday ordered Mustafa Balcı, an editor at the Sözcü newspaper, and Şaban Sevinç, editor-in-chief of the online news channel Bizim TV, to be jailed pending trial on accusations of “publicly disseminating misleading information.”

Their case concerns allegations linking AKP spokesman Ömer Çelik to Osman Arıkan Nacar, a suspect in the fund investigation.

Balcı had shared a photograph on X showing Nacar sitting one row behind Çelik at a concert in the coastal town of Bodrum on August 22. He denied spreading false information, explaining that the photograph had previously been published and that his post identified the allegations as such.

Journalists İsmail Saymaz and Murat Ağırel, who appear on Sözcü TV, were initially released Friday with overseas travel bans and requirements to report to police.

Another court ordered house arrest for both journalists on Saturday after İstanbul prosecutors challenged the earlier decision, the Sözcü newspaper reported.

Bolat had filed a criminal complaint against the two over a broadcast questioning his links to the scandal.

Earlier, AKP secretary-General Eyyüp Kadir İnan accused the opposition and the media of targeting the party with a “web of lies.”

“We will absolutely not let our party and government be targeted, whether by the New [Party] or the journalists they paid to act as their mouthpiece,” İnan told the private NTV television station.

“Legally, we will hold them accountable for all attacks on our party.”

The crisis erupted when several funds could not repay investors seeking to withdraw their money.

The Capital Markets Board (SPK), Turkey’s securities regulator, ordered the liquidation of 131 investment funds managed by seven companies, affecting 455,758 investors. Liquidation involves selling the funds’ assets to repay investors.

The funds had reported assets worth about $17 billion.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek said investigators had identified 214 individuals and legal entities that earned a combined 187.6 billion lira ($3.8 billion) through funds managed by three firms: Tera, Pusula and Hedef.

Investors in Tera-managed funds earned more than 90 billion lira, he told the pro-government Sabah newspaper.

Investigators were examining investors who sold their fund holdings in the days before the crisis became public, suspecting that some had acted on advance information, Gürlek said.

Prosecutors sent their names to the Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF), which began notifying them to return the money to an account established to collect repayments, he added.

It was unclear whether those notified would face prosecution.

The AKP on Friday submitted a bill to parliament governing fund liquidation and investor repayments, but there was no immediate announcement of a voting date.

Göktaş also filed criminal complaints against three New Party lawmakers over the allegations concerning her family and vowed to pursue “media outlets that spread these allegations.”

“Özgür Özel and his colleagues will pay the price for these lies and slanders,” İnan warned, referring to the New Party leader.

With reporting by Agence France-Presse
