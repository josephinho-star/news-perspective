Protests erupt in İzmir after mayor defects to Erdoğan’s ruling party

2026-10-08 08:46

More than 1,000 protesters gathered in Turkey’s opposition stronghold of İzmir on Wednesday after Mayor Cemil Tugay defected to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP).

The move marked a major symbolic victory for Erdoğan, who has long sought control of the secular city of 4.5 million people on Turkey’s Aegean coast, governed by the opposition since 1999.

Erdoğan announced Tugay’s decision at a meeting in Ankara, personally welcoming the mayor at a celebratory event broadcast live on television.

The announcement sparked anger in İzmir, Turkey’s third-largest city, where police barricaded the main squares ahead of an evening protest.

As night fell, protesters waving Turkish flags gathered by the barriers around Cumhuriyet Square, chanting “Tugay, resign!” and “Tugay, the traitor!” Riot police and at least four water cannon trucks watched the crowd.

Some demonstrators carried a makeshift coffin bearing the words “Politically dead,” referring to Tugay’s political career.

“This is a bad day for İzmir … Taking votes from the people then breaking away like this is irresponsible,” said Melihat Ağgül Özdoğan, 60.

“The vote I cast was wasted,” she told Agence France-Presse.

“We don’t want a mayor we did not choose. When we elected Tugay, we voted for a party,” said another protester, Yasemin Köseer, 51.

“We do not want AKP to impose its rule on İzmir. People across the country have had enough of the government.”

Addressing the crowd, Çağatay Güç, head of the İzmir branch of the main opposition New Party, challenged Tugay.

“You have been dismissed, Cemil Tugay, you are not the mayor. You sit there with the votes of the nation, but today this nation has removed you from that position,” he said.

“Today you are a big zero in the eyes of the nation.”

Police repeatedly warned protesters to disperse. Officers briefly chased a small group, but the demonstrators eventually dispersed peacefully.

Tugay’s defection gives the AKP the mayorship of a city long governed by opposition social democrats, but it does not give the party control of the opposition-dominated municipal council.

Around a dozen cultural figures resigned in protest, including the artistic director of İzmir’s municipal theaters, nine members of the municipal theater advisory board and a poet who runs the İzmir International Literature Festival, BirGün newspaper reported.

The defection comes amid an intensifying crackdown on Turkey’s opposition, with arrests and lawsuits targeting mayors and other elected officials in cases widely seen as politically motivated.

The crackdown began about six months after the Republican People’s Party (CHP), then the main opposition party, defeated the AKP in the March 2024 local elections.

Since then, six of the party’s provincial mayors have been arrested, including İstanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, along with 29 district mayors, 13 of them in İstanbul, independent news website Diken reported.

At least 17 opposition mayors have switched to the AKP, Medyascope news website reported on September 30.

Tugay became mayor in April 2024 but resigned from the CHP in June this year after a controversial court ruling replaced its leadership.

Ousted CHP leader Özgür Özel subsequently left the party and founded the New Party, which effectively replaced the CHP as Turkey’s main opposition force.

Tugay did not follow Özel, saying he would remain independent and fueling speculation that he planned to join the AKP.

The New Party holds 91 seats on İzmir’s 184-member municipal council, while the CHP holds 35. Tugay’s defection increases the AKP’s representation from 38 to 39 seats.
