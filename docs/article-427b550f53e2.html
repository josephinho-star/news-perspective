Michigan AD's first move should be to fire Kyle Whittingham and write a $24 million buyout check: Trey Wallace

2026-11-19 19:13

Is it time for Michigan to pull the plug on the Kyle Whittingham experiment after just one season?

That’s the question looming in Ann Arbor after the school hired Texas A&M athletic director Trev Alberts to the same position this week.

Known for his business decisions over the past few years at both Nebraska and in College Station, Alberts is being brought in to right the Wolverines ship, which could include a decision as to whether Kyle Whittingham is the right man to lead Michigan into 2027.

At 5-5, the Wolverines have given fans little to get excited about. What was supposed to be a season of stability has instead delivered expensive mistakes, frustrating football and a series of cringeworthy moments from the head coach.

Trev Alberts hired to clean up Wolverine mistakes, and football program

When outgoing athletic director Warde Manuel hired Whittingham following the Sherrone Moore debacle, the expectation was straightforward: Stabilize the program and win football games.

Nearly eleven months later, Michigan fans are wondering how Moore managed a better record in 2025 than Whittingham has delivered in 2026 with such an expensive football team.

With two regular-season games remaining, including the showdown with Ohio State after playing No. 4 UCLA this weekend, those questions aren’t going away.

It probably doesn’t help that Michigan spent upward of $40 million on this roster, which included a lucrative deal for quarterback Bryce Underwood that is currently being wasted after losing the starting job.

Oh, and Michigan will assuredly have to overpay for players this transfer portal season thanks to a disappointing 2026. Sure, the right price goes a long way, but these athletes would also like to compete for a championship.

So, the Wolverines could be put in a spot where paying 30% more for a player is the only way to get them to sign, if Whittingham returns in 2027 with the same structure.

Oh, and the Wolverines also fired offensive coordinator Jason Beck after just ten games into the season.

Did I mention that Michigan is also having to pay former offensive coordinator Jason Beck nearly $10 million to leave, thanks to his buyout?

Simply put, this has been a disaster for the Michigan football program.

And no, it doesn’t feel as though it's going to get any better over the next year.

So when Michigan decided that Trev Alberts was the right man for the athletic director position, the conversations quickly shifted as to whether Whittingham would return in 2027.

Wolverines have to make a tough decision on Kyle Whittingham

During his interview process with the school, multiple sources told OutKick that Michigan made it clear that it wanted Alberts to conduct an internal review of the football program that included what steps needed to be taken to ensure a profitable future.

That's not good news for Whittingham, unless you're handling his financial accounts.

After signing a five-year deal that includes around a $24 million buyout if fired without cause, the Michigan head coach is sitting in a comfortable position in terms of his checking account.

But, that doesn’t mean he's fine with having his legacy take a hit based off one season in Ann Arbor.

Unfortunately for Whittingham, that will not be his decision.

If Michigan feels as though Trev Alberts can put together a plan that will get the Wolverines back on track with a potential new head coach, which would be the fourth in six years, the school might need to just pay the buyout and start over.

His first season at Michigan has already led to questions about whether he would like to remain the head coach in 2027, amid this abysmal situation.

"Yeah. But I guess anything I say, you can say, ‘Oh, that means he wants to get fired.’ To me, that’s ridiculous," Whittingham responded. "Do I want to continue? Yes sir, I would like to continue. … That might be the most bizarre thing I’ve heard there."

"I don't know who spun it that way, but like I said, you can take anything and spin it any way you want and try to get the desired outcome you're looking for. That was not the intention."

There are plenty of coaches in college football that would take the Michigan job, and certainly understand what it entails during this period of transition in Ann Arbor.

Are we about to have that sort of conversation, again? If Kyle Whittingham loses to both UCLA and Ohio State over the next two weeks, that call could be made for him.

But, this is the reason why Trev Alberts was hired to lead the Michigan athletic department.

It might be an expensive decision, but the Wolverines don't have time to sit around and wait for Whittingham to figure out how to right the Michigan ship.
