Gérard Depardieu drops appeal against sexual assault conviction

2026-11-16 13:40

French film star Gérard Depardieu has dropped his appeal against a conviction for sexual assault, admitting that his behaviour towards women had often been offensive and offering a "deep and sincere" apology.

On the day that the appeal was due to start in a Paris court, the 77-year-old actor's unexpected mea culpa marks a dramatic reversal of his previous denials of sexual impropriety.

"Justice was pronounced. I was found guilty. I understand and bow to this conviction… I have therefore decided to abandon the appeal," he wrote.

It means he is now guilty in law of abusing two women during a film shoot in Paris five years ago, and his 18-month suspended prison term stands.

"Victory is ours, and we welcome it with head held high," said one of the two women, named only as Amélie, who was at the court.

"There is huge relief because the fight is over. The giant of film has fallen," said the other woman in the case, who is known in court by the false name Sarah.

In his statement, Depardieu appeared to have undergone a major shift from his earlier stance of unapologetic intransigence.

"For a long time on film shoots I hid myself behind provocation and smut. I thought that the remarks I made, and certain gestures that I made, were of no consequence.

"I see now that there is a border between impertinence and offence. Never, ever did I intend to hurt the women with whom I came into contact. But I see now that intention has no bearing on the hurt that people feel.

"Cases before the courts are damaging. Everything got out of hand… including my defence. Many people have suffered in this story. I have not the power to go back in time. I have come to understand that I belonged to a world that has disappeared."

The reference to his defence "getting out of hand" follows widespread criticism of the aggressive line taken by Depardieu's lawyer during the original trial in March last year.

His new lawyers, Delphine Meillet and David-Olivier Kaminski, said that with his admission of guilt Depardieu wanted to "move on", adding that "basically, as he turns 78 years old, he is saying to himself that life is more beautiful than 10 days of court hearings".

In the original trial, Depardieu was said to have touched the two women on intimate parts of the body during the making of Les Volets Verts, which is a film about an ageing actor coming to terms with his declining powers.

He himself admitted to using vulgar language but denied being "a metro-train groper". However, he also said he did not consider touching a woman's buttocks to be an act of "sexual aggression".

The trial was the first time the star of films like Green Card and Cyrano de Bergerac appeared in court on sexual assault charges. Several other women have made similar allegations in the media.

A decision on whether an alleged rape case can come to trial is expected this month.
