Macron hosts security council meeting ahead of planned weekend of protests – Europe live

2026-10-22 07:52

Morning opening: Macron hosts a security council meeting on protests

French president Emmanuel Macron is back from his tour of the Gulf and convening a meeting with relevant ministers and intelligence services to figure out what can be done to prevent unsavoury scenes on the streets of French cities as more protests are planned for this Saturday.

“The government must hold its nerve,” Macron told reporters earlier this week.

He is expected to speak after the meeting, so once to watch.

Later today, French prime minister Sébastien Lecornu will hold a separate meeting with party leaders to discuss their concerns, including about policing and the next year’s presidential campaign.

Over in Ukraine, there are no signs of Russian attacks stopping anytime soon, after a night of “massive strikes” on port infrastructure facilities in Odesa region and on Kyiv.

Ukraine’s foreign minister Andii Sybiha commented on the attacks, saying:

“Kyiv residents heard what Russian ‘humanism’ truly sounds like: a brutal attack with dozens of ballistic missiles striking energy infrastructure, medical facilities, shopping centers, and residential areas. Other cities across Ukraine came under attack as well.

Appeasement, servility, and flattery will not stop this war. Only strength will.”

No prizes for guessing who he may be addressing these comments to.

Thailand, Malaysia urge citizens to avoid protest-hit EU countries

Thailand has advised its citizens to postpone non-essential travel to parts of Europe rocked by protests, while Malaysia also warned its nationals about visiting France, AFP reported.

“Those travelling from Thailand to Europe must closely monitor the situation as it is changing rapidly and France remains a concern as the situation is still ongoing,” Thai foreign minister Sihasak Phungketkeow told reporters late Wednesday during a trip to Italy.

He cited concerns about protests in Italy, noting his Italian counterpart had to cancel their bilateral due to an urgent government cabinet meeting on the unrest, adding: “We are also worried about Spain.”

“If it’s not necessary, with everything going on in those countries, I think it’s better to delay the trip for a while.”

The Malaysian embassy in Paris had on Sunday advised Malaysians in France “to exercise caution and remain vigilant” due to the protests.

China warned its citizens travelling to France to take safety precautions, state broadcaster CCTV reported Monday, after town halls and shops were looted and set on fire at the weekend.
