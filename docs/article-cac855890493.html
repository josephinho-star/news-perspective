Cypriot leaders agree to open two new crossings on divided island

2026-11-10 10:10

The Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot leaders agreed Monday to open two new crossing points on the divided island as part of efforts to revive reunification talks.

Outgoing UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres launched a new drive in July to resolve the decades-old conflict, proposing additional crossings as a confidence-building measure.

The Mediterranean island has been divided since 1974, when Turkey invaded and seized its northern third following a coup backed by Greece’s then-military government.

Decades of negotiations have failed to reunify Cyprus, with the last formal round collapsing in 2017. But the election last year of the north’s first pro-unification leader since 2020 raised hopes of renewed talks.

Nikos Christodoulides, president of the internationally recognized Republic of Cyprus, met Turkish Cypriot leader Tufan Erhürman on Monday in the United Nations Protected Area under the auspices of UN envoy María Ángela Holguín.

A UN statement described the meeting as positive and said the leaders had continued discussions on confidence-building measures, negotiating methodology and substantive issues.

They agreed to open crossings linking Louroujina/Akıncılar with Lympia/Limya and Kampos/Kambo with Karavostasi/Gemikonağı simultaneously.

The statement gave no opening date. Christodoulides said the crossings would open “once the necessary preparatory work has been completed.”

The leaders also agreed to share archival material through the Technical Committee on Cultural Heritage and advance environmental projects through the Technical Committee on Environment.

The Imagine Project will restart under the auspices of the Technical Committee on Education, while the Technical Committee on Crisis Management will be instructed to establish a rapid-response mechanism for emergencies.

The Technical Committee on Cultural Heritage will also proceed with restoring specific Maronite churches and an Armenian monastery.

Discussions on additional crossings and other outstanding issues will continue ahead of and during a trilateral meeting with Guterres in the coming weeks, the statement said.

There have been calls for more routes across the 180-kilometer ceasefire line, which thousands of people cross daily to work, shop, study and seek medical treatment.

Nine crossing points operate between the two sides. The first opened in 2003, ending decades of closure, and the most recent opened in 2018.

An agreement last March to open four crossings ultimately failed.

During his July visit, Guterres said he would convene new talks on ending the conflict, bringing together the island’s leaders and its three guarantor powers: Greece, Turkey and Britain.

He said he did not want more summits “without results,” calling for “adequate preparation” to build confidence.

Agence France-Presse, with reporting from Turkish Minute
