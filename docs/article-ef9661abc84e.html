Ryan Reynolds’ ‘Green Lantern’ Movie on HBO Max Is Still A Flop — But A Strangely Important Superhero Film in 2026

2026-09-01 12:00

The Green Lantern Corps. really can do the impossible. Not only can they use their fancy rings to do stuff like fly, temporarily materialize any objects they can think of, and travel through space, they can apparently get people to fire up the half-reviled, half-forgotten 2011 superhero movie Green Lantern on HBO Max — with some viewers going so far as to checkout the entirely-forgotten 10-minutes-longer extended cut also offered on the service.

It’s only natural that the only live-action feature film featuring the Green Lantern might experience an uptick in interest concurrent with the season-one success of Lanterns, the new TV show that ties into James Gunn’s still-nascent DCU series of superhero movies. On the big screen, the DCU has stayed in the Superman family, with Superman and Supergirl, though a Green Lantern appears in the former. That’s Guy Gardner, the irritable blowhard played by Nathan Fillion; Lanterns is mainly about the relationship between Hal Jordan (Kyle Chandler) and John Stewart (Aaron Pierre). (At any given time, there are a bunch of Green Lanterns.) But it’s not our first-ever look at a live-action Hal; he was played by none other than Ryan Reynolds in Green Lantern.

Despite its 2010s release date and even in its extended version — which mostly just adds a lengthy early-movie flashback to Hal Jordan’s pre-Lantern childhood — Green Lantern is very much a product of the initial 2000s-era superhero boom. After X-Men and Spider-Man hit, studios were suddenly eager to get a ton of superhero characters into multiplexes, even without the clear sensibility of a Sam Raimi or Christopher Nolan. Hence movies like Daredevil, Fantastic Four, Ghost Rider… and Green Lantern. The latter made its obligatory $100 million or so at the domestic box office, but bad reviews and indifferent audiences meant that any expected franchising would not come to pass. Rewatching it outside of the hype window doesn’t help much; despite some appropriately vivid colors, director Martin Campbell (who twice rebooted the James Bond series with great success!) doesn’t have much sense for how to originate a superhero. The movie is lightweight without being funny, and dopey when it tries to be serious.

Yet Green Lantern did prove oddly influential on the trajectory of superhero movies — from multiple directions, including both DC and Marvel. Here are some of the unexpected results of Green Lantern’s initially dim light:

Green Lantern Bombing Got Ryan Reynolds to Double-Down on Deadpool

Ryan Reynolds was obviously keen to play a superhero. Back in 2004, he made his first big action-fantasy movie with Blade: Trinity, where he and Jessica Biel played vampire hunters who were once, briefly, eyed to star in their own spinoff series. That didn’t pan out, but he managed to pick up the role of mercenary Wade Wilson — the man who would be Deadpool — in X-Men Origins: Wolverine. After a snappy opening sequence, that movie mangled Wilson’s character beyond recognition, which seemed to put the kibosh on future Deadpool adventures.

Reynolds then moved on to Green Lantern and it seems like the combination of this movie’s failure and the timeline-resetting shenanigans of subsequent X-Men movies likely led to Reynolds taking another crack at the character. Reynolds played a more comics-accurate, more Ryan Reynolds-y version of Deadpool in a stunning test reel that was leaked to the public and helped stir up interest in what became a major, unexpected smash in 2016.

Reynolds finally got his superhero franchise and then some. Deadpool is still one of the surest bets in superhero box office and the actor got to use his alter ego’s well-established self-referential snark to goof on the animated supersuit he donned as Hal Jordan.

Green Lantern Also Prompted the Original DC Comics Extended Universe

When Green Lantern was in production in 2010, Christopher Nolan was also teeing up a third movie in his Dark Knight series, while the MCU was still in its pre-Avengers early stages. (Thor and Captain America: The First Avenger bookended Green Lantern’s June 2011 release.) While there may have been thoughts of creating a DC equivalent at some point, it was much less of an imperative than the traditional sequel-izing of individual hits. Following Green Lantern‘s failure and the conclusion of the Dark Knight series the following summer, Warner Bros. got going on Man of Steel, which they decided would kick off a broader extended universe of DC characters.

Had Green Lantern done well, it might have encouraged DC to focus on more stand-alone superhero movies — or build its DCEU around Reynolds, rather than the Zack Snyder version of Superman! The DCEU may not have been as successful as the MCU, but it did produce 15 movies over the course of a decade, including five or six big hits.

Reaction to many of the DCEU movies has been mixed, but honestly, all 15 of them are better than Green Lantern! Even if you don’t care for some of them, this kinda-sorta means highlights like Wonder Woman and Aquaman come courtesy of Reynolds’s flameout.

Green Lantern‘s Silly Style Probably Inspired HBO’s Lanterns to Get Gritty

The Green Lantern characters have rich enough comic-book backgrounds that they don’t really need a failed film adaptation to nudge them in another direction for a TV series debuting 15 years later. Even so, the idea of doing a True Detective-ish HBO prestige version of the Green Lantern Corps was probably not the easiest corporate sell in the world, especially given that the big hit of the newly rebooted DCU is the colorful, relatively kid-friendly Superman. The fact that the earlier movie went in a cartoonier direction — a direction you’d have to assume is mandatory, taking a look at most Green Lantern comics — probably helped convince anyone who needed convincing that different tactics should prevail.

Lanterns has become a substantial DCU success story, introducing at least one character slated to appear in the Superman sequel Man of Tomorrow. (Spoiler: that’s Aaron Pierre’s John Stewart.)

Green Lantern Weirdly Brought Taika Waititi into the World of Superhero Movies

In 2011, Taika Waititi was best-known for writing and directing a couple of indie movies. His nerdy-sidekick acting role in Green Lantern was his introduction to big-studio filmmaking.

Six years later, he was behind the camera for Thor: Ragnarok, which more successfully utilized loopier, more cosmic characters and settings that Green Lantern couldn’t quite master. Reynolds and Waititi subsequently collaborated on 2021’s Free Guy.

Green Lantern Brought Ryan Reynolds and Blake Lively Together (Forever?)

Reynolds was already married in 2010 when he shot Green Lantern, but making the movie introduced him to Lively, whom he began officially dating after his divorce from Scarlett Johansson in 2011.

Maybe that’s why Reynolds, upon watching the full Green Lantern film for supposedly the first time in 2021, tweeted that it wasn’t nearly as bad as he feared.

How could he hold anything against a movie that measurably improved his life and career? And just as fans of the Christopher Nolan Batman movies might have the epic flop Batman & Robin to thank for Batman Begins and its subsequent beloved sequels, Green Lantern has resulted in any number of better superhero movies and shows down the line. That’s its heroic legacy.
