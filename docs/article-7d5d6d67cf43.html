GTA 6 craze could ruin relationships from excessive gaming, expert says

2026-11-22 00:36

Angelica Stabile, Fox News

Published Nov. 21, 2026, 4:36 p.m. PT

See more of our coverage in your search results.

The long-awaited arrival of Grand Theft Auto (GTA) 6 has become a craze among gamers, and a point of contention between couples.

Ahead of the video game’s release on Nov. 19, couples on social media anticipated how the game might create space in their relationships — with users posting about men gearing up for hours of gameplay and women worrying about the resulting distance.

While some posted videos of men gifting their partners flowers and treats ahead of the download, others took a firmer stance on prioritizing gameplay over relationship time.

One husband jokingly created a contract where his wife signed in agreement of 10 days of uninterrupted play, allowing requests “at my convenience.”

The husband also reminded his wife that everything in the game is fictional.

Grand Theft Auto 6 has drawn scrutiny from regulators and commentators for its heightened mature content, which includes strong sexual themes, nudity, strip club missions and satirical references to an in-game adult subscription brand modeled after OnlyFans.

Those elements are among the features most concerning to spouses, according to social posts.

Relationship coach, podcaster and author Matthew Hussey weighed in on how couples can avoid letting GTA 6 negatively affect their relationships. The Los Angeles-based expert acknowledged that it’s a “real problem” if a video game results in a partner not having their needs met.

Hussey noted that there are worse habits people can have, like staying out late drinking every night, although gaming does have the potential to become addictive.

In most cases, a new, exciting game release is a special interest and a passion that should be supported by one’s partner, according to the expert.

Hussey argued that gaming deserves the same respect from partners as any other hobby, such as practicing jiu-jitsu, playing golf or going to the movies, rather than being treated as something to shame.

“If we know GTA 6 is about to come out, and it’s really important to them, and they’ve been looking forward to it, we should be the ones saying, ‘How do we carve out more time for you this week to go and play? Because I know this is a big moment for you,'” he said.

While it’s healthy to support a partner’s hobby, it shouldn’t come at the expense of the relationship, Hussey noted.

“If you’re suddenly at a point where you’re like, ‘I’m not getting my needs met, and I literally am coming second to this video game all the time,’ then that’s a real conversation [that needs to be had],” he said.

While sexually explicit gameplay can cause friction for some couples, Hussey called it “interesting,” comparing it to reading popular, steamy “romantasy” novels.

“If my wife was reading a romance novel, and she was fantasizing things that she was reading in that book, I don’t mind. It’s a book, it’s fantasy,” he said. “I think the reverse argument might be made in some ways for GTA 6, where it’s not living in the world of reality.”

Hussey cautioned that interactions in a game should not become a “surrogate” for the real thing, pulling one partner away from a real-life relationship.

“[If] you’re doing things for a character in a game, like you haven’t texted your wife back, but you are worried about texting your character girlfriend back … that’s a different story, and I think that’s a real problem.”

The expert said he’s more worried about single people who don’t have a partner who can “call them out” if they’re spending too much time in a fictional reality.

“There’s no one there to ring the alarm and say, ‘You’re on your 14th night in the house, and you haven’t interacted with a real person or given yourself a real shot at finding love,'” he said.

“I think video games are awesome. … We’re just going to have to be really disciplined about asking ourselves, ‘Am I still investing in real relationships or has this become a surrogate for them?'”

Talking to a partner about excessive gaming

Bringing up unmet needs caused by excessive gaming can be difficult for couples. Hussey suggests starting not with criticism, but by showing you have your partner’s best interests at heart.

It’s important to clarify that you aren’t trying to take away a hobby they love, and instead are framing it around your own needs, he added.

“Somewhere in the process you’re starting to feel overlooked, and there are things that are important to you that you don’t feel like you’re getting,” he said.

“If you show someone … that you genuinely care about their happiness and them getting their needs met … they’re not seeing someone who’s at odds with them,” he went on.
