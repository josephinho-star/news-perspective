New Zealand election 2026 live results: Labour draws level with National as count continues in close race

2026-11-07 09:24

Two of National’s most senior MPs, Nicola Willis and Paul Goldsmith, arrived at the party’s event just after 9.30pm, where they told media they were unlikely to return to parliament.

On current numbers, National has secured more electorate seats than their party vote, meaning there will be no room for MPs who don’t win their electorate or are running as list-only MPs to enter parliament.

Willis, who was finance minister, and Goldsmith, who was justice minister, appeared downcast as they reckoned with their fate.

“It is looking very unlikely that I will be returning to parliament, so that does come with some disappointment. At the same time I feel immense gratitude for the opportunity I have had to serve as minister of finance in Luxon’s government,” Willis said.

When asked how she felt about several top ministers at risk of being out of a job, Willis said she was confident in the remaining team.

Goldsmith said politics was “a rough business” but he was proud of his achievements.

Jamie Tahana has been watching as Green party co-leader Marama Davidson addresses supporters in a central Auckland church. Davidson said the party had seen a “Green wave” of Māori support.

“It looks like about 40% of voters in the Māori seats have placed their trust in us and we do not take that lightly,” she said.

That’s true. The party is second in party votes in all seven Māori electorates, trailing Labour.

That could show Māori voters heeding Te Pāti Māori’s call for its supporters to not give them their party vote, just their electorate vote. (TPM is currently at 0.57%, which it’s claiming as a success).

But the Greens have also been pushing hard to boost its support among Māori, and in the past three years, has been one of the most vocal critics of a slew of Māori-related bills from the coalition government.

It’s also worrying for Labour, who once had a near monopoly in the Māori seats. They’re still leading in party votes in Māori seats, but their historic dominance has definitely waned.

Matthew Hooton, editor of Wellington paper The Post, says there are three big winners so far tonight: Act, the Greens and Opportunity. Hooton is on Stuff’s pundit panel tonight.

In Nelson, 35-year-old hopeful Blair Cameron has taken an early lead. Cameron is vying for the seat again, after narrowly missing out on becoming a National MP in 2023 when Labour’s incumbent Rachel Boyack held on to the seat by 26 votes.

This election, with 72% of the vote counted, he leads by 878 votes. At this time of the night, a margin of less than 1000 is still considered to be a tight race.

In another election race picked as one to watch, just up the coast from Wellington in Kāpiti, young climate activist Sophie Handford is trailing National party incumbent Tim Costley. With half of that vote counted, he leads by 1,319.

Jamie Tahana has spotted that John Farnham’s You’re The Voice was blasting at the Duke of Marlborough pub in the historic settlement of Russell for the arrival of Winston Peters, the leader of the nationalist NZ First party.

His party currently sits at 9.6% of the vote, a certain return to parliament with 11 seats.

“A lot of people are getting carried away right now about what the outcome means,” he said. “It would be wise if they held their counsel a bit longer.”

“As with all good decision-making, you first need to know all the facts.”

He said it was the most difficult campaign of his extensive political career (he first entered parliament in 1979).

So, how does the current count translate into parliament seats?

National 42, Act 13, NZ First 12 = 67

Labour 35, Greens 17, Te Pāti Māori = 57

In this projected 131-seat parliament, 66 seats are needed to win power. The number of seats keeps jumping around because of the overhang issue. Read more about that here.

As you know, Michelle Duff is in Upper Hutt tonight, with the Labour faithful at the local Cosmopolitan Club; that’s in Chris Hipkins’ electorate.

Michelle has this important update on the food and vibe:

The generally buoyant atmosphere is not reflected in the food, which is majority fried and beige. “There’s no fruit,” said one punter, before standing corrected; there was, in fact, a lemon wedge.

Guests are otherwise snacking on chicken tenders and sausage rolls, listening to a playlist of New Zealand reggae hits from the 90s, and biting their nails.

Labour party candidate Barbara Edmonds has arrived at the Labour party event, hot off a campaign that has seen her gain double the votes of her closest National party contender in Kenepuru, a Wellington electorate.

Surrounded by family, she said she was thrilled to have won her electorate and increased voter turnout, and “nervously excited” about the numbers for her party so far. She wanted to see Labour with a count in the 30s. “My hopes are high, but it’s still early days.”

Senior government minister Mark Mitchell has just offered a pretty strong hint that National has softened its stance on working with the Opportunity party.

Christopher Luxon has repeatedly ruled out working with Opportunity. But Mitchell just told TV3 that “at the end of the day it’s an MMP environment and the voters decide”.

Mitchell, one of National’s most senior ministers, added it was “too early to say” if working with the new party was possible.

No one really believed Luxon wouldn’t call Qiulae Wong if he needed her to form a government.

With National at 29.41% of the party vote, that might be starting to play out.

Jamie Tahana has this on David Seymour from a little earlier, after the leader of the libertarian Act party addressed his faithful at Auckland’s Viaduct Harbour.

Flanked by his MPs, he told a cheering crowd that it’s unlikely we’ll know who will form the next government tonight.

“This election has a few machinations, it’s fair to say the final result may not be known tonight and it may not be known until the final count is released in two Fridays time.”

Seymour looks set to win his Epsom electorate, currently 900 votes ahead of National’s Paul Goldsmith – a senior government minister who probably won’t make parliament if he doesn’t win the electorate.

But in neighbouring Tāmaki, Act looks set to lose that electorate.
