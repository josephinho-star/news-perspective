Russian ‘attempts to intimidate Europe will fail’, says EU’s Kallas as Germany blames Moscow for Leipzig drone incident – Europe live

2026-09-02 15:04

Russian 'attempts to intimidate Europe will fail,' Kallas says

Kallas also repeats her earlier line that the Leipzig drone incident “had the hallmarks of state sponsored terrorism,” as she says it was “not an isolated incident.”

“Attempts to intimidate Europe will fail,” she insists.

Asked about where the funds should come from to further help Ukraine in its fight against Russia, Kallas says that in the ideal scenario they “should come from Russia, because they are causing this damage.”

She says that’s why some countries are proposing to go back to the proposals on using frozen Russian assets, and that this discussion will continue.

As we know from last year’s prolonged discussions on this point, it remains a controversial suggestion for some countries.

Belgium has previously voiced concerns about the legality of the scheme and says it will only agree if it has guarantees Euroclear will be reimbursed in full if Russia successfully sues for its money.

Kallas also gets pushed on whether EU countries can offer support when it comes to air missile interceptors for Ukraine, so often requested by Zelenskyy and the government in Kyiv.

She doesn’t want to reveal the detail as she says the countries will speak for themselves, but insists “we have actual movement” on this.

On sanction listings, she says the work on that continues, with additional proposals expected in October.

'We really need to curb visas' for Russians, EU's Kallas says

EU’s Kallas gets asked about German media reports suggesting that one of the suspects entered the EU using an Italian tourist visa (14:18).

She says “if it’s true – and it’s up to German authorities to confirm or deny this – it shows very clearly that visas are a tool used by these people who are meaning harm to the EU.”

She says it also came up in discussions earlier today, as an argument that “we really need to curb the visas” for Russians.

“It’s not okay that a country [Russia] us bombing Ukraine, killing civilians … more than ever … and at the same time Russians are entering and enjoying the hospitality of European countries.”

She says “it should not work this way,” and confirms previous reports from some countries concerned about how tourist visas are being abused.

She says that if the reports are confirmed to be true, she hopes it will “convince certain member states that have been against moving on visa restrictions,” but she does not name them.

Speaking for the Irish presidency, Ireland’s foreign minister Helen McEntee also gives a quick summary of today’s talks, stressing the unity in the room – both among the EU27, but also their global partners – and their absolute commitment to keep supporting Ukraine.

She also says that “Russia continues to show no interest in peace” with “a clear escalation in attacks in recent weeks.”

On the Middle East,Kallas talks generally about the situation in the strait of Hormuz, Gaza, and West Bank, noting that the situation there “has deteriorated sharply.”

“Settler violence against Palestinians continues to rise and too often goes unchecked. The E1 settlements will further undermine the prospects of the two state solution by driving a wedge through the West Bank. The EU will continue to support the Gaza peace plan and work with partners towards a two state solution.”

Russian 'attempts to intimidate Europe will fail,' Kallas says

Kallas also repeats her earlier line that the Leipzig drone incident “had the hallmarks of state sponsored terrorism,” as she says it was “not an isolated incident.”

“Attempts to intimidate Europe will fail,” she insists.

Kallas also says there is “broad support” to move ahead with an EU visa entry ban for Russian ex-combatants and tougher visa restrictions on Russian tourists.

'Moscow has not changed,' EU's Kallas says, as she pledges continued support for Ukraine

Kallas says the ministers had “a very substantive” discussion, not just between the EU 27, but with “friends from Ukraine, Canada, Iceland, Norway, the UK, and Switzerland” also attending.

She says it is important for the EU and its close partners to “tackle the security challenges together.”

She says “Moscow has not changed,” as they “make it abundantly clear” with continued bombing that they “do not want peace.”

“Our immediate focus continues to be to give Ukraine what it needs to defend itself,” she says.

The EU will continue contributing financially, but alsowork on tightening its sanctions targeting Russia’s military industrial complex.

“Ukraine has identified ore than 5,500 western and Asian components in Russian weapons, after four years of war. This is inexcusable,” she says.

The EU will also “continue” – that’s usually diplospeak for ‘we didn’t get anywhere really on this’ – to discuss the use of frozen Russian money.

Kallas also flags potential risks associated with Russia’s continued strikes in the Black Sea, which pose a continued risk for global food security.

We are expecting to hear from the EU’s foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas, soon-ish, with a debrief after today’s meeting of EU foreign ministers in Ireland.

She will give us the EU’s view on the Leipzig incident and the latest with Russia and Ukraine.
