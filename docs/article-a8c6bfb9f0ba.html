It’s fair to say that the G7 decision is much awaited as several countries continue to face high fuel prices.

For example, British motorists face more pressure at the pumps after the average price of diesel climbed to a record £2 a litre.

The price of a litre at UK forecourts has risen 40.5% since late February, when the US-Israel war on Iran began disrupting supplies of crude and refined oil products from the Gulf, leading to cost increases across the economy.

Some countries are taking domestic decision to lower the price: in Germany, fuel subsidies aimed at softening the impact of the Middle East war energy shock came into force yesterday, AFP reported.

The new restrictions put in place by the government do allow for filling stations to raise prices once a day at 12pm.

“On average, consumers are paying today, at around 12:15 p.m. (local time), between 14 and 15 cents less than yesterday, depending on the type of fuel,” the German automobile association (ADAC) told AFP.

G7 to release 100m barrels of diesel, coordinate maintenance schedule to stabilise energy supplies

Here is the G7’s statement in full, as released by the Élysée Palace.

Just a quick reminder that G7 includes Britain, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, and Japan.

I have highlighted some bits in bold for key information for you.

“Today, We, the Leaders of the G7, convened a virtual meeting to address the deepening challenges to our energy security. Facing unprecedented volatility in oil markets - with surging prices threatening economic stability and the well-being of our citizens - we have agreed on decisive, coordinated measures to stabilize immediate energy supplies, shield households and businesses from price shocks, and strengthen the long-term resilience of global energy systems.

We will coordinate maintenance schedules across G7 refineries to prevent simultaneous capacity shutdowns and temporarily increase utilization rates where feasible. We also encourage engagement with countries holding significant refining capacities to boost global production of refined products, particularly diesel, in light of ongoing market pressures in this segment.

The G7 requests the IEA to monitor the immediate and full implementation of the March 2026 commitments. In this regard, taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled, we will implement our commitments with a coordinated release through the IEA of 100 million barrels (MB) to begin immediately over 4 months, including a frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners. We will convene in the context of the IEA in the coming days to discuss the possibility of additional diesel releases as necessary.

We reaffirm our commitment to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries and call on all producers to refrain from imposing bans that could exacerbate market tensions.

We invite the IEA to monitor the impact of these measures on energy security and market stability and to monitor implementation of commitments. A follow-up report should be delivered before 20 days, include actionable recommendations to enhance future responses, including stocks replenishment.

We condemn Iran’s continued attacks against its regional neighbours and its disruption of international trade, energy security and the global economy. We call for the immediate and full restoration of navigational rights and principles in the Strait of Hormuz and express our determination to redouble our collective efforts to that end. We commend the United States for its efforts to ensure the free flow of commerce through the strait of Hormuz.

We will maintain sanctions against Russia while working with the IEA and global partners to prevent further spillovers into fuel, gas, and other commodity markets.

Our citizens’ concerns about energy prices remain a top priority. We will monitor developments closely and stand ready to adjust measures as needed. Today’s agreements mark a step toward a more secure, transparent, and resilient energy future.”

As dozens of school pupils sat on top of a pile of wheelie bins blocking a high school entrance in north-eastern Paris, Sophia, 17, was on the frontline of the barricade, when she would normally have been attending Spanish class.

“We hope this grows into a massive youth protest movement,” said the final year baccalaureate student at Paris’s Lycée Voltaire, who did not want her real name published. She was on her second day of blocking the school entrance along with hundreds of others, ensuring all lessons were cancelled. “We just want to be listened to by the government. “School is a right, not a privilege,” read a cardboard sign as Sophia listed the grievances of teenagers across France: a lack of state investment in education, overcrowded classes of up to 40 children in crumbling buildings, poor teacher pay, staff absences and an unequal system in which social background determined success. “The education budget is being cut to shreds while so much extra funding is being put into the army,” said Sophia, who wanted to go on to study art and work in animation. 17-year-old arrested after police officer violently attacked in student protest, prosecutor says

A police officer deployed to the school protests in Belfort, north-east France was brutally attacked after another off-duty cop arrested a young man, Belfort prosecutor Paul-Edouard Lallois told the AFP news agency.

The officer was “attacked by a mob of young people” near the Courbet secondary school in Belfort at 9.15am local time. The group “knocked him to the ground and beat him with their fists and feet, particularly around the head”, said Lallois, who announced he will open an investigation for attempted murder of a public official. He is scheduled to hold a press conference this afternoon.

Police backup “quickly intervened to disperse the group of attackers” and provide assistance to the officer, Lallois added. He suffered “a brief loss of consciousness” and sustained “several wounds” to his face.

A 17-year-old student at the school was arrested shortly afterward. He has not been formally identified as having struck the officer, Lallois said, adding that investigations are ongoing to establish his involvement.

Commenting on the attack, the French interior minister, Laurent Nuñez,said: “These images, which are exceptionally violent, are unbearable.

“The police officer, who was deliberately targeted, was taken to hospital after losing consciousness. Fortunately, his condition is no longer a cause for serious concern at this time. My thoughts are with him.”

France's justice minister brands far-right Bardella's anti-semitism explanations as 'shameless lies'

In other news in France, the country’s justice minister has accused Jordan Bardella of “shameless lies” after Marine Le Pen’s de facto deputy dismissed new evidence from an investigative website that he posted antisemitic messages on social media as “crude fabrications, obscure and easily manipulated screenshots … vile statements I obviously never made”.

The Mediapart website on Monday published the messages, dating back to 2013, in which Bardella – then a 17-year-old activist in Le Pen’s far-right National Rally (RN) – allegedly wrote comments including “the Jew has to dominate other peoples, crush them, and rob them; a Zionist is necessarily a Jew, and fundamentally so” and “all the banks are owned by Jews”.

Mediapart said on Thursday it had authenticated the messages, mainly on Messenger, Facebook’s instant messaging service, and published screenshots. It said they were provided by two sources close to Bardella, had been authenticated with Meta, Facebook’s parent company, and the data extraction had been certified by a bailiff.

“Extracting the conversation allows us to date it, to confirm that this conversation has not been subsequently modified, and to confirm that the person involved in the conversation is listed by Meta/Facebook under the name Jordan Bardella.”

It added that an analysis of the content also “possible to verify that it is indeed the real Jordan Bardella”: biographical details in the conversation included mention of a mother born in Italy, a 2013 interview Bardella gave to Le Parisien newspaper, and his failure to pass the entrance examination for the prestigious Sciences Po university.

Bardella, who created a new Facebook profile dedicated to his burgeoning political career late in 2013, said on Tuesday he had filed a formal legal complaint for defamation.

On Thursday he repeated his denial and added he would now also file complaints for “forgery and use of forgeries”.

The evidence was “therefore not evidence at all, and will not hold up for a second in court”, he said on social media.

“The so-called ‘investigation’ is already collapsing. The French people will be the judge of this blatant fabrication.”

The allegations against Bardella come just seven months before next year’s presidential elections, with Le Pen leading in the opinion polls.

Bardella is widely expected to become prime minister if she wins and the affair threatens to damage her decades-long “detoxification” of the RN, long associated with the antisemitic views of some of its members including its founder, Le Pen’s father Jean-Marie.

The French justice minister, Gérald Darmanin, told French radio on Friday there was “little doubt” Bardella had made the comments.

“Do we want a prime minister who is not only antisemitic, but also lies to the French people in the face of overwhelming evidence? … His strategy raises serious doubts about his ability to hold political office,” he said.

The French Socialist party’s leader, Olivier Faure, said Bardella was “lying. So we have a two-pronged issue here: his antisemitism and lying in politics. Neither is forgivable.”

Faure added: “He could have had another defence – he could have said: ‘I was young, I’ve changed since then.’ That’s not what he’s saying.”
