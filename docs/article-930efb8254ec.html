Middle East crisis live: Riyadh airport hit again as Houthis claim responsibility for attack that killed 12

2026-10-11 17:02

Saudi authorities say latest airport attack 'resulted in injuries'

The Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation said in a statement that today’s attack on King Khalid international airport in Riyadh “resulted in injuries” but did not specify how many.

The statement said that an “assessment of the damage and verification of the safety of the facilities is ongoing”.

Earlier on Sunday, an “unidentified projectile” hit Riyadh’s international airport, the same site targeted in Saturday’s strike which injured hundreds of people and killed 12. Minutes earlier, Yemen’s Houthis claimed responsibility for the attack.

Yemen’s Houthis are claiming responsibility for both Saturday and Sunday’s attacks on Riyadh Airport. The group said on Sunday they had targeted Riyadh’s airport several times as well as Saudi energy facilities, a day after Saturday’s deadly attack.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the Iran-backed group “carried out four qualitative operations in the past 24 hours using ballistic and cruise missiles and drones,” AFP reported.

Saree said the group targeted the Riyadh airport multiple times, as well as the Dammam airport and two facilities belonging to the Saudi state oil company Aramco.

Israeli prime minister condemns attack on Riyadh airport

Benjamin Netanyahu, the Israeli prime minister, condemned on Sunday the “horrific terrorist attack” on Riyadh airport in Saudi Arabia.

“I condemn in the strongest possible terms the horrific terrorist attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh,” Netanyahu wrote in a social media post, adding that he extended Israel’s “deepest condolences” to the families of the victims.

Yemen’s Houthis claim responsibility for Riyadh airport attack that killed 12 people

Yemen’s Houthis have claimed responsibility for the attack targeting Riyadh airport, according to a statement released by the group.

The Houthis claimed to have launched strikes against Dammam airport and Saudi Aramco facilities located in Saudi Arabia’s Eastern Province. It is not immediately clear whether the group is claiming just yesterday’s attack or the strike from earlier today as well. Saudi authorities have not yet issued a confirmation.

SriLankan Airlines announced that it has suspended all flights to Riyadh, Saudi Arabia until October 18 “in view of the recent security incident affecting Riyadh Airport and ongoing safety and operational assessments.”

“The safety and security of our passengers and crew remain our highest priority and services will resume once the prevailing conditions are assessed to be suitable for commercial flight operations,” the statement said.

Attacks on Riyadh airport and capital come on eve of major oil summit

Saudi Arabia of course is the world’s top oil exporter and its oil refineries and facilities have been repeatedly targeted by the Houthis this past month.

It was forced to temporarly close its key East-West pipeline last month after a drone attack on the 1,200km Saudi Aramco pipeline which spans the Arabian peninsula.

That temporarily halted oil flows; the kingdom had been diverting much of its oil that used to go out through the Strait of Hormuz through the pipeline to be shipped out through the Red Sea, however that trade route has now also been targeted by the Iran-allied Houthis.

That has driven up global oil prices and spooked the market. The kingdom was in fact due to host a major oil summit – the World Petroleum Congress - this week in Riyadh.

The CEOs of major companies Shell, ExxonMobil, BP, TotalEnergies had been due to attend but given the attacks, it’s unclear if exceutives will take the risk. Saudi authorities were saying this morning that the summit would still proceed.

If confirmed as a strike, this marks the fourth attack on the Saudi capital’s airport just this week alone.

The Houthis haven’t claimed responsibility for Saturday’s attack on King Khalid International in Riyadh, but earlier this morning issued a threat warning travellers and airlines and trade operators to stay away from all Saudi airports and airspaces – barring those of Islamic holy cities Mecca and Medina.

The Houthis have declared that all Saudi airspace is a theatre of their military operations.

They’ve escalated their attacks as Saudi Arabia has launched hundreds of airstrikes on Houthi areas in Yemen this week, as part of the Yemeni government force’s counter-offensive this week to repel the Houthis from a critical Red Sea strait.

Riyadh international airport reportedly hit again in second strike

The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen says an “unidentified projectile” hit Riyadh’s international airport on Sunday – the same site targeted in Saturday’s strike which injured hundreds of people and killed 12.

Coalition spokesman Major General Turki al-Malki said the projectile struck Terminal 4 at King Khalid International Airport, causing a fire, AFP reported.

Al-Malki said air defences also “intercepted and destroyed a cruise missile” in the vicinity of the airport.

The airport has been closed today following yesterday’s deadly attack.

Houthi airport attack forces swift rethink of Trump’s new 'Blame Ukraine' strategy

The reckless missile attack on King Khalid international airport in Riyadh on Saturday did not just export the death and misery inside Yemen to Saudi Arabia, it also left Donald Trump forced to reconsider his diplomatic and political strategy designed to take him into the midterm elections next month.

The US president’s plan had only been unveiled on Saturday.

It was to blame what he described as “a world problem” – spiralling diesel prices – on the Ukrainian president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, saying his attacks on Russian oil refineries were the cause. He was going to keep the political temperature down on his war of choice in Iran and its spillover in Yemen until after the midterms.

In pursuit of this strategy, he claimed the Iranian blockade in the strait of Hormuz was in effect broken, producing figures on daily oil barrel exports to assuage the markets. As a further insurance he also claimed it was impossible to negotiate with the Iranians since there was no single leadership in Tehran.

Saudi Arabia, in conjunction with Turkey, and Pakistan, were largely to be left to sort out the Houthis along the Red Sea. The fate of Yemen, the poorest country in the world according to the International Monetary Fund, was not going to shift votes in Kansas or Alaska.

Now, after the Riyadh airport attack, it is doubtful, for a basket of reasons, that this anyway threadbare strategy, largely born of desperation, can hold.

For all the talk of the Gulf becoming responsible for its own security, it is doubtful that Saudi Arabia can be left to fend for itself after the carnage in Riyadh. One of the the 12 dead is an American and there may be more among the severely injured. Something is clearly wrong with Saudi defences since the Houthis are firing rockets from 500 miles away, and they are reaching their target too often.

The US has had a unilateral ceasefire with the Houthis since 2025, and once the hostilities between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis recommenced in the summer, the pact was quietly renewed.

Trump has twice rejected Saudi appeals for help. Can he spurn them three times? The pressure to act as allies are expected to may prove overwhelming.

Egyptian flight attendant and Syrian woman among 12 killed at Saudi airport

We have some more detail on the 12 people killed in the Houthi missile strike on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airporton Saturday.

The attack on a civilian space – the airport had no military facility- has drew widespread condemnation from Arab and Western leaders and the UN.

Saudi authorities said the dead included four Saudi citizens, two Bangladeshi nationals, a Palestinian, a U.S. citizen, an Egyptian, a Jordanian, a Syrian and a Sudanese.

The Egyptian woman killed has been identified as Azza Ramzy, 28, a flight attendant who worked for the Saudi airline Flynas, according to the Egyptian state-run TV.

The Syrian woman has been identified as Buthaina Ahmad Oweida by the Syrian embassy in Riyadh.

She was a widowed mother of four who had been living in Saudi Arabia for the past five years after fleeing the Syrian civil war, her niece told AP. She had been visiting her sister in Riyadh and was on her way home to Mecca, where she lived, when she was killed in the attack.

There is attention too on the still yet to be identified American citizen who was killed at the airport because that will add to Saudi pressure on Donald Trump for the U.S. to join in the fight.

As we await more detail on this reported blast in the Saudi capital, it’s worth noting that there’s been a distinct lack of information coming from Saudi authorities on the Houthi attacks and other dangers.

Officials have announced that schooling will move online until the end of the week, meaning schools and campuses are closed.

But they have otherwise been largely quiet on the major attack on the country’s largest airport yesterday which killed 12 people and injured 300 more.

They have not issued a wider emergency alert, nor given a national address or offered any assurance to residents on why and how the kingdom’s air defences failed. We know that Saudi Arabia has been requesting more missile interceptors from the US and other European allies – but the US is also facing its own shortage due to the Iran fighting.

The Houthis have targeted Riyadh’s airport for the past few weeks – including two attacks earlier in the week, which killed four people. But Saudi authorities have not publicly announced a closure of King Khalid International Airport, with the suspension of flights detected through air traffic monitoring sites instead.

This withholding of information the public is in line with a pattern of communication from not just Saudi but other Gulf state authorities these past seven months since they’ve come under attack from Iranian missiles and drones.

Gulf authorities have tended to suppress the flow of information – ostensibly for national security purposes and to maintain public confidence in the countr'y’s defences.

However, the lack of information is also leading to confusion and speculation online, particularly as the Houthis continue to threaten new strikes against the kingdom.

Houthi state media says capital's airport hit by Saudi strike

Houthi-run state TV is reporting that Saudia Arabia has carried out a strike on the main airport in Sana’a, the Yemeni capital currently controlled by the Houthis.

Al Masirah TV said the strike had hit Sana’a International Airport.

No comment yet from Saudi authorities or the Saudi-backed government forces in Yemen.

Earlier however, the coalition had issued a issued a statement saying they would “respond decisively” to the Houthis’ “terrorist attack” on Riyadh’s airport on Saturday which killed 12 people and injured 309.

The Saudis have launched hundreds of air strikes on Houthi positions in Yemen since fighting recommenced last month.

UK advises against travel to parts of Saudi Arabia

The UK Foreign Office has updated guidance for British nationals following the deadly Houthi attack on Saturday.

All travel to within 10km of the border with Yemen

All but essential travel to Aseer province, Riyadh province, Najran province, Jizan province and Taif city and Taif International Airport

There are an estimated 20,000- 30,000 British nationals living in Saudi Arabia, although those numbers have presumably fluctuated since the war began and Iran and its allies fought back against US and Israeli strikes by targeting cities like Dubai and Doha, in Gulf states allied with the US.

In the first fortnight of the war earlier this year in March, more than 102,000 Britons across the Middle East registered with the Foreign Office for help.
