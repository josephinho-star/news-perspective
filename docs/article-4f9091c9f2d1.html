TCU fires coach Sonny Dykes months after agreeing to new contract

2026-11-23 01:14

Four years after leading TCU to the brink of a national championship, Sonny Dykes has been let go by the university.

The Horned Frogs fired their head football coach Sunday, less than 24 hours after a 38-17 home loss to No. 2 Utah dropped them to 5-6 on the season.

Dykes, 57, finishes his tenure with a 41-23 record, including a historic 2022 campaign in which TCU reached the College Football Playoff National Championship game, memorably losing 65-7 to Georgia in the final.

The decision comes just seven months after Dykes agreed to a multi-year extension with TCU, though as part of the buyout, he is reportedly set to receive over $20 million.

“While Sonny’s tenure began with the incredible achievement of winning the 2022 Fiesta Bowl and advancing to the national championship game, the proceeding years have seen our program slide further away from that mountain top than can be accepted,” TCU Athletic Director Mike Buddie said in a statement. “I am grateful to Sonny for his belief in TCU, his family’s love for the Fort Worth community, and for his coaching staff’s commitment to recruiting and developing men of high character.”

Dykes arrived at TCU from SMU in 2022 and immediately engineered one of college football’s most improbable turnarounds, bringing the Horned Frogs into the College Football Playoff one year removed from a 5-7 season.

Dykes assumed the role at TCU left vacant after the firing of 21-year Horned Frogs head coach Gary Patterson.

In 2022, the Horned Frogs completed an undefeated regular season and ultimately finished 13-2 and No. 2 in the country.

Since then, TCU has not recorded another 10-win season and finished under .500 in 2023, with a chance they will again in 2026.

Defensive coordinator Andy Avalos will assume the interim head coaching role amid Dykes’ firing.

The university’s statement noted that TCU’s average attendance sat at 37,849 fans per game, the school’s lowest tally since 2008.

“TCU’s investment in football has led to dramatic growth of our university and city in the past 20 years, and we cannot allow that investment to fall short of its due return, especially at such a pivotal time of change in college sports,” Buddie said.

“We expect to compete for Big 12 championships, to be a central figure in the College Football Playoff conversation, and our continued investment and growth potential reflects that. I expect our new leader will echo those sentiments, rally our fan base behind that challenge, and put a team on the field that our alumni, fans, students, and community will be excited to support.”

TCU has one more game, a visit to No. 9 Texas Tech for the regular-season finale on Thanksgiving.
