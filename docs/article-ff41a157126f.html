Iconic 1950s Hollywood star’s dressing-room trailer possibly unearthed in Oregon barn

2026-11-23 22:33

“Big girls need big diamonds.” Or, in this case, big superstars need big movie trailers.

A luxury real-estate agent may have stumbled on the movie find of the decade when he was exploring a sprawling 100-acre estate in Oregon.

Sharing a video of his finding to social media, Terry Sprague of Luxe Oregon was traipsing through a paddock when he came across a weathered barn. Parked inside the old building, he found a vintage pink Aljo movie trailer that had all the hallmarks of the Hollywood Regency period of the 1920s through the 1950s.

Online chatter was immediately sparked: Could this be Hollywood icon Elizabeth Taylor’s long-forgotten custom dressing-room trailer from the 1963 epic “Cleopatra”?

The original trailer was no ordinary star wagon. Film production studio 20th Century Fox reportedly spent around $75,000 in the early 1960s — that’s the equivalent of more than $800,000 in today’s value — to customize the 36-foot palace on wheels that’s fit for the queen of Egypt. Or, in this case, the queen of Hollywood.

The trailer is decked in dazzling décor reminiscent of a head-on collision between the Gilded Age and Hollywood Regency. Think opulence, grandeur and maximalism executed through faux columns, rose-hued carpeting, hand-painted ceilings, Egyptian and Roman-influenced murals, floor-to-ceiling mirrors, gold-edged furnishings and swathes of silky curtains.

At the time, “Cleopatra” was one of the most expensive movies ever made. Foreign diplomat and real estate investor Nicolas Salgo, who built Washington, DC’s Watergate, helped secure funding for the film.

After the movie wrapped, the pink trailer was delivered to Salgo’s ranch in Oregon, where it spent roughly two decades, until the 1980s.

Salgo sold his ranch, transferring ownership of the trailer to the neighboring rancher who also ran the now-defunct Lakeview Fantastic Museum of eccentric memorabilia and other oddities.

In 2012, an Aljo movie trailer that looked remarkably identical to this Oregon find sold at auction for a reported $42,000.

That plush pink palace on wheels then made headlines again later that year when it was loaned to the production of the Taylor and Richard Burton biopic “Liz & Dick” starring Lindsay Lohan, where it was defaced and damaged, reportedly to the tune of $100,000, according to Fox 8.

The newly unearthed treasure in the Oregon barn has the spot-on vintage vibe and geographic history to make avid cinephiles light up with recognition.

However, no independent authentication has verified that it’s indeed the original Taylor trailer from the epic film, and calls from the California Post to treasure-hunter Sprague, and the trailer’s last listed owner, have so far gone unanswered.

Until such time as someone with an official chain of custody proving pedigree weighs in with confirmation, this gilded piece of film history remains a tantalizing “maybe.”

Still, the idea that a piece of peak Hollywood Golden Age glamour might have been haunched idly in a Beaver State barn all these years is pure allure for Hollywood hounds.

Whether it’s the real “Cleopatra” trailer or simply a glamorous sibling, one thing is certain: Elizabeth Taylor’s aura still sparkles like diamonds.
