Turkish parliament advances Saudi-Pakistan defense pact amid deployment concerns

2026-11-05 13:25

Turkish lawmakers have approved the first article of a bill to ratify a mutual defense pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan but adjourned before completing the legislation amid debate over whether the agreement could draw Turkey into regional conflicts, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

The vote came during a parliamentary session that began Wednesday. Deputy Speaker Celal Adan closed the session after committee representatives failed to return following a recess, scheduling parliament to reconvene at 2 p.m. on Thursday.

Approval of the first article does not complete the ratification process.

The Mecca Joint Defense Agreement treats an armed attack originating outside any member’s borders as an attack against all three. Assistance can include military support, but its form and scale would be determined through consultations and each country’s assessment of its capabilities and the needs of the country attacked.

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed the agreement on August 7. Parliament’s Foreign Affairs Committee approved the ratification bill on October 27.

The debate comes as Saudi Arabia faces missile and drone attacks by Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthis, raising questions about the military commitments Ankara would assume under the pact.

Faik Öztrak, parliamentary group chairman of the Republican People’s Party (CHP), said his party supported regional security cooperation in principle but warned against using the agreement to justify open-ended military operations.

He called for parliament’s authority over overseas deployments to be preserved.

Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Fuat Oktay, from Erdoğan’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP), said the agreement would not automatically take Turkey into a conflict against its will.

Sending Turkish forces abroad to participate in fighting would require parliamentary authorization under Article 92 of the constitution, he said.

Opposition lawmakers also questioned the implications of Saudi Arabia’s conflict with the Houthis and tensions between Pakistan and India, seeking clarity on when military assistance would become obligatory.

The government has previously indicated that it intends to seek a separate deployment mandate. An AKP lawmaker told Reuters on October 21 that a request to send military personnel to Saudi Arabia would follow approval of the treaty, with technical personnel and specialists initially supporting air defenses, electronic warfare systems and other defensive capabilities.

Defense Minister Yaşar Güler has said any support would aim to strengthen deterrence and that Ankara did not envisage its forces entering combat against the Houthis.

Another point of contention is a provision preventing withdrawal during the agreement’s first 10 years in force. After that, members may leave with at least three months’ written notice. Withdrawal would not automatically terminate existing projects or obligations to protect classified information.

The nationalist İYİ (Good) Party opposed the agreement over its ambiguities and withdrawal restrictions, while the pro-Kurdish Peoples’ Equality and Democracy Party (DEM Party) objected to military commitments whose scope it said remained unclear.

The AKP’s ally, the Nationalist Movement Party (MHP), supported the pact.

The three countries have already taken steps toward implementing the alliance. At an October 5 emergency meeting in Riyadh, they agreed to arrange the rapid deployment of military forces and capabilities to Saudi Arabia in accordance with their national laws.

The statement gave no troop numbers or deployment timetable. Opposition politicians subsequently warned that Ankara was making military commitments before securing parliamentary approval.

Beyond collective defense, the agreement provides for intelligence sharing, joint training and exercises, logistical support and defense industry cooperation. Turkish officials have said it would preserve the country’s existing NATO commitments.
