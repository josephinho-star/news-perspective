Alia Syed wins £10,000 Jarman award for ‘depth and maturity’ of her experimental films

2026-11-24 19:30

Alia Syed has won this year’s Film London Jarman award, the prestigious UK prize for groundbreaking artist film-makers in the UK.

The Welsh-Indian film-maker’s work, which incorporates themes of memory, migration and hidden histories, was praised by judges for its “restless will to play, experiment and change”. Syed will receive £10,000 in prize money.

Born in Swansea, but working between London and Glasgow, Syed has been making cutting-edge work since the 1980s when she was part of the London Film-Makers’ Co-operative. Her films often revolve around stories handed down from her parents and grandparents. 2019’s Snow is a video diary using hi-8 footage originally shot by her father on a snowy day during the winter of 1995/6. At the time it was filmed, the artist and her father were not on speaking terms, and Syed recounts her experience of viewing the footage, reflecting on elements of the past that cannot be uncovered.

Other works have looked at the diaspora experience. Last year’s The Dhaba was a poetic work that used the testimonies of two men who had migrated to Glasgow to examine racism, colonialism and the ever-changing laws around UK immigration. Ka Ba Ddi: A Breath, A Move, A Game, also from 2025, explores the popular south Asian team sport and draws parallels with the way territory is contested in other areas of life.

Three other artists made up this year’s shortlist: Sadia Pineda Hameed, Ilona Sagar and Rhea Storr. Commenting on what helped Syed clinch the prize, the jury said they were “struck by the depth and maturity of Alia’s work, which continues to surprise audiences and defy predictability. The beauty and compelling nature of her work is combined with a deep understanding of and inventiveness with the craft of film-making.”

Now in its 19th year, the Jarman has built a reputation for spotlighting artists who then go on to gain international acclaim. Previous shortlisted artists have included future Turner prize nominees Lawrence Abu Hamdan, Monster Chetwynd, Heather Phillipson, Sin Wai Kin and Project Art Works. Syed joins a recent winner’s list that includes Grace Ndiritu (2022), Rehana Zaman (2023), Maryam Tafakory (2024), and Onyeka Igwe and Morgan Quaintance (2025).

This year’s award was announced during a ceremony at the Soho Hotel, London. Work by the four shortlisted artists is being screened at Whitechapel Gallery, London, until 13 December. A conversation with Syed, alongside viewings of four of her works, will take place at the Barbican on 8 December.
