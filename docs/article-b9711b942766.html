Congress has been ‘asleep at the wheel’ over AI, says Bernie Sanders – US politics live

2026-09-15 17:46

Sanders decries rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, says Congress has been 'asleep at the wheel'

Bernie Sanders, the independent senator from Vermont, has decried the advancement of artificial intelligence the Pro-Human Assembly in Washington today.

The leading progressive lawmaker has emerged as a leading voice in warning against the rapidity of the technology’s evolution.

“If AI surpasses human intelligence, as many scientists believe could happen, this technology will escape human control with potentially catastrophic consequences,” Sanders said in Washington today. “AI is advancing exponentially, exponentially every single day. In other words, what you are looking at right now is the least capable version of AI that we will ever have – the least capable, the dumbest version. The speed and scope of AI development in just the last four years has been almost beyond human comprehension.”

Sanders added that Congress, under both Republican and Democratic control, has been “been asleep at the wheel”.

“We now have a president whose ignorance regarding this issue is truly embarrassing,” he said, referring to Donald Trump’s insistence that the calls for AI guardrails are a “hoax” that would put the US on the back foot in the ongoing technological arms race with China.

Sanders, who we reported earlier, has proposed bicameral legislation with congressman Greg Casar to permanently ban the development of artificial superintelligence, said that the pledges from leading AI company CEOs for the industry to “slow down” are encouraging but ultimately not enough.

“When you are racing towards a cliff, you don’t just ease up on the gas pedal. You hit the brakes,” Sanders added. “When the future of humanity is at stake, we need binding international safety rules, not voluntary standards from the industry.”

In a short while Todd Blanche, the attorney general, will hold a White House press briefing for reporters. A reminder that Donald Trump has yet to announce a permanent replacement for Karoline Leavitt, the former press secretary who resigned last month.

Sanders capped off his speech by noting that we are at “a dangerous and unprecedented moment in world history”, and it is “imperative” to stand up to AI industry leaders, but also to a “a government of both political parties, which have not been as aggressive in any way, shape, or form as they should be”.

While the senator said that AI has the potential to do “some extraordinarily good things” when it comes to healthcare and prescription drug development, but he stressed the need to make sure that AI “works for all of the people and does not simply end up making a handful of the richest people on Earth even richer”.

Bessent defends Trump's $5k midterm incentive in House hearing

Scott Bessent, the US treasury secretary, defended Donald Trump’s $5,000 so-called “dividend” that he promised to every adult US citizen if Republicans win the House and the Senate in the upcoming midterm elections.

“I think it’s a good idea for the American people to keep more of their money,” Bessent said in response to a question from Democratic congressman Juan Vargas at a financial services committee hearing today.

“Does that hurt the deficit or the debt? Does that increase the deficit or does it increase the debt?” Vargas asked the treasury secretary.

“I believe that there are ways to do it without increasing the debt or deficit,” Bessent said. “I’m not ready to discuss it at present, but at Treasury, we’ve been working on it for quite a while.”

This comes as US debtreached $40tn for the first time last month. The milestone marks years of government spending that grew under both Donald Trump and Joe Biden.

Judge blocks Kennedy Center board from putting Trump's name on the building or grounds in any way

A federal judge has ruled that Donald Trump’s name must stay off the facade of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, blocking an effort by the center’s (Trump-appointed) board to restore his name to the building.

The judge also said the board cannot proceed with plans to rename the campus where it sits the “President Donald J. Trump Plaza”.

“Simply put, defendants cannot install memorials for President Trump or anyone or anything else at the Kennedy Center without Congress’s blessing,” Judge Christopher Cooper in Washington wrote in the order. “The board resolution bucks a federal court order and a statute Congress enacted.”

Per my colleague Joseph Gedeon, Cooper ordered Trump’s name removed from the building in May, ruling that only Congress has authority to rename it, and the lettering came down in June. The board voted in August to reinstate an inscription and proceed with a closure for renovations, the latter of which a vote is expected to formalize today.

And yesterday, the Washington Post (paywall) reported that the Trump-installed leaders of the center claimed the institution was on the brink of bankruptcy, and argued that putting the president’s name on its facade may be the only way to avoid imminent and “certain fiscal collapse”. The documents obtained by the Post were prepared in advance of today’s meeting.

Congressman Casar calls for US to lead AI diplomacy and end AI arms race

Congressman Greg Casar has called for the United States to lead the world in artificial intelligence diplomacy, saying it must end the AI arms race instead of trying to win it.

In remarks at the Pro-Human Assembly in Washington today, the Texas Democrat, a leading progressive voice calling for pausing the development of super-advanced AI, warned, “I believe we are closer than ever to breaking the dam on serious AI protections.”

He outlined three major threats to human existence that loom with the rise of advanced AI:

Controlled by big business, superpowerful AI could put countless Americans out of work.

Controlled by an out of control government, superpowerful AI would allow constant monitoring of citizens’ every move.

And controlled by the wrong humans – or controlled by no human –superpowerful AI could threaten countless lives.

Evoking the cold war, where humanity was pulled back from the brink of nuclear annihilation, he went on:

Far too many people seem to be trying to wina race where millions if not billions of lives could be lost at the finish line … No one can winthe AI arms race. It’s up to us to endthe AI arms race.

Casar referenced his bill with Vermont senator Bernie Sanders, which proposes to create “a cabinet-level federal agency to protect the public from dangerous AI … requires monitoring of frontier AI systems at all stages of development. And it bansthe development of AI superintelligence”.

The bill would also “direct the United States to pursue international agreements to prevent superintelligence from being developed anywhere in the world”, Casar said.

“But while we pass safety rules here at home, AI diplomacy may be the most important thing we can do,” he said.

Patel spars with Democratic senator over Atlantic article that alleges FBI director's excessive drinking

The FBI director sparred intensely with Democratic Senator Peter Welch, of Vermont, after the lawmaker questioned Patel over his defamation lawsuit against a journalist at the Atlantic, who wrote earlier this year about Patel’s alleged excessive drinking and unexplained absences on the job.

The FBI director has sued the magazine, and the author of a story it published, filing a defamation lawsuit in US district court for the District of Columbia that seeks $250m in damages.

“I have every right to claim the truth,” Patel said, accusing Sarah Fitzpatrick, the reporter, of printing “false material”. The FBI director added that he was acting as a “personal private citizen” in the lawsuit. “I am allowed to participate in my own defense, especially when there’s a smear campaign, one which you are participating and continuing the falsehoods and lies that have been directed at me,” he told Welch.

Patel also said that he did not direct the FBI to investigate Fitzpatrick for her reporting.

Welch also questioned Patel about after a video surfaced earlier this year, which shows the FBI director chugging a bottle and spraying beer in a locker room with the men’s USA hockey team in Milan, following their gold medal victory against Canada at the Winter Olympics.

“What you’ll never understand is the players who are my friends invited me into the locker room. What you’ll never understand is that during that trip, where 300,000 Americans went to Italy to participate in the games and watch them, not a single security incident occurred,” Patel said.

Patel does not commit to keep FBI agents away from polls during midterm elections

Kash Patel did not commit to keeping FBI agents away from polls during the November midterm elections.

While responding to Democratic senator Richard Blumenthal, the FBI director said that his department has an election counselor coordinator in every field office where they “quarterback these election activities”.

When Blumenthal pushed Patel on whether he would confirm that agents wouldn’t be physically present at ballot sites, Patel said: “The FBI will follow the law if there’s a reason to go there because there’s been a violation of law”.

The FBI director noted that the priority to “monitor elections” rests with the Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

“I don’t know that we can’t legally do it. I’m telling you, we’re going to house our agents and intel analysts in our field offices for election security,” Patel told Blumenthal.

Maria Ressa, the Nobel Peace Prize laureate, has told Reuters that artificial intelligence will take away our agency unless we legislate now to limit it.

Ressa, a Filipino and American journalist and CEO of Rappler, jointly won the 2021 award in part for her work documenting the power of large tech companies. She also serves as co-chair of the UN’s international scientific panel on artificial intelligence, tasked with assessing how AI is transforming lives worldwide.

“It is not a freedom of speech issue to ask for regulation,” Ressa told Reuters. “It’s a public safety issue.”

She added that if AI regulation doesn’t happen, it will “be beyond our control if we don’t step in now”.

Ressa also dismissed Donald Trump’s argument that any guardrails could allow China to gain ground in developing AI and outpacing the US.

“China actually puts more safeguards in place for its citizens than Silicon Valley does for the rest of the world. If you’re on TikTok, the version of TikTok that is in China has age restrictions,” she said.

Ressa’s interview with Reuters happened before leading AI company CEOs called for further checks and to slow down the development of the technology.
