Former NYC mayor Bill de Blasio warns Dems not to ‘get lost’ in endless investigations of Trump admin

2026-11-22 17:58

Former Big Apple Mayor Bill de Blasio warned Democrats not to “get lost” in endless investigations of President Trump and his administration — instead urging them to focus “nonstop” on addressing Americans’ cost-of-living woes.

“The Democratic Party is much more unified than a lot of pundits realize. We have moderates. We have progressives. But there’s agreement on a whole host of issues in the end. I hope the Democrats will focus on affordability nonstop — on how we can lighten the burden on the American people in terms of just day-to-day life: health care, the price of gas, pharmaceuticals, you name it,” de Blasio said Sunday on 770 WABC’s the “Cats Roundtable.”

“Of course, there will be some investigations of one thing or another, but [don’t] get lost in that stuff. People don’t want bickering and endless negativity. They really want to see some results,” he added.

The former mayor insisted it is time to tamp down the rhetoric a “few notches” and “find common ground where we can.

The Democrats reclaimed majorities in both the House and Senate in January following the blue wave midterm elections on Nov. 3. New Yorkers Chuck Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries are expected to be elected Senate majority leader and House speaker, respectively.

Meanwhile, de Blasio praised the relationship between Mayor Zohran Mamdani and President Trump, despite their ideological differences, as “very, very important to the city.”

“I think both have handled it well, and both, of course, have been criticized by people in their own parties for doing it. But I think it’s been the right thing,” said de Blasio, mayor from 2014 to 2021.

He lauded Mamdani for pitching the president for federal support to build a massive 12,000-unit affordable housing complex at the 180-acre Sunnyside Yard in Queens. Some critics have questioned the feasibility, including the cost, of building over the tracks.

In 2015, de Blasio himself proposed building 11,000 apartments on a platform over Sunnyside Yard, but the idea failed to gain traction.

“Mayor Mamdani, the last time he was in Washington, proposed that amazing idea of the Sunnyside Yard housing development. That’s something my administration put forward, but we could not find federal support for it … It’s about 12,000 apartments that could change the lives of so many families in the city,” he said.

“I hope the president jumps into that energetically.”

Trump is the X factor, the former two-term mayor said.

“It begs the question,” de Blasio said. “Will the president be receptive on at least a few issues?”

He suggested that Trump and Democrats could work together to address the high cost of prescription drugs and perhaps even deal with the polarizing issue of whether to grant citizenship or legal residency to the millions of undocumented or illegal immigrants.

“They’re in this country now, and they are part of our community. Can we find a way — for the people who are law-abiding and contributing to the community — can we find a way to get them on that pathway to citizenship?” he asked.

“I actually think that would be good for our country and for healing all the back-and-forth that has happened.”

He said it’s Trump, a staunch opponent of illegal immigration, who could make it happen.

“The impact of immigrants on our economy in New York and far beyond is so important … It could be historic,” de Blasio said.
