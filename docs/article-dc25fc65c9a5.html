NFL suspension was most tolerable thing Puka Nacua has endured this week because he's now out for the season

2026-11-20 19:10

It turns out the NFL suspension of Puka Nacua announced this week was not the worst news for the Los Angeles Rams wide receiver because he is likely out for the remainder of the season.

Rams coach Sean McVay announced on Friday that Nacua underwent surgery on his MCL on Tuesday. Nacua will be out "a couple of months," McVay said.

The Rams' regular season ends the week of Jan. 10. So, less than two months from now.

And that means Nacua's season is for all intents over.

One supposes there is a small chance Nacua might be able to contribute if the Rams make a deep postseason run, assuming they get into the playoffs in the first place. But that is speculative at best and McVay said the Rams are not counting on it.

McVay's announcement comes one day after the NFL suspended Nacua for violating the NFL Personal Conduct Policy.

That suspension, without pay, was for three games and scheduled to end Dec. 14. Nacua, who has been on the injured reserve list, wasn't much affected by the actual inability to play because he could not come off IR until, you guessed it, Dec. 14.

Yes, a very strange coincidence. And questionable.

And this: The NFL was unaware Nacua had undergone surgery on Tuesday when it announced its suspension on Thursday, per a league source.

But this surgery supersedes all of that.

The Rams have been managing injury trouble with their wide receiver corps for some time. The good news is the team still hopes Davante Adams can return by Week 12 from a ruptured hamstring tendon. McVay has previously called that a "realistic timeline."

If Nacua cannot make an improbable playoff comeback, it is possible he's played his last game for the Rams.

Nacua is on an expiring contract and is scheduled to be an unrestricted free agent next offseason. That means he can decide to sign with the highest bidder if he wants and while the Rams are expected to have ample salary cap space to keep Nacua, it's unclear where the team stands on that decision.
