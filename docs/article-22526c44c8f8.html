Macron says there’s ‘no more US support’ for Ukraine

2026-11-12 21:35

French President Emmanuel Macron voiced support for Ukraine in its ongoing war against Russia, noting that “there is no more U.S. support.”

“Without Europeans … there is no support for Ukraine,” Macron told the press while attending the European Political Community Summit in Ireland, according to a clip posted on the social on platform X Thursday.

“We are those to support Ukraine, we are those to help them in the resistance war. There is no more U.S. support,” he added. “There is some mediations, fine, but at the end of the day we will be the one to define and decide for our future security as Europeans.”

Macron’s remarks come a day after Russia and Ukraine agreed to a “large-scale” exchange of prisoners of war followed by two days of U.S.-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has previously praised European leaders for their support of Ukraine saying, “I am so thankful that we have such huge support from EU Council, Commission and leaders, real leaders so that they stay with us from the very beginning of this war.”

In recent weeks, the U.S. has acted as a mediator between Russia and Ukraine, although its own relations with Ukraine have grown tense over the Starlink slowdown in Ukraine and America’s diesel deal with Russia.

Zelensky has criticized President Trump for striking a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin on supplying diesel to the U.S. and global market. Trump struck back during an interview on Newsmax, saying Zelensky is “not a good guy for us to deal with.”

Last month, first lady Melania Trump secured a localized ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine in the front-line city of Oleshky. The temporary pause in conflict, which started at 3 a.m. local time on Oct. 27, was set to last through Oct. 31 to allow for the delivery of humanitarian aid. It was originally set to begin on Oct. 29.

Shortly before the Oleshky ceasefire announcement, Ukraine faced a Starlink slowdown which restricted its access to Starshield.

Starlink services were cut off for objects that traveled more than 100 mph, impacting the capabilities of Ukraine’s drones, airplanes and helicopters. This sparked a backlash as well as finger-pointing as to who was behind the decision.

SpaceX CEO Elon Musk initially claimed the choice to restrict the speed or usage of Starshield is made by the U.S. military, but later alluded to being involved in the decision in a post on social media, writing, “Ukraine military violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people. That loophole was closed last week.”

On Thursday, Zelensky said in a post on the social platform X that the country is again facing restrictions on Starshield.

“This means more Russian strikes get through, and more of our people are killed by Russia,” Zelensky said. “We are trying to get the details from the American side, but so far, we know very little.”
