White House pulls nomination of Heidi Overton to lead Food and Drug Administration – as it happened

2026-11-18 02:57

White House pulls Heidi Overton's nomination to lead FDA

The White House has dropped its nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration, a White House official told the Guardian.

Donald Trump nominated Overton – a physician and prominent abortion opponent – in August. But Overton faced a challenging path to Senate confirmation, particularly after Republican senator Bill Cassidy said today that he remained undecided about her nomination ahead of a Senate health, labor and pensions committee vote scheduled for tomorrow.

This concludes our live coverage of the second Trump administration for the day. Here are the latest developments:

The National Security Agency’s internal network, NSA Net, was down today. An NSA spokesperson told the Guardian the outage was the resulted of “scheduled routine maintenance.” Speaking in Manila, Donald Trump brushed off the news saying, “It’s like when you’re big and powerful, and you have all the money. Things like that happen. It’s fine.” Secretary of state Marco Rubio said there is “no evidence” that any of the many outages this week “are related to each other” or “nefarious actors” and director of national intelligence Jay Clayton said the outages are likely due to system upgrades and not a cyber-attack.

The White House has dropped its nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration. Overton faced a challenging path to Senate confirmation, particularly after Republican senator Bill Cassidy said today that he remained undecided about her nomination ahead of a Senate health, labor and pensions committee vote scheduled for tomorrow.

Chuck Schumer again insisted today that he has the votes to secure the leadership position when Democrats take the majority in the new year. “We are all united together,” Schumer said, despite the fact that several members of the caucus have pledged support for senator Chris Van Hollen.

Sean Duffy, the transportation secretary, saidhe’s making “a nice ask” for an additional $50bn in funding for air safety upgrades following the nationwide ground-stop of thousands of flights last week. Duffy also said today the FBI is now assisting the investigation into the newly installed router responsible for the grounding.

Hung Cao, who currently serves as the acting Navy secretary, faced lawmakers on the Senate armed services committee to be confirmed as the branch’s permanent top official. During his hearing, Cao said the eight suicide attempts on board the USS Abraham Lincoln – during a record-breaking deployment– were actually “suicide mitigations”, and defended conditions on the warship.

Jensen Huang, the embattled former CEO of Nvidia, will appear before House lawmakers on three key committees on 1 December. Today, the chair of the company’s board said Nvidia “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”

After the United States lost its measles elimination status, which it achieved in 2000, health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr, a longtime vaccine skeptic, blamed “worldwide COVID-era disruptions” for interrupting routine childhood vaccination.

Trump was granted a temporary delay before he must give a witness statement in his $10bn defamation lawsuit against the BBC. A federal district court judge extended the deadline for Trump to sit for a deposition by BBC lawyers from 11 December until 22 January.

Bill Cassidy, the Louisiana senator who chairs the Senate health committee that was scheduled to vote tomorrow on Heidi Overton’s nomination to lead the Food and Drug Administration, said the White House’s decision to pull her nomination today was “odd.”

“Tonight, the White House abandoned a viable confirmation path for Dr. Overton. The WH said that this was because she did not have the votes to pass. This was odd as I was having productive conversations with members,” Cassidy said in a social media post. “I believe she would have had the votes to pass out of committee and pass the full Senate.”

He added, “My conversations with her convinced me she would follow the science and push back against decisions that put Americans’ health at risk. The last issue was that pro-life Senators sought final assurances from the Administration regarding the Administration’s commitment to life issues. The Administration withdrew Dr. Overton rather than make such commitments. Unfortunately, with the limited time left, confirming a permanent FDA Commissioner will fall to Senate Democrats.”

A federal appeals court has blocked the defense department from forcing 27 transgender troops out of the military, while the Trump administration asks the Supreme Corut to hear the case.

A three-judge panel from the US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit voted 2-1 today to reject the Trump administration’s request to pause the implementation of an earlier ruling by the same court. In June, the court ruled that the administration’s ban on transgender troops was unlawful.

“The Trump administration asked the court to let it keep pushing these service members toward the door while it tries to get a different answer from the Supreme Court. Today, the D.C. Circuit said no,” Shannon Minter, legal director of the National Center for LGBTQ Rights, said in a statement.

A spokesperson for Hegseth’s office declined an Associated Press request for comment on the ruling.

The White House is considering nominating senator Roger Marshall to lead the Food and Drug Administration, after it pulled its backing of Heidi Overton earlier today, Reuters reports citing two people familiar with the matter.

Marshall is finishing out his term representing Kansas in the Senate after losing his reelection bid in an upset that saw Kansas elect its first Democratic senator in 94 years. An OB/GYN, Marshall previously served in the House of Representatives and currently serves on the Senate’s health committee. Like Overton, he opposes abortion.

“Marshall is viewed as an alternative if Heidi can’t make it through,” a Trump administration official told Semafor. “Senators have an easier time getting confirmed, traditionally. At this point, it’s hard to get anyone approved.”

Pete Hegseth will deliver military equipment and meet with defense minister Jorge Eduardo Mora in Colombia tomorrow.

In remarks to the country’s congress, Mora said Hegseth would deliver a Hercules C-130 aircraft and added “I think two more are coming.” Mora said he and Hegseth would also discuss security cooperation.

In June, Colombians elected the Trump-backed millionaire lawyer Abelardo de la Espriella, who announced Colombia would join the US-led Shield of the Americas initiative after he took office in August. After de la Espriella was sworn in as president, the Trump administration announced plans to provide Colombia with $1bn in security assistance.

Earlier today, Hegseth shared a photo of himself visiting the US military’s Southern Command in Miami.

“The idea here is to be kinetic, to kill the enemy: the designated terrorist organizations who have been ravaging these countries, ravaging our communities,” Hegseth said in a video posted by the Pentagon from Southern Command headquarters, which oversees US military operations in Latin America.

Lawmakers have agreed to a plan to require civilian aircraft carry systems that help pilots locate nearby aircraft, spurred by the deaths of 67 people in an American Airlines and Black Hawk helicopter crash in January 2025.

Senators Ted Cruz, a Republican from Texas, and Maria Cantwell, a Democrat from Washington, announced the agreement today at an event organized by a group representing the families of those killed in the 2025 crash. Although the House and Senate have passed competing aviation safety reform bills since the American Airlines crash, today’s announcement represents efforts to draft a compromise bill.

“When military aircraft fly in the same airspace, they must meet the same standards as everyone else,” said Cantwell.

Cruz added that he believes Congress can pass the bill before the end of the year. Lawmakers are still working to agree to air safety provisions for the military.

Also in Manila, Marco Rubio told reporters Donald Trump is serious about wanting to meet with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, although he said no such meeting is scheduled yet.

“If we have a problem with North Korea the only way we’re going to solve it is by meeting with them,” Rubio said.

Marco Rubio said there is “no evidence” that any of the many outages this week “are related to each other” or “with nefarious actors.” The secretary of state made the remarks to reporters while traveling with the president in the Philippines today.

“We have no indication, no evidence, and no reason to believe at all that any of these incidents are related to each other,” said Rubio.

He added, “We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber hackers, with criminals, with another nation-state, with a malign actor of any kind.”

Reproductive rights group celebrates Overton's failed confirmation and issues warning to Republicans

A prominent reproductive rights group celebrated the White House’s decision to pull its nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration.

“Heidi Overton posed a serious threat to the future of medication abortion access. Voters rejected that anti-abortion agenda nationwide, and our members kept the pressure on to tank her confirmation. If Republicans try to rush through another nominee who threatens our healthcare, we’re ready to keep fighting,” said Mini Timmaraju, the president and CEO of Reproductive Freedom for All (formerly NARAL Pro-Choice America).

As head of the FDA, Overton would have of overseen the agency that regulates medication abortions. In a 2023 policy brief for the conservative America First Policy Institute, Overton said Congress should strictly regulate the abortion pill mifeprestone “to protect women and children”, criticized federal policies that allow for the mailing of abortion pills and wrote that abortion “is corrosive to children, women, and society broadly”.

A number of lawmakers have raised concerns about a spat of outages over the past week, offered their perspective on what may be happening and called for increased regulations on artificial intelligence and tech companies.

“Over the last week, we’ve heard A LOT about ‘system outages’ from government agencies such as the FAA and NSA, as well as major companies like Microsoft and JPMorgan Chase. We don’t know the full story yet, but the chaos makes one thing obvious: Letting a handful of tech giants control this industry is just too dangerous,” said congresswoman Pramila Jayapal, a Democrat from Washington state.

“At this point, there is no indication that these incidents are the result of a cyberattack, foreign interference, or some coordinated effort. What appears to be happening instead is a consequence of how quickly artificial intelligence is changing the cybersecurity landscape,” Mark Warner, a Democratic senator from Virginia, said in a social media post. “While we still need to get to the bottom of these incidents, the evidence so far points toward legitimate efforts to secure our systems, not malicious activity.”

“The American people can see everything happening with these service outages, and they feel like they’re not getting the whole story. You can’t blame anybody for that. I’m a member of Congress, and I feel that way!” said senator Rick Scott, a Republican from Florida. “We need congressional briefings. These companies and federal agencies need to be fully transparent about what’s going on.”

“We don’t yet know what has caused recent outages at major companies and government agencies. We do know that advanced AI threatens to undermine cybersecurity for systems that make our hospitals, banks, and government run. And that could make these sort of outages much more common and damaging,” said congressman Greg Casar of Texas, who called for increased regulations on artificial intelligence.

Donald Trump has nominated Gen Chris LaNeve to be the US army’s top officer, making permanent his interim service after defense secretary Pete Hegseth unexpectedly fired his predecessor in April.

LaNeve had been serving as Hegseth’s senior military commander before his nomination for a fourth star and service as vice chief of staff of the army in February.

Democrat Raymond Smith haswon his race to represent North Carolina’s 3rd district in the House of Representatives. Smith beat out the Republican incumbent Greg Murphy in a narrow race.

The Associated Press has yet to call seven other House races and the Alaska Senate race as votes are still being counted from this month’s midterm elections.

The Board of Peace, which is overseeing the US-brokered ceasefire in Gaza, told the United Nations Security Council today that the ceasefire will collapse if steps are not taken to uphold the full peace agreement.

“The distance between what has been agreed and what is happening on the ground nonetheless remains the principal risk to the process,” says the report, a copy of which was obtained by The Associated Press.

It adds, “Restrictions imposed by Israel on the entry of supplies and equipment, compounded by funding shortfalls, continued to impede preparedness and response efforts,” calling for immediate “scaled-up entry of shelter and winterization materials.”

The Board delivered its first report to the United Nations in May, at which time it warned: “A ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely.”

Defense secretary Pete Hegseth said the Pentagon “will pass a full, clean audit by 2028” in response to news that the defense department failed a financial audit for the ninth year in a row.

The Defense Department was first audited in 2018 and given 10 years to pass an annual audit. An inspector general report released yesterday showed the agency had failed audit again for fiscal year 2026. The news comes as the Trump administration is seeking a record $1.5tn for defense spending.

Despite failing the audit, Hegseth wrote in a social media post: “We are more than halfway there – huge, historic progress so far.”

As defense department CFO Jules Hurst told Reuters, a majority of the Pentagon’s units passed audit for the first time. According to Hurst, 14 of the Pentagon’s 25 stand-alone reporting entities passed audit in the 2026 fiscal year, up from 11 the prior year.
