Democrat Mary Peltola closes in on victory in closely watched US Senate race in Alaska – live

2026-11-17 15:14

The final Senate race of this year’s midterm elections could be resolved tomorrow if Alaska election officials run the state’s ranked choice voting system to determine the result.

With 98% of ballots counted, Democratic challenger Mary Peltola is out in front with 49.98% of the vote. By contrast, incumbent Republican senator Dan S Sullivan has a 45.93% share, according to the latest Associated Press figures.

In terms of actual voting numbers, Peltola is ahead by just over 11,000, with 141,637 compared to Sullivan’s 130,162. All eyes will be on Peltola to see if she can keep the pace to achieve 50% of the ballot, two weeks on from the election.

If not, then voters’ second choice votes will be counted. The outcome will determine the final balance of power in the chamber for the next two years, with Democrats already holding a 55-44 majority.

It comes in a turbulent Alaska race, which has seen president Trump demand that Peltola be removed from the ballot and prosecuted. Piling on, Alaska’s Republican governor has since ordered an investigation into Peltola’s campaign.

A New York Times investigation reported that operatives working with Peltola’s campaign helped recruit another Dan Sullivan – Dan J Sullivan – a retired teacher from Petersburg, to split the incumbent’s vote. Peltola and her campaign have denied this.

The ‘other’ Sullivan, who stood as an independent candidate, has only received 4,684 votes – a 1.65% share – and is looking unlikely to be the cause of the Republican senator’s failure should he not retain his seat.

Elsewhere, president Trump has arrived in Manila, the capital of the Philippines, for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) summit, where he is due to meet south east Asian leaders and take part in the first leaders’ meeting of the Quad partnership – consisting of the US, Japan, India and Australia – since he returned to the White House in January 2025.

In lighter news today, new members in the US House are all taking part in the time honored tradition of the suite selection lottery, where every incoming representative has the chance to snag one of the available offices for the new Congress.

El-Sayed: 'It does not matter that you win elections if you don't do anything with the seat'

Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic-Senator elect from Michigan, said today that he would be happy to work with the Trump administration to pass one of his tentpole policy agenda items – Medicare or All.

“Everybody’s like, ‘ah well, you’d work with Donald Trump to get Medicare For All?’. I’m like hell yeah, because the point for me is Medicare For All. I don’t care who puts their name on it. I need to go back and talk to Michiganders, and I need to know that our service made their lives easier,” he said at the Atlantic Summit in Washington DC.

Earlier, when asked by interviewer Jonathan Lemire whether El-Sayed would prefer for Democrats to elect more progressive members, or grow a broader electoral map with more diverse ideologies, the incoming lawmaker said that he was less concerned about his colleagues’ politics, as opposed to their ability to unite and pass legislation.

“I don’t need everybody in my caucus to agree with me on everything, but I do need us to get into the business of legislating in ways that protect our rights and provide better, more durable public goods and protect us from the exceeding power of a few very, very large financially oriented organizations in our lives,” El-Sayed said. “It does not matter that you win elections if you don’t do anything with the seat.”

As Republicans scramble to shift blame for the recent chaotic FAA flight stoppage, JD Vance took a new tact, claiming a Democratic focus on transgender sports issues were to blame.

During an interview on Fox News on Monday, host Laura Ingraham pressed Vance on why his fellow Republicans had not been able to fix issues with air traffic control even though they had controlled the White House and Congress for the last two years.

“A lot of the stuff that needed to get fixed, we have been working on and we have been patching that stuff, Laura. But in terms of rebuilding the infrastructure using 21st-century technology, that is something that Congress needs to reinvest, give us the resources to do, and we will absolutely get it done,” Vance said.

Acknowledging that the Democratic party had taken back control of both the US House and the Senate after the 3 November midterms, Vance continued: “We could absolutely do this with Democrats in the new Congress if they set to side the ridiculous obsession with men in women’s sports and did what they said they would do, which was focus on affordability and focus on rebuilding our infrastructure.”

The comment sparked immediate outrage days after a four-hour nationwide flight stoppage on Friday that appears to have been caused by a transition from old copper telecommunication lines to fiber optic cable. The stoppage resulted in thousands of US flights being delayed or canceled, leaving passengers stranded or forcing them to resort to alternate travel methods to try to keep their weekend plans.

“What is this idiot talking about?” Fred Wellman, an incoming Democratic congressman from Missouri, posted on X.

“HE’S SO BAD AT THIS,” posted Pod Save America host Jon Favreau.

“Whatever consultant keeps telling him to randomly connect any issue to trans people is just stealing money,” journalist Jemele Hill posted on X. “The only person who is obsessed with this issue is JD Vance, and he’s not self-aware enough to realize how positively clueless this discourse makes him look.”

And one quick note about the US Senate race in Alaska. If Mary Peltola wins, she will be the first Democrat from the state to be elected to the Senate since 2009.

News of the rule commerce department rule comes at an inopportune time for Donald Trump, who is set to meet with China’s Xi Jinping this week at a trilateral summit alongside Russian president Vladimir Putin.

As my colleague Graeme Wearden notes at our designated business blog, China’s foreign ministry spokesperson GuoJiakun said Beijing would take “necessary measures” to safeguard its own interests, noting that the US has “repeatedly overstretched the concept of national security, abused export controls and even interfered in China’s normal trade and technological cooperation with other countries”.

Trump administration announces ongoing investigation into China-linked data center company Megaspeed

Two departments have confirmed they are investigating computing company Megaspeed, suspecting it of violating US export controls by helping to ship tech giant Nvidia’s chips to China.

A joint statement issued by the Department of Commerce and the Department of Justice explains that Megaspeed has been under investigation since 2024, for “potential export control violations.”

In May 2025, Department of Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed. In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its “no sell” list, they explain.

And they also state that commerce department agents conducted an on-site, in-person, end-use check at Megaspeed in June 2025 and interviewed CEO Alice Huang.

The Department of Justice then took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed.

Touting their own work, the departments added:

This investigation is the result of the excellent work of the Department of Commerce under Secretary Howard Lutnick and the law enforcement efforts of the Department of Justice under Attorney General Todd Blanche.

That, presumably, is a response to suggestions that Lutnick could be fired following accusations that he had shielded Nvidia from federal oversight, and had failed to blacklist Megaspeed.

The news of this investigation comes after the commerce department announced an interim final rule on Monday, written by and at the direction of the treasury department and the National Security Council, which curbs remote access to advanced artificial intelligence chips by third countries. This includes Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Brazil.

Notably, it addresses the concern that China has been able to use third countries to access advanced chips in the wake of the Nvidia scandal. The involvement of the US treasury department is particularly striking given secretary Scott Bessent’s jabs in an interview with CNBC last week that he has been pushing “for months” for greater exports controls to China on advanced chips, but Howard Lutnick’s commerce department ultimately “has the pen”, when it comes to regulation.

Trump announces deal with Thailand he says will remove tariffs on US exports

Donald Trump is meeting with Thailand’s prime minister Anutin Charnvirakul to sign what the White House has described as a “trade deal”.

The US president said Thailand will eliminate all tariffs on US exports and remove substantial non-tariff barriers as part of a trade agreement Trump has hailed as “historic”.

“Today’s deal lays the foundation for much stronger trading relationship that will bring the United States and Thailand closer than ever before,” Trump said, adding that Thailand will buy $2.6bn in additional US agricultural products.

Trump said he is looking forward to “finalising the details of our formal agreement in the coming weeks”.

On a far more muted statement on Tuesday, Thailand’s foreign ministry said the two leaders would sign a “joint statement marking progress in negotiations”.

Washington and Bangkok have been negotiating a trade agreement for the last year. The US is Thailand’s largest export market, having accounted for about 24% of the south-east Asian country’s total exports as of October.
