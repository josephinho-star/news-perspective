Dow rallies 500 points as benchmark heads for best week since April 2025: Live updates

2026-11-27 15:44

U.S. equities rose on Friday as investors try to wrap up a turbulent shortened trading week.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average was higher by 510 points, or 1%. The S&P 500 added 1.2%, while the Nasdaq Composite advanced 1.7%. The moves come amid a volatile week during an even more volatile month.

Technology stocks supported the bounce. "Magnificent Seven" members Meta Platforms and Amazon popped around 3% each, while chipmaker Broadcom increased 2%. Shares of artificial intelligence lab Anthropic added more than 8%.

The major U.S. stock benchmarks were headed for strong weekly gains. Entering Friday's session, the Dow is up 5%, on pace for its biggest weekly advance since April 2025. The S&P 500 has climbed nearly 5% in that time, while the Nasdaq has surged more than 5%.

The week kicked off with Jensen Huang getting reinstated as CEO at Nvidia, a move that sent the stock up 31% during Monday's session and drove the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite to post their best days since April 2025. The Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced more than 1,400 points, or roughly 3%.

Since then, however, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq have come under pressure as Nvidia and other AI-related stocks have given back some of Monday's gains.

Prior to Huang's departure earlier this month, sentiment toward the AI trade was already downbeat, with investors growing concerned about debt financing, signs of moderation in capital expenditures and Democratic wins in the midterm elections resulting in a slowdown in data center development.

"The hyperscalers ultimately have to get an ROI, and they ultimately need to rebuild their free cash flow, and people like Anthropic need to prove that they can generate the revenue growth that people expect and ultimately be very profitable and free-cash-flow generative as well," Janus Henderson portfolio manager Richard Clode said Wednesday on CNBC's "The Exchange".

"That's all got to go hand in hand ultimately or that capex is not going to sustain," he added. "It is all part of the AI technology wave that we think will play out for five to 10 years at minimum to come."

To be sure, some on Wall Street believe that while there are long-term anxieties surrounding the AI trade, the recent weakness has had more to do with the prospect of the Federal Reserve raising interest rates. The latest personal consumption expenditures price index reading this week reinforced traders' expectations for multiple hikes over the coming months.

"Most of this stuff really comes down to the Fed. That's the intermediate term factor," Warren Pies, 3Fourteen Research co-founder, told CNBC's "Closing Bell" on Wednesday. He added that "many" people his firm spoke with expressed worries of a "replay of 2018," when the central bank hiked rates four times. "I think that there's so many eerie similarities, and so that's what we're watching."

The U.S. stock market was closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving, and it's scheduled to close on Friday at 1 p.m. ET.
