How wrecked GOP must rebuild, getting tired of ‘winning’ and other commentary

2026-11-06 23:35

Published Nov. 6, 2026, 6:35 p.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Conservative: How Wrecked GOP Must Rebuild

The core cause of Tuesday’s epic Republican losses is clear, explains Henry Olsen at The Washington Post: “Independents combined with a significant number of non-MAGA Republicans to reject the Trump GOP.” Democrats won “independents by 17 points” and “about 15 percent of non-MAGA Republicans” plus about “98 percent of Democrats,” an “almost unheard-of” party unity. “Every Republican gerrymander failed,” and just about “every place with any significant farm sector swung massively against Republicans.” In all, “the consequences of Trump’s decision to ignore public opinion throughout his second term have now been laid bare.” Now that “much of the Trump GOP has come crashing down,” bet that “any Republican with an eye on the future will be looking for a new set of plans for the rebuilding.”

Republican: Getting Tired of ‘Winning’?

The president “has managed to tarnish the MAGA brand so much that now it has a higher unfavorable rating than the Democratic Socialists of America,” marvels Kevin Igoe at The Hill. With a terrible approval rating, Trump still asked “voters to pretend he was on the ballot” — an act of “pure political malpractice.” He pushed Sens. Thom Tillis and Jodi Ernst into retiring, and Dems won both seats; he tapped Ken Paxton and Darline Graham to run in Texas and South Carolina, and both lost. Worst of all was “his preoccupation with issues most voters did not care about”: “As American families became more and more concerned about the rising costs of groceries, the president told them those price increases were ‘peanuts’ and a Democratic ‘hoax.’ ”

Libertarian: Stop Doing Foreigners’ Dirty Work

US companies should quit “doing dirty work for foreign censors,” thunders Jack Verrill at Reason. “When Russia designated the Human Rights Foundation as ‘undesirable’ earlier this year,” HRF soon got “a notice demanding it block Russians from accessing its signature Tyranny Tracker” — a notice from its web host, WP Engine, warning that if it didn’t “geo-block Tyranny Tracker, the web hosting company would ‘geo-block the site on [the HRF’s] behalf.’” This was a US firm “censoring an American nonprofit at the behest of a foreign government — dutifully passing the order along as if it were an overdue library book notice.” It’s not just Russia: UK regulator Ofcom has sent hundreds of “notice to U.S. companies under its internet censorship law.” The proposed “GRANITE Act would be a start” on ensuring that US firms don’t enforce foreign censorship schemes.

Eye on Europe: EU Elites’ Power Grab

Poland’s liberal prime minister, Donald Tusk, “overreached” in sending riot police and suspending two judges in the political fight over the country’s Constitutional Tribunal — then “the European Commission, unsurprisingly, sided” with him, grumbles Jakub Grygiel at The Wall Street Journal. The drama now sparking “domestic unrest and European instability” is “symptomatic of a much larger conflict” over whether an EU member’s constitution should be “interpreted by domestic authorities” or “higher European institutions.” EU elites “have pushed hard for top-down integration,” pushing liberal policies on such issues as immigration, a green transition and same-sex marriage onto member states. “The EU claim of superiority is tearing apart the Continent,” even as it faces “rapacious powers to the east and formidable demographic pressure from the south.”

Pharma watch: Trump Brings Prices Down

“While the overall CPI rose 3.7 percent year to year, prescription drug prices fell 4.5 percent—the largest year-over-year decline since 1963,” cheers Joel Zinberg at City Journal. Thank President Trump: His TrumpRx discounts have “been a major factor in lowering prescription prices throughout 2026,” particularly for GLP-1s. Plus: Faster generic-drug approvals “bring down prices” by creating competition. And his Most-Favored-Nation initiative has “Medicaid drug prices down to the lowest rates paid in other developed nations.” Overall higher prices “remain a worry,” but “Americans can cheer the declines in drug prices this past year and look forward to future price cuts.”
