Turkish sailors fear for their lives amid Black Sea drone attacks

2026-09-23 10:35

Turkish fishermen injured in a deadly attack off Crimea say they have yet to return to sea, as a rise in strikes on civilian vessels in the Black Sea puts crews under growing pressure.

Ahmet Özlü, 40, was aboard the Turkish-flagged fishing boat Duru 67 when it was struck on June 5. He said the crew had just finished breakfast when an explosion sent them onto the deck. A second blast hit while they were there.

“There was fire and smoke, the blast lifted us up and threw us left and right,” he told Agence France-Presse.

The fishermen found their captain, 45-year-old Cüneyt Varlık, badly wounded on the bridge. The boat caught fire and began to sink. According to the Turkish Coast Guard, another fishing vessel rescued all five injured crew members. Varlık died during the return voyage.

Özlü said he and the others feared another strike as they waited for help. He suffered extensive shrapnel injuries, while his older brother Mehmet, 53, sustained internal and external injuries. Neither has returned to sea.

No one has claimed responsibility for the attack. Ahmet Özlü said coastguard personnel told the survivors that a Ukrainian drone had struck their boat by mistake. The coastguard’s public statement did not identify who carried out the attack.

Mehmet Özlü said he wanted an explanation and compensation from Ukraine. “This has ruined my and my children’s future,” he told AFP.

The attack is one of a growing number involving vessels in the Black Sea as Russia and Ukraine intensify their attacks on each other’s maritime trade. President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan told the UN General Assembly on Tuesday that recent attacks on commercial ships were unacceptable regardless of who carried them out.

According to figures cited by Tamer Kıran, chairman of Turkey’s Chamber of Maritime Commerce, 226 civilian and commercial vessels had been attacked in the Black Sea since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

He said on August 27 that 163 of those attacks had occurred in the first eight months of this year.

Kıran said 22 Turkish-owned vessels had been attacked in the preceding two months. Industry sources told AFP that four more had been hit since then. The figures are attributed to the chamber and the industry sources; they are not an independently verified count of attacks.

Among those injured was Enes Torun, 21, who was aboard the Reyhan Sarı when it was attacked on July 22 after taking on a cargo of Russian coal. One crew member was killed and three were wounded.

Torun, who is training to become a captain, said shrapnel struck him as he slept. His physical injuries are healing, but he has not gone back to sea.

“It’s impossible to work in the Black Sea, especially now,” he told AFP. He said many sailors were turning down jobs there because of the danger.

Turkey maintains ties with both Russia and Ukraine and has sought an agreement to protect shipping. Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said the two sides had responded to a recent Turkish proposal with demands that were difficult to reconcile, according to AFP.

Ahmet Baran Can, a spokesman for the Turkish Maritime Workers’ Platform, accused both countries of disregarding seafarers’ lives in their efforts to damage each other’s economies.

Serhat Güvenç, an international relations expert at Kadir Has University, said the attacks were part of a broader effort by each side to deny the other the use of Black Sea shipping.

He suggested Ukraine’s frustration with Turkey’s stance toward vessels linked to Russia’s so-called shadow fleet could also be a factor.

For fishing families along Turkey’s Black Sea coast, staying ashore can mean losing their livelihood. Mustafa Yılmaz, a local leader in the fishermen’s neighborhood, said crews also worried about hauling unexploded war debris into their nets.

“You go out at night wondering if you’ll hit something,” he told AFP. “It’s really tough.”
