Met settles claim brought by Palestine activist ‘treated like a criminal’ during arrest

2026-11-05 09:47

The Metropolitan police have settled a claim brought by a 68-year-old pro-Palestinian activist who accused the force of treating him “like a criminal” when he was arrested under anti-terrorism legislation.

Tej Singh Bhandal was returning to the UK on Eurostar earlier this year when he was pulled from a queue by police at St Pancras International, London.

After refusing to give authorities the pin code to his mobile phone, he was arrested and charged under schedule seven of the Terrorism Act 2000, he said.

He spent several hours in police detention, and brought a legal challenge over his arrest. The Met has agreed to settle the claim and close the criminal investigation against him, the Press Association reported.

Bhandal’s lawyers said Scotland Yard accepted that supporting and funding lawful political protest organisations – naming the BDS (Boycott, Divestment, Sanctions) movement and the Palestine Solidarity Campaign – did not automatically establish a link to terrorism.

The Met police has been approached comment.

Bhandal described his arrest as “incredibly humiliating” and “an abuse of power”.

“I was handcuffed and led through the station in full view of the public. I felt like the tabloid newspaper stereotype of a person of colour, and I was treated like a criminal,” he told the Press Association.

Bhandal, who is involved with the Palestine Solidarity Campaign, was previously arrested in 2025 as part of a police investigation into a Palestine Action-linked incident at the Elbit Systems factory in Bristol.

He was questioned by counter-terrorism police over four days, during which his house was searched and his devices seized, but was released with no further action taken.

He believes an “intelligence flag” was placed on his name after the incident, leading to his arrest at St Pancras.

The Met’s decision to settle Bhandal’s claim came as more than two dozen protesters were arrested outside the supreme court on Wednesday morning while an appeal was being heard against the ban on Palestine Action.

He said people were being “singled out by the authorities for activism” and that “the creeping criminalisation of protest action is a consequence of government policy.”.

Alex Kenney, a civil liberties lawyer at Hodge Jones + Allen, which acted for Bhandal, said “adequate safeguards” were essential to prevent the “arbitrary” use of anti-terrorism laws.

“Our hope is that Tej’s case leads to more stringent adherence to the statutory obligations under the Terrorism Act and changes how the police conduct themselves in future,” she said.
