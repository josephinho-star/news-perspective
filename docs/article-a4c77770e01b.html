Boy, 5, dies in blistering hot car while family hosts party in 100-degree heat, dad pins blame on teen son

2026-09-16 00:08

A 5-year-old boy in Arizona died after being left in a sweltering vehicle while his parentes hosted a party — his father shamelessly blamed his teen son for the tragedy.

Theo Sabo was left in his family’s car around 1 p.m. Sunday after returning home from a baptism of another child at their church, FOX 10 reported.

Sabo’s family was hosting a party with around 50 friends and family when, more than two-and-a-half hours later, the family realized Theo was missing.

The family discovered the boy unresponsive in their sweltering vehicle outside their Peoria home — where temperatures reached a scorching 106 degrees that day, historical forecast records show.

CPR was administered before Theo was rushed to a nearby hospital where he was tragically pronounced dead.

The couple have four other children aged 18, 15, 14, and 9.

Mihail reportedly told police that he asked his 14-year-old son to get Theo out of the car upon arriving home from the baptism — but the teen told cops that he was never asked to do so, the outlet reported.

During separate court hearings on Monday, the boy’s mother Anca was visibly upset and sobbing while her husband Mihail kept quiet with his head down, according to a livestream of the Maricopa County Superior Court.

“This was a, I can’t overstress this, a tragedy,” the couple’s defense attorney Leah Dodd told the court during Anca’s hearing.

Dodd doubled-down on the account of Mihail asking their 14-year-old son to get Theo out of the car.

“Mom is hosting a party at their home, assumes dad is gonna get [Theo]. Dad asks the 14-year-old. That’s not asking a young child to get another child out of the vehicle,’ Dodd argued, while Anca clutched her chest and wept.

Theo “was sleeping, and there was a lot going on, but this does not deviate from what a normal person would do in this situation,” the defense attorney continued.

Meanwhile, Maricopa County Attorney Brooke Gaunt argued that “the child was in the car for more than two and a half hours and the parents were not aware of his location during that whole time.”

The couple’s defense attorney asserted that criminally negligent homicide and child abuse charges were not warranted in this case and called their son’s death “an unimaginable tragedy.”

“She called 911, she administered life-saving efforts, she answered all questions of detectives. This is not someone who is running away,” Dodd argued.

At one point, Anca loudly wailed and pleaded with the judge: “I want to see my kids. Please let me see my kids.”

During Mihail’s appearance, immediately after his wife’s, the husband appeared disheveled and showed little emotion while he hung his head low for much of the hearing.

The couple, who have been ruled as co-defendants in their separate matters, were each given $150,000 bond and are not allowed to have contact with each other.

They can, however, have supervised visitation with their four other children.

Mihail and Anca have been married for almost 20 years and have lived in the Arizona area since 2011, Dodd stated during the hearing.

“Theo was deeply loved, and his precious life will never be forgotten,” an online fundraiser for the family mourned.

“Thank you for loving, praying for, and supporting the Sabo family as they walk through this unimaginable loss.”

Mihail and Anca are due back in court on Sept. 21.

The Post reached out to the Peoria Police Department and Leah Dodd, the couple’s defense attorney but did not immediately hear back.

The Maricopa County Attorney’s Office declined to comment as the cases remain ongoing.
