Amtrak stops all train service around Washington DC after person struck on the tracks

2026-11-16 21:28

A train struck and killed a person on the tracks just outside Washington, D.C., halting Amtrak service around the busy Capitol on Monday afternoon as police investigate.

First responders in Prince George’s County, Maryland, were sent to the tracks near 57th Avenue and Tuxedo Road after a 1:15 p.m. report that a train had struck a pedestrian, the county’s police department told The Post.

Police are “actively investigating,” the department said.

The victim has not been identified.

The train was Amtrak No. 2112, which left Washington for New York at noon and struck a trespasser near New Carrollton, NBC Washington reported, citing the railroad.

Amtrak’s own X account was much thinner on detail.

“Service around Washington, D.C., has stopped due to local municipal officials placing a hold on the tracks,” the railroad wrote, in a post that did not mention the death.

Riders who asked when trains would move again got no details, either.

“As this was done by a local municipal government, we are unable to provide an ETA,” Amtrak told a rider on X.

Amtrak did not immediately respond to The Post’s request for comment.
