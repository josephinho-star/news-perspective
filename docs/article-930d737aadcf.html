Turkey’s central bank revokes license of Papel payments company amid money laundering case

2026-09-23 11:59

Turkey’s central bank has revoked the operating license of electronic money and payment services company Papel, which has been at the center of an investigation into allegations that proceeds from illegal betting, fraud and other crimes were laundered through its payment infrastructure, the T24 news website reported.

The decision, taken by the central bank on September 18, was published in the Official Gazette on Wednesday. Papel had been licensed to operate as an electronic money institution since October 2022.

The central bank cited provisions of Turkey’s law governing payment systems, payment services and electronic money institutions in revoking the license but did not provide further details about the reasons for the decision.

The move comes eight months after an İstanbul court ordered the seizure of Papel’s assets and appointed the state-run Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF) as trustee to the company as part of an investigation into alleged money laundering and illegal betting.

The investigation into the company was launched in January by the İstanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office after central bank inspections and analyses by the Financial Crimes Investigation Board (MASAK).

Prosecutors alleged that proceeds from illegal betting, unauthorized forex trading and fraud were funneled into the financial system through electronic money and payment companies and then transferred through numerous companies in Turkey and abroad in an attempt to launder the funds.

Police carried out raids in İstanbul and seven other provinces, detaining 38 suspects. Fifteen were subsequently jailed pending trial, while 23 were placed under judicial supervision.

Prosecutors also alleged that some software engineers, IT staff and project managers within the organization helped transfer and conceal criminal proceeds through electronic payment infrastructure, software systems and financial routing mechanisms.

The investigation later resulted in an indictment against 44 suspects. Prosecutors in June sought prison sentences ranging from nine to 27 years over allegations that proceeds from illegal betting were laundered through Papel.

Company founder Seyhan İbrahim Yıldırım and four other suspects were accused of managing a criminal organization, while 39 others were charged with membership in the organization.

Turkish media reports citing the case file said a MASAK analysis identified TL 1.5 billion ($35 million) in transactions linked to Papel and examined connections involving Dubai, Georgia, Cyprus and Europe.

Investigators allegedly described the company as a “backup” mechanism used to launder funds after other illicit payment channels were disrupted.

The case file also reportedly drew attention to Papel’s rapid increase in capital from TL 7 million to TL 300 million despite the company recording losses, as well as an alleged TL 827 million in cash inflows by Yıldırım whose source investigators said could not be established.

Papel was founded by Yıldırım in 2020 and received its electronic money license from the central bank in 2022. At the time the investigation became public in January, Papel and another company founded by Yıldırım, banking software provider P Tech, employed more than 150 people, while Papel reportedly had around 200,000 users.

The revocation follows a similar move against fintech company Papara, whose operating license the central bank canceled in October 2025 after the company became the subject of a separate investigation into alleged illegal betting activities.
