Iconic California bridge reopens after $8.3M restoration — only for officials to deface it

2026-11-21 18:10

California taxpayers just shelled out $8.3 million to restore a graffiti-covered landmark, but the fresh coat of paint isn’t staying fresh for long.

Stevenson Bridge, the famously spray-painted span over Putah Creek, has barely reopened after months of construction, and new graffiti is already appearing across its freshly restored arches and columns.

Officials from Yolo and Solano counties celebrated the bridge’s reopening Friday, but the festivities came with an eyebrow-raising twist. Photos released by Yolo County show officials spray-painting a “Putah Creek” stencil onto one of the bridge’s pillars.

Other messages and drawings have since begun appearing on the newly painted structure, raising questions about whether the bridge’s decades-old graffiti tradition is already being welcomed back.

“Is graffiti now legal? Can we do it anywhere?” one person commented.

Whether officials intend to allow the artwork to return remains unclear but have previously declined to say whether the graffiti that once covered virtually every surface would be permitted after the rehabilitation.

The bridge closed in May for the $8.3 million project, which included seismic retrofitting, foundation repairs, scour protection and concrete restoration.

Rather than replace the aging landmark, officials chose to preserve its distinctive arches and historic character.

Built in 1923, the 298-foot Stevenson Bridge spans Putah Creek, connecting Yolo and Solano counties west of Davis.

Officials describe it as California’s oldest surviving reinforced concrete through-tied-arch bridge and possibly the oldest of its kind in the country.

Before construction, virtually every inch of the bridge had been covered in graffiti, making its paint-splattered appearance a local trademark for generations.
