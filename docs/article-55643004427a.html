49ers release Aiyuk, ending WR's tumultuous tenure with team

2026-11-23 23:08

The tumultuous relationship between the San Francisco 49ers and Brandon Aiyuk has ended with the team releasing the wide receiver Monday.

The move ends Aiyuk's tenure with the 49ers after about 6½ years. Because his release is happening after the trade deadline, Aiyuk is subject to waivers. If he's unclaimed in the next 24 hours, he will become an unrestricted free agent who is able to sign anywhere.

Because the move is happening after June 1, the dead money remaining on his deal will be split into $12.35 million for 2026 and $19.29 million in 2027. The Niners will receive a cap credit of nearly $5 million in March and are also aiming to recoup additional bonus money from Aiyuk.

Aiyuk's release has been months in the making after Niners general manager John Lynch said Jan. 21 that the team would be moving on from him. It happened now only because Aiyuk hired agent Drew Rosenhaus in late October, applied for reinstatement with the NFL and reported to the team Nov. 6.

That day, Aiyuk, Rosenhaus and the Niners met for a little more than an hour with the receiver saying he is feeling "great" and "healthy."

"It was a long recovery process, a long rehab process," Aiyuk said then. "But I'm back to 100% and looking to get reinstated to the National Football League."

For their part, the Niners have sought repayment from Aiyuk for about $13 million in bonus money they previously paid him.

The NFL officially reinstated Aiyuk on Nov. 9 after he reported for a physical. The league provided the Niners a two-week roster exemption to sort through any remaining business with Monday as the day the exemption expired.

Because the exemption ended after the NFL trade deadline, the 49ers had just two options: release Aiyuk or keep him. Coach Kyle Shanahan and Lynch had remained adamant since January that Aiyuk was done as a Niner, which meant his release was always a matter of when, not if.

While Aiyuk's release always seemed inevitable, the Niners clung to hope that they could find a trade partner rather than releasing him. Along the way, Aiyuk repeatedly voiced his displeasure with how the Niners handled the situation as they continued to retain his contractual rights.

Despite a lack of interest and momentum toward a deal, San Francisco held on to Aiyuk through free agency, the draft and every other offseason benchmark. In early June, Aiyuk began to speak out publicly for the first time. In a pair of videos posted to Instagram, he called the 49ers "dumb" and "stupid" for paying him and suggested San Francisco was scared to release him because of how he might play for another team.

Just before that, on June 3, Aiyuk also dealt with the Santa Clara County District Attorney's Office issuing a warrant for him on the misdemeanor charge of exhibition of speeding. That warrant is in reference to video Aiyuk posted to YouTube in December in which he appears to tape himself from the driver's seat going well over the posted 40 mph speed limit on a road that runs past Levi's Stadium.

Through all that, rumors of Aiyuk's desire to play for the Washington Commanders and with quarterback Jayden Daniels (Aiyuk's close friend from Arizona State) persisted. He posted photos of himself in a Commanders hat, and Daniels and the Commanders were among the five accounts he followed on Instagram.

After an apparent rift with Daniels, Aiyuk and the Washington quarterback patched things up, ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported Nov. 18. That could open the door for Aiyuk to land in Washington, though Rosenhaus has indicated several times that he has multiple interested teams in Aiyuk.

Though Aiyuk is now free to sign anywhere, his departure ends a confounding partnership between team and player that began to deteriorate when the sides started negotiating a contract extension in 2024.

The Niners and Aiyuk went back and forth on a potential contract early in 2024 and, as other receivers around the league got paid, the price for his services continued to climb. Finally, the stalemate that had Aiyuk holding in during that summer's training camp seemed to reach a conclusion when he signed a four-year, $120 million extension with $76 million in guarantees Aug. 29, 2024.

At the time, the 49ers hoped the new deal would put any built-up issues with Aiyuk in the past, but that vanished less than a month later. On Sept. 27, 2024, Aiyuk and Shanahan had an awkward exchange on the practice field when the receiver showed up wearing red shorts instead of the requisite black ones his teammates were wearing.

From there, the sides never seemed to get on the same page again with Aiyuk struggling early in the season as he averaged 58.5 receiving yards with no touchdowns in the first six games. He tore the ACL, MCL and meniscus in his right knee against the Kansas City Chiefs on Oct. 20, 2024, ending his season. It would be the last game he'd play as a Niner.

The 49ers also hoped to trade Aiyuk last offseason, sources told ESPN, but the injury made getting a good return in a potential deal unrealistic, and that never gained traction. Aiyuk's $24.935 million roster bonus for 2026 became guaranteed on April 1, 2025, effectively ensuring he'd be with the Niners for the 2025 season.

Hopes that Aiyuk would return and contribute existed for chunks of the offseason, but when he didn't show up for multiple in-house rehab sessions, the Niners voided the roughly $27 million in guarantees his contract held for 2026 in July of last year.

On Nov. 22, 2025, Shanahan said that in more than two decades coaching he'd never had a situation when a player's contract was voided. Around that time, Aiyuk stopped reporting to the Niners facility altogether and cut off communication with the franchise's leadership.

"It's something I've never seen in 22 years of coaching," Shanahan said at the time. "It's unfortunate and it's confusing because it's confusing for all of us, but it eventually becomes it is what it is."

On Dec. 13, the 49ers placed Aiyuk on the reserve/left team list, a designation that ended his season and any chance he would play for the team again. He did not report for or participate in the team's offseason program or training camp.

At his end-of-season news conference, Lynch said moving on from Aiyuk would allow for more salary cap flexibility, though most of that will come in future years. Lynch added he "wish I knew" why Aiyuk stopped communicating with the team.

Before his knee injury, Aiyuk had emerged as one of the league's most productive wideouts. He had 75 catches for 1,342 yards and seven touchdowns on his way to second-team All-Pro honors in 2023.

A first-round pick (No. 25) in the 2020 NFL draft, Aiyuk had 294 receptions for 4,305 yards and 25 touchdowns in 69 regular-season games for the 49ers.
