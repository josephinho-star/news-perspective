What it's like at a climate conference as talks collapse

2026-11-22 22:40

After two weeks of negotiations, international climate talks fail to reach an agreement. NPR's Rachel Waldholz was in Turkey for the conference.

International climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended today after negotiations fell apart. NPR's Rachel Waldholz has been at the conference and sent us this report.

RACHEL WALDHOLZ, BYLINE: I'm here in the Antalya Expo Center, which, for the past two weeks, has been packed with some 70,000 people who came to the COP31 climate conference. Now it's almost completely empty. In fact, they're disassembling parts of the venue around me. The climate talks ran two days past their scheduled end. By the time the gavel fell, many negotiators had already left to catch their flights home. Australia climate chief Chris Bowen led the negotiations.

CHRIS BOWEN: I haven't slept for the last three nights, been negotiating all the way through, but more importantly, people on delegations came at it and gave it everything they had.

WALDHOLZ: Delegates came to Antalya with long wish lists, from electrifying more of the global economy to money for developing countries to help deal with the impacts of climate change. Looming over it all is that this is the first summit to squarely face the reality that the planet will miss a key target of the Paris Climate Agreement, reaching warming of 1.5 degrees Celsius or 2.7 degrees Fahrenheit above pre-Industrial levels. The U.N. says limiting that overshoot requires cutting the use of fossil fuels nearly in half in the next 10 to 15 years. Many countries wanted an agreement explicitly calling for a transition away from coal, oil and natural gas. But big oil-producing countries, like Saudi Arabia, blocked any mention of fossil fuels. Ultimately, the European Union decided no deal was better than the deal on the table. Darragh O'Brien is Ireland's environment minister.

DARRAGH O'BRIEN: We will not endorse a cover decision or an Antalya pact that does not even mention fossil fuels, which is the key driver of climate change.

WALDHOLZ: Many negotiators left the expo center dragging their suitcases on the way to the airport. They'll pick up negotiations next year at the next climate summit in Ethiopia. I'm Rachel Waldholz in Antalya, Turkey.
