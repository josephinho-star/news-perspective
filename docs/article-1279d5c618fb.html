Ranking the top six 'Grand Theft Auto VI' in-game radio stations, from V-Rock to Vice City FM

2026-11-22 00:00

If you’ve played even five minutes of "Grand Theft Auto VI" since its release, you know just how immersive this video game is.

There are miles of swamps and beaches to explore, realistic NPC interactions and I even made an entire article about how realistic the game’s version of South Florida is when compared to its real-life counterpart.

But one mechanic that has been included in the new game that’s been around since the early days of the series are the radio stations, and "GTA VI" has the most diverse radio dial yet.

All in all, there are 22 unique radio stations in the game, and each of them offers a different soundtrack to your wheeling and stealing as you traverse through Leonida.

Being a huge fan of the series and music in general, I thought it would be fun to rank my six favorite "GTA VI" radio stations.

This wasn’t easy, as there are a ton of great stations to choose from, and I even had to omit a few that I really liked (sorry, Back Country Radio).

With that said, let’s rank the best that "GTA VI" has to offer in the form of music.

By virtue of hitting the "quantity over quality" metrics, Flash FM sneaks its way onto the list at number six.

Don't get me wrong, there are heaps of awful songs on this station, but there are some gems in there too.

Echosmith, Foster The People, Gwen Stefani, and MGMT will have Millennials bobbing their heads as they commit vehicular homicide (in the game), and newcomers like Ella Langley and Addison Rae offer enough to be passable cruising songs when there's nothing else on.

Flash FM isn't the stalwart it was back in the '80s for "Grand Theft Auto: Vice City," but there is enough fun to be had here for this station to find its way into the rotation.

This one might be a bit of a nostalgia pick for me, but I don't care.

Having grown up and gone to high school in South Florida, a lot of the songs on this station were popular right around the time my life was centered around algebra tests and getting rejected by chicks, so playing a game where I can drive around a virtual representation of my hometown while blaring rap songs that were popular 15 years ago is an auto win.

If you're into 2000s and 2010s rap, you'll probably have your radio dial locked on Dirty South Classics, with heavy hitters like Lil Wayne, Outkast, Drake, Rick Ross and even Jay Z being well represented here.

There is no better feeling than cruising Miami Beach at night listening to "No Lie" by 2Chainz, and it's even cooler when that is also a core memory from your adolescence, minus the cocaine and dead hookers.

Now we are getting into rarified air, and Vice City FM begins a string of stations with way more good than bad on them.

For those of you who wanted the '80s vibes of "Vice City" to carry over to the new game, you're in luck.

Vice City FM is a carryover from the old Playstation 2 game, and the songs you'll find on here will have you thinking you hopped in a DeLorean and landed in 1986.

Genesis, Culture Club, Lionel Richie, Billy Idol, The Cars and so much more, you'll be pulling out the AquaNet and legwarmers before your first hour of gameplay.

The neon lights of South Florida mesh perfectly with the 80s vibes, and this station makes for a cool nighttime drive soundtrack.

For an added bonus, Weird Al Yankovic is one of the in-game DJs, which I thought was a nice touch.

Metal, metal and more metal. That's what you're going to find on The Chamber.

While I'm partial to the older stuff, this station has a great mix of classic and newer metal to bridge the generational gap for headbangers.

If you're a spiritual Boomer like me, you'll be happy to know acts like Iron Maiden, Judas Priest, Megadeth and, of course, Metallica are littered all over The Chamber.

New-school metal disciples will be pleased to hear the likes of Linkin Park, System of a Down, Tool and Sepultura mixed in.

Committing virtual crimes is so much more fun when it's set to the brutal riffing and blistering solos of Judas Priest's "Freewheel Burning," but don't just take my word for it, try it yourself.

And hey, the station is hosted by Slayer's Tom Araya and Kerry King, so you know it's curated by two legends.

Can you name a better combination than the sun-soaked streets of South Florida and some soothing yacht rock?

Of course you can't, which is why Emotion 98.3 is sitting in the silver medal spot for "GTA VI" radio stations.

Emotion makes its triumphant return as one of the stations featured in "GTA: Vice City," and its setlist will have you looking for the nearest super yacht to steal so that you can enjoy the songs properly.

You want soft rock? How does Ace, Chicago, The Doobie Brothers, and Bruce Hornsby sound?

And that's just the tip of the iceberg.

I spent my entire life being serenaded by these guys while navigating the intracoastal waterways of South Florida, and now I get to do it in a video game.

Life sure is sweet sometimes!

It was never going to be anything else.

If you played "GTA: Vice City," you know how legendary this station was.

Well, it's back and better than ever, featuring a wide range of classic rock artists that offer the best company on any long drive around Leonida.

There are too many bangers to even count, but I'll try: Def Leppard, AC/DC, Aerosmith, Dire Straits, Foreigner, Blue Oyster Cult, Motley Crue, Styx, and ZZ Top, just to name a few.

Seriously, I haven't even mentioned another half dozen artists who rank as some of my favorites of all time.

Oh, and they have Van Halen, too. In case I needed to nerd out any more than I already am.

I also love how they included several Tom Petty songs, given his connection to the Sunshine State.

Finally, the station is hosted by Lindsey Buckingham of Fleetwood Mac fame and Michael Freaking McDonald.

When the king of Blue Eyed Soul is spinning tunes, it's probably a station worth listening to.
