Paris Hilton and AOC team up in Capitol crusade to stop AI porn nightmare that targets many female lawmakers

2026-11-17 23:46

WASHINGTON — Reality TV star Paris Hilton on Tuesday begged Republican House leaders to pass the DEFIANCE Act, pointing out that more than half the female members of Congress are victims of deepfake porn.

“Of the 156 women serving in the 119th Congress, 83 have sexual deepfakes online right now. That is more than half the women in Congress,” Hilton said at a press conference on Capitol Hill, surrounded by Democratic and Republican members of Congress.

It was the second trip to the Capitol this year for Hilton, the 45-year-old celebrity businesswoman who has become an advocate for the legislation, which combats non-consensual sexually explicit AI-generated deepfakes and digital forgeries.

“I will come back to these halls as many times as it takes until the Defiance Act is signed. Because the abuse hasn’t stopped, and neither will I,” she vowed.

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) and Rep. Laurel Lee (R-Fla.), co-sponsors of the legislation, joined Hilton in appealing to the GOP leadership to put the DEFIANCE Act on the House calendar.

Everyday the DEFIANCE Act isn’t signed into law “is a day that more children, women and people are victimized with no recourse,” AOC said.

“Thousands of people in this country, if not tens of thousands and eventually millions, could be subject to sexual abuse on our watch just due to complacency.”

Hilton had a one-on-one meeting with Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) on Tuesday as part of her lobbying effort but declined to tell reporters if he was receptive to her appeal.

In an impassioned plea, the Hilton hotels heiress addressed Johnson and other members of the GOP leadership directly: “I am speaking directly to you. Will you allow this to continue to happen to your colleagues? How about the women in your district? How about your daughters, your wives, or your sisters?”

Hilton spent much of her Tuesday evening on Capitol Hill, standing outside the House chamber as lawmakers were voting. She shook hands, lobbied for the DEFIANCE Act and posed for pictures with anyone who asked — including many lawmakers.

The DEFIANCE Act has seen little movement in the House since being introduced in May 2025 despite having a range of bipartisan support.

That may be because of AOC and her future political ambitions. Republicans, who still control the House, are reluctant to hand the Democratic firebrand a major legislative victory, particularly as she mulls a 2028 presidential bid.

“This is a bipartisan bill that enjoys broad support among the Republican and Democratic caucuses, and it is an amazing opportunity for us to unify as a country for the protection of women, girls, and people everywhere. We can do this. And survivors cannot wait,” Ocasio-Cortez said.

The legislation has passed the Senate unanimously but, if the House doesn’t approve it before this legislative session ends on Jan. 3, the DEFIANCE Act will have to start the legislative process all over again.

The legislation protects everyone, Hilton noted.

“This is not a Republican or Democratic issue. This is a human issue,” she said. “Our likeness is not your content. Our trauma is not your entertainment. And our consent is never optional.”

But the two work in conjunction, covering criminal and civil penalties.

The Take It Down Act implemented criminal measures for those who threatened or knowingly published deepfake pornography. It also requires social media companies and online apps to establish “report and remove” mechanisms and delete non-consensual sexual images within 48 hours of a report.

Hilton’s DEFIANCE Act creates a civil path for victims to directly sue individuals in court for monetary damages.

The actress and reality star has spoken of the harassment and humiliation she experienced when she was 19 and her ex-boyfriend published an explicit sex tape featuring her without her consent.

Hilton never saw any profits from the video posted by her ex and said she donated her $400,000 settlement with him to charity.

“I will keep showing up, speaking out, and fighting for every survivor who deserves justice, dignity, and control over their own likeness,” Hilton told The Post on Monday.
