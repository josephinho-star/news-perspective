Forensic report says ex-Turkish intel officer died of heart disease, not poisoning

2026-11-05 13:58

A new forensic report has concluded that former Turkish intelligence officer Kaşif Kozinoğlu died of heart disease while in custody in 2011 and found no medical evidence that poisoning caused his death, despite earlier tests detecting antimony, a toxic chemical element, in his exhumed remains, the DHA news agency reported.

Kozinoğlu, a veteran National Intelligence Organization (MİT) officer who had served in Bosnia, Syria and Afghanistan, died on November 12, 2011, while in pretrial detention at what was then called Silivri Prison and is now Marmara Prison.

He became ill after exercising and was pronounced dead after he was taken to a hospital. His death was originally attributed to cardiovascular disease, and an autopsy found chronic ischemia, thickening of the heart walls and narrowing of the coronary arteries but no signs of physical violence or poisoning.

However, questions about the circumstances of his death persisted, with members of his family disputing claims that he had a pre-existing heart condition.

The Council of Forensic Medicine’s (ATK) First Specialization Board reached its conclusions in a report dated November 3 after reviewing radiological examinations, the original autopsy, the recent exhumation and analyses of samples taken from Kozinoğlu’s remains.

It also found no medical evidence that trauma had caused his death.

The new assessment follows the disclosure on November 3 of an October 19 report by the ATK’s morgue specialization department, which revealed high levels of antimony in samples taken from Kozinoğlu’s remains.

Tests found antimony in his hair, fingernails, tissue and bones but none in seven samples of soil taken from his grave. The report recorded concentrations of 7,002 nanograms per gram in his fingernails, 720 in tissue, 548 in hair and 175 in bone.

The morgue specialization department did not determine whether the substance caused Kozinoğlu’s death, instead referring the findings to the First Specialization Board for an assessment of the cause of death.

The Silivri Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office, which is conducting the reopened investigation, plans to challenge both conclusions and seek a review by the ATK General Assembly, DHA said.

Kozinoğlu had been arrested in March 2011 in a prosecution targeting journalists from the government-critical OdaTV news website and others accused of links to Ergenekon, an alleged clandestine network that prosecutors claimed was plotting to overthrow the government.

Prosecutors closed the original investigation into his death in April 2012 after finding no evidence of wrongdoing, negligence or an avoidable delay in medical treatment.

The case was reopened this year after a review by a Justice Ministry unit investigating unsolved crimes and a court decision overturning the original decision not to prosecute. Kozinoğlu’s remains were exhumed on September 12 for new toxicological and forensic testing.

Nine people are being held in pretrial detention as part of the reopened investigation, which has expanded to include former prison employees, healthcare workers, media workers, former prosecutors and Kozinoğlu’s former cellmates.
