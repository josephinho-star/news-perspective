Miles for Military program turns volunteer work into flights home for service members

2026-11-26 11:00

After more than a year apart, Marine Cpl. Dominic Morimando surprised his mother with a visit home for the holidays through a program that allows service members to earn flights home by volunteering.

He told "Fox & Friends Weekend" on Sunday that the enthusiasm of the students he worked with made the experience rewarding.

"It was such a great experience because they are so excited and so happy to have us there, and I was able to accumulate so many hours," the corporal said.

He volunteered at Spotswood Elementary School, working with students in kindergarten through fifth grade during physical education classes.

Dominic's mother, Ivonne Morimando, said she was "very, very proud" of her son. She recalled her surprise at seeing him standing at her front door.

"My heart was – I still have that emotion in front of me because just seeing him right in the front door, it was a big thing for me, for real."

"It's so much fun and happiness to have him over here with us, especially in these days, this holiday," she said.

Dominic's trip was made possible by Miles for Military, a nonprofit that provides flights home to U.S. service members in exchange for volunteer work.

Founder Maureen Byrne, whose son served in the Marines, told Fox News she chose the community service model instead of simply providing free flights so that service members could "get off base, get into community, do something, learn something, have fun."

"It's so rewarding. I was talking to [Dominic] when I was in Las Vegas yesterday about his volunteer work and he lit up talking about the kids."

Byrne said her son's positive experience volunteering, along with the high cost of flying home, helped inspire Miles for Military.

"The ticket was almost $900 from Camp Lejeune to Boston."

The high cost prompted her to ask her son how other service members paid for trips home.

"'Mom, they don't. Nobody gets to go home. They can't afford it,'" Byrne recalled her son saying.

"Well, what if we have these kids do some volunteer work and, in exchange, we give them a ticket home? And that's how Miles for Military came."

For Dominic, the value of the program extended beyond the flight home.

Service, he said, is "very important because you're taking your time out of your life to serve not only your country, but your community and everybody else around you as well."
