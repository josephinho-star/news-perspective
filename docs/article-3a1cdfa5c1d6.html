And so Katie Zacharia joins the mayfly ranks of Liz Truss, Anthony Scaramucci, New Coke, CNN+ and Quibi. To say she burned twice as bright and half as long would be too kind. She went out like a firework on a soggy night.

The White House confirmed on Friday that Zacharia is leaving her role as press secretary after just five days on the job. No one was surprised after a lacklustre press briefing debut and scrutiny over her legal credentials.

But even more importantly, Zacharia lived by the sword and died in the one arena that Donald Trump truly cares about: television.

She reportedly got the job by closely monitoring the US president’s public schedule and tracked his active hours on Truth Social to predict exactly when he would be watching TV. She then timed her own appearances on air to make sure she caught his eye. The fact that she fitted the glossy blond aesthetic from what Trump calls “central casting” also helped.

But TV also proved her undoing. On Thursday, Zacharia gave a series of interviews to Trump-friendly outlets in a desperate bid for redemption. Instead, she sealed her own fate with more hapless performances.

She must have expected Fox News host Sean Hannity, who has even spoken at Trump rallies, to give her an easy ride. But even he was sceptical when she admitted she had “stepped up to the plate with very little prep time” for the first briefing. Hannity asked: “Am I hearing that you think the staff should have prepped you better?”

Zacharia rejected the notion that she had only taken questions from conservative outlets, incorrectly asserting that the Daily Mail is “not known to be a conservative outlet”. Hannity also quizzed her about her dubious legal credentials. “Why not just say, ‘I went to Pepperdine law school, and then took a different career path, and I took the bar once and didn’t pass?’”

Zacharia tried again on Fox & Friends but ran into host Lawrence Jones, who said: “You know, Katie, it’s been a tough week for you. It’s not an easy job to be the press secretary and you never want to be the story.”

He also asked: “Do you think you still have the confidence of the president?” Zacharia responded, “I’m standing here,” referring to the fact that she was speaking from the White House north lawn.

Her exit hours later was met with widespread ridicule – and a measure of human compassion for someone whom Trump had subjected to a very public humiliation.

Trump plays old clip from CNN polling analyst who said last night 'Trump’s numbers are absolutely garbage'

Donald Trump has concluded his lengthy speech to hundreds of supporters in Dalton, Georgia, wrapping up just short of the one hour and 40 minute mark.

Close to the end of the remarks, Trump asked his aides to play a video clip for the crowd, which showed a CNN polling analyst, Harry Enten, pointing out that the president was still popular with voters who describe themselves as “MAGA Republicans.”

Trump has played this clip, from an August broadcast, at multiple stops on his current campaign tour, as if to block out less flattering recent polls that find him at record-low approval ratings from the public as a whole.

In a more recent segment broadcast on Thursday morning, that Trump did not play for the crowd, Enten explained to CNN viewers this week that a new Fox News poll shows that Trump’s overall approval rating has fallen to 36%, with 64% disapproving of the job he has done as president.

As Enten noted, the most dramatic shift was among voters under 30 surveyed by Fox. In October 2024, that demographic had a net positive approval of Trump of plus-3. Now, the same group disapproves of Trump by 50 points.

On Thursday night, Enten was asked on another CNN show about Trump’s current standing in polls and said this: “Trump’s numbers are absolutely garbage, they’re trash.”

“He has the highest disapproval rating of all time when it comes to inflation going into a midterm election,” Enten continued. “In fact he has the worst inflation net approval ratings that I’ve ever seen for any president, we’re talking all the way back to James Earl Carter. He’s the first guy ever to hit minus 50 points or worse on the net inflation.”

Trump repeats lie that Abdul El-Sayed is 'a member of the Muslim Brotherhood'

During his speech in Georgia, which has blown past the 90-minute mark, Donald Trump just repeated the lie he has told multiple times this week about Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, telling supporters that the Michigan Senate candidate is “a member of the Muslim Brotherhood”.

Despite Trump’s false statement, there is no evidence that El-Sayed, the US-born son of Egyptian immigrant parents, is a member of the Egyptian Islamist opposition group. El-Sayed has flatly denied that he has any ties to the Egyptian group.

Trump made this false claim on Thursday in public remarks and in a video message recorded in the Oval Office and posted on the president’s social media platform.

As if to make the anti-Muslim racism of his message entirely clear, the president has insisted on referring to Abdul El-Sayed as “Mohammed” each time he’s told this lie.

Trump repeats false claim about praise from Saudi king

Donald Trump’s remarks to supporters in Georgia are mainly a rehash of previous speeches, filled with false claims we have debunked repeatedly, suggesting that the president is not a regular reader of the Guardian.

“Less than two years ago, our country was dead. Now we have the hottest country anywhere in the world,” Trump said, before claiming, as he has in the past, that this was an observation made to him by the Saudi king during his visit to that country last year.

“So that statement was true. The king of Saudi Arabia, when I was over there, he said, ‘A short time ago, you had – you were a laughingstock’ – meaning our country was a laughingstock, all the way, all the way around the world, everybody was laughing at the USA,” Trump said. “‘And in a very short period of time, you made it the hottest country,’” the president continued, attributing this remark to the Saudi king.

As the Guardian has reported previously, Trump did not meet the Saudi king, who is elderly and not often seen in public, during his visit last year. Instead, he was hosted by the Saudi crown prince, Mohammed bin Salman. There is also no evidence that the Saudi prince made this statement to Trump, but there is video of Trump making a version of that claim to Prince Mohammed during an address to a US-Saudi investment event in Riyadh on 13 May 2025.

“The days of economic misery under the last administration are rapidly giving way to the greatest economy in the history of the world,” Trump said before directly addressing the crown prince in the audience. “We are rocking. The United States is the hottest country, with the exception of your country, I have to say, right? … No Mohammed… You’re hotter. At least as long as I’m up here, you’re hotter. But groceries, gasoline, energy and all other prices are down with no inflation. We have no inflation.”

Two weeks after his return to the US, Trump began saying that this observation had been made not by him, but to him. “I went to Saudi Arabia,” Trump told reporters in the Oval Office on 28 May 2025. “The king told me, he said, ‘you got the hottest com-’ we have the hottest country in the world right now.,” Trump said. “Six months ago this country was stone cold dead. We had a dead country.”

As he repeated the story, again and again, Trump began to also attribute his own claim, that the US was “dead” under Joe Biden, to the Saudi king as well. In remarks at the Nato summit in the Hague in June 2025, Trump insisted that the Saudi king had told him: “A year ago, your country was dead. You had a deadhead running it. You had a country that was dead”.

Since then, Trump has repeated the tale dozens of times.

Trump repeats imaginary tale about being blamed for high prices on second day of his term

Speaking in Georgia, Donald Trump is spending most of his time defending his record as president, rather than discussing Republican candidates at the rally who are up for election next month.

Much of what he said was simply not true.

To take one example, the president repeated an entirely imaginary story he has repeated frequently in recent weeks, about how he was supposedly blamed for high prices by reporters on the second day of his term, in January 2025.

“When I came in, it was January 20th. I came in and on January 21st I had a news conference and some of those same people were at the news conference and they were screaming at me. I was only there for less than one day, substantially less than one day. And I’m having a news conference. I thought it would be a victorious conference. I thought the fake news would be saying, ‘Oh, sir, what a great race you ran,’” Trump said.

“My first news conference, January 21st. So I take office on the 20th. I figured maybe I won’t have a news conference. So the next day I have a news conference and I’m waiting for accolades from the press. And they said, what about the cost of eggs? What about the cost of beef?” Trump claimed.

In fact, a review of the transcript from that news conference reveals that no such exchange took place on 21 January 2025, when he was mainly pressed on the pardons he signed the day before for Capitol rioters convicted of violence against police officers on January 6 2021, in support of his attempt to stay in power despite losing the 2020 election.

Trump boasts about helping defeat Jon Ossoff in 2017 House race, skips over his failed effort to stop him in 2020 Senate race

As he has in the past, Donald Trump just bragged to supporters in Georgia about what he called his key role in helping to defeat Democrat Jon Ossoff in a 2017 special election for a Georgia House seat, but made no mention at all of the fact that he also campaigned hard against Ossoff in the 2020 Georgia Senate race that Ossoff won, when he defeated the Trump-backed Republican David Perdue.

“Ossoff was leading by a lot and they called me in to help. It was a special election,” Trump recalled. “And I came in and I held a massive rally for the person, very nice woman. And we ended up beating Ossoff. So I beat him. And that was in 2017. I beat him once and now we’re going to beat him more importantly a second time.”

Trump failed to mention that the second time he campaigned against Ossoff was at a rally in Valdosta, Georgia, on 5 December 2020 – ahead of the runoff election Ossoff won in January 2021.

That rally was supposed to encourage Georgia Republicans to vote for Perdue, and a second Republican Senate candidate, Kelly Loeffler, but Trump devoted most of his speech to complaining about his loss in the 2020 presidential election and giving voters the idea that the elections were “rigged” against Republicans.

Weeks later, both incumbent Republican senators, Perdue and Loeffler, lost runoff elections to their Democratic opponents, Ossoff and Raphael Warnock, which flipped control of the Senate from Republican to Democrats.
