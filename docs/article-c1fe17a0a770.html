NFL legend claims ‘racist double standard’ amid Aaron Rodgers-Steelers debacle

2026-11-26 00:42

Aaron Rodgers’ decision to publicly humiliate Mike McCarthy on television has opened the door for former 49ers wide receiver Terrell Owens to call out the NFL for a “double standard.”

Hours after Rodgers finished his rant on McCarthy, Owens went on Instagram live and went on an 11-minute rant of him saying he was treated differently when he spoke out about his own problems.

“This is the double standard of how white players get treated, and how black players get treated,” Owens said.

“Everybody’s always like, ‘why does it have to be about race?’ That’s a good damn question. Why does it? Why does it? Why does it have to be about race?” Owens says in the video. “Why can’t everybody be treated equally?”

Owens, who had a falling out with the 49ers in 2003 because of his fractured relationship with then quarterback Jeff Garcia, followed up and said he wasn’t treated equally to Garcia.

One of the points in Rodgers’ rant was that McCarthy would fall asleep during team meetings.

Owens admitted that he used to fall asleep during meetings, but added that Garcia would show up to practice “inebriated.”

“I used to smell alcohol on his breath when we came in the meetings, sitting in the front row,” Owens said. “So if you’re going to talk bad about me for saying that, say the same thing about Aaron Rodgers that’s going on his rant, about his coaches and things that were possibly supposed to be kept in-house.”

“That’s what I’m talking about. There’s the double standard. Look at the color of the two players. That’s all I got to say,” Owens said.

Owens’ rant ended with him talking about his recent Amazon Prime documentary “Terrell & T.O.” which covered the electric career of the Hall of Fame wide receiver.

The documentary followed Owens from when he was selected in the third round of the 1996 NFL Draft by the 49ers through his journey in the NFL, covering football culture, locker-room media conflicts, personal life, and into the present day.

Owens said that he originally didn’t want to do the documentary, but was talked into it by Mike Hill, who served as an executive producer.

“He’s the one that encouraged and pushed me to do it,” Owens said. “He heard, and he saw the things that people were saying about me, behind closed doors.”
