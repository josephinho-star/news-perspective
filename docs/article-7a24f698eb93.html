Kilmar Ábrego García leaves for Spain. But the Trump administration got what it wanted

2026-11-26 11:00

Kilmar Ábrego García, the man illegally deported to his native El Salvador last year and whose immigration case spurred a 20-month-long battle with the Trump administration, arrived in Spain on Wednesday.

Trump administration officials and Ábrego García supporters are both claiming voluntary departure from the US to avoid deportation is a victory for their respective sides.

And they’re both sort of right, if such an ugly immigration spectacle can even be associated with concepts of victory.

On the one hand, Ábrego García has the relief of leaving the country seemingly his own accord, given that he was not going to be allowed to remain legally in the US. By going to Spain he avoids the pitfalls he had been facing. These were: being indefinitely detained inside a horrific prison in El Salvador, being sent to an unfamiliar African country where, if some other examples are anything to go by, he could end up in a prison or detained under harsh conditions in a secured hotel, indefinitely, or detained indefinitely in a US cell. His attorneys hope that, under a different president in the future, he may be allowed to return to the US.

And on the other hand, the Trump administration has won by getting rid of a high-profile immigrant they were determined not to let rest.

Ábrego García first arrived in the spotlight in March 2025, after the Trump administration deported him to El Salvador despite a US court order protecting him from being deported there, for his own safety. His case led to a nearly two-year-long “David and Goliath” type battle between him and the Trump administration, raising concerns about due process and constitutional rights, the separation of powers and accountability, the US government’s deals with countries for deportations and questionable federal prosecutions.

The entire case transcends the complicated character of Ábrego García himself. It has shown the world the lengths the Trump administration will go to when carrying out its anti-immigrant crackdown, and was an early example of the emboldened returning president defying federal court orders, to say nothing of fairness.

Ábrego García arrived in the US in 2011 from El Salvador without proper authorization and lived in Maryland for years, regularly checking in with Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). He married a US citizen and had a child with her.

In 2019, he was arrested outside a hardware store in Maryland after local police, relying on an informant and what his lawyers called “no evidence”, accused him of being a gang member. He was turned over to ICE, but a judge ruled he should not be deported to his country of origin due to danger in that country. He continued with his life with a valid work permit.

Then in March 2025, ICE officials arrested him while driving home with his five-year-old son. And after the Trump administration had struck a deal with the Salvadorian government, run by the rightwing and self-proclaimed “world’s coolest dictator”, Nayib Bukele, ICE quickly deported Ábrego García to El Salvador along with dozens of Venezuelan men in one of the most infamous immigration actions of the second Trump administration. As the flights were midair, a federal judge ordered the Trump administration to turn them back, due to accusations of due process violations. The administration defied the federal judge. The men arrived in El Salvador and were violently taken to a notorious anti-terrorism prison called Cecot, ostensibly to be detained indefinitely. Many claimed they were tortured, assaulted, raped and traumatized by the ordeal.

In court filings, the Trump administration admitted they had mistakenly deported Ábrego García due to an “administrative error”. After a lengthy legal battle, including input from the US supreme court, the administration was ordered to return him to the US. They initially refused, but eventually he was brought back in June 2025.

The Trump administration then quickly charged him with crimes relating to human smuggling, based on a traffic stop in Tennessee dating from 2022, and he was detained again. In parallel with the criminal case, this allowed ICE to begin attempts to remove him to a series of African countries. Ábrego García and his legal team resisted. Then, presumably concluding he had no future as a free man in the US, he offered to be removed to Costa Rica. The administration refused to send him to the Central American country and pushed to deport him to Africa.

The government denigrated him publicly as a “gang member”, “human trafficker”, “wife beater” and “child predator”. They pointed to accusations that he had allegedly abused his wife. He had never committed any of those crimes.

However, his human smuggling case was dismissed in May of this year, with the federal judge saying it was a “vindictive prosecution”.

Negotiations continued behind closed doors. A court order unsealed on Wednesday shows that the federal judge presiding over his case had ordered the Trump administration to effectuate Ábrego García’s voluntary departure from the US. The judge ordered the administration not to intervene in his departure. On Tuesday, it was announced he was leaving the US voluntarily, but to Europe, not Central America. He duly arrived in Spain’s capital, Madrid, on Wednesday morning with his wife. Competing claims of victory broke out from Ábrego García’s supporters and the Trump administration.

“I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free – including from this administration’s constant persecution,” said Senator Chris Van Hollen, who visited Ábrego García in El Salvador and pushed for his return.

“We are delighted to announce that Kilmar Ábrego García has self-deported from the United States to Spain,” said homeland security secretary Markwayne Mullin, using a favored Trump administration phrase when people leave the country specifically to avoid deportation.

The years-long and tired narrative of the “good” immigrant versus “bad” immigrant has only helped immigration hardliners justify the propping up of an aggressive enforcement system that targets all and tramples on due process rights, regardless of criminal history.

One of Ábrego García’s attorneys said he is leaving the US because he thought he was “never going to get a fair shake” under the Trump administration. They aren’t wrong. But this is purely by design. And his is one case of thousands in which people are not getting a fair shake under an immigration system that resembles more closely punishment than merely civil immigration proceedings.

Ábrego García “humiliated” the Trump administration “because he exposed their failures and beat them in court; even all the way to the Supreme Court”, immigration policy analyst Aaron Reichlin-Melnick said on social media. “For that, they tried everything to punish him. And they lost, again and again. Now, they lose a final time: he leaves as a free man.”

But the Trump administration does have reason to feel smug. No one in the administration has been held responsible, in any significant way, for the catastrophic Cecot ordeal, its violation of court orders and the alleged torture they helped facilitate. And no one is being held responsible for wielding an immigration enforcement system targeting thousands – although the midterms “blue wave” for the Democrats was certainly a signal of increasing voter opposition.

Ábrego García’s departure to Spain means perhaps no more than the closing of a very disturbing chapter in an ongoing horror story.
