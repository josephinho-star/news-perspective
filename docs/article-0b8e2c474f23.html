Carney: Trump not concerned about growing Canada trade ties with China

2026-11-18 19:19

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Wednesday that President Trump is not worried about Ottawa’s deepening trade ties with China.

“In my conversation with the president, he’s tried to understand where we are with China, where we’re going with China, but it’s not — I wouldn’t — I shouldn’t speak for him, but in his hierarchy of issues, it’s not the top issue at all,” Carney told reporters while in China.

Earlier this year, Trump said that Carney should go through with a trade deal with Beijing.

“That’s OK. That’s what he should be doing. I mean, it’s a good thing for him to sign a trade deal. If you can get a deal with China, you should do that,” Trump said when asked about what he thought of the trade deal at the time.

In January, Canada and China came to an initial trade deal, getting rid of tariffs on electric vehicles and canola. The Associated Press reported that Canada would initially let in a maximum of 48,000 Chinese electric vehicles with a 6.1 percent tariff. Beijing was expected to then bring down its tariff on canola seed by early March.

Carney’s Wednesday comments also follow recent trade tension between Ottawa and Washington, with both countries slapping tariffs on each other’s goods.

On Saturday, Carney said trade negotiations between Ottawa and the U.S. were progressing. He told reporters in Manila that he had a discussion with Trump, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller and other officials “just before” going to the Philippines for the Association of the Southeast Asian Nations’ (ASEAN) summit. The Canadian prime minister added that Canada and the U.S. were “making progress.”

Carney also stressed Saturday that he would not “settle for a deal” not in line with “a viable Canadian auto sector.”
