Russian warship fires flares at Danish helicopter as Nato jets shoot down drone over Lithuania

2026-09-15 10:37

A Russian warship has fired two emergency flares at a Danish helicopter in the Baltic Sea, and Nato fighter jets have shot down a drone over Lithuania, in the latest security incidents on the alliance’s eastern flank linked to Russia’s war against Ukraine.

Poland also scrambled fighter jets “due to the activity of Russian jet-powered unmanned aerial vehicles conducting strikes on Ukrainian territory”, a day after retrieving a suspected Russian drone adrift in its waters.

In the Netherlands, train services were severely disrupted by deliberate acts of sabotage in more than 30 places around the country hours before the ceremonial state opening of parliament, the national rail infrastructure operator said, adding that it was not yet known who was responsible.

The Danish prime minister, Mette Frederiksen, on Tuesday condemned “reckless action from Russia towards Denmark’s military”, adding: “Russian actions are meant to intimidate and divide. They will not succeed. We will not waver.”

Denmark’s armed forces said in a statement late on Monday that one of its air force’s Fennec helicopters “was fired at with flares during a routine photographic operation of a Russian frigate” which was in international waters off southern Denmark.

The frigate had fired “two flares towards the helicopter. One of the flares passed at close range to the helicopter”, the military’s statement added.

The Danish foreign minister, Lars Løkke Rasmussen, said on Tuesday that he had summoned the Russian ambassador over the incident. “Russia is gradually shifting the boundary for what they consider acceptable behaviour,” he said.

“This is completely unacceptable and could have put human lives at risk. That is something we cannot accept,” he said. The Danish straits are the main sea route in and out of the Baltic Sea for commercial and military shipping.

They are also a major artery for global oil shipments, including from Russia’s so-called shadow fleet of tankers. Danish military intelligence has said Russian warships have sailed on collision courses with Danish vessels, targeted Danish helicopters and ships with tracking radars, and pointed weapons at them in the straits.

In Lithuania, President Gitanas Nausėda said early on Tuesday that a drone “that just entered Lithuanian airspace was destroyed by Nato fighter jets”, adding: “With Russia intensifying its aggression against Ukraine, such readiness is vital for our region.”

Citing preliminary data from Lithuania’s national crisis management centre, LRT public radio said the drone had come from neighbouring Belarus and was heading west, before being shot down by an Italian fighter jet taking part in a Nato mission. It fell in a rural area near Lake Kaunas, in the centre of the country.

Lithuania’s foreign minister, Kęstutis Budrys, said Nato allies had been informed about the incident and discussions were under way “both in the EU and Nato, because it is obvious that we all still have a lot of homework to do”, LRT reported.

A presidential security adviser, Deividas Matulionis, said the drone may have strayed off course, but Lithuania’s state defence council would meet on Wednesday to review air defences. The defence minister, Robertas Kaunas, said the drone was downed near a pumped-storage hydroelectric plant.

Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia – all of which are Nato members and share a border with Russia – have regularly reported drone incursions since Moscow launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. A Nato fighter shot down a Ukrainian drone over Estonia in May.

European officials have said Russia has been diverting Ukrainian drones meant to strike industrial facilities and oil terminals in the St Petersburg region on the Gulf of Finland.

The Dutch rail infrastructure operator, ProRail, said on Tuesday morning that lengths of steel pipe had been found attached to railway tracks in more than 30 locations, mainly in rural areas in the middle and the east of the country.

“We have been to these places and carried out investigations and found materials that were deliberately placed there,” a ProRail spokesperson, Joke van der Cruysen, said. “We are therefore now classifying this as sabotage of railway infrastructure.”

All trains in the vicinity of the track blockages were halted, while there were also serious disruptions elsewhere, including to international services to Germany. “It is not yet known who is responsible or what the motive is,” ProRail said.

The Dutch intelligence agency, AIVD, said it was investigating the incidents with police. King Willem-Alexander was due to open a new session of parliament on Tuesday with a speech setting out the government’s 2027 budget plans, which have drawn protests from groups including labour unions and farmers.

Poland said early on Tuesday it had launched “aviation operations” in its airspace because Russian jet-powered drones were “conducting strikes on Ukrainian territory”. The operations were “preventive” and concluded without any violation of Polish airspace being observed, it later said.

They came after the country’s defence minister, Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz, said on Monday that the military had located and recovered a drone from the Baltic Sea near the beach in Rusinowo, close to the coastal town of Jarosławiec.

“Preliminary inspection indicates that it is a Russian military drone,” Kosiniak-Kamysz said. An armed forces spokesperson said the drone appeared to be a Gerbera, a cheap long-range aerial drone made of materials such as plywood and foam.

Gerbera drones are often used as decoys, but can also carry explosives. This one was recovered by a hydrographic vessel, the military said.

Poland’s prime minister, Donald Tusk, has repeatedly said Nato countries should expect an increase in provocations from Moscow. A Russian drone hit a passenger train on Ukraine’s border with Poland on Sunday, shortly after the former British prime minister Boris Johnson and other foreign diplomats had used the same line.

Agence France-Presse and Reuters contributed to this report
