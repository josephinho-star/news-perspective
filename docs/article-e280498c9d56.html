Budget 2026 live: Burnham faces PMQs before Healey delivers budget

2026-10-28 11:42

Burnham tells cabinet current Brexit deal 'bad for UK', as he restates determination to get 'closer relationship' with EU

Downing Street has issued its readout from cabinet today. It says Andy Burnham told colleagues the last government’s Brexit deal was “bad for the UK”, and he restated his determination to get a closer relationship.

A No 10 spokesperson said:

The prime minister opened by setting out how he would shortly write to cabinet on the UK’s relationship with the EU. He said the Brexit deal left by the last government had been bad for the UK and had led to a harder Brexit than had been promised at the referendum. The prime minister said the deal had led to low growth and also made it harder to control immigration. He said the upcoming EU summit offered the UK the opportunity to forge a closer relationship with the EU, which was good for the British people and good for British business. He added the status quo is not good enough for our country.

Turning to the budget, the prime minister said the chancellor would shortly be on his feet in the house. He then asked the chancellor to update cabinet on the key measures in the budget.

The prime minister then asked the foreign secretary to update cabinet ahead of the Commonwealth heads of government meeting, which he and the prime minister will attend.

Bank shares jump on reports budget bank tax will be ‘temporary and below 8%’

As Graeme Wearden is reporting on the business live blog, bank shares have gone up in response to a Bloomberg report saying the bank tax won’t be as high as the sector inspected.

Bloomberg are reporting that John Healey’s bank tax rise will be temporary and come in at less than 8% – less than campaigners had hoped.

That would mean that the chancellor is not fully reversing the cut to the bank surcharge introduced by Jeremy Hunt in 2023.

Reminder: This surcharge is applied to bank profits on top of corporation tax. Hunt lowered the surcharge from 8% to 3% to compensate for a rise in corporation tax.

Banks shares are rallying in response. Natwest (+2.4%), Lloyds Banking Group (+2.4%) and Barclays (+2.3%) are now among the top risers on the FTSE 100 this morning.

Kemi Badenoch has also been commenting on a bank tax this morning. (See 11.05am.) In a post on social media, she says that higher taxes for banks will end up meaning lower wages for staff, higher charges for customers, or lower returns for shareholders.

The TUC is firmly in favour of this and it wants to see an additional 3% corporation tax on the profits of banking companies above £100m. It has released figures today that it says show the big banks making £13bn in windfall profits from charging higher interest rates.

The TUC says these four banks have made profits worth £200bn over the past five years, and that in the first half of 2026 they were making £1bn a week.

And it says the CEOs of those four banks made an average of £8.9m in 2025 – “182 times more than the average (mean) full-time UK employee”.

Paul Nowak, the TUC general secretary, said:

The chancellor has a lot to weigh up, but banks have seen profits soar while everyone else has struggled. Britain needs a living standards budget that cuts people’s energy bills– and taxing banks’ windfall profits is the fairest way to pay for it.

With eye-watering profits, bonkers bonuses, and CEOs making more in two days than most make in a year, it’s clear that banks can afford to contribute more. The case is now irrefutable – it’s time to tax the banks to cut bills.

'My advice to John is don't delay' – Zelenskyy urges UK to speed up raising defence spending

Despite some confusing messaging, John Healey is not expected to set out precise details today of how the UK might raise defence spending to 3% of GDP by 2030. This is despite the fact he resigned as defence secretary when Keir Starmer was PM because Starmer’s defence investment plan did not commit to 3% by 2030.

In an interview with the Sun, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the Ukrainian president, urges Healey to go faster on raising defence spending. He says:

Can the United Kingdom trust Russia or not? If not, defence needs to be 100 per cent the priority.

My advice to John is don’t delay. Sorry John … but it’s important to be direct.

Green leader Zack Polanski says budget could be 'betrayal of future', after it emerges tax forecasts assume Rosebank going ahead

Today Kiran Stacey and Pippa Crerar report that the giant Rosebank oilfield can be approved without breaching the UK’s emission targets. They also reveal that, if the government were to block Rosebank (which is far more controversial with environmentalists than the Jackdaw gasfield approved last week), the Treasury would lose up to £500m a year in tax revenue – money which is already included in the government’s fiscal planning assumptions.

There may be more on this in the budget documents published this afternoon.

For the Green leader, Zack Polanski, this is one of the most important revelations of the day. In response, he said:

Today’s budget looks like being worse for our future and the young than even anything Starmer came up with.

A budget based on approval of mass new oil drilling is a betrayal of our future.
