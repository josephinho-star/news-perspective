Rams, 49ers seek early control in season-opening showdown in Australia

2026-09-10 12:00

The NFL could not have scripted a more compelling introduction to Australia: two California rivals, two Super Bowl contenders and one game that will count long after the novelty of playing at the Melbourne Cricket Ground wears off.

For the Rams and 49ers, Thursday’s season opener is less about exporting football than establishing early control of the NFC West. Seattle begins the season as the defending Super Bowl champion, leaving the Rams and 49ers with no room for a leisurely start. One of them will return from the longest road trip in league history with a divisional win. The other will spend the flight home confronting immediate questions.

The Rams arrive with perhaps the NFL’s most complete roster. Matthew Stafford is coming off an MVP season, Puka Nacua led the league with 129 receptions and Davante Adams remains one of football’s most dangerous red-zone receivers. Sean McVay can attack with power, play action or spread formations, forcing a defense to defend every blade of grass.

San Francisco knows the problem well. Kyle Shanahan’s team must create pressure without committing extra defenders and exposing its secondary to Stafford. Nick Bosa’s return from a torn ACL changes that equation, even if the 49ers must be measured with his workload. If Bosa can disrupt Stafford’s timing, the 49ers can turn a potential track meet into the kind of tense, possession-by-possession game these rivals usually play.

The Rams’ defensive headline changed Tuesday when Aaron Donald was ruled out and left off the trip. His comeback will wait while the Rams continue ramping him up after two seasons away. But the Rams hardly arrive defenseless. Myles Garrett, the reigning Defensive Player of the Year after recording 23 sacks last season, gives the Rams a game-wrecker capable of changing every protection Shanahan calls. Kobie Turner, Braden Fiske, Byron Young and an upgraded secondary make this a formidable unit even without No. 99.

That puts the burden on Brock Purdy and San Francisco’s offensive line. The 49ers cannot let Garrett turn obvious passing situations into his personal showcase. Christian McCaffrey, motion, misdirection and quick throws must keep the Rams guessing and Purdy upright. George Kittle’s potential return would give Purdy another essential answer, especially in the middle of the field.

Then there is the trip itself. San Francisco arrived early to acclimate; the Rams chose a compressed schedule intended to preserve their normal preparation rhythm. Nobody will know which approach was wiser until bodies begin responding Friday morning in Melbourne (Thursday night in the U.S.).

The setting is unprecedented. The rivalry is not. These teams know each other’s systems, stars and pressure points. Across an ocean, with nearly 90,000 watching in person, the winner will likely be the team that makes the unfamiliar surroundings disappear first.

Rams DE Myles Garrett vs 49ers LT Trent Williams

With Aaron Donald sitting out, the spotlight shifts to two generational players going head-to-head. Garrett, coming off a historic 23-sack season, will move around the formation, but his battles with Williams will be appointment viewing. Williams remains powerful and athletic enough to neutralize elite rushers without help, allowing San Francisco to release its backs and tight ends into routes. If Williams can contain Garrett 1-on-1, Brock Purdy can operate comfortably, and Kyle Shanahan retains his entire playbook. But if Garrett forces the 49ers to chip, slide protections or speed up Purdy’s clock, he can dictate the game without recording a sack. It’s future Hall of Famer against future Hall of Famer — and potentially the matchup that decides the opener.

San Francisco’s early arrival should help, and Bosa’s return gives the 49ers a chance to make Stafford uncomfortable. But the Rams possess more answers across the roster. Their receiving combination of Nacua and Adams will create just enough advantageous matchups, while Garrett will produce one drive or game-altering play against Purdy. Expect the 49ers to use McCaffrey creatively and keep the game tight into the fourth quarter. In the end, Stafford’s experience and McVay’s red-zone imagination deliver the final scoring drive in a memorable Australian debut.

First Down: Make the body clock irrelevant

This game begins at 10:35 Friday morning in Melbourne, a start time neither team can truly simulate. San Francisco arrived a week early, choosing acclimation and local practice. The Rams stayed home longer, protecting their normal workweek before making the 16-hour flight on Tuesday night. They will spend more time in the air going and coming back than on the ground. Both approaches carry risk. The opening quarter will reveal which team adjusted better. Energy cannot become an excuse in a divisional game. The Rams need a clean, fast start that prevents the 49ers from feeding off the crowd and turning Los Angeles’ travel strategy into an immediate storyline.

Second Down: Protect Stafford from Bosa

Bosa will play after returning from the ACL injury that ended his 2025 season, but nobody knows how many snaps he can handle or how explosive he will look. The Rams should not wait to find out. McVay can slow him with Kyren Williams, play-action passes, screens and formations that force Bosa to diagnose before attacking. Stafford remains lethal when he can hitch once and deliver on schedule. Allow Bosa to collapse the edge, however, and San Francisco’s entire defense becomes faster. The Rams’ offensive line does not have to dominate him. It must keep him from controlling the game.

Third Down: Win without Donald

Donald staying home removes the reunion everyone wanted, but it does not remove the Rams’ ability to torment Purdy. Garrett will command protection, creating opportunities for Turner, Fiske and Young. The challenge is maintaining rush-lane discipline. Purdy is dangerous when pressure opens an escape route and allows him to create on the move. Los Angeles must also stop McCaffrey before worrying exclusively about sacks. If San Francisco stays ahead of the chains, Shanahan can disguise everything with motion and play action. Force third-and-long and the Rams can unleash Garrett against an unsettled left-guard situation. That is where the game can turn.

Fourth Down: Finish drives with touchdowns

Divisional familiarity tends to shrink margins. Both coaches know the other’s favorite concepts, personnel groupings and situational tendencies, making red-zone execution even more valuable. The Rams have a major advantage in Adams, whose route running and body control give Stafford a trusted answer in compressed space. Nacua’s physicality and the Rams’ tight ends complicate every coverage choice. San Francisco can counter with McCaffrey and, if available, Kittle. Field goals will feel safe early, especially after the travel and unfamiliar routine. They will not be enough. The team that converts one extra red-zone possession into seven points should leave Melbourne with the win.
