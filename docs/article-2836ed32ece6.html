Kraków shooting latest updates: six killed and more than a dozen injured, police say

2026-11-07 21:02

Two women and one man, most likely members of the family of the Polish gunman who killed six people shooting from a car in the heart of the southern city of Krakow, were found dead inside of his house, Polish prime minister Donald Tusk said on Saturday.

Drone footage appeared to show the dead bodies in the home of the man behind the mass shooting, Tusk said.

Authorities have not yet identified the shooter, but they say he is a 25-year-old Polish man with “ultranationalistic” views.

A British national was among the six victims killed in the shooting in Krakow, Polish police chief Marek Boron said on Saturday.

“There are six fatalities: four women and two men – three Polish women, one British national, and two people who have not yet been identified,” Boron told reporters.

Krakow mayor Łukasz Gibała said the city will be in official mourning until Wednesday, but vowed that Krakow will remain open to visitors despite the tragedy.

“The square will belong to us again,” Gibała said in a video posted to X.

Five foreigners were among wounded victims of the shooting in Poland’s city of Krakow, the interior minister Marcin Kierwinski said after a gunman killed six people in the city’s old town.

Sixteen people were hospitalised following the shooting, “among these sixteen people are five non-Polish nationals,” Kierwinski told reporters. He added that “there is one person, a foreign national” among the killed.

A gunman who killed four women and two men in Krakow on Saturday was fascinated by far-right ideology, the interior minister Marcin Kierwinski told a news briefing.

“Everything points to the fact that the shooter was influenced by far-right ideology, a matter that will also be subject to investigation,” Kierwinski said.

“Police officers are on-site, as are agents from the Internal Security Agency, both here at the scene and at the individual’s family home.”

A 67-year-old man called Julek told Reuters that he saw a dark car appear suddenly before the driver started shooting at a group of people and drove off.

“If we had been two minutes earlier, we might have been shot,” he said.

Poland’s PM Donald Tusk wrote on X: “My deepest condolences to the families and loved ones of the victims of the tragic attack in Kraków. The highest praise for the heroic police officers who risked their lives. And a heartfelt appeal to politicians of all stripes: do not exploit this tragedy. We will establish all the circumstances of this crime.”

A man who gave his name as Konrad, and witnessed the shooting, told news website Interia: “I saw shots being fired from the driver’s side window, and smoke was coming out. I ran to the police station in the main market square; when I burst inside, it was full of people hiding on the floor.”
