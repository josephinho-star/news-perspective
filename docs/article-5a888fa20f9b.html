White House pulls Trump’s FDA pick Heidi Overton on eve of Senate panel vote

2026-11-18 01:04

The White House pulled Heidi Overton’s nomination to lead the Food and Drug Administration, a White House official confirmed to Fox News, one day before a Senate committee vote.

President Donald Trump hailed Overton as a "ROCKSTAR" in August when he picked the physician and White House advisor to succeed Marty Makary, who resigned in May.

At her September confirmation hearing, Overton clashed with Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., as he pressed her to endorse universal healthcare as a human right.

Senate health committee Chairman Bill Cassidy, R-La., pointed to Overton’s appearance alongside Trump in the Oval Office as Trump signed an executive order calling for changes to childhood vaccine recommendations, including splitting up the combined measles, mumps and rubella vaccine.

Cassidy said there was no scientific evidence for Trump’s suggestion that the combined shot could be "quite lethal" and warned the remark could discourage vaccination.

"Yes, Senator, the MMR vaccine is safe and effective," Overton told Cassidy. When he pressed her on what she would do if Trump asked her to disregard the FDA’s safety standards, Overton said she would give the president her best advice and follow the legal requirements for reviewing individual products.

Sen. Maggie Hassan, D-N.H., asked Overton directly whether Trump was wrong. Overton reiterated that the vaccine was safe and effective and called it the best tool available during the measles outbreak. Sen. Ed Markey, D-Mass., questioned why she hadn’t corrected Trump in the Oval Office.

"So why should we expect you to stand up to the president as FDA commissioner?" Markey asked.

Overton worked on several of the administration’s Make America Healthy Again initiatives as a White House advisor.

Before returning to the White House, she served as vice chair and chief policy officer at the conservative America First Policy Institute, where she criticized expanded access to abortion pills in a 2023 policy brief.

Kyle Diamantas has led the agency in an acting capacity since Makary’s departure.

Erik Baptist, senior vice president for legal strategy at SBA Pro-Life America, said the group wants the next FDA nominee to commit to ending mail-order access to abortion drugs and changing how the agency studies their safety before the Senate holds a confirmation vote.

"The administration must immediately end the Biden FDA’s mail-order abortion-drug scheme and protect pregnant women from predators who coerce or poison women with these drugs," Baptist said in a statement to Fox News Digital. "And the FDA must fix its flawed safety study by obtaining robust data, increasing real-time transparency, and inviting external experts to help with this study."

"Confirmation is not a coronation," he added. "It is a time to vet nominees and seek commitments before they take office."

Fox News’ Pat Ward, Caroline McKee, and James Cirrone contributed to this report.
