Turkey-born NASA astronaut prepares for first mission to space station

2026-10-07 14:40

NASA astronaut Deniz Burnham, who was born at İncirlik Air Base in southern Turkey, is preparing for her first journey into space, a roughly seven-month mission aboard the International Space Station (ISS) targeted for March 2027.

Burnham spoke about her ties to Turkey and her family’s role in inspiring her interest in space in a video shared by the US Embassy in Turkey.

“I was born at Incirlik Air Base in Adana and have many great memories from childhood,” Burnham said.

NASA announced in July that Burnham would serve as a flight engineer during Expedition 76. She is expected to launch from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan aboard Russia’s Soyuz MS-30 spacecraft with Roscosmos cosmonauts Dmitri Petelin and Konstantin Borisov.

The crew will spend approximately seven months aboard the ISS conducting scientific research and technology demonstrations.

Speaking in Turkish in the embassy video, Burnham said her grandmother Aynur is from Eceabat in the northwestern province of Çanakkale and her grandfather Kadir is from the western province of Afyonkarahisar.

“My grandfather’s curiosity about space influenced our entire family,” she said. “I remember him building his own telescopes. We would spend countless hours in our backyard watching the stars and planets.”

Burnham said her grandparents live in Turkey and that she enjoys visiting them during holidays.

“In a few months I will launch to the International Space Station,” she said, adding in Turkish that she would wave from space to her grandparents, her fellow Adana residents “and the whole world.”

Burnham was selected as a NASA astronaut candidate in 2021 and graduated with the agency’s 23rd astronaut class in 2024. Before joining NASA, she spent more than a decade managing drilling operations in the energy industry and served as an engineering duty officer in the US Navy Reserve.

In the video, Burnham also welcomed Turkey’s accession to the Artemis Accords, a US-led set of nonbinding principles for peaceful and transparent civil space exploration. Turkey became the 71st signatory on August 31.

She highlighted the country’s growing space program, citing the flights of two Turkish astronauts and progress on AYAP-1, Turkey’s planned first lunar mission.

Alper Gezeravcı became Turkey’s first astronaut in January 2024, spending about two weeks aboard the ISS. Tuva Cihangir Atasever later undertook a suborbital research flight.

Burnham also cited Turkey’s hosting of Teknofest, an aviation, aerospace and technology festival, and the International Astronautical Congress, which is taking place in Antalya from October 5-9.

“On behalf of NASA, we are honored to welcome Türkiye to the Artemis Accords community and look forward to what we will accomplish together in the years ahead,” Burnham said.
