Anthropic IPO bonanza sparks worries that newly minted millionaires could leave the company: analysts

2026-11-19 19:44

The average Anthropic employee was worth $44 million following Anthropic’s public debut, which initially priced the stock at $83 per share but which surged in its first day of trading to as high as $111.50 a share, giving the company a market capitalization near $2 trillion.

More than half of Anthropic employees — or 2,800 of the company’s workforce of 5,000 — will be worth at least $10 million, according to estimates by Hill.com. A stunning 600 non-founder employees will have at least $100 million in equity.

Some of those employees could look to cash in their shares and retire – or strike out on their own by forming their own startups, according to Jay Ritter, a University of Florida finance professor and expert on IPOs.

“One of the downsides of going public is it typically does result in an increase in employee turnover,” Ritter told The Post.

Anthropic admitted as much in the so-called S-1 prospectus for its IPO, warning investors that its continued success hinged in part on its ability to “retain the continued service of our co-founders, senior management team, and key technical employees, and attract, retain, and motivate leading researchers, engineers, and technical talent in an intensely competitive and evolving market.”

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, his sister Daniela and five other cofounders are poised for the largest on-paper surge in wealth – with each set to rake in $20 billion to $25 billion from the IPO, according to estimates. Several other early employees who have remained at the five-year-old company also could become billionaires.

At the initial $83-per-share IPO price, Anthropic employees had combined wealth of roughly $232 billion, based on company filings. Workers are permitted to sell up to 10% of their shares after Anthropic begins public trading.

“It wouldn’t be surprising to see some employees take the allotted 10% off the table and turn some of their hard work into cash,” said Daniel Newman, CEO of Futurum Group.

At the same time, many Anthropic employees may decide to sit tight and see if the Claude chatbot maker is able to deliver on the exponential growth that some Wall Street insiders expect from the company.

“With its parabolic growth and the expected multi-year share price appreciation, I tend to believe many will hold a large portion of their shares expecting it to further appreciate,” Newman added.

As The Post has reported, Anthropic and its workforce have extensive ties to the wacky Effective Altruism and AI “doomer” movements – meaning some employees may have ideological reasons for staying at the company.

At the same time, Anthropic faces tough competition from OpenAI and Google – and all of the frontier AI labs have shown a willingness to spend top-dollar to lure talented AI researchers away from their rivals.

Anthropic managed to beat OpenAI to the punch with its IPO filing, and industry insiders widely see Amodei’s firm as having an advantage over Sam Altman’s firm in the race to build ever more advanced AI models.

That could work in Anthropic’s favor when it comes to talent retention, according to Nic Puckrin, a former Goldman Sachs analyst and founder of Coin Bureau.

“I do think Anthropic is probably viewed as the premier frontier lab right now, while OpenAI is looking like it’s losing ground,” Puckrin said. “So in that sense, I don’t think we will see people flooding out of Anthropic.”

“If they sell, they will likely be selling because they’ve been working there for so long and it’s a major opportunity to get liquidity on their shares,” Puckrin added.
