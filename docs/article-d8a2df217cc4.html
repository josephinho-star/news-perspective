My plea to Spencer Pratt voters: Don’t hand LA to socialists out of spite

2026-10-26 01:09

In June, I voted for Spencer Pratt for mayor for one reason. I wanted City Hall to hear one message: this city is a mess, and the people running it should be ashamed.

Spencer didn’t make it. But I know enough about politics to understand two things. Things can always get worse.

Start with the carpenters’ union.

The Western States Regional Council of Carpenters backed Karen Bass in 2022. Then Bass made one of the best calls of her term. Her decision to fast-track affordable housing has moved more than 29,000 units through city approval. Crucially, she refused to load those projects with a union-wage mandate after her advisers warned it could raise costs by as much as 20%.

The union wanted that mandate. A carpenters’ union vice president complained that Bass’s plan “left workers out of the equation.” In August, the union dropped Bass and endorsed Raman.

The carpenters’ consultant is Rick Jacobs, Eric Garcetti’s former senior adviser and one of the most powerful operators in this city. When the union couldn’t get what it wanted from Bass, it found a candidate who would comply.

The same day the union endorsed Raman, Bass was in Koreatown cutting the ribbon on 19 apartments for veterans, built under her directive, without the union mandate.

That’s the choice in this race: Housing under Bass; union control, and less housing, under Raman.

Then there’s Raman’s political home. She is a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, whose LA chapter endorsed her in September.

Read DSA-LA’s voter guide to understand what that means. It praises Raman for joining “the rest of DSA-LA in opposing the massive raises for LAPD officers in 2023.” It attacks Bass for her “steadfast support to raise salaries for the Los Angeles Police Department.” And on taxes, it says: “yes, we’re going to talk about repealing Prop 13,” the last protection for homeowners in California.

DSA-LA says its goal is to “build a governing majority” at City Hall, and it is running a slate, including Marissa Roy for City Attorney, to get there. A Mayor Raman wouldn’t be a lone progressive. She would lead a movement. Spencer himself warned Democrats never to trust the DSA. On that, he was right.

Then there’s homelessness, the issue that sent so many of us to Spencer in the first place. When the city finally cleared the encampment that had taken over Echo Park Lake in March 2021, Raman tweeted that “a forced and sudden eviction is not the way to move forward now.”

Her own constituents noticed. Local media reported that “some residents in her district said they were frustrated that Raman had not taken similar action to eliminate an encampment along Berendo Street.” One neighbor said she had “time to show up for a photo op in Echo Park” but not to “meet with the neighbors who have been asking for months to meet her face to face.”

It wasn’t a one-off. Raman voted against the city’s anti-camping law in 2021. She voted against keeping encampments 500 feet from schools and daycare centers in 2022. When the council named 54 sites for enforcement, she proposed none in her district. This April, as The California Post reported, she voted against a no-camping zone in Venice.

We’ve seen this movie before. George Gascón came down from San Francisco with a radical experiment in criminal justice “reform” and ran it in LA. It took a landslide defeat to end it.

Raman arrived in town in 2013 with her own theories about how a city should work, and she has been testing them on her district ever since. The people who run these experiments get to move on. Those of us who grew up here live with the results.

Karen Bass was not my first choice. Pratt voters have every right to be angry with her. Spencer lost his home in the Palisades Fire while she was in Ghana, and I’m not asking anyone to forget that.

But this election turns on one question: will Los Angeles be run by pragmatists or by an ideological movement? On that question, Bass has made the grown-up call when it counted.

Bass has plenty of problems. But she is not a radical, and she isn’t backed by the interests behind Raman.

A protest vote feels good for about a day. Then you live with it for four years.

If you voted for Spencer because you wanted LA to stop getting worse, don’t hand the city to the people who would make it worse faster. On Election Day, vote for Karen Bass.

Shervin Natan is a voting Angeleno and member of Thrive LA, a nonpartisan movement of Angelenos.
