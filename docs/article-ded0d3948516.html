Ms. Rachel calls silence on Gaza conflict ‘f–ked up,’ says it’s upset her so much she now swears

2026-11-26 11:04

Joseph A. Wulfsohn, Fox News

Published Nov. 26, 2026, 6:04 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Rachel Accurso, the progressive children’s content creator known as Ms. Rachel, is now turning to explicit language over the conflict in Gaza.

“We made enough money for our lives, so I can say anything I want, and I do, like, acknowledge the people who spoke out a long time ago and lost their jobs and didn’t have that,” Accurso told podcast host Hasan Minhaj.

“Like, they were the brave ones. They were amazing. But, like, I’m sorry, that is so f–ked up. I never swear, but I’ve started swearing because I’m like …”

“Go off,” Minhaj reacted.

“I’ll see something, and I’ll just be like, how is this possible?” she continued. “Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch, the UN — they’re not wrong. B’Tselem, I met with them, the Israeli human rights org.

“They’re so clear that this is genocide, and you’re going to be mad that Macklemore says to end the genocide? That’s a great thing to say when there is a genocide.”

Accurso, who has been an outspoken critic of Israel’s actions in Gaza, appeared on Wednesday’s installment of the “Hasan Minhaj Doesn’t Know” podcast and was asked about her condemnation of music superstar Ed Sheeran, who she accused of caving to pressure after his opening act, Macklemore, was pushed out of Sheeran’s tour for chanting “Free Palestine” at a concert.

“I see people prioritizing language over children’s lives. And I’m not going to play about that,” Accurso said. “Because children’s lives, Palestinian children’s lives matter, and they’re still being killed every day. …

“When there’s a genocide going on, a genocide which we all have a duty to speak out against — actually, countries have a duty to stop a genocide.”

The YouTube star shrugged off “Free Palestine” being a controversial slogan and insisted celebrities not speaking out against the war in Gaza is “costing people’s lives.”

She insisted speaking out was “the best thing of my life” despite whatever backlash she has faced.

“Do you want to meet insert huge celeb? Me: No,” she wrote last month in an Instagram post. “I don’t fan girl over someone who is doing nothing during a genocide.”
