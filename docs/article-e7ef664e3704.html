These 7 newly-elected Democrats voted against Jeffries for House speaker

2026-11-18 18:23

A bloc of seven incoming House Democrats voted against Rep. Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) being the next Speaker in an internal caucus vote on Wednesday.

The seven Democrats then made their votes known in a joint statement. They were among 11 votes against Jeffries in the internal vote. Jeffries’s bid was supported by 251 lawmakers.

The seven who voted against Jeffries and made their views public were Democratic Rep.-elects Darializa Avila Chevalier (N.Y.), Adam Hamawy (N.J.), Frederick Haynes (Texas), Melat Kiros (Colo.), Donavan McKinney (Mich.), Chris Rabb (Pa.) and Claire Valdez (N.Y.).

“Whether on Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics, or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these issues,” the group wrote in a statement.

“We have worked to secure commitments from Leader Jeffries in good-faith conversations,” they continued. “While we have seen meaningful progress, there is still work to be done to ensure that the direction our party takes this coming Congress reflects what our constituents are demanding of leadership.”

“Those conversations are ongoing. As such, our vote in caucus today was no,” the bloc concluded.

Five of the members who voted against Jeffries and signed the statement were backed by the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA), which made waves this election cycle for its progressive stances.

Newly elected DSA lawmakers were expected to create headaches for Jeffries as the group reportedly remained divided on how to approach changing the Democratic party’s stances. They’ve hammered their own party for what they see as an abandonment of progressive ideals and a soft-glove approach to President Trump’s second term.

Kiros’s race in Colorado drew national attention after she ousted 15-term incumbent Rep. Diana DeGette (D-Colo.). She ran on securing universal childcare and eldercare and supported an ultramillionaire tax on people with a net worth of more than $50 million.

Others in the group ran on similar platforms, overthrowing long-term centrist incumbents.

Nomiki Konst, a progressive strategist who is an inactive member of the DSA, predicted in October that most of the left in the caucus are going to side with Jeffries, noting Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s (D-N.Y.) struggles against then-Speaker Nancy Pelosi (D-Calif.)

The new DSA congressional members “have learned the lessons” of Ocasio-Cortez, Konst told The Hill.

Still their lack of support marks a new challenge that Jeffries will be expected to handle as division between centrists and progressives within the party continues to grow.

Hamawy and Haynes are not affiliated or a part of the DSA but campaigned on progressive platforms.

A spokesperson for Haynes shared a statement with Time that the incoming lawmaker ‘woke up unexpectedly ill this morning” and “narrowly missed the vote.” His team shared that if he did cast his vote, Haynes would have voted against Jeffries becoming House Speaker.

Haynes is replacing outgoing Rep. Jasmine Crockett’s (D-Texas) open House seat after her failed bid for Texas’s Senate seat. He differed from Crockett who voted to send U.S. military to Israel.

Christ was against genocide and that if “it’s in Gaza, he’s against it,” he told Texas outlet WFAA.

While Hamawy, a doctor and veteran, took hold of outgoing Rep. Bonnie Watson Coleman’s (D-N.J.) seat who served in the House since 2015. On the campaign trail, he wouldn’t commit to backing Jeffries, telling the New Jersey Globe that current Democratic leadership is “failing to meet the moment.”
