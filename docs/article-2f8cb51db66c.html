Stream It Or Skip It: ‘Heartland’ on Netflix, a Pulpy Mystery Starring Jessica Chastain as a Take-No-Guff Country Singer

2026-11-13 21:00

Jessica Chastain headlines Heartland (now on Netflix), playing a take-no-guff retired country singer who reluctantly returns to Nashville to find her missing niece. Notably, filmmaker Shana Feste returns to the subject matter of her most prominent film, 2011’s Country Strong, a Best Original Song Oscar nominee in which Gwyneth Paltrow stood out by singing with some Tennessee twang while playing a troubled star. Chastain’s character is cut from similar cloth, but her honeyed voice is cut with plenty of piss and vinegar.

HEARTLAND: STREAM IT OR SKIP IT?

The Gist: One guy calls Misty Jones (Chastain) “The Queen of Mean,” but she prefers a different nickname: “The C— of Country.” Misty, see, she knows herself. We meet her as she punctuates a beef with a neighbor by pulling a pistol and shooting out the old lady’s windows. She holds her liquor and brings randos home for one-night stands. Misty’s many years removed from nine hit songs and a Grammy — a Grammy she keeps in her Sad Empty Fridge, the likes of which you only see in movies — retired to a not-at-all-extravagant Georgia pecan farm. The details are sketchy, but we glean that her salty disposition and various interpersonal entanglements found her run out of Nashville, and she doesn’t miss it at all.

Hence, why she’s reluctant to return to Music City for any reason. But she doesn’t really have a choice now. Her brother (Paul Sparks) calls. Her niece, budding country star Charlie Heart (Carter Faith), is missing. No word from her for three days. He needs her help. And Misty reluctantly obliges. She still knows people in the music scene, people to whom she probably owes apologies, but she doesn’t seem like the apologizing type.

Some of those people include Jace (Ross Lynch), Charlie’s ex, another star on the rise, and Roy (Garrett Hedlund), Charlie’s manager, an old associate of Misty’s, emphasis on the ass. Misty’s reputation precedes her like a tsunami, and she keeps on with generating undersea earthquakes. You don’t get anywhere with skeezy music-biz characters, hostile cops and deranged stalkers without throwing some punches and cursing people out, y’know. Will Misty find her niece? Will she just find more trouble? And will she find herself in the process?

What Movies Will It Remind You Of? Heartland plays less like a movie and more like a pilot for a female-led version of Justified.

Performance Worth Watching: Chastain doesn’t necessarily elevate the material here, but she leans into it in a way that bolsters her character with acidic charisma.

Sex And Skin: One shot of dude butt.

Our Take: Where Country Strong leaned toward prestige, Heartland is very much a genre exercise, fronted by a character who seems ripe for boozin’ and cussin’ her way to solving the mystery of the week on whatever network that airs Yellowstone. Remember, Misty is likely independently wealthy from royalties from nine hits, so she has plenty of time to be an amateur sleuth, parrying with men with badges and shooting it out with scummy lowlifes, occasionally stopping to be persuaded to reluctantly sing one of her old favorites. It’s not a bad formula at all — but that doesn’t bely the fact that the movie is formulaic, with its twisty reveals and slightly beyond-the-pale pulpy plotting.

Which is to say, Feste’s screenplay isn’t terribly insightful in the realm of the human condition, although Chastain manages to find some nuances to Misty as she ponders her past, present and future. The story is overly convoluted and rife with uh huh sure yeah right moments that threaten to snap our suspension of disbelief like a frayed rubber band. And Feste maintains a hyperactively over-the-top tone that pushes her cast to the brink of overacting. But the pace is quick and the suspense is taut, and watching Chastain wrecking-ball her way through this borderline-nonsense is undeniably entertaining.

Our Call: Don’t take Heartland too seriously, and you’ll enjoy it enough. STREAM IT.

John Serba is a freelance film critic from Grand Rapids, Michigan. Werner Herzog hugged him once.
