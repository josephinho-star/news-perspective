Pence leaves door open on 2028 presidential run

2026-11-12 21:36

Former Vice President Mike Pence, who was President Trump’s second-in-command during his first term, is leaving the door open to a 2028 presidential bid.

In a video interview with Fox News Digital published Thursday, Pence was pressed on if he was thinking about a presidential bid and a “timeline” on his decision, but did not give a direct answer to either question.

“The truth is, if we don’t return the Republican Party to the principles of a strong defense, limited government, fiscal responsibility and the right to life, it won’t matter who we nominate in 2028,” Pence said in the interview.

“Because this version of Republicanism was just rejected by the American people,” he continued. “But, I have great hope, I was there the last time we lost the majority in 2006. I have great confidence in our new leadership in the Congress, that they know the path forward. But, I can tell you that I don’t know what the future holds, but all of my energies, all of my focus and my calling, now, is to be a champion and an advocate for the conservative principles that have defined our party.”

Two months ago, Pence said he had “no plans” for a bid for the White House or any other elected office in 2028.

Economic Club of D.C. Chair David Rubenstein, during an event for the organization at the time, pressed the former vice president about if he would consider another go at the White House.

“What I can tell you is that we have no plans,” Pence responded.

Pence took on Trump in the 2024 GOP presidential primary. However, he later dropped out of the race following fundraising and polling trouble. Pence did not endorse Trump’s 2024 run and did not congratulate the president until after he won.

Trump and Pence’s relationship went south at the end of Trump’s first term, with Pence not stopping Congress’s certification of former President Biden’s victory in the 2020 election.
