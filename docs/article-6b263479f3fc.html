The best tennis players of 2026: Zverev, Rybakina and many more

2026-11-23 23:07

The 2026 professional tennis season has come to an end, and what a wild, unpredictable, slightly chaotic year it was.

From the long-awaited emergence of Elena Rybakina to the surprising rise of Alexander Zverev to the countless stunning upsets and heralded comebacks and farewells, 2026 truly was one to remember.

Few could have predicted at the start of the year how the majors would fare. Rybakina won two, as did Zverev. Aryna Sabalenka didn't earn a Slam trophy for the first time since 2022. Carlos Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner, who had combined to win every major in 2024 and 2025, struggled with injuries and each added only one more to their growing collections. Mirra Andreeva and Linda Noskova claimed their first Slam titles, at the French Open and Wimbledon, respectively.

Yup, it all made for one unforgettable -- and, did we mention, unpredictable -- year.

Who deserves to take home the top honors for 2026? Here are our WTA and ATP Players of the Year, in addition to some more titles on other (perhaps slightly less-quantifiable) favorites from the year.

WTA Player of the Year: Elena Rybakina

After she won her first major title at Wimbledon in 2022, few could dispute Rybakina's talent and potential, but injuries, illnesses and some off-the-court setbacks dictated much of her next few seasons.

But that was then.

After winning the WTA Finals to close out 2025, a resurgent Rybakina was virtually unstoppable at the Australian Open. With her dominant -- and often spirit-crushing -- serve and cool-as-ice demeanor, Rybakina didn't allow top-level opponents such as Iga Swiatek and Jessica Pegula to win a set. In the final, in a rematch of the 2023 tournament final, Rybakina secured her revenge against Sabalenka with a 6-4, 4-6, 6-4 victory.

"She played [an] incredible match," Sabalenka said afterward.

Rybakina reached the final at Indian Wells and won her second title at Stuttgart in April on the clay, but it was the summer hard-court season where she officially proved her championship mentality and made her case for the world No. 1 ranking. The 27-year-old advanced to the final at the Canadian Open, but after retiring because of an Achilles injury during a quarterfinal match at the Cincinnati Open, her status for the US Open was in doubt. She was unable to practice for a week -- a near eternity in the lead-up to a Slam -- before the major got underway.

No one, Rybakina included, knew what to expect from her when the tournament began. But she quickly quieted any speculation with one strong showing after another throughout the tournament. Defeating Coco Gauff in three sets in the semifinals -- and all but silencing the American crowd at Arthur Ashe Stadium in the decider -- Rybakina defeated Sabalenka yet again in the final for her third major title and second of the year.

"I always believed that it [was] possible," Rybakina told reporters after the final. "We've been having great results. Of course, not every year is the same. I won Wimbledon in 2022. Then I wasn't winning the Slams, but I played the final of Australian Open the next year. I think we were always there.

"It's never easy. Even now I finish here, I rest, I celebrate, and then still the tournaments are coming up. You still need to play. You still need to compete with the same players, and you still need to do your best. It's an amazing journey, and hopefully I can continue [to] stay at the top as long as possible."

Two days later, she took over the world No.1 ranking from Sabalenka, who'd held it for 99 weeks.

Rybakina collected another trophy at the 500-level Ningbo Open before having to withdraw and shut down her season at the WTA Finals due to illness. She now has a slim lead over Andreeva atop the rankings -- but was the clear choice for Player of the Year and she should remain a contender for every Slam in 2027.

ATP Player of the Year: Alexander Zverev

Entering the year with an 0-3 streak in major finals and a lopsided losing record against both Alcaraz and Sinner, Zverev was an unlikely candidate to win two major titles and claim the world No. 1 ranking.

But no one capitalized on the extended absences and upsets of the Big Two more than the 29-year-old German. While top seed after top seed -- including Sinner -- faltered at Roland Garros, Zverev stayed the course. In the final, he defeated Flavio Cobolli in five sets, 6-1, 4-6, 6-4, 6-7 (5), 6-1 to finally secure what he had been working toward for his entire career.

"We've been through injuries," an emotional Zverev said to his team during the trophy presentation. "We've been through heartbreak. We've been through losses. We've been losers at times in the important moments. But at the end of the day, we're Grand Slam champions now and that's what counts."

Just weeks later, he reached his first Wimbledon final, where he lost to Sinner. And at the US Open, he once again seized the opportunity with Sinner's absence and after Alcaraz's quarterfinal loss in his first event back.

Zverev had to fight through tricky early opponents -- needing five sets in both of his first two matches -- but he then was in firm control of his destiny. He didn't drop another set until the final against Ben Shelton, but even that was just a temporary hiccup. He won his second Slam title 6-3, 7-6 (2), 5-7, 6-2.

When Sinner announced he would not be able to return for the rest of the year, Zverev's path was cleared. He would be the year-end No. 1, marking his first time achieving the milestone ranking. He closed out the year with a loss to Alcaraz in the title match at the ATP Finals on Sunday.

A polarizing figure in the sport due to multiple domestic violence allegations, Zverev has also faced many questions about the legitimacy of his top ranking due to a poor top-10 record and lopsided head-to-head showings against Sinner and Alcaraz -- punctuated by his loss to Alcaraz on Sunday. Zverev's stay at the top might ultimately be one of the more forgettable stints in recent memory. (Although he jokingly told reporters his five losses to Sinner in 2026 shouldn't count because "he's a robot.")

Still, it would be impossible to discount what he has accomplished on the court this season, and he is undisputedly the player of the year for what he has been able to do.

Breakthrough Player of the Year: Mirra Andreeva

Since bursting onto the scene as a 15-year-old in 2023, Andreeva's future as a major champion seemed all but inevitable. But still, having the potential and fulfilling it are two different things.

Earlier this year, Andreeva, now just 19, secured the trophy at Roland Garros with a dominant run in which she dropped just one set. She finished the year -- which also saw her win two other titles and reach two 1000 level finals -- with a statement victory (and undefeated week) at the WTA Finals. She became the event's youngest champion since Maria Sharapova in 2004.

Andreeva, who still doesn't even have her driver's license despite winning a car at the year-end championships, is now ranked a career high of No. 2 in the world -- and trails No. 1 Rybakina by just 293 points. Could 2027 be an even better year for the fast-rising star?

Match of the Year: Ben Shelton vs. Carlos Alcaraz, US Open quarterfinals

Heading into this much-hyped match, Alcaraz held a 3-0 edge over Shelton and was the US Open defending champion. Despite having just returned from a wrist injury that had sidelined him for five months, Alcaraz had been in control of his matches at the tournament -- and he looked well on his way to winning his third US Open trophy.

But Shelton, who had been playing some impressive tennis of his own throughout the summer hard-court season, had other plans.

In a late-night clash between two of the ATP's most athletic and dynamic players, the two battled until the early morning hours in front of a captivated crowd on Arthur Ashe Stadium. Finally, after five sets and a mind-blowing array of electrifying points, Shelton pulled out one of the biggest victories of his career 6-7 (5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6 (7). The match, which concluded at 3:33 a.m., marked the latest finish in tournament history -- and will be one that fans will be talking about for years.

"That was a war, [an] epic match," Shelton, who later reached the final, said afterward.

"The most enjoyable match that I've had in my career for sure."

Women's Doubles Team of the Year: Katerina Siniakova and Taylor Townsend

There is no doubt that Siniakova and Townsend are the best duo in women's tennis. The pair completed the career Grand Slam together by winning the French Open and the US Open, won the trophy at the WTA Finals and captured three 1000 level titles, including recording the rare Sunshine Double in Indian Wells and Miami.

They are currently ranked No. 1 and No. 2 in doubles -- and Siniakova clinched the year-end top ranking for a record-breaking sixth time. She has the second-most weeks at No. 1 in WTA doubles history, behind only Martina Navratilova. While speaking on court during the trophy presentation at the WTA Finals, Townsend was effusive in her praise for her partner.

"Kat, it is an honor and a privilege to stand beside you, to play with you, to witness you, to create history and be a part of that any way that I can," Townsend said. "It's not very often that someone can say they've played with the greatest of all time, someone who is creating history in real time."

And Townsend -- who made history herself as the first Black woman to win the WTA Finals doubles title -- and Siniakova are far from done. They both indicated that their partnership would continue into the new season, and they should remain the favorites at nearly every event they play.

Men's Doubles Team of the Year: Christian Harrison and Neal Skupski

There was a strong argument to be made for Harri Heliovaara and Henry Patten, who won five titles together this year, including Wimbledon, and are the year-end No. 1s -- and used their moment of recognition to boldly criticize the ATP's treatment of doubles. But ultimately, Harrison and Skupski narrowly take this honor for what they were able to achieve in their first year as a team.

The American-British duo won the Australian Open trophy in just their second tournament together. It was Skupski's second career major title and Harrison's first, and Skupski credited his teammate for giving him "a new lease on life."

And the success didn't stop there. The pair also became champions at the US Open and ended the season with a semifinal run at the ATP Finals. They finish the year ranked No. 3.

Rookie of the Year: Rafael Jodar

At the beginning of 2026, Jodar had never played an ATP tour-level event and was ranked No. 168.

Now 20, he ends the year as an ATP champion, a major quarterfinalist and ranked No. 10 in the world. It marked the fastest-ever climb into the top 10 in men's tennis. And not only that, but he even reached the ATP Finals and became the first player to do so in his debut year on tour.

"It's unbelievable the way he's playing in his first year on the tour," Alcaraz, a fellow Spaniard, told reporters earlier this month.

With a game and a name suited for greatness, the future is bright for tennis' latest Rafa. The 2024 US Open junior champion, Jodar -- who played a memorable semester at Virginia before turning pro -- will look to build on his incredible achievements in 2027.

Biggest Surprise of the Year: Maja Chwalinska

Before the French Open, Chwalinska had played in just two major main draws and had won just one match. Ranked No. 114, she came through qualifying in Paris to make the main draw -- and what she did next seemed like it came straight from a movie script.

Chwalinska, now 25, rattled off one big victory after another against established veterans and seeded players and became the first qualifier in tournament history to reach the final. While she remains focused on simply playing her game, even she admitted she hadn't exactly anticipated the run.

"Let's not pretend someone expected it," Chwalinska said after her semifinal win. "I mean, I was outside the top 100, and now I'm in the finals of a Grand Slam, so I feel like it's a big thing."

She lost to Andreeva in the final 6-3, 6-2, but her ranking skyrocketed to No. 21 and she nearly tripled her career prize earnings in her three weeks at Roland Garros.

She struggled at the Slams following her Cinderella showing but found her footing by the end of the year. She won her biggest title to date at the 1000 level Wuhan Open in October and reached a career-high ranking of No. 11. Perhaps most of all, she proved her French Open result was far from a fluke.

The 'Did that really happen?' Award: Serena Williams

After months of speculation, the 23-time major champion returned to competition at Queens Club in June. Her comeback started strong with a doubles victory alongside Victoria Mboko before she had to withdraw following Mboko's season-ending knee injury. Williams, now 45, played her lone singles match at Wimbledon -- losing to Maya Joint in a deciding set in the opening round -- and suffered a knee injury, forcing her to withdraw before teaming up with Venus in doubles. The sisters played together in Cincinnati and at the US Open but were winless at both.

She didn't speak to the media at either of her major appearances and her status for 2027 remains uncertain, but Serena has implied she will "probably" play singles again. Let the "Will she or won't she?" chatter begin!

The 'You'll be missed' Awards: Stan Wawrinka, Gael Monfils and Sorana Cirstea

The 2026 season marked the end of an era in men's tennis, as beloved veterans Stan Wawrinka, a three-time major champion, and Gael Monfils, one of the most electrifying showmen in the sport, retired following a season of well-deserved farewells.

Wawrinka, 41, who awkwardly received the last wild card at the US Open, finished his career at his home tournament at the Swiss Open in October. Monfils, 40, played in his final tournament at the US Open but received touching tributes at both the French Open and the Paris Masters.

And Cirstea, a three-time major quarterfinalist, announced she would retire at year's end in December -- and then had the best season of her career. The 36-year-old won a home title at the Transylvania Open, her fourth WTA singles title. She also reached the semifinals at the Italian Open, including a win over then-No. 1 Sabalenka, and the quarters at Roland Garros -- and achieved a new career-high ranking of No. 15 in September. Cirstea's time as a professional tennis player presumably came to a close with a quarterfinal loss at the Hong Kong Open earlier this month, but she didn't post the standard official goodbye on social media, so it is possible the door might not be completely closed. We'll see.

Novak Djokovic: The man is 39 years old, with nothing left to prove, and he still reached the Australian Open final and the Wimbledon semifinals and won his record-extending seventh title at the China Open. It was far from his best year, and it's clear his career is nearing its end, but what he is still able to achieve -- while most of his cohorts are retired -- is worth recognizing.

Venus Williams: There was significant criticism in regard to the number of wild cards Williams received this year -- and her now 17-match losing streak in singles didn't exactly help that -- but the 46-year-old had some success on the doubles court. Partnering with a number of different players this year, Williams reached the doubles semifinals at Guangzhou Open and the quarterfinals at Bad Homburg, Washington and Hong Kong. And along the way, she has shared her joy and knowledge of the game with younger players who seem delighted to be on court with her.

Carlos Alcaraz: The 23-year-old missed nearly half of the season with injury and still had a year almost any other player would dream about. He became the youngest man in history to complete the career Grand Slam with his first title at the Australian Open, and he won four titles after coming back, including the Paris Masters and his first-ever ATP Finals. Not too shabby for a down year.

Alex Eala: The pride of the Philippines and easily one of the most popular players on tour, the 21-year-old earned her first WTA title at the 500-level Washington Open in July and had her best showing at a major with a fourth-round run at Wimbledon. Drawing massive crowds wherever she goes, Eala appears poised to become one of the game's next big mainstream superstars.

Jannik Sinner: Like Alcaraz, Sinner also missed much of the season -- but he made the time he did have on tour count. In addition to defending his Wimbledon title for his fifth major title, Sinner, 25, won the season's first five Masters 1000 tournaments, making him just the second man to win every Masters title on tour and extending his record of six consecutive titles won at the level. Having not played since Wimbledon, and with his return timeline still questionable, he lost his -- once seemingly untouchable -- grasp on No. 1 but remains No. 2 in the rankings. He will reportedly undergo surgery this week, pushing back his comeback further.

Linda Noskova: It was a banner year for the 22-year-old. She won her first grass title in Berlin in June -- and then carried her momentum to Wimbledon, where she stunned the field to claim her first major trophy. Since that breakthrough victory, Noskova reached the quarterfinals at the US Open and the semifinals in her WTA Finals debut -- and helped lead the Czech team to a Billie Jean King Cup victory.

Ben Shelton: The 24-year-old continues to make his push to the game's upper echelon with statement victories, titles and major runs. He reached his first-ever Slam final at the US Open and won four titles overall, including his second straight Masters 1000 level Canadian Open. He reached the semifinals at the ATP Finals and finishes the year ranked at No. 4 as the top-ranked American man. Will Shelton be the one to finally end the Slam drought among his countrymen in 2027? Stay tuned.

Marta Kostyuk: There was no woman with a hotter streak on clay in the spring than the 24-year-old Ukrainian. She won the title in Rouen and then captured her biggest trophy to date at Madrid -- which went viral thanks to a celebratory back handspring -- following one big win after another. She carried that momentum all the way into her first major semifinal at Roland Garros. Though her 17-match win streak came to an end against Andreeva, her confidence remained. She backed it up by advancing to the semifinals at Wimbledon and now sits at a career-high ranking of No. 10.

Arthur Fils: Yet another player to make the most of a season marred by injuries, the 22-year-old battled through multiple stops and starts to clinch the biggest title of his career at the Masters 1000 level Cincinnati Open, in addition to the 500 level Barcelona Open, and earned five victories over top-10 players. He reached the year-end championships for the first time and secured his best-ever ranking at No. 6 to end the year. Imagine just how good he could be if he is able to stay healthy in 2027.

Bianca Andreescu: It has been a challenging stretch for the 26-year-old Canadian since winning her lone major title at the 2019 US Open, filled with extended absences and countless injuries. But if the way she ended 2026 is any indication, Andreescu might finally be back. She played in a number of ITF and 125-level events in order to boost her ranking and find some match rhythm and experience, winning two ITF titles to open the year and closing it out by reaching four straight 125 finals, winning three of them. Andreescu began the year ranked at No. 228 -- and needed to come through qualifying for entry at majors -- and ends it at No. 77. She will now have direct entry to play in her first Australian Open since 2023.
