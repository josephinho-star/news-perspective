Bahrain intercepts Iranian strikes on US airbase

2026-11-15 17:57

Iran ramped up strikes in Bahrain early Sunday morning, carrying out a six-drone attack on a U.S. airbase.

Bahrain said it had intercepted “several aerial attacks” before they hit their targets. Iran, meanwhile, claimed it had struck a base.

This comes two days after Iran reported it had attacked a U.S. early-warning radar facility also in Bahrain, but the U.S. military denied it, saying it had intercepted the drones without damage.

The Gulf kingdom had not been struck by Iran since a blitz in September. U.S. Navy secretary Hung Cao at the time said that the Iranian military “blew the hell” out of it.

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said that Sunday’s strikes were in retaliation for “hostile US actions,” Iran’s Fars news agency reported.

Esmail Baghaei, a spokesperson for the Iranian Foreign Ministry, said at a press conference on Sunday that the country would continue to respond to military aggression by the U.S. and other countries.

The U.S. continues to maintain a naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz as the war pushes past the eight-month mark.

It has also continued to carry out limited defensive strikes. Earlier this month, U.S. warplanes struck an anti-aircraft site on Qeshm Island and another island in the Strait of Hormuz.

Diplomatic attempts to end the conflict have stalled. President Trump earlier this week called the war “effectively over” and “won” by the U.S. He also said he was unlikely to order fresh attacks as long as talks to end the war continue positively.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian on Wednesday accused the U.S. of threatening to attack his country with America’s “mother of all bombs,” its largest non-nuclear weapon, even as the U.S. pursues a peace deal.

Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, Egypt condemned Iran’s Sunday attacks in solidarity with Bahrain.
