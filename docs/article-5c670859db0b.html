Epstein reporter erupts at media over Pablo Torre slight after his Clippers vindication

2026-09-04 02:10

After the NBA handed the Clippers a historic punishment, one of the country’s most decorated investigative journalists believes Pablo Torre still isn’t getting enough credit.

Miami Herald reporter Julie K. Brown, whose “Perversion of Justice” investigation helped expose Jeffrey Epstein’s abuse and the system that protected him, ripped The New York Times and The Athletic for what she viewed as minimizing Torre’s role in uncovering the Clippers scandal.

“It is shameful that @nytimes @TheAthletic buried the fact that the biggest punishment in NBA history was the result of an investigation by journalist @PabloTorre,” Brown wrote on X. “And to call it the result of a ‘podcast episode’ is a further slap in the face.”

Brown received a 2026 Pulitzer special citation for her groundbreaking Epstein reporting. The Pulitzer board honored her work for exposing Epstein’s abuse, the justice system that protected him and the powerful network surrounding him.

Torre and the staff of “Pablo Torre Finds Out,” meanwhile, won the 2026 Pulitzer Prize for Audio Reporting specifically for their investigation into how the Clippers seemingly circumvented NBA salary cap rules.

The New York Post first detailed Torre’s bombshell reporting on Kawhi Leonard’s $28 million “no-show” endorsement deal in September 2025.

Torre continued uncovering pieces of the financial relationship in the weeks that followed, including a $21 million Aspiration paper trail involving the Clippers.

A year later, the NBA concluded that the Clippers committed a “pattern of misconduct and multiple significant rules violations” by facilitating outside income opportunities for Leonard with companies doing business with the franchise. Los Angeles was fined $30 million and stripped of five first-round picks, while owner Steve Ballmer received a one-year suspension.

The league also suspended Clippers executives Gillian Zucker and Lawrence Frank, fined Leonard $700,000 and banned Leonard’s former business representative Dennis Robertson from conducting NBA business for five years. The NBA said its findings followed an independent investigation by Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz.

The findings landed after months in which Torre’s reporting had faced significant pushback.

Ballmer and the Clippers repeatedly denied wrongdoing. When Torre’s original report surfaced, the team called allegations that it circumvented the salary cap “provably false.”

Mark Cuban became one of Torre’s most prominent skeptics, publicly declaring himself “Team Ballmer” and arguing Torre was focusing on the wrong villain. Cuban warned that Torre’s theory involving Ballmer would “back fire on him.”

The NBA’s ruling prompted a markedly different response from Cuban.

It was an extraordinary two-word concession after a year in which Torre’s reporting had repeatedly been questioned.

Brown argued the resistance Torre encountered made the lack of recognition more glaring.

“Pablo’s work was so threatening that the Clippers hired ‘fixers’ to try to smear him — yet he continued to report on this even in the face of immense pressure,” Brown wrote, an allegation she made without providing additional evidence in the post.

She also praised Torre for pushing sports journalism toward greater accountability.

“Pablo Torre won a Pulitzer Prize for a reason,” Brown wrote. “He is changing sports journalism: forcing transparency and accountability to a system that has operated in the dark for a long time.”

The Pulitzer board itself has been considerably less coy about Torre’s impact.

After the NBA announced its findings, the organization published a piece highlighting the real-world impact of the reporting and noting that Torre had “broken the whole affair” the previous year.

Brown’s point was simpler.

Torre didn’t merely host a podcast episode about the Clippers.

He uncovered the story that ultimately triggered an NBA investigation and one of the most consequential punishments in league history.
