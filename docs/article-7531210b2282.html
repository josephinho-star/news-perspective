French PM blames ‘ultra-left’ for exploiting protests as he seeks partial curfews – Europe live

2026-10-19 15:52

Lecornu blames 'ultra-left' and criminals for exploiting protests

French PM Lecornu says he has asked prefects to implement “a number of partial curfews” in certain “localised” urban areas to stop further disorder, which he attributes to “the extreme-left” and some criminal elements.

We will no doubt hear more on this from local authorities, as in the example of Marseille (10:32).

He also says the government is determined to respond to the events as needed, and if declaring a state of emergency is deemed necessary, they would do so – but that’s a major decision to make and currently not being considered.

He says be believes “there is an agenda of chaos being pushed by the far-left,” with the intention of disrupting next year’s presidential election, and that’s why he urges the NR leaders to attend his briefing later today, despite their plans no to do so (12:20).

Lecornu also says that there will be a “strong” response from the country’s judiciary, cracking down on the violence and crime on the margins of the protests. Investigators are also looking through social media footage, where rioters often did not wear masks.

New measures to stop riots from reoccurring will be put in place starting tonight, but tomorrow’s football game between PSG and Barcelona is currently scheduled to still go ahead.

He also says that furlough measures for businesses affected by riots will kick in on Tuesday to ensure all jobs are saved despite any disruption to business operations in the build up to the busy Christmas period.

He says there will also be further targeted measures, but with little detail at this stage.

Separately, the government will also push to expedite various assessments required to pay out any insurance claims.

Lecornu also says that the right to legitimate protest needs to be safeguarded and considered separately from the riots and clashes with police forces.

Four police officers were injured and a police station was vandalised in the Garenne district of La Roche-sur-Yon by “around sixty individuals dressed in black and wearing balaclavas” who fired fireworks and other projectiles, Liberation reported.

‘It looks like a war zone’: France reels as looting continues for second night

Just before 1am on Monday morning, two motorbikes carrying one passenger each stopped at a traffic light on a backstreet near rue de Rivoli,one of central Paris’s busiest shopping streets.

“You’ve got to go where there are a lot of people, where they’ve done the shop windows,” said one motorbike rider to another. Minutes later 10 other men, their faces covered with ski balaclavas, came down the street on foot pursued by a police van.

Soon after Emmanuel Macron’stelevised speech on Sunday night telling the nation it was “unbearable to see our streets ransacked”, groups of men looted shops in central Paris for hours for a second night running. Dozens of people attempted to smash windows near Les Halles shopping centre and grab goods. Others broke into an electrical shop in Belleville in the north of the city.

“Men were battering shop windows, trying to break in, then they were chased by police,” said a 73-year-old woman near Les Halles.

“There were different groups running everywhere with police chasing on foot or by car, and at the same time, some people were setting bins and bikes alight. The groups breaking into shops aren’t hostile to passersby so you’d see local residents walking down the street on their way home late at night, and masked teenagers running past in the other direction carrying looted goods. It’s so strange – it’s like two different universes in parallel worlds.”

“It says a lot about society – it shows we have neglected our education system and that we’re living in a consumer society where all we say to our young people is: ‘buy, buy, buy’. In this type of extreme capitalism, some people don’t have enough money to buy all of this and so they attack.”

Macron begins Gulf trip aimed at securing petrol, diesel supplies

Meanwhile, as France was busy cleaning up its streets, the country’s president Emmanuel Macron – who last night delivered an address to the nation on the events – arrived in Kuwait for a Gulf trip aimed at securing additional petrol and diesel supplies to bring down pump prices, Reuters reported.

During the trip, he is also visiting the United Arab Emirates.

Interior minister concedes police ‘lost control’ over looting

Over the weekend, several public buildings including a town hall were attacked and set ablaze and an estimated 2,000 shops were ransacked and looted, authorities have said. Nearly 2,400 people – 70% of them adults – were arrested and almost 1,000 police and gendarmes injured.

Interior minister Laurent Nuñez said 60,000 police and other security forces had been deployed around the country, but acknowledged it had not been enough to control determined, organised groups of violent rioters in major cities including Paris, Marseille and Lyon.

“You couldn’t look shopkeepers in the eye after hundreds of shops had been looted and say we hadn’t lost control,” the minister told French radio, but added that the responsibility of “rioters, looters and ultra-left militants” should not be glossed over.

The justice minister, Gérald Darmanin, also said intelligence failings had left the state vulnerable. “There’s no good policing without good intelligence, he said, adding that many of those arrested had police records and “about 80%” were being prosecuted.

Vinted, other platforms move to block resale of stolen goods after French riots

After widespread looting reported over the weekend, several reselling platforms – including Vinted – saw a sudden surge of new (and often heavily mispriced) listings from France, suggesting some sellers, erm, wanted to quickly dispose of some of their new belongings.

The French finance ministry is set to chair a round of talks with Vinted, Leboncoin, Cdiscount and other online platforms this evening to make sure they block such transactions.

Vinted said this afternoon that it is actively working with authorities to identify and prevent any attempt to resell stolen goods, adding that there is “zero tolerance” for such activity, according to a statement quoted by BFMTV.

France hit by debt sell-off as protests unnerve investors

France has been hit by a fresh wave of selling in its bond and stock markets after violent protests over the weekend left public buildings burned, hundreds of shops looted and thousands of arrests across the country.

The chaos has spooked investors in France’s government debt, with the yield on its 10-year yield rising by 10 basis points to 4.89% on Monday. Yields rise when prices fall.

This widened the difference between the French and German 10-year borrowing costs – which is a metric for the risk premium attached to French debt – towards the highest levels seen in 14 years.

Analysts at Goldman Sachs said European sovereign risk was back in focus, with fiscal concerns about France raising the question of whether there could be a repeat of the eurozone debt crisis of 15 years ago.

“The risks of disorderly market escalation and real economy contagion look notably lower. But the fundamental fiscal challenges for some countries – especially in France – look harder now than in 2011-15,” the investment bank said.

“The issue of how to deal with fundamental sovereign risk remains unresolved in the Euro area, requiring credible fiscal consolidation,” the analyst added.

The two-year French bond yield rose by eight basis points to 3.63%.

Concerns about France’s instability knocked the euro down to a 17-month low against the pound on Monday morning, with sterling rising to €1.188.

Trump repeats his baseless claims linking French protests with migration

On Truth Social today, Donald Trump has repeated his baseless claims that the riots sweeping France – which initially began as protests against deteriorating school infrastructure and an overstretched education system – are due to mass migration.

“The migrants have taken over France. They must CLOSE THE BORDERS, FAST!” Trump wrote on social media.

Worth remembering that French authorities have not linked the riots to immigrants.

Over the weekend, hundreds of thousands demonstrated throughout the country. However, chaos erupted at a number of protests, which saw buildings burned, hundreds of shops destroyed and looted and at least 2,400 people arrested.

French president Emmanuel Macron and the prime minister, Sébastien Lecornu, have blamed the violence on “factious” ultra left-wing groups.

Several European governments tell travellers to France to avoid crowds, be cautious

Meanwhile, several European governments advised citizens travelling to France to avoid large crowds and exercise caution, warning of a deterioration in security conditions after thousands were arrested and shops looted in protests over the weekend, Reuters reported.

Italy said its citizens should register their presence in France on a website or an app to enable them to be located, as it warned them to avoid large crowds and exercise extreme caution.

The German foreign ministry said further riots in the coming days couldn’t be ruled out and urged German nationals to follow instructions from local police. It warned citizens that they could face fines or claims for damages if they failed to leave unauthorised demonstrations when asked to do so.

Czechs in France are advised to avoid demonstrations, marches and public gatherings, ČTK news agency reported. The security situation in France has deteriorated, according to the Czech foreign affairs ministry.

Poland said in its country guidance that visitors should avoid places where demonstrations are taking place, though there was no change to its overall guidance about whether France was safe to visit.

The Dutch government in an advisory last updated on 7 October warned travelers to France to avoid protests.

Similarly, Denmark, Sweden, Finland and Norway have issued advisories urging their citizens in France to avoid demonstrations and follow the guidance of local authorities.

Albania's Rama accusses protesters of crossing 'a red line' after police station was set ablaze during protests

Albania has also experienced days of disturbances, and today prime minister Edi Rama has accused protesters of crossing a “red line” after a police station was set ablaze during anti-government demonstrations.

“Protests are a right, whereas violence is a crime; this red line must be drawn clearly at this critical stage of our democratic development,” AFP reports Rama said in a Facebook post.

About 300 people have been injured in the clashes, more than half of them police, according to the authorities. Petrol bombs were thrown into a police station and the interior ministry entrance on Sunday.

The British prime minister has added his voice to the chorus of politicians condemning the violence in France.

A spokesperson for Andy Burnham in Downing Street said “Clearly, the violence we’re seeing on the streets of France is unacceptable. While the power to protest is at the heart of democracy, this can never excuse violence.”

British nationals in France were told by the UK government to “follow local advice from the authorities” if they were concerned about the situation.

Two men self-reported to police after security fence damage at Leipzig airport

German police have said that two men turned themselves in after a security fence was damaged at the airport in the city of Leipzig.

The incident had sparked fears as it took place at the same airport where an attack with an explosives-laden drone happened in August, an incident German authorities attributed to Russia.

Authorities had been investigating how a gaping hole was cut into a fence around the airport. The two men say they lost control of the car and crashed through the fence, then left the scene of the accident and arranged for the car to be towed away. AP reports that German police say the car has been seized and the investigation is continuing.

Swedish centrist leader Thand Ringqvist tasked with leading exploratory talks on new government

As promised, we have an update from Sweden, with the Centre party’s Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist given a chance to lead talks about the new government in the country.

Thand Ringqvist welcomed the opportunity to lead the discussions and said she would look to progress the talks, but made it clear she had no intention of becoming the next prime minister.

She will try to break a deadlock among the four opposition parties that emerged with a theoretical majority in the new parliament after last month’s elections, but continue to disagree on the composition of the future government.

The Centre party has ruled out being in government with the Left, while the Left has demanded a place in government in return for its cooperation. Without an agreement, the opposition bloc would not have a majority in the new parliament.

Thand Ringqvist will have to report back on the progress of her talks – including any exploration of the so-called “middle option” of centrist parties - by the end of the week.

The move by the parliament’s speaker Andreas Norlén comes days after the Social Democrats leader and the September election winner Magdalena Andersson failed in her efforts to unite the opposition in the first attempt.

Outgoing prime minister Ulf Kristerssontold Swedish broadcaster SVT it was the “logical” decision to give the theoretical opposition bloc another go at forming the next administration.

EU condemns 'looting' in France, says right to peaceful protest 'must be protected'

The European Commission has condemned “unacceptable” acts of “arson, looting and attacks on people or public buildings” in France over the weekend, but stressed the right to peaceful protests was “a fundamental right … and must be protected.”

EU internal affairs spokesperson Markus Lammert said:

“The right to peaceful protest is a fundamental right in all democratic countries and must be protected. Those who demonstrate peacefully must not be conflated with those who commit violence. Arson, looting and attacks on people or public buildings are unacceptable.

We support the efforts of the French authorities to protect people, property and public institutions in full respect of fundamental rights.

It is national authorities that are competent for law enforcement matters, and investigations are currently ongoing at national level.”
