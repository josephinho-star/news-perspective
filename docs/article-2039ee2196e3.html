Turkey denies claims SDF-linked foreign fighters will be allowed to enter country

2026-09-03 14:30

Turkey’s Defense Ministry has denied reports that foreign fighters affiliated with the recently dissolved Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) will leave Syria and that some will be allowed to enter Turkey, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported Thursday.

Following a weekly press briefing by Defense Ministry spokesperson Rear Adm. Zeki Aktürk, the ministry said it was closely monitoring the dissolution of the SDF as part of efforts to preserve Syria’s territorial integrity and strengthen the principle of “one state, one army.”

“Claims that foreign elements affiliated with the SDF will leave Syria and that some of them will come to Turkey do not reflect the truth,” the ministry said.

The denial followed reports in Turkish and regional media that as many as 2,000 non-Syrian fighters affiliated with the SDF could leave Syria as part of the group’s integration into the Syrian army.

Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, a London-based Arabic-language news outlet, citing a source familiar with the process, reported last week that about 800 could move to northern Iraq while roughly 1,200 could return to Turkey.

Turkish media subsequently linked the possible returns to legislation adopted as part of Ankara’s ongoing peace process with the outlawed Kurdistan Workers’ Party (PKK), designated as a terrorist organization by Turkey and its Western allies.

The process gained momentum after jailed PKK leader Abdullah Öcalan called on the group in February 2025 to lay down its arms and dissolve itself, followed by steps toward disbandment and legislation setting out a legal framework for the process.

The ministry also reiterated Ankara’s support for Syria’s new authorities, saying Turkey would continue to support efforts to strengthen the country’s security and stability, develop its institutional capacity and promote the peace and prosperity of the Syrian people.

The remarks come little more than a week after SDF commander Mazloum Abdi announced the dissolution of the US-backed force as an independent military organization following the integration of its fighters into the Syrian army.

The SDF, formed in 2015 and dominated by the Kurdish People’s Protection Units (YPG), served for years as Washington’s main local partner in the fight against the Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) in Syria.

Turkey considers the YPG an extension of the PKK.

The SDF’s dissolution followed months of negotiations and intermittent fighting with forces loyal to Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa. An agreement reached in January provided for the integration of the SDF’s military forces and the Kurdish-led civilian administration in northeastern Syria into state institutions.

Abdi said on August 25 that the process of integrating SDF forces into Syrian army brigades had been completed and declared the group dissolved as an independent military force. Three days later, al-Sharaa appointed Abdi as an adviser to the Syrian presidency.

The integration strengthened Damascus’s control over northeastern Syria and ended the separate military and administrative structures that had underpinned Kurdish-led self-rule there.

Turkey had strongly supported the integration process, repeatedly insisting that Syria should have a single central authority and unified military rather than autonomous armed groups.

Turkey has expanded military cooperation with Syria’s new authorities since the ouster of longtime president Bashar al-Assad in December 2024.

The two countries signed a defense cooperation memorandum in August 2025 covering military training, consultancy, weapons systems, military equipment and logistical support.
