Luke Gosling no longer facing breach of bail charge

A charge of breaching bail conditions has been dropped against the federal Labor MP Luke Gosling who still faces two charges of assaulting the Northern Territory administrator, AAP reports.

In the Darwin local court on Monday, the charge was withdrawn by the prosecutor Jo Poole.

No explanation was provided.

Gosling, who holds the federal seat of Solomon, taking in Darwin, was not required to appear.

The 54-year-old was charged in July with assaulting the NT administrator, David Connolly, the jurisdiction’s equivalent to a state governor.

After Gosling was charged with breaching bail on 14 August, he stood aside as special envoy for defence, veterans’ affairs and northern Australia.

The assaults are alleged to have occurred at the NT Thailand Grand festival in Darwin on 18 July.

Connolly, a former cattle station manager nominated to the position by the territory’s Country Liberal party government, was sworn in in February.

Gosling’s lawyer, Marty Aust, told the court in September he would be writing to the prosecutors on the basis there was insufficient evidence to support the assault charges.

The assault matters will return to court on 25 November.

The prominent Sydney-based barrister Sophie Callan SC has taken on the case against Gosling for the NT Director of Public Prosecutions after the Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions declined to take it.

As part of his bail conditions, Gosling is banned from going within 50 metres of the NT’s Government House building.

He must give the administrator’s office 48 hours’ notice before attending any event where the two men would be present.

If they are at an event together, the Labor politician must not be within 15 metres of Connolly.

Budget estimates hears ‘contracted’ costs of pre-Cop event more than $30m

Pre-Cop climate summit events held in Fiji earlier this month cost the Albanese government close to $35m.

The federal government told a budget estimates hearing on Monday that “contracted commitments” for the preparatory meeting held in the lead-up to the Cop31 climate summit came to $34.6m. But the final tally was expected to be lower, partly because some costs were expected to be reimbursed by other delegates.

The bulk of the figure comprised $20m for event management and $8.4m for accommodation and venues.

The climate change, energy, environment and water department and the environment minister, Murray Watt, told the hearing that some costs, including a portion of the accommodation and venue fees, were expected to be reimbursed by other countries.

Canavan rejects idea that Haines attack marks ‘turning point’ in political discourse

The Nationals leader, Matt Canavan, has pushed back against suggestions that the attack on the independent MP Helen Haines marks a “turning point” in Australian politics.

Speaking on ABC’s Afternoon Briefing, Canavan dismissed the suggestion that rhetoric in Canberra has deteriorated, describing parliament as a “release valve” for society.

“I don’t particularly think our political rhetoric is overly different from eras gone past,” he said.

After Andrew Hastie’s call to leave “the nasty personal politics of Maga” behind in the wake of the attack on Haines, Canavan rejected any direct link between political commentary and community violence. He also pushed back against calls for politicians to receive increased security.

Police officers, emergency officers, nurses face probably more constant threats of assault and real physical challenges than we do …

I’m not asking for any kind of special protection here.

Canavan added that he was “a little bit wary” of a proposed parliamentary civility pledge, saying:

If we go too far the other way, that’ll actually increase tensions and reduce social harmony.

Ryan hits out at ‘tone deaf’ Taylor comments after Haines attack

Following parliament’s tributes to Helen Haines, the independent MP Monique Ryan has said that politicians must change their conduct in the wake of the “unthinkable” attack on her crossbench colleague.

Appearing on the ABC’s Afternoon Briefing, Ryan said political leaders should listen to security experts who correlate the tone of political language with rising community aggression.

She criticised the opposition leader Angus Taylor’s speech paying tribute to Haines this morning as “tone deaf” and “hugely disappointing”, particularly in light of remarks made by Haines’s husband earlier in the day.

If Angus Taylor doesn’t think that the words that we use matter, I don’t understand how he aspires to lead a political party …

If we can’t rise to the occasion now after something as unthinkable as what happened to Helen and think about how we behave in the house and in the public space … we’re never going to do that.

First international flight touches down at Western Sydney airport

Western Sydney airport welcomed its first international passengers this morning when Air New Zealand flight NZ165 touched down.

The arrival marked the start of commercial international services at the new airport – Australia’s first major new airport in more than 50 years.

Following a brief turnaround, the return flight (NZ166) departed fully booked. Air New Zealand will operate three return flights weekly on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays.

The prime minister, Anthony Albanese, welcomed the arrival as a milestone for the area:

Western Sydney is now connected directly to the world, bringing more jobs, more visitors and more opportunities for local businesses.

And importantly, it means people in Western Sydney can fly overseas without having to travel across the city first.

Government accused of ignoring lessons from robodebt

An algorithm used by the federal government to assess the level of care older Australians are entitled to is unreasonable, unfair and lacks human oversight, according to a damning investigation by the commonwealth ombudsman.

The investigation also found transparency recommendations from the robodebt royal commission have been ignored.

The investigation followed reporting by Guardian Australia that older people were being left distressed and without adequate care after their home support needs were assessed by an “inhumane” algorithm as part of the government’s mandatory Integrated Assessment Tool (IAT).

Aged care assessors are barred from overriding the IAT’s funding package recommendation, even when the decision is wrong.

Documents released under freedom of information reveal departmental panic, severe clinical distress among frontline assessors, and secret IT workarounds after officials realised in the hours before the rollout of the algorithm that legislation legally barred human override, and that this was causing distress within days of implementation.

The shadow health minister, Anne Ruston,said the report shows: “The algorithm must be scrapped.”

Labor defended this system for months while the Coalition and the crossbench said it was broken. Today the independent umpire has agreed with us.

NSW police minister says gun buyback reflective of ‘very sensible approach’ by government

The NSW police minister, Yasmin Catley, and police commissioner, Mal Lanyon, held a press conference earlier today to detail the state’s gun buyback program. As we reported earlier, eligible licence holders are now able to book an appointment to surrender any excess or reclassified firearms from next Monday, 2 November.

Catley said bookings were essential as the buyback program began, saying people cannot walk into police stations without an appointment. Those who do turn in eligible weapons will receive compensation ranging from $450 to $1,000, depending on the firearm. Small businesses affected by changes to gun laws will also be eligible for grants up to $25,000.

A second phase for high-value firearms will begin next year.

“This has been a very sensible approach by the government,” she told reporters.

A very sensitive way of responding to the worst terrorist attack that we have seen on our streets in this country.

When asked about a draft proposal to allow some to apply for more than 10 firearms in limited circumstances, Catley said people would need “a very good reason”, such as competitive sport shooting.

“If you are at those elite levels, then it may be the case that you do need more than the 10 guns,” she said.

Greens leader expects Steele-John to remain on leave until investigation complete

Greens leader David Shoebridge says he’s “glad” Jordon Steele-John made the decision to stay away from Senate estimates after a week of confusion following the release of a letter accusing him of inappropriate behaviour.

Over the weekend, Steele-John said he would not attend parliament next week after seven members wrote to Shoebridge urging an investigation into claims he had acted inappropriately towards women between 2017 and 2025.

Shortly after the letter’s release to parts of the media, Shoebridge said Steele-John would go on leave while an independent investigation took place. Steele-John publicly rejected the idea that he was on leave and has denied all allegations made against him.

In a press conference before question time this afternoon, Shoebridge said other members of the Greens will ask questions of the departments and agencies involved in the NDIS when they appear later this week. He added he would not go into a “blow-by-blow discussion about the exchanges”.

Asked whether Steele-John would remain on leave until the investigation is complete, Shoebridge responded:

Well, I’ve made it very clear to Senator Steele-John what I believe the community expectations are. If you ask me tomorrow, I’ll say the same. If you ask me the day after it, I’ll say the same. I’ve made it very clear what I believe the community expectations are.
