Mathew Barzal’s return has been an Islanders boost — even if it involves some rare patience

2026-11-21 23:33

DETROIT — Three games into his return from injury, Mathew Barzal is, perhaps with a veneer of caution, feeling good.

His minutes, as you’d expect being on the third line, are down over four minutes per night, but he’s produced a point per night with good underlying numbers, albeit over a small sample. It is, in short, as good as the Islanders might have expected after Barzal missed the season’s first 18 games with lingering knee issues from an injury suffered two seasons ago.

“Not bad,” Barzal told The Post before the Islanders faced Detroit on Saturday. “It’s gonna take a second just to get my legs and cardio back. Just timing and everything. I think I’ve been fairly solid. Feel pretty comfortable out there. Especially the Columbus game. I feel pretty similar to last year.”

That is an interesting coda, as Barzal said upon his return that he felt at 80 percent for much of last season, and was managing his knee extensively in between games. Much like it has so far this year, though, that didn’t seem to impact his production, as Barzal had 72 points in 81 games and averaged the most ice time of his career.

While the Islanders will almost certainly look to boost Barzal’s workload at some point soon if everything keeps going well, they are in the luxurious situation of not needing to rush. Their top two lines are humming along well, and they have enough depth down the middle that the matchup advantage created by Barzal being on the third line is something coach Pete DeBoer has been happy to have in his pocket.

“I think he’s getting better every night,” DeBoer said. “I like his mindset. He’s playing a mature game for us as far as with the puck and not trying to push too much, which is the natural instinct when you come back. You want to make a difference. He’s a difference-making guy. But he’s picking his spots. He’s got good patience in his game.”

Patience hasn’t always been Barzal’s M.O., but — whether related to his knee or not — he does seem to be playing a more concise game. His average shift length, for example, is down by five seconds compared to last season, though that could be affected both by a small sample and by DeBoer’s bench management, which has seen drops in that statistic throughout the lineup.

Perhaps the biggest difference with Barzal back in the fold is simply the added depth it gives the Islanders lineup, and the subsequent confidence that seems to give everybody. Being able to slot Jean-Gabriel Pageau on the fourth line, and send Calum Ritchie to AHL Hamilton for a de facto rehab assignment — Ritchie featured in a 2-0 loss to Belleville on Friday, notching five shots on goal — are things most teams in the NHL simply couldn’t do.

So too is letting Barzal return on the third line, with fewer minutes, as opposed to tossing him right back into a heavy workload.

“It’s been awesome,” captain Bo Horvat told The Post. “Obviously a special player. Drives offense, he’s creating plays. He should have a couple goals by now. He’s creating opportunities, so he’s just gonna continue to get more comfortable.”

That process, Horvat said, is “gonna take time,” even as Barzal’s baseline continues to be positive.

“I think he’s the type of player that, he’s been off for that long and he still looks great out there,” Horvat said. “He still has his legs, he’s still making plays, still has that confidence in his game and in his abilities. I’ve already noticed it in three games.”
