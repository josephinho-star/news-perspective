Victorian Greens seek urgent injunction over how-to-vote cards mimicking party’s branding but preferencing it last

2026-11-21 00:26

The Victorian Greens are seeking an urgent injunction from the supreme court to stop an independent candidate distributing how-to-vote cards that allegedly mimic the party’s branding and urge people to preference it last.

As prepoll opened on Wednesday morning, Tosh-Jake Finnigan’s campaign team handed out how-to-vote cards that used the minor party’s colour scheme, font and triangle logo with the banner “Vote 1 Green Values”.

The card instructed people to vote in a way that would ultimately benefit the Liberal party. It asked voters to give their first preference to Finnigan followed by an Animal Justice party candidate, another independent candidate and then the Liberals in fourth place.

The Greens are determined to win back the ultra-marginal seat it lost to the Liberals at a February 2025 byelection with a 1.3% margin. Labor is running an open ticket in Prahran, which the Greens fear could deprive it of the preference votes it needs.

On Wednesday afternoon, the Greens state director, Martin Shield, requested Finnigan immediately stop distributing the material and threatened to take legal action if an undertaking was not given to do so in less than two hours.

Finnigan agreed to the request and produced an amended how-to-vote card within hours that included different colours with a revised banner that said “Vote 1 Trans Greens Values”.

“Due to legal threats from the Greens party, I confirm that I am not an endorsed Greens party candidate,” a disclaimer on Finnigan’s revised how-to-vote card said.

But the changes did not satisfy the Greens. On Friday afternoon, the party’s lawyers lodged papers in the supreme court.

At an initial hearing on Friday night, the Greens’ lawyer, Adam McBeth, alleged people handing out Finnigan’s material at prepoll wore shirts with the exact shade of green as worn by genuine Greens party volunteers.

McBeth alleged Finnigan’s team stood next to Greens party volunteers and Greens placards. The party argues voters at prepoll were deliberately misled.

“There is no other reason for an independent candidate who has an infinite range of colours and styles to choose from to so closely mimic the Greens, including the way people paid to hand out were dressed and where they were directed to stand,” McBeth said.

Finnigan, who was self-represented, said they had gone above and beyond to quickly make changes to their initial how-to-vote card and accused the Greens of using “legal manoeuvres” to stop them campaigning.

“It is my belief that they don’t want me to hand out on Saturday, which will be one of the busiest days of prepoll,” Finnigan said.

Finnigan told the court that they did not have enough money to print new cards. They said their campaign team had not been instructed to stand next to the Greens at prepoll.

“I am someone who holds Greens values, of course I am going to have some kind of green shirt,” Finnigan said.

“The honest reason I went for that colour of green is because the colour is called Irish green and I am of Irish background. That’s why I went for it. I just like the sound of Irish green.”

On Friday evening, after the supreme court action was lodged, the Victorian Election Commission (VEC) accepted Finnigan’s subsequent how-to-vote card and published it online.

Justice John Watson said the first how-to-vote card “looked like a pretty deliberate attempt to pass yourself off as a Greens candidate”, but he adjourned the matter until 3pm on Saturday to hear testimony from the VEC.

Finnigan became Victoria’s first openly transgender councillor in 2023 when they were elected to the Colac Otway shire council after a countback. They resigned from the council in October 2024.

The former Labor field campaigner founded the Restore Democracy Sack Dan Andrews party in 2022. They unsuccessfully contested an upper house seat for the party, securing 1% of first preference votes.

After working in the offices of Labor ministers during the 2014 state campaign, Finnigan became a whistleblower in the so-called Red Shirts scandal, which saw taxpayer funds used to pay electorate officers campaigning for Labor.

Finnigan’s campaign Facebook page carrying an authorisation message describes them as a “Green Independent” and contains attack ads against the minor party.
