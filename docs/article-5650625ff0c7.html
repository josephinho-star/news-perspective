Pope Leo warns against AI and says technology ‘must remain at service of humans’ during first visit to France – Europe live

2026-09-25 14:49

Technology 'must remain at service of humans,' and not become instrument of 'domination and injustice,' Pope Leo warns

In his deliberations on what it means to be human, Pope Leo says “we are all born naked and in need; poor by birth, in need of bread, and words, and words of care and affection.”

Only through becoming adult, “we risk forgetting it,” with people’s “humanity betrayed or cast aside, exploited or deceived.”

“Life is no longer understood as a gift, but as a commodity. It is no longer viewed as a summons to care and responsibility, but as a product to be exploited.”

He “to be human is to be free by nature,” and to “recognise [life’s] grandeur” and “immense potential.”

But he says “to be human … implies a responsibility before history,” as “the human being is one who grows, learns, and works with both hands and mind.”

He then talks about the tragedy of the second world war, when “humanity had come to realise that scientific progress, when severed from wisdom, can turn into a weapon of destruction.”

He says 80 years later, the founding charter of Unesco – built on that recognition – still stands.

Turning to the issue of technology, he says “our efforts in this domain must ensure that new technologies remain at the service of the human person, rather than becoming yet another instrument of domination and injustice.”

In a strong passage, he warns:

“Over the centuries, many tasks once performed by human beings have been entrusted to machines whose capabilities and applications have continued to expand. More recently, certain artificial systems have even been given the name of our human faculty of thought intelligence.

At the same time, however, our ideas and relationships risk being impoverished by a process of dehumanisation.

The paradox has become increasingly evident.

While intelligence is attributed to computational systems, we struggle to recognise the inalienable dignity of human persons whose lives are judged according to the criterion of efficiency and, when deemed no longer productive, the rejected and discarded.”

Pope Leo ends with a call for action to help Unesco and other similar bodies “in the realisation that every people has its own precious part to play in the pursuit of that authentic human progress which peace alone can guarantee.”

“This is not to embrace a naive optimism that ignores difficulties, but rather to persevere in the hope that the desired good is always attainable.”

And he ends on that note.

He will be at the Notre Dame Cathedral later this afternoon, before joining the French youth at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis of Paris.

International bodies face 'crisis of effectivness' as some countries see them as obstacle, 'charade'

Pope Leo also offers a comment on the ongoing discussion about the crisis of the UN and other similar international organisations as some countries see dialogue “an obstacle to action” and a “charade” and not a genuine attempt to resolve differences peacefully.

“Our world regards international institutions with mixed feelings.

On the one hand, these institutions are expected to respond to challenges that no individual state can face alone.

On the other hand, these same institutions are experiencing a crisis of effectiveness, especially in situations where dialogue is seen as an obstacle to action rather than a resource for peace, a charade rather than an opportunity to seek the common good together.

In this case, we see the urgent need to promote a virtue that seems as greatly desired as it is really found, namely, the virtue of trust.

Trust that differences do not necessarily constitute a threat. Trust that honest dialogue can bring about a greater good than what individuals could achieve on their own.”

Leaders in power treat law instumentally and justice as 'a commodity to be bought and sold,' Pope Leo warns

Pope Leo warns that “without truth, language becomes subject to the logic of violence and to the arbitrary will of the powerful.”

In what will be seen as a criticism of world leaders that disrespect the rule of law – you can think of some names here, I guess - he says:

“Instead of respecting the law, those in power invoke it when it serves their interests and disregard it when it restrains them, treating even justice itself as though it were a commodity to be bought and sold.”

“If we realise that the corruption of the law begins with the corruption of the heart, we can recognise how every science risks losing its proper meaning when pursued without ethics, and thus ceasing to serve and reflect human dignity.”

AI, rapid technological advancement risks 'hindering dialogue rather than fostering it,' Pope Leo warns

Pope Leo also warns against the pursuit of power without limits, as – using the analogy to the Tower of Babel – he says:

“The more power seeks to oppress, the more it weakens itself, exposing its inhumane face. We see this in the suffering and injustice caused by despotism, as well as in forms of exploitation that damage both society and the environment.

As history teaches us, many empires have revelled in their power before being consumed by it and collapsing like a tower…. The presumption of making oneself like God and subjecting peoples to a single yoke leads to a confusion of languages; the failure to understand one another instead of reaching heaven as it presumed to do.”

He warns that “today, the Temptation of Babel reappears in a globalisation pursued without fraternity and rapacious forms of neocolonialism in ideological impositions”

He says that technological progress meant that “communication has become much faster, but not necessarily more truthful” and “information is abundant, but that is not synonymous with wisdom.”

“Without a shared ethical foundation,the many channels of communication risk multiplying false information and giving currency to words devoid of meaning, thereby hindering dialogue rather than fostering it.

He then turns to English, saying the pace of changes globally pushes us to question “what does it mean to be human.”

“Every culture is challenged by this question, which lies at the heart of every form of knowledge, every art and every religion. Even in the 21st century, after all, we cannot take for granted that we know who the human person is or what it means to live as a human being.”
