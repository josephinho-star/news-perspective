Marilyn Monroe's 1950s stuffing recipe fetched $16,000 at auction — and holds clue to Joe DiMaggio marriage

2026-11-23 11:00

A Thanksgiving recipe of Marilyn Monroe's was just as fabulous as she was and offers a glimpse into a lesser-known side of the Hollywood star.

Her handwritten stuffing recipe is a far cry from the boxed bread-and-herb side many families serve on Thanksgiving, loaded with 18 ingredients including sourdough, ground beef, poultry livers or hearts, hard-boiled eggs, raisins, Parmesan and nuts.

Written in pencil on both sides of a sheet of City Title Insurance Company stationery, the recipe was cataloged by Julien's Auctions as circa 1954. It sold for $16,000 at the auction house's June 2026 sale, more than five times its estimate.

The recipe is attributed to Monroe because it is written entirely in her hand, according to Julien's Auctions, and came from the estate of her acting coach, Lee Strasberg, who inherited her personal effects.

"Something as simple as a handwritten recipe helps humanize Marilyn and reminds us that behind one of the most recognizable faces in Hollywood history was a woman who enjoyed many of the same ordinary pleasures as everyone else," Scott Fortner, a lifelong Monroe scholar and collector who is often consulted on authenticating her personal effects, told Fox News Digital.

"I think it offers a wonderful glimpse into another side of Marilyn Monroe, beyond the glamorous movie star and Hollywood sex symbol," he added, noting that she had "a genuine interest in cooking and domestic life."

She reportedly enjoyed cooking and entertaining, especially during her later marriage to playwright Arthur Miller when the couple had a farm in Connecticut.

"Her personal library also included several cookbooks, some with markings and annotations," Fortner said.

The San Francisco address on the stationery and the note "No garlic" have fueled speculation about a connection to baseball legend Joe DiMaggio, who reportedly disliked garlic and with whom Monroe lived in San Francisco after their 1954 marriage.

Fortner said it is possible she made the stuffing for the former Yankees star, but there is no documented evidence of that.

He said the San Francisco stationery makes it reasonable to think Monroe wrote the recipe during her relationship with Joe DiMaggio, whom she began dating in 1952 and married in January 1954, but the timing remains unclear.

The couple separated in October, so if Monroe wrote it later that year, they would have been apart by Thanksgiving, Fortner noted.

The sourdough, he added, could reflect San Francisco's food culture, but it is also a common stuffing ingredient.

"Just because the recipe was written on stationery from a San Francisco business doesn't necessarily mean it was written while Marilyn was in San Francisco, or even during her relationship with Joe," Fortner told Fox News Digital. "Without knowing exactly when she wrote the recipe, we can only speculate about its connection to Joe or whether she ever prepared the dish for him."

Regardless, the relic reminds us that Monroe "valued her personal relationships and had a genuine interest in the everyday experiences of life outside the movie studio," Fortner said.

For home cooks hoping to bring a bit of Monroe's kitchen to their own Thanksgiving table, The New York Times published a modern adaptation of the recipe that could feed a crowd.

It yields nearly 20 cups and follows Monroe's handwritten instructions to use sourdough soaked in cold water instead of broth. Her note suggests the stuffing for chicken, turkey or even pheasant.

Below is an adaptation of Monroe's recipe.

- 1 (10-ounce) loaf sourdough bread

- ½ pound chicken or turkey livers or hearts

- 1 tablespoon cooking oil

- 4 celery stalks, chopped

- 2 hard-boiled eggs, chopped

- 1 ¼ cups chopped walnuts, pine nuts or roasted chestnuts, or a mix

- 2 teaspoons each dried rosemary, oregano and thyme

- 1 tablespoon salt-free, garlic-free poultry seasoning

- 1 tablespoon kosher salt, plus more to taste

- 1 tablespoon black pepper

If you don't have poultry seasoning, The New York Times version suggests substituting 1 teaspoon each dried sage and marjoram, plus ½ teaspoon each ground ginger and nutmeg.

1. Tear the bread into pieces and soak it in a large bowl of cold water for 15 minutes. Drain and squeeze out the excess water, then shred the bread into smaller pieces.

2. Boil the livers or hearts in salted water for 8 minutes. Drain and chop finely.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it into small pieces, until browned and cooked through.

4. In a large bowl, combine the bread, livers or hearts, beef, celery, onion, parsley, eggs, raisins, cheese and nuts. Mix the rosemary, oregano, thyme, bay leaves, poultry seasoning, salt and pepper together, then stir them into the stuffing.

5. To serve as dressing, divide the mixture among shallow baking dishes, using about 2 quarts per 9-inch-square dish. Bake at 350 degrees Fahrenheit for about 1 hour, until browned and a food thermometer inserted in the center registers 165 degrees Fahrenheit.
