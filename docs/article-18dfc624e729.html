Turkish court orders Manifest’s manager released in obscenity case

2026-11-12 13:56

A Turkish court on Thursday ordered the release under judicial supervision of Tolga Akış, the founder and manager of the popular all-female pop group Manifest, at the first hearing of his trial on an obscenity-related charge, the DHA news agency reported.

Akış, who has been jailed since August, will remain subject to a foreign travel ban while his trial continues. Prosecutors are seeking between six months and three years in prison for facilitating the publication of allegedly obscene material.

The İstanbul 43rd Criminal Court of First Instance adjourned the proceedings until March 18, 2027.

Manifest, a six-member group formed through the “Big5 Türkiye” talent competition in 2025, has gained a large young following with its blend of Turkish pop and choreographed dance performances. The group has also sought to reach international audiences, releasing its first English-language single and performing at London’s Wembley Arena in October.

Formed through the “Big5 Türkiye” talent competition in 2025, the six-member group combines Turkish pop with tightly choreographed dance performances. Its rise has brought both chart success and scrutiny from authorities over its costumes and stage routines.

Akış was detained at İstanbul Airport in August during an investigation into allegations of obscenity, drug use and money laundering. A judge ordered him jailed on August 28 specifically on the charge of facilitating the publication of obscene material.

His detention followed allegations published online by photographer Mesut Adlin, who had previously worked with the group. Akış denied any wrongdoing, saying messages had been taken out of context, while his lawyer filed a complaint alleging threats and blackmail.

At Thursday’s hearing Akış denied that the concert performances were criminal and challenged his responsibility for footage circulated online.

“I have spent 77 days in prison for an offense I believe I did not commit,” he told the court, according to DHA.

He said the concert at issue admitted adults only because of the venue’s rules. Footage had been shared by third parties rather than through the group’s official channels, he said, adding that complaints had been filed to prevent its circulation.

Prosecutors argue that his role went beyond managing the group’s business affairs. The indictment alleges that Akış helped determine the content and commercial strategy of its performances and enabled material prosecutors considered obscene to reach audiences without age restrictions.

He has denied directing the dance routines.

Manifest’s members had already faced a separate prosecution over their September 6, 2025, concert at İstanbul’s Küçükçiftlik Park.

Prosecutors opened that investigation over allegations of obscene acts and public indecency. The performers denied the accusations, saying their choreography met international standards, their costumes were ordinary stage outfits and that the event was ticketed for adults only.

The performers were initially put under judicial supervision, including a foreign travel ban. A separate court blocked social media posts showing concert footage, citing national security and public order.

In December 2025 an İstanbul court gave the six members and singer Ayça Dalaklı suspended prison sentences of more than three months for “exhibitionism.” It also lifted their travel restrictions. The case sparked criticism over the use of broadly worded morality laws to restrict artistic expression.

Manifest has scored major streaming hits in Turkey. “Hileli,” its collaboration with veteran pop singer Ajda Pekkan, was the country’s most-streamed song on Spotify in summer 2026, according to Rolling Stone UK. Another Manifest single, “Toz Pembe,” ranked third.

Rolling Stone described an enthusiastic young audience at the group’s İzmir festival and reported that its members spent much of October in London while pursuing international projects. Manifest released its first English-language single, “Tralala,” on October 9.

Plans for a London base had prompted reports in August that the group was leaving Turkey. Akış initially announced that its shared İstanbul home would close and a new one would open in London, but later clarified that the members were not moving abroad permanently.

He said they were establishing individual homes in İstanbul and would use London for international work and collaborations. The group subsequently performed at London’s OVO Arena Wembley on October 16, a show its representatives described as sold out in a statement published by the Record of the Day music industry outlet.
