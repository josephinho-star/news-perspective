Armed intruder lunges at Ohio gubernatorial candidate Amy Acton during event, injuring multiple people

2026-09-07 00:47

An armed man lunged at Ohio’s Democratic gubernatorial nominee, Dr. Amy Acton, at a local fair on Sunday — injuring two people in the chaos before he was quickly arrested, according to authorities.

The intruder, identified by local outlets as Patrick Havas, “lunged” toward Acton while she was visiting the annual Canfield Fair in Mahoning County, according to Addie Bullock, a Communications Director for the Acton campaign.

“Dr. Acton and Eric are grateful for the swift and decisive action of law enforcement and are praying for the recovery of those injured. This kind of violence has no place in Ohio. Dr. Acton will always stand up to the chaos, hate, and vitriol that pits us against one another,” Bullock said in a statement.

Havas, 38, busted into the Mahoning County Democratic Party tent and pushed his way to the front, knocking multiple volunteers to the ground in the process, Mahoning County Democratic Party Chair Chris Anderson wrote in a statement on social media.

Mahoning County Sheriff Jerry Greene told 21 News that the Havas — who was holding a phone camera and carrying two firearms and brass knuckles — pushed down two older adults in the pandemonium.

Members of Acton’s security team, comprised of Ohio State Highway Patrol troopers, apprehended Havas before he could take out either of the guns he was carrying. Canfield Fair Police then assisted and took him into custody, according to the outlet.

“Let me be clear – The Mahoning County Democratic Party abhors political violence in all forms. What happened at our tent today during today’s visit from Dr. Acton is reprehensible and should be condemned by leaders on all sides of the aisle,” Anderson said.

“MCDP is grateful for the quick response by our local law enforcement officials as well as the Ohio State Highway Patrol providing Dr. Acton security,” the Chair added.

Inmate records show Havas, a large, bearded bald man, has been charged with two counts of assault and one count of disorderly conduct.

Acton, a physician running in the state’s governor’s race against Republican Vivek Ramaswamy, was not hurt in the incident and remained on site to attend to one of the injured, NBC News reported.

“Candidates should be able to meet with voters without having to worry about threats or violence. What happened today is completely unacceptable and has no place in politics, and we hope that no one was hurt,” Connie Luck, the Communications Director for Vivek for Ohio, said in a statement to 21 News.

An investigation into the incident remains ongoing.
