Turkish finance minister authorizes probe into former banking regulator over fund scandal

2026-11-26 09:17

Treasury and Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek has authorized an investigation into Şahap Kavcıoğlu, the former head of Turkey’s banking regulator, over a multimillion-dollar investment fund scandal, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported Thursday.

Şimşek approved the investigation after a preliminary review by the ministry’s inspection board.

Kavcıoğlu, who stepped down this month as head of the Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK), faces allegations of bribery and abuse of office and was already subject to a travel ban. The investigation required ministry approval under Turkish law.

Kavcıoğlu said he resigned to avoid causing further harm to the agency.

Prosecutors are investigating allegations that investment funds were used to drive up the prices of certain shares, allowing investors with advance knowledge of the scheme to make substantial profits.

The scandal, which erupted in September, has caused heavy losses for nearly half a million investors and increased pressure on President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and his ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP).

The investigation has implicated hundreds of suspects, including two former AKP deputy chairs, a deputy finance minister, two former heads of the Capital Markets Board (SPK) and the general manager of Turkey’s stock exchange.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek has described the alleged scheme as the work of an “extensive criminal network” involving market manipulation, bribery and senior financial officials.

Hayati Yazıcı, a former deputy prime minister and trade minister, resigned as an AKP deputy chair on Tuesday after allegations surfaced linking his son to the scandal.

He was the second senior AKP figure to step down, after former cabinet minister Fatma Betül Sayan Kaya. She was released from jail and placed under house arrest on Tuesday after nearly three weeks in detention.

Prosecutors have frozen the assets of 108 people suspected of making more than $2 million each from the alleged scheme, according to a legal document cited by an opposition lawmaker.
