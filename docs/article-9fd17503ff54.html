Georgia mom missing after Nepal-Tibet floods last texted family how ‘grateful’ she was before disaster struck

2026-09-01 04:43

A Georgia mother who was about to return to the US from a bucket-list spiritual pilgrimage vanished after deadly floods ripped through the Nepal-Tibet border on Wednesday, according to her family.

Mary Sorenson, 60, who lives just north of Atlanta, traveled to the region for a spiritual pilgrimage to Mount Kailash to practice yoga, pray, and meditate with a group, her son, Riley Wildeman, told ABC News.

Sorenson completed the pilgrimage she had long hoped to cross off her bucket list before she went missing during the devastating floods. The death toll in the disaster has since climbed past 900 people.

The adventurous and spiritual mother was beginning her descent down the mountain to return to Atlanta when her family last heard from her, the outlet reported.

“My brother likes to say that she walks to the beat of kind of her own drum,” Wildeman told the outlet. “She’s so able to find good in everything and find good in everyone and to enjoy every moment.”

“The last thing that she sent to us was Tuesday morning, and she was on her way down the mountain,” Wildeman said.

Sorenson last told her family that she was looking forward to her long trip back to the US to reflect on her pilgrimage and, chillingly, ended a text letting them know she was “so grateful” for them.

“She ended that text by saying, ‘I’m so grateful that you guys are my family,'” he recalled.

The catastrophic floods struck the Nepal-Tibet border region on Wednesday morning, after Sorenson last contacted her family.

Rescue teams have been working tirelessly around the clock at the border to search for a total of more than 4,700 missing people in the muddy ground, Nepal’s disaster agency said Monday.

Of the missing, 592 are foreigners, including at least 85 Americans, officials said.

Long Island, New York locals Amita Amin, 67, and Jitendra Manubhai Patel, 63, along with New Jersey resident Tejal Vyomesh Patel, 64, were also taking part in a pilgrimage to Mount Kailash when they vanished in the disaster.

Wildeman, who is a public school teacher, revealed that his father called him at work after the organizer of Sorensen’s trip contacted the family with the harrowing news.

“There was really very little about where they were, about where the flood had hit, about how severe the flood was,” he said. “And then all the news coverage and all the videos and all of the social media popped up, and so that’s how we kind of understood the severity of the situation.”

The terrified family has reached out to the US State Department and government agencies in Nepal and China in hopes of finally reaching Sorenson.

“We’ve called in every favor and every possible thing that we can call in,” Wildeman said.

“We feel so much heartbreak and so much sadness and pain, like a deep, guttural pain,” he said, noting his family feels “the grief of the situation, not the grief of the loss.”

“My whole family and my mom all believe in a God that does miracles, and that the miracle that we’re gonna get out of this is gonna be something that we might not necessarily understand right now, but it’s gonna be something that we look back on and we feel.”
