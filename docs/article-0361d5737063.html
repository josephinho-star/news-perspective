Trump administration deported US citizen to Mexico, DoJ admits in court

2026-11-23 11:00

The Trump administration recently admitted in federal court that it deported a US citizen to Mexico after American authorities incorrectly concluded he was an undocumented immigrant.

Attorneys for the US justice department made that admission on Friday while requesting the dismissal of a lawsuit for damages that the deported man – Brian José Morales García of Austin, Texas – has been pursuing against the federal government.

Morales García’s case took that turn as the White House has faced bipartisan criticism for the aggressive mass deportation campaign pursued by Donald Trump throughout his second presidency – widely seen as a factor in the electoral battering his Republican party suffered during the 3 November midterms, costing it control of Congress.

The Denver-born, Mexico-raised Morales García was riding with his boss to his job as an air-conditioning installation technician on 3 April when they were pulled over by state and local officers near Fredericksburg, Texas.

Morales García was his boss’s passenger and did not have his identification on him. The 25-year-old also told the officers that he had his birth certificate and social security card at home, but they didn’t believe him – so they called immigration agents, who detained Morales García.

Court filings recount how Robert Gil Jr, a US Customs and Border Protection agent, consulted records from Mexico that listed a Mexican birthplace for Morales García. Gil Jr also accused Morales García of saying he was born in Mexico and observed that he only spoke in Spanish, according to court records.

Morales García was deported on 7 April, with the Trump administration later accusing him in a media statement of being in the country illegally.

Morales García’s attorneys sued on 1 May, maintaining that he had falsely said he entered the US illegally and signed papers for a rapid deportation in response to threats of imprisonment by agents. His lawsuit, which seeks an unspecified amount of money, also contended that family and friends tried to deliver documents proving his US citizenship before this deportation.

But those efforts were unduly rebuffed, Morales García’s lawsuit said.

The Trump administration denied Morales García’s assertions, saying on Friday that he went to Mexico voluntarily and therefore lacked any basis to sue the federal government for damages. Nonetheless, in court filings on Friday, the administration conceded that Morales García “was permitted entry” back into the US on 30 August after it reviewed “information concerning plaintiff’s place of birth”.

“Plaintiff is a dual citizen of the United States and Mexico,” Justin Simmons, the US attorney for the western district of Texas, wrote in a court filing first reported by the Texas Tribune.

Americans are not required to carry proof of citizenship when going about their daily business. Lawyers across the US have seen cases such as Morales García’s, which begin after an encounter with local authorities demonstrate how those officials are generally not aware of the nuances of immigration policy.

Texas’s public safety department is among many US state and local agencies that collaborate with federal immigration authorities under their 287(g) program.

The judge presiding over Morales García’s lawsuit, Robert Lee Pitman, did not immediately rule on the Trump administration’s request on Friday to dismiss the case. The lawsuit also named unspecified local and state Texas law enforcement officers as defendants.

An October 2025 report by ProPublica found that immigration agents had detained more than 170 US citizens during the first nine months of Trump’s second presidency.

Meanwhile, the media statement incorrectly saying Morales García was in the US illegally is among several related to immigration enforcement under the Trump administration that has unraveled under scrutiny.

A Guardian analysis published in July reviewed Trump administration press releases on 26 cases of cars allegedly being used to assault immigration enforcement agents – and nearly all were either disproven or dropped.
