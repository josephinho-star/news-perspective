Chilling moment killer husband prays for wife’s survival after claiming he mistook her for an intruder

2026-11-27 15:20

An Arizona husband who shot and killed his ballerina wife was seen praying for her survival while claiming he mistook her for an intruder, newly released bodycam footage shows.

The footage of Christopher Hoopes, 41, being arrested for killing his wife, Colleen, was released after he was allowed to plead down his second-degree murder charge to negligent homicide, according to AZFamily.

“I shot my wife three times,” he had told a 911 dispatcher, according to audio that also caught him crying, “Oh my God, Colleen, please!”

The killer hubby was then seen coming out of the family’s home in Tempe in the early hours of May 20, 2022, with his hands up and covered in blood while naked apart from his boxer shorts.

“I fired at my wife,” the calm-sounding husband tells officers as EMTs desperately try, but fail, to save his wife inside their home.

Hoopes admitted that his wife — a professional dancer — was in bed beside him when he shot her, while claiming that he somehow mistook her for an intruder.

“Were you still dreaming?” a confused-sounding officer asks him. “Were you fully aware of what was going on?” an officer asks him.

“Can I call my priest?” the husband asks as he is led, cuffed and still half-naked, to a squad car, where he is heard asking if “she is alive.”

“Oh my God — Lord, please save her,” Hoopes is heard crying in the squad car.

In the footage, he asks police at least four times to call his parents or father, a lawyer, the same number of times he asks to call a priest.

The ballet dancer died, with one bullet entering the base of her neck and exiting through her upper back.

Investigators also reviewed text messages and smartwatch data, speaking with people who knew the couple and detailing concerns about controlling behavior, according to the police report.

A fellow dancer told police Colleen claimed her husband had slapped her during an argument about a year before her death, according to the report.

Hoopes was originally charged with second-degree murder, which carried up to 25 years behind bars.

He pleaded guilty to a lesser charge of negligent homicide after cutting a deal with the Maricopa County Attorney’s Office on Nov. 17.

That charge carries a minimum sentence of four years and a maximum of eight, the court records state.
