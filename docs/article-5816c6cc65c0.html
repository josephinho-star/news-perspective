Lefty judge who freed illegal migrant before Chinatown slay made now-chilling comment at hearing

2026-11-24 19:42

The lefty judge who freed an illegal-migrant career criminal the day before he allegedly killed a Chinatown woman said in a now-chilling throwaway comment at his hearing that she didn’t want him “to fall through the cracks.”

Manhattan Criminal Court Judge Juliet Howard — a former Housing Court jurist and ex-Legal Aid Society lawyer — apparently bought the sob story being peddled in court Thursday by Alexander Florentino’s lawyer that the then-theft suspect was “without any support, and he’s struggling.”

Never mind prosecutors noted to Howard that Florentino had five open criminal cases against him and repeatedly skipped out on other court dates — while law-enforcement sources say he was racking up 39 busts since just 2025 and wanted by federal immigration officials.

Prosecutors asked the judge to hold the illegal Honduran immigrant even on just $3,000 cash bail or a $9,000 bond.

Manhattan Assistant District Attorney Benson Clements argued for bail despite the fact that the charges against Florentino in this case — petty larceny and criminal possession of stolen property over the theft of an Amazon package — did not qualify for bail under the controversial statutes.

Clements tried to invoke a stipulation in the law known as “harm on harm,” which allows bail set for defendants who already have pending felony or A misdemeanor cases and get arrested again while causing harm to a person or property, which was applicable.

The ADA listed several other cases against Florentino, including for arson, when he allegedly lit a trash can on fire outside family court, and the theft of jeans and a dress from a store.

“Now the defendant is back before the Court for stealing Amazon packages and riding off on a Citi Bike,‘’ the ADA said.

“Based on the defendant’s continued pattern of criminal conduct and recent failures to appear in court, the People believe that monetary bail in the aforementioned amounts is reasonably necessary to assure the defendant’s return to court.”

But public defender Margaret Bergmann argued for the judge to instead free Florentino with supervision at a live-in mental-health and substance abuse clinic in Harlem while he awaited trial.

Bergmann downplayed Florentino’s alleged latest theft, claiming Amazon won’t feel the loss because it is a “billion dollar corporation.

“To be specific, in this case it is alleged that a bag was taken and recovered,” she told the judge. “In addition, it is alleged that this bag was an Amazon bag.

“Amazon is a billion-dollar corporation, so it’s my position that if your honor disagrees with me that there was harm, that any harm is negligible.

“So, I would ask your honor to give Mr. Florentino a chance to get the help that he so desperately needs,” Bergmann added. “These are issues that spur from his being unhoused and without any support.”

Howard apparently bought into the defense’s argument, freeing Florentino, who ended up allegedly killing a 62-year-old woman during a home burglary the next day.

During the proceeding, the judge had at least acknowledged that “a level of supervision is required to ensure that Mr. Florentino continues to come to court.”

When the defense lawyer assured the judge that her camp would be in touch with the treatment center to make sure Florentino complied with classes and stayed there, the judge signed off on the no-bail deal.

“I just want to make sure he’s not going to fall through the cracks,‘’ the jurist said. “So as long as that’s part of whatever I’m going to sign off on, I’ll sign off on that.”

She then told the alleged heinous suspect, “I want to make sure you understand that it’s very important that you go to this facility because the next time you come back to court I really want to hear that — you know, we’re expecting you to go to this facility.

Florentino replied, “OK.‘’

It’s unclear if Florentino ever checked into the facility, much less got any treatment before Friday afternoon — when he allegedly burglarized the home of 62-year-old Jin Yu, strangled her to death with a cord and sexually molested her before smashing her roommate in the head with a beer bottle and leading cops on a wild foot chase.

Only after he was caught was he finally locked up.
