Elderly Alzheimer-stricken pianist crosses 2 states searching for childhood home — and ends up on random Philly street with a piano in the yard

2026-11-25 03:18

An elderly Maryland pianist with Alzheimer’s wandered off to try to find his childhood home in Pennsylvania — and ended up driving to a random Philly street where a good Samaritan stepped in to save him.

Frank Grow, 78, who has battled the dreaded disease for six years, vanished from his home in Bowie on Thursday in search of the house where he was raised in Norristown, Pennsylvania, according to NBC Philadelphia.

But Grow ended up driving miles away in Northeast Philadelphia, when Angelo Torres spotted the lost musician wandering near an intersection.

“I noticed a gentleman standing on the corner,” Torres told NBC10.

“So I pulled up to him and asked, ‘Sir, are you okay?’ And he replied, ‘No, I’m not okay.’”

Torres quickly realized something was wrong and searched Grow’s vehicle for anything that would help ID the man.

“I started checking the car and he didn’t have any identification,” Torres recalled.

“I couldn’t find nothing. The last thing I did was look in the trunk and there was an invoice from the Nissan dealership and it had a phone number on it,” he explained.

By a stroke of luck, the phone number listed belonged to Grow’s daughter, Maggie Henkin — and Torres was able to reunite the old man with his family.

Meanwhile, while waiting for his daughter to arrive, Grow spent time playing tunes on a piano that Torres happened to have in his front yard.

“Angelo happens to have a piano in his front yard. My dad is a musician. He’s had Alzheimer’s for six years now,” Henkin told NBC.

“But it’s something he does all day. He still remembers how to.”

The piano was put outside of Torres’ home by neighbors who were looking to get rid of the instrument.

Grow’s family said they were relieved that he was safe and sound and now back in Maryland playing the piano again.

“I’m just so grateful and thankful. It was truly a miracle and I’m so grateful for Angelo and there are still good people out there,” Henkin said.

Torres believes the wild series of events was a manifestation of his own late father.

“All week I was thinking about my dad — you know, my dad was good to me — and then all of a sudden he shows up and I’m thinking my dad came in this form,” Torres said.
