Duffy, Mullin call for $50B air travel ‘overhaul’ in fiery ground stop briefing

2026-11-14 03:37

Trump administration officials reupped their calls for a multi-billion-dollar investment into updating the U.S.’s “incredibly old” air traffic control systems, calling out Congress for “[kicking] the can down the road” on improvements they argued could have prevented Friday night’s nationwide ground stop.

“What we have is an incredibly old air traffic control system,” Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said during the Friday night briefing after the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) lifted the stoppage. “Let’s not do a patchwork, a fix to a system that’s critical for every single family.”

“I want $50 billion,” he said, noting President Trump had asked for $30 billion in the past. “Let’s finish this job: new hardware, new software, new telecommunication.”

Duffy in later September asked Congress for $30 billion to fund new technology, control towers and airport improvements across the country. The push came as the FAA began to roll out its Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories — or SMART — program, an AI tool designed to help air traffic controllers anticipate congestion and reduce delays.

The FAA issued the nationwide ground stop at around 4 p.m. EST Friday. The rare protocol was in place for nearly four hours. The agency’s last widespread stoppage, which lasted approximately 90 minutes, was in 2023 due to a contractor outage.

Prior to that, the lasted time the FAA halted all American air travel was on Sept. 11, 2001, in response to the terror attacks — which involved commercial plane hijackings.

During the briefing, Duffy said Friday’s incident was due to an overload of data on routers, with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin adding the “public was never in danger in this process.”

“Our switches, our routers, actually got overloaded with data,” Duffy said. “They started to give us inconsistency in the data from those … routers. And because of that, we shut the system down.”

Mullin also said officials have “zero evidence” a cyber-attack led to the telecommunications issue, though he added federal authorities are still investigating the situation.

The stop was lifted at around 8:15 p.m. and airspace reopened, Duffy confirmed during the meeting, though he clarified the return to normal flight operations is expected to be “slow.”

“It’s going to take us some time to unwind what’s happened this afternoon,” Duffy said.

Mullin echoed Duffy’s call for funding to build a new updated system, saying, “It has to be a complete overhaul of the system, which we estimate to be about $50 billion.”

“I think every member of Congress should say yes … every American, I guarantee you, would tell those members, ‘Yes, give President Trump — give Duffy the money. Let them finish this job so we don’t have to do a press conference at 8:30 at night ever again,'” Duffy said.
