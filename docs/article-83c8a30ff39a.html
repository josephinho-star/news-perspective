Australian politics live: Hanson calls Coalition and Labor a ‘uni’ party; Minns ends presser after heckling from Greens candidate

2026-11-23 01:06

Pauline Hanson says One Nation has ‘set the agenda for the Liberal party’

One Nation’s Pauline Hanson and Barnaby Joyce are also out the front of Parliament House.

They may have shared a stage with Taylor and Canavan but they’re certainly not being friendly.

Hanson accuses the Coalition and Labor of being a “uni” party (could have come up with a better name there in my opinion) and that they’ve both led the country “to its demise”.

We have been [the ones] that have set the agenda for the Liberal party. They gave up the ghost. They’ve agreed with the uni party in here, and they are the uni party. They’ve, you know, supported each other on the policies that have led this country to the demise that it is in now.

Hanson talks about how she backs small businesses and that two-thirds of her party are small business owners.

Hanson, for reference, has said she wants to make it easier for businesses, particularly small businesses, to fire their workers, and pushed back against wage rises for minimum wage workers.

Prime minister allocates himself more political staff

Anthony Albanese has allocated himself more political staff and is paying them more than his predecessors, while reducing the hire count for the cross bench, AAP reports.

A new Centre for Public Integrity report into political staffing allocations over the last 13 years shows a top-heavy prime minister’s office, with a ballooning proportion of well-paid senior roles.

Three quarters of Albanese’s current 59 political staff are categorised as senior, compared to 61% when he took the role, and under half during Tony Abbott’s prime ministership.

Annual salaries, not including travel and other allowances, afforded to senior staff range from $156,510 to $317,152, while non-senior staff top out at $167,530.

Political staff – who are employed over and above the regular allocation of electorate officers to every MP and senator – and their pay bands are set at the prime minister’s discretion.

Using this power, Albanese has also grown the prime minister’s office to the largest it has ever been.

Albanese was asked about this at his press conference earlier. He replied:

It’s not the system that I created. It’s one I inherited, and we increased the staffing for every member of the House of Representatives and the Senate.

NSW premier calls off press conference after Greens candidate shows up to heckle

The NSW premier, Chris Minns, called off a press conference earlier this morning after it was interrupted by a Greens candidate who proceeded to heckle the premier about Labor preselections.

Minns was making a media appearance at a medical centre in the marginal electorate of East Hills earlier this morning on reforms allowing GPs to prescribe ADHD medication when Shane Bazzi, fourth on the Greens’ upper-house ticket at March’s state election, crashed the press conference.

In audio of the brief press conference, Bazzi can be heard accusing the premier of making “captain’s calls” on Labor preselections, shouting “Shame on you, Chris Minns, shame”. The Sydney Morning Herald reported that Bazzi also shouted comments about the war in Gaza.

The premier quickly called off the event, describing the incident as “appalling”. At a reconvened press conference at NSW parliament this morning, Minns said:

Now he [Bazzi] has got every right to have his own press conference. He’s got every right to run for state parliament. But I think that when you’re trying to disrupt the ability for another candidate just to speak to the media, it goes too far.

There’s an opportunity for him to have his own press conference as soon as we’re finished, we respect that … If, at the end of the day, political candidates are just going to try and disrupt and stop other candidates from speaking, then it’s the public that will lose out in the end.

Wells criticises Google for reportedly terminating news deals

Anika Wells also responded to news that Google had told a host of news publishers that it wouldn’t renew commercial deals under Labor’s news bargaining incentive program.

The Australian Financial Review reported Google had told the Public Interest Publishers Alliance that “Google has had to reassess its commercial position and, unfortunately, has reached the difficult decision to exercise its right to terminate this agreement”.

Wells said companies subject to the NBI that chose not to make commercial deals under the framework would have to pay more.

I don’t think we can be any clearer than, if you don’t do deals, you will pay more. We have delivered laws in Australia that seek to uphold public interest journalism, an important tenet of our democracy. And if you choose not to do deals, you will pay more under those laws.

So it is for these commercial companies to make decisions in their interests, but we believe we’ve created settings that best incentivise deals for Australian journalists.

Anthony Albanese has released a tribute to Molly Meldrum.

With an interview style that was inimitable and an enthusiasm that was uncontainable, Molly Meldrum loved music almost as much as we loved him.

On Countdown and beyond, he championed Australian artists and brought international acts to our shores. He gave new artists a leg up and established artists a welcoming embrace. And over the years as he implored us to do ourselves a favour, he became a central part of the Australian soundtrack. We were lucky to have him.

Labor considering Greens’ amendments on digital duty of care

Anika Wells says the government is considering the Greens’ amendments to the digital duty of care bill (DDOC), including allowing Australians to directly sue big tech companies, and increased financial penalties.

Labor wants the DDOC through parliament this week and Guardian Australia understands the government and the Greens are keen to make a deal. But the Greens aren’t ready to back it yet, holding out for stronger legislation.

A Labor-chaired Senate report on Friday recommended the government amend its own bill to allow a private right of action. In a news conference this morning, the communications minister said she had been “speaking a lot across the weekend” with her Greens counterpart, Sarah Hanson-Young, and indicated things were moving. Wells said:

We’re working through what [private right of action] might look like, as well as the request for amendments on revenue as a penalty. We understand the argument.

We’ve got a lot of considerations to settle and I look forward to both negotiating with parliamentarians and with the cabinet to try and secure something that delivers Australia a basic safety standard in an industry that has been the wild west for far too long.

Wells was critical that the Coalition had “benched themselves” in this discussion, with the Liberal communications spokesperson, Sarah Henderson, having consistently said she wouldn’t back the bill. Wells said:

I find it baffling when online safety and the safety of our kids, the mental welfare of our kids, is one of the most compelling and pressing policy questions of the generation. To bench themselves on this topic is for [the Coalition] to explain.

In pics: Liberals, Nationals and One Nation appear at rally against tax changes

Angus Taylor, a group of his Coalition MPs, and One Nation were out the front of parliament earlier addressing a rally against the government’s capital gains tax changes.

Taylor promised he would reverse them if elected (something he’s said previously).

The political contingency probably made up about 10-20% of everyone there. I’m told there were about 50ish attendees.

Independent pushes government to publish ministerial diaries

Independent MP Monique Ryan has introduced a bill this morning that would force ministers to regularly publish their diaries.

She told a news conference earlier that her constituents were sick of seeing a government they said was not acting in the interests of the public on issues such as gambling and gas.

They’d [constituents] like to know who our ministers meet with, when, where and why. They’re incredibly frustrated that a government that claimed before it was elected that it would be transparent and open has been so manifestly opaque.

Ryan said she had put in a freedom of information request for attorney general, Michelle Rowland’s diary. When she received it, 45% was redacted.

The government is extremely unlikely to support the bill.

Taylor and Canavan appear with Joseph Daoud outside parliament

Joseph Daoud, the man who won an auction to play tennis against the prime minister so he could criticise reforms to the capital gains tax, is back lobbying against the reforms.

Daoud formed “Stop the Ambition Tax”, a self-proclaimed grassroots movement of small business owners against the CGT changes, which is campaigning out the front of Parliament House today.

The Liberal leader, Angus Taylor, and the Nationals leader, Matt Canavan, are appearing with Daoud out the front of parliament too.

Canavan makes some claims that the CGT changes are just the start for “socialists” who supposedly want to control everyone. It’s a stretch to say the least … Canavan must have been doing some yoga this morning.

This is not just about taxes, it’s not just about a budget strategy. This is socialism, ladies and gentlemen. That’s what they are. They are socialists. And they want to control you.

The address becomes a bit of a press conference with Taylor then turning his attention to Cop.

He says it’s the second “flop” for the government after the failed Indigenous voice referendum.

This is the second major flop from this government. This effectively, this climate change Cop flop is [the voice] 2.0. The government has now spent half a billion dollars on two major initiatives that have delivered absolutely nothing but division and hopelessness for the Australian people.

The shadow energy minister, Dan Tehan, isn’t pulling punches on the failure of the Cop climate summit to reach agreement on a deal.

“Chris Bowen must resign today,” Tehan said in a statement.

As the lead negotiator, Chris Bowen is personally responsible for this catastrophic diplomatic disaster.

The Coalition, and Tehan specifically, have consistently poured scorn on Australia’s initial bid to host the conference, and then on Bowen’s installation as president of negotiations. The failure of the summit to reach a consensus is being heralded by the Coalition as vindication of its doubts.

Australian taxpayers have funded Chris Bowen’s $150m Cop pretensions, only for the so-called president to embarrass Australia on the world stage by making jokes about his penis. Of course, the one thing Chris Bowen has not failed to do is spend taxpayers’ money.

If Chris Bowen does not resign immediately, the prime minister should sack him.

Tributes are flowing for Molly Meldrum, the Australian music journalist, broadcaster and producer, who has died at the age of 83. He died in the early hours of Monday morning at a hospital in Melbourne.

On social media, the Liberal leader, Angus Taylor, wrote:

Like so many, I remember watching Molly on Countdown and Hey Hey it’s Saturday. Donning his cowboy hat, Molly became a beloved and recognisable face on Australian television. Kind, gentle and cheerful, Molly’s qualities beamed into our living rooms.

Molly will be dearly missed and fondly remembered as an Australian cultural icon.

The former Nationals leader Michael McCormack wrote on social media “like many young people growing up in that unforgettable decade or so starting in the mid 1970s, I recall sitting in the TV room with my sisters ‘doing ourselves a favour’ watching Countdown hosted by the late, great Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum. Vale, an Aussie rock music icon.”

Cop breakdown a ‘comprehensive failure’ by Labor, Greens say

The Greens have joined the pile-on against Labor after the Cop climate conference, at which Chris Bowen was a co-president, failed to reach consensus.

The minor party is pretty mad that the government and the proposed deal failed to mention fossil fuels.

The Greens leader, David Shoebridge, told the media:

The prime minister went to a global climate conference and delivered a keynote speech, which failed to mention fossil fuels, and that gave comfort and strength to the fossil fuel industry, who ran that narrative for the whole of Cop31.

At the end of Cop31, we have the Albanese Labor government choosing to side with Saudi Arabia and Trump, and not mention fossil fuels, rather than back in Europe and our Pacific members.
