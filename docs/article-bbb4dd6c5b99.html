Turkey’s annual inflation slows to 29.7 percent in September

2026-10-05 12:13

Turkey’s annual inflation slowed to 29.73 percent in September, falling below 30 percent for the first time since November 2021, official data showed Monday, while an independent research group continued to report substantially higher inflation.

Consumer prices rose 1.84 percent from August, the Turkish Statistical Institute (TurkStat) announced, bringing annual inflation down from 31.51 percent in August. Prices have risen 24.32 percent since the beginning of the year, while the 12-month average inflation rate stood at 31.49 percent.

The monthly increase was lower than economists had expected. A survey conducted by the state-run Anadolu news agency had forecast a 2.18 percent rise in September and annual inflation of around 30.2 percent.

Treasury and Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek said annual inflation had fallen below 30 percent for the first time in 57 months and claimed that disinflation was spreading across most categories apart from transportation.

In a statement on X on Monday, he said recent increases in oil prices were adding to inflationary pressure.

Turkey relies heavily on imported energy, leaving domestic prices exposed to swings in global oil and gas prices, while the war in the Middle East has pushed up energy and transportation costs across the region.

Citing a central bank estimate, he said inflation would have been 7 percentage points lower this year without the war.

He said the government would continue policies aimed at increasing housing and food supply and predicted further easing in rents, education costs and commodity-driven price pressures in 2027.

Housing remained one of the strongest sources of price pressure. Prices for housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels rose 39.99 percent from a year earlier, while transportation prices increased 35.10 percent and food and non-alcoholic beverages rose 27.62 percent.

On a monthly basis, transportation prices rose 2.79 percent and housing costs increased 2.71 percent, while food prices edged down 0.20 percent. Education recorded the largest increase among the main spending categories.

An independent inflation research group continued to calculate significantly higher price increases.

The Inflation Research Group (ENAG), formed by independent economists and academics, put monthly inflation at 2.10 percent in September and annual inflation at 46.61 percent. ENAG had calculated annual inflation at 49.03 percent in August.

The gap between TurkStat and ENAG has persisted for years, with opposition parties, economists and labor groups questioning the official figures. TurkStat denies allegations that its data are politically manipulated and says its methodology follows international statistical standards.

Economic confidence remains fragile

The inflation figures come as the government is also dealing with a separate crisis in Turkey’s capital markets.

A major investment fund scandal has shaken investor confidence, triggered the liquidation of 131 funds and led to expanding criminal investigations into alleged market manipulation.

The Capital Markets Board (SPK) ordered the liquidation of the funds, managed by seven portfolio companies, after some were unable to meet investor redemption requests during a market selloff.

The funds have 455,758 individual investors and held assets valued at about $18.3 billion when the liquidation process began.

The turmoil also hit the greater stock market. The benchmark BIST 100 lost 16.65 percent in September, its worst monthly performance since 2008.

Criminal investigations have since expanded. Turkish courts had jailed 85 people pending trial in the fund investigation and related stock-manipulation cases by Saturday.

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has said the crisis poses no systemic threat to the economy and pledged to protect investors while ensuring that those who obtained illegal gains are held accountable.

Central bank keeps rates high

Turkey’s central bank kept its benchmark interest rate at 37 percent in September, saying the underlying trend of inflation was slowing while warning that inflation expectations and energy prices remained risks.

The bank’s September survey showed market participants expected inflation to end 2026 at 29.61 percent, while households’ 12-month-ahead inflation expectation stood much higher at 45.6 percent.

September’s official reading marks another step in Turkey’s disinflation process, but the continued gap with independent estimates and persistently high housing and transportation costs show that price pressures remain significant for households.
