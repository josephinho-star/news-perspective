US, Iran race to revive Qatar talks as Trump weighs new strikes after midterms

2026-10-31 15:59

The US is poised to reenter talks with Iran in Qatar as diplomatic efforts to avert another war collide with warnings that President Trump could unleash a new round of military strikes immediately after Tuesday’s midterm elections, according to reports.

Qatari newspaper Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reported that the sides had agreed on preliminary measures addressing Iran’s nuclear program and the Strait of Hormuz, where a US fleet has been escorting oil tankers to market.

The reported framework also includes “financial incentives” for Tehran, the Times of Israel reported.

The diplomatic push followed marathon talks in Tehran Friday, where Qatari mediators spent 12 hours trying to prevent another military escalation, Axios reported. The mediators remained in “regular contact” with the White House during the discussions, the outlet said.

But a breakdown in negotiations could quickly put the two sides back on a path to war. The last US strike against Iran came in September.

Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei warned the US in a Telgram post Saturday not to strike again.

“If the enemies once again launch military or terrorist attacks, it is the armed forces of the Islamic Republic and the Iranian nation that will determine the time and conditions for ending them,” he said.

Khamenei also threatened a “geographically unrestricted response” — raising the prospect that US allies across the region could again come under fire, as they did following US and Israeli attacks in February.

A preliminary draft framework stated that Iran could transfer its stockpile of highly enriched uranium to an agreed third country, Al Arabiya reported.

The proposal also calls for the Strait of Hormuz to reopen within seven days of an initial agreement, with China and Russia potentially providing guarantees of implementation, according to the report
