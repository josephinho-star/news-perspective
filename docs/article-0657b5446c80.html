33 jailed in $72 million citizenship fraud probe in Turkey

2026-09-25 11:50

A Turkish court has jailed 33 suspects pending trial in an investigation into alleged sham real estate transactions used to obtain Turkish citizenship, in the latest development in a widening probe into abuses of the country’s citizenship-by-investment program, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

The İstanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office said the suspects were among 74 people detained in an İstanbul-based operation carried out across 13 provinces.

Twenty other suspects were placed under house arrest, while 21 were released under other judicial supervision measures.

Prosecutors accuse the suspects of taking part in sham property transactions designed to make foreign nationals appear to meet the investment requirements for Turkish citizenship.

Investigators allege that the required foreign currency was not actually brought into Turkey and that purchases were instead financed through intermediary companies or sellers, while properties were assigned artificially inflated values through fraudulent appraisal reports.

The investigation has identified 1,070 people, including investors and their family members, who allegedly acquired Turkish citizenship through the transactions.

Prosecutors said the disputed transactions were worth $72.25 million and that administrative proceedings have begun to revoke citizenship obtained through the alleged scheme.

The 33 suspects jailed were detained in the second phase of the investigation, launched earlier this week with warrants for 88 people. Police detained 74 suspects in raids across 13 provinces.

The investigation began in August with transactions linked to Babacan İnşaat. In that first phase, police detained 72 suspects accused of helping foreign nationals obtain citizenship through inflated property valuations and sham sales.

Investigators said 687 people had acquired citizenship through that alleged scheme and began proceedings to revoke their citizenship. Authorities also seized seven companies, 1,045 properties, a hotel, 15 vehicles, a yacht and 10 bank accounts.

Turkey’s citizenship-by-investment program allows foreign nationals to apply for citizenship after purchasing property worth at least $400,000 and agreeing not to sell it for three years.

The government lowered the minimum real estate investment from $1 million to $250,000 in 2018 in an effort to attract foreign capital before raising it to $400,000 in 2022.

The Interior Ministry said earlier this week that citizenship decisions covering 6,134 people had been canceled or withdrawn over irregular investment transactions or national security and public order concerns.

Of those, citizenship decisions covering 5,391 people were canceled after investment eligibility certificates belonging to 1,150 investors were revoked over alleged sham or otherwise irregular transactions.

A further 743 people, including investors and their family members, had citizenship withdrawn on national security or public order grounds.
