Turkey summons Ukrainian ambassador over Black Sea attacks

2026-08-29 20:37

Turkey summoned Ukraine’s ambassador on Saturday over attacks this week on a Turkish vessel and a second ship sent to tow it in the Black Sea, the Turkish Foreign Ministry said.

The attacks took place on Wednesday and Thursday, the ministry told Agence France-Presse.

“During the meeting, Turkey warned about the absolute need to ensure the safety of people, of assets, of navigation and of the environment in the Black Sea,” the ministry said in a statement.

The Turkish container ship Lider Bordo Mavi was hit on Wednesday off Russia’s Black Sea coast. The Lider Kocatepe, which was sent to tow the cargo vessel, was also targeted the next day, the ministry said.

The ministry did not say whether anyone was killed or injured.

The Lider Bordo Mavi was towed to the Turkish port of Samsun, according to Turkish media reports.

Turkey has for weeks called on Ukraine and Russia to suspend attacks on civilian vessels in the Black Sea. Both countries have sought to damage the other’s export revenue during the conflict, which began with Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Turkish commercial ships operate in the Black Sea under various flags, and several have been hit by drones suspected of being launched by Ukraine.

Turkey, a NATO member that maintains ties with both Kyiv and Moscow, has not joined Western sanctions against Russia.

Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan called for a “moratorium” on Black Sea attacks on August 8.

“The conflict has spread across the whole Black Sea,” he said, adding that “they are attacking all commercial shipping without distinction.”
