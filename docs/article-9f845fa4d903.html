[UPDATE] Captain among 9 held as 20 remain missing after northern Cyprus ferry tragedy

2026-08-31 11:24

A court in northern Cyprus on Monday ordered the captain and eight others to remain in custody for three more days as rescue teams searched for 20 people missing after a passenger ferry capsized and sank off the coast of Kyrenia, killing eight, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

The Turkish-flagged Filo Jet was carrying 259 passengers and eight crew members from Kyrenia, also known as Girne in Turkish, to the port of Taşucu in Turkey’s southern Mersin province when it began taking on water shortly after departing at around 12:30 p.m. on Sunday.

The catamaran capsized about four nautical miles, or 7.5 kilometers, from shore before sinking to a depth of 514 meters.

Turkish Cypriot Prime Minister Ünal Üstel said on Monday that 239 people had been confirmed rescued, eight had died and 20 remained missing. Officials had earlier reported 241 survivors and 18 missing before Üstel announced the revised figures.

The identities of the eight people killed and the 20 missing passengers have been released.

None of the survivors was reported to be in critical condition, with hospitals beginning to discharge patients whose treatment was complete.

Search-and-rescue operations continued for a second day with support from Turkish and Turkish Cypriot coast guard vessels, aircraft and drones as well as private boats.

The depth of the wreckage is expected to complicate the investigation. Turkish Cypriot officials said specialized equipment and personnel were being sent to inspect the vessel and recover data that could help establish the cause of the sinking.

Turkish Cypriot President Tufan Erhürman promised a full investigation.

“Whatever errors, negligence, mistakes or faults there may have been will be brought to light,” he said.

Fourteen suspects, including the captain, seven other crew members, a ferry company employee and port officials, appeared in Kyrenia District Court as part of an investigation into suspected negligence.

The court ordered nine of them, including the captain, to remain in custody for another three days while investigators collect technical evidence, examine passenger videos and question witnesses. Five others were released pending trial, and police were seeking two additional suspects.

The investigation concerns suspicion of causing death through negligence, according to Turkish media reports.

The captain and crew told the court that the vessel’s bow broke off after being struck by waves, causing it to capsize. They said weather reports had not indicated a problem when the ferry departed and maintained that the vessel had the necessary certificates and permission to sail.

The cause of the accident has not been established. Turkish Cypriot Transport Minister Erhan Arıklı said conditions at sea had been severe, while Erhürman said the weather had not been particularly bad and urged the public not to speculate before the investigation was complete.

Several survivors said they heard what sounded like an explosion shortly before the vessel capsized. Others said it began taking on water and attempted to return to Kyrenia before overturning.

Television footage showed passengers wearing life jackets sitting on the exposed orange hull after the vessel capsized.

Some survivors alleged that there was not enough safety equipment for everyone aboard.

“Some people had to jump without lifebuoys or life jackets,” survivor Efe Multici told CNN Türk. “It was literally a fight for survival.”

Filo Denizcilik, the company operating the vessel, said it was “deeply saddened” by the accident and was cooperating with search-and-rescue teams. It said it had established a crisis center and a dedicated hotline for passengers’ relatives.

Northern Cyprus declared three days of mourning following the disaster.

Turkey sent vessels, aircraft and drones to assist with the search. The internationally recognized Republic of Cyprus, which controls the southern part of the island, also offered assistance.

Cyprus has been divided since 1974, when a Greek-backed coup aimed at uniting the island with Greece prompted a Turkish military intervention. The Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus declared independence in 1983 but is recognized only by Turkey.

Northern Cyprus maintains regular air and sea connections with Turkey.
