Kilmar Ábrego García, man at center of Trump deportation battle, leaves US

2026-11-24 23:50

Kilmar Ábrego García has left the United States of his own volition, his lawyers confirmed on Tuesday, with the Department of Homeland Security confirming that he was travelling to Spain.

A source close to Ábrego said Madrid was his first stop but that it would not be his final destination.

The Salvadorian immigrant was at the center of a months-long legal battle since he was deported to El Salvador in March 2025 by the Trump administration, in defiance of a court’s protection. He was held in Cecot, a notorious mega-prison.

He was ultimately returned to the US after a decision by the supreme court three months later. Since then, Ábrego has been fighting a second deportation to a series of African countries proposed by Department of Homeland Security (DHS) officials.

The Washington Post reported earlier on Tuesday that he negotiated a deal with federal authorities to depart the US voluntarily in order to avoid being forcibly removed.

In a statement, the US senator Chris Van Hollen, who has advocated for Maryland resident Ábrego and who met with him in El Salvador last year, described him as a “free man” and referenced Ábrego’s agreement with the Trump administration.

“While the Trump Administration had been pursuing their vindictive prosecution against Kilmar and desperately trying to ship him against his will to countries known for human rights abuses, the Administration was forced to come to this agreement that respects Kilmar’s will and the due process rights guaranteed to him and to all in our nation,” Van Hollen said. “I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free – including from this Administration’s constant persecution.”

The 31-year-old has an American wife and child and has lived in Maryland for years, but he immigrated to the US illegally as a teenager. In 2019, an immigration judge ruled that he could not be deported to El Salvador because he faced danger there from a gang that had threatened his family. The judge allowed him to live and work in the US under Immigration and Customs Enforcement supervision, but he was not given residency status.

He was arrested in Baltimore in March 2025 and deported to a Salvadorian prison in what an official later called an “administrative error”. Facing public pressure and a court order, the Trump administration brought him back in June 2025, but only after securing an indictment charging him with human smuggling in Tennessee. A federal judge dismissed that case in May, finding that the justice department’s pursuit of criminal charges was designed to punish Ábrego for challenging his deportation. The government is appealing.

The Trump administration vowed to deport him to a third country, threatening to send him to Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana and, most recently, Liberia.

“We will always admire his remarkable courage in staring down and defeating a vindictive prosecution instigated by the highest reaches of the Justice Department,” one of Ábrego’s attorneys, Sean Hecker, said in a statement provided to the Washington Post. “He, in turn, is grateful to those who stood by him.”

Additional reporting by Ashifa Kassam in Madrid and the Associated Press
