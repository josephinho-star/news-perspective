Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO after Trump intervenes

2026-11-23 22:23

Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive after Donald Trump directly intervened with the US AI chipmaker’s board of directors, in a stunning reversal of the tech billionaire’s downfall.

Speaking on Monday shortly after Nvidia’s surprise announcement that Huang had been restored to the top job – less than two weeks after he resigned – Trump said he had “convinced” the board to do it. “I made the deal,” he told CNBC. Huang had resigned after reports of extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy and two Nvidia employees.

“It’s very exciting news. I felt very strongly that he should come back,” the US president said.

Huang said he was “honored to return to Nvidia” and lead “one of the most consequential technology companies in the world”. He added: “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.”

Nvidia shares surged on the news of Huang’s return, jumping 32% and adding more than $1tn (£760bn) to the company’s market value in New York on Monday, while Trump, who had persistently called for Huang’s return, said: “The country needs Jensen’s leadership.”

The chipmaker’s move to bring Huang back marks a sharp reversal from its board’s decision days earlier to rule out his return. The US vice-president, JD Vance, played a key role in discussions between the White House, Huang and Nvidia, according to the New York Times.

The US government has been investigating Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company formerly run by Huang’s alleged former lover, Alice Huang (no relation), over suspected diversion of semiconductors in breach of export controls, amid concerns Nvidia’s powerful AI chips may have been smuggled into China. Alice Huang has also been named in multiple reports as a Chinese spy.

The same day as Huang’s reinstatement, the US government announced it had added Megaspeed to a federal trade blacklist, preventing US companies from selling chips or other advanced technology to the Singaporean business.

Trump said on Monday that the US government had “no evidence that [Jensen Huang] has engaged in any unlawful conduct” and that the attorney general, Todd Blanche, felt Huang was “exonerated”.

The US president told CNBC that the US had to “protect our geniuses”, adding that the Chinese president, Xi Jinping, praised Huang at last week’s Apec summit in Shenzhen. Adding that he had talked to Nvidia’s directors, he said: “I’ve been speaking to the [Nvidia] board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it. I felt more strongly as I saw what was happening with the company.”

Nvidia’s share price had fallen by a quarter in the days after Huang resigned on 12 November. The falls were mirrored by a wider AI rout in global stock markets last week as investors feared new leadership at Nvidia could dampen prospects for AI companies that rely on the company’s chips to develop and operate AI tools such as chatbots, as well as having close financial ties with the chipmaker.

News of his shock reinstatement sparked a wider rally not just in Nvidia’s shares but on Wall Street as a whole, sending the Nasdaq composite index up 6.5% and the S&P 500 by 4.7%. The US dollar was lifted to a 19-month high.

In a statement, Nvidia said its board of directors had talked to Huang and were “satisfied with respect to Mr Huang’s candor and ability to lead Nvidia”. Nvidia added that it had received input from “senior levels” of the US government and was confident his reinstatement “poses no national security risk”.

A senior Democrat, Elizabeth Warren, a senator for Massachusetts, said the decision was “reckless”, adding that it did not change the fact that Huang owed Congress answers about Nvidia’s compliance with trade restrictions on its powerful AI chips. He is due to give evidence to Congress on 1 December. The state of California is also investigating whether Nvidia’s advanced chips ended up in China in violation of US restrictions.

She said his return increased the urgency of Congress passing export control bills “and making sure that Nvidia does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk”.

Nvidia said an independent investigation into allegations Huang was the target of a Chinese intelligence operation was ongoing and “will proceed to conclusion”.

Harvey Jones, the Nvidia chair, who was appointed this month after Huang’s resignation, said: “The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.”

Huang built Nvidia from a video-game chip designer into the dominant supplier of AI processors, steering its roadmap, customer deals and Washington ties along the way. He resigned after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, whom US authorities suspect of spying for China, an allegation Beijing denies. It was also reported that Huang had an intimate relationship with two other Nvidia employees.

Trump praised Nvidia’s board for making “the correct and proper decision” and posted: “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.”

It came hours after reports that Alice Huang had been in contact with an official in China’s spy agency while she was having an affair with the Nvidia boss. People with knowledge of US intelligence reports claimed she was in touch with the Chinese official until this autumn, according to the New York Times.

The Arm Holdings chief executive, Rene Haas, who had been approached to succeed Huang, has signed a new deal that will keep him in his current role until 2033. Huang had been replaced on a temporary basis by Colette Kress, Nvidia’s finance chief.

Elon Musk, who had called for Huang to return in an open letter backed by other Silicon Valley heavyweights, posted on X: “Welcome back the main man,” alongside a picture of him shaking hands with Huang. Trump said he had spoken to Musk “over the last week or so” about the Huang situation.

Sam North, a market analyst at the trading platform eToro, said: “Jensen Huang’s return underlines how much of the AI trade still rests on one man.”

Huang’s reinstatement has echoes of another recent real-life Silicon Valley drama. His return has taken place almost three years to the day since OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, brought back its chief executive, Sam Altman, after a similar bout of turmoil. Altman was removed by his board on 17 November 2023 but was restored five days later following an internal power battle.

In a post on X on Monday Altman wrote: “Jensen!”
