Turkey awaits sales contract from Qatar for secondhand Eurofighter jets: ministry

2026-10-22 12:18

Turkey’s planned purchase of Eurofighter Typhoon fighter jets from Qatar remains at the negotiating stage, with Ankara waiting for Doha to submit a sales contract and proposed training arrangements, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported on Thursday, citing the Defense Ministry.

The Qatari aircraft are part of Turkey’s effort to obtain secondhand fighters while awaiting the delivery of 20 new Typhoons ordered from Britain. Ankara has previously outlined plans to buy 12 aircraft each from Qatar and Oman, potentially building a fleet of 44.

In response to questions at its weekly briefing, the ministry said Turkish and Qatari delegations had held meetings in August and September. Discussions covered which aircraft and ammunition would be included, training for aircrew and maintenance teams and support needed to operate and maintain the jets.

Qatar is now expected to prepare and forward the training proposal and sales contract, according to the ministry’s statement carried by Anadolu.

The proposed transfers from the Gulf countries require their own contracts, separate from the agreement Ankara signed with Britain in October 2025. That package, covering 20 newly built aircraft, was valued by London at up to £8 billion, about $10.7 billion at the time.

With the first new aircraft expected in 2030, Turkey has been pursuing secondhand jets that could become available sooner.

Preparations for the British aircraft have meanwhile moved into flight training. Turkish pilots began flying the Typhoon in Britain in September, following an introductory aviation terminology course.

Ankara and London also concluded a support agreement in March providing for the training of 10 pilots and nearly 100 technicians. It includes spare parts, simulators and long-term maintenance support.

Turkey is seeking to modernize an air force predominantly comprising F-16s after losing access to the US-led F-35 program in 2019. Washington removed Ankara following its acquisition of Russian S-400 air defense systems, citing risks to the stealth fighter’s security.

The Eurofighter, developed by Britain, Germany, Italy and Spain, would provide additional combat capability while Turkey develops its domestic KAAN fighter. Ankara has also pursued talks with Washington on returning to the F-35 program.

Thursday’s update followed the signing in Doha a day earlier of the Badr Memorandum of Understanding by Turkey, Qatar, Pakistan and Indonesia. The agreement provides for cooperation in defense production, research and development and technical military training.
