Trump again calls for prosecution of Mary Peltola as turmoil grows over Alaska campaign investigation – live

2026-10-29 23:43

‘This is voter fraud at the highest level,’ president tells rally crowd in South Carolina; Alaska governor orders inquiry into Peltola campaign amid Republican outcry

On the campaign stage in South Carolina, Trump again calls for prosecution of Mary Peltola

Speaking to a campaign audience in South Carolina, Donald Trump reiterated his belief that Mary Peltola should be criminally prosecuted and disqualified from the Alaska Senate contest.

“This is voter fraud at the highest level,” Trump said of the Peltola campaign’s alleged scheme to recruited a man who shared the name of her Republican opponent, Dan Sullivan, to run, all in a bid to peel away votes.

Trump added that Peltola “should be removed from the ballot and prosecuted, and so should the fake Dan Sullivan”.

“I think it’s a criminal act, what they did,” Trump said.

Man who compared Trump supporters to cult members live on Fox News says he was ejected from rally by Secret Service

Tom Hoyle, who achieved instant viral fame on Thursday by comparing Donald Trump supporters to a cult in a live interview with Fox News before the president’s rally in South Carolina, told the Guardian that he was quickly removed from the venue by three Secret Service agents.

Hoyle, 57, explained in a phone interview that he was offended to have received an invitation to what he called the president’s “Nazi rally” in a text sent to his screen-printing business’s phone earlier this week.

He accepted the invitation, he said, with the intention of “screaming vulgarities” at Trump during his speech.

As he was waiting inside the relatively small venue in Rock Hill, South Carolina, however, he was approached by the Fox News correspondent Lucas Tomlinson.

Hoyle said he agreed, and then had just a few seconds to “come up with something not vulgar” to say on air and express his point that most Americans do not support Trump. (“It’s all a charade,” he told the Guardian later, calling Maga Republicans a minority of voters, but a very loud one.)

When Tomlinson then asked Hoyle “why are you here this morning?” he responded by comparing the assembled Trump supporters to members of a cult who committed mass suicide in 1978 by following their leader’s order to drink cyanide-laced fruit punch in Jonestown, Guyana (giving rise to the phrase that true believers “drink the Kool-Aid”).

“Well, the late 70s, I was a little too young to get involved and go to South America for the Jonestown massacre, but this is as close as I’ll ever get, so that’s why I’m here,” Hoyle said.

“Wait, the Jonestown massacre?” Tomlinson asked.

“That’s right,” Hoyle replied. “There’s nothing like seeing a cult right up front, man.”

“All right, so maybe this is not a Trump supporter,” Tomlinson told the studio before stepping away to find someone else to interview.

Before the camera moved, Hoyle took the opportunity to point to his cap and plug his business, MadPark Designs in Charlotte, North Carolina.

Hoyle said after he was ejected from the venue by the Secret Service, he spoke with a group of college student protesting against the president outside.

Speaking to the Guardian later, Hoyle pointed out that his business is in the same office park as a Customs and Border Protection office in Charlotte, adding that he has regularly told officers involved in Trump’s immigration crackdown what he thinks of them to their faces.

Trump lashes out at Reuters reporter who asked why he canceled rally in Kansas

After his return from South Carolina, Donald Trump lashed out at a Reuters reporter who asked him why he just canceled a planned appearance at a rally in Kansas on Saturday in support of a embattled Republican senator, Dr Roger Marshall.

After the reporter inquired about Trump’s health, and Trump said he was well, the reporter asked: “So you not going to Kansas has nothing to do with you being sick of travel?”

With a look of disgust, Trump responded: “Oh, you’re sick to ask that question.

“You are a sick guy. Reuters is terrible,” the president added.

Then, stepping away to address other reporters, Trump said: “Can you imagine? Look at this dope. Look at this guy, he’s way out of shape.”

“He comes to me, ‘Sir, you’re not going to go to Kansas.’ I’m not going to Kansas because he’s doing very well. I’m going to do a tele-rally,” Trump continued, imitating the reporter and suggesting that Marshall no longer needs his help to win his Senate race.

“He said, ‘what’s the reason’, after doing what, 28? He says ‘what’s the reason for this, sir, that you’re not going to Kansas?’” the president continued, recapping the exchange for journalists who had just witnessed it for themselves.

“You know what the reason is? I don’t feel I have to go to Kansas,” Trump went on. “I’m going to Florida tomorrow, and I’ll do- if something else comes up – but he said, ‘You’re not going to Kansas, sir, are you feeling well?’ You’ve been following me for two weeks. I feel perfect,” the president said, with indignation.

“You are disgusting,” Trump said, turning back to the Reuters reporter. “Put yourself in shape – you look like hell.”

He then ended the discussion and walked away from the press corps.

JD Vance writes to top Iranian security official in sign Washington has not abandoned diplomacy

Hopes of averting a major new conflagration between Iran and the US soon after Tuesday’s midterm elections could rest on Iran’s response to a personal letter from the US vice-president, JD Vance, offering to restart diplomacy.

The letter, written after predominantly Qatari mediation, was sent to the secretary of Iran’s supreme national security council, Mohsen Rezaei, on Sunday. It requires Tehran to agree to make commitments on its nuclear programme at the start of a process that would lead to the opening of the strait of Hormuz within seven days in return for US sanctions relief.

A reply is imminent, but Iran is reluctant to give upfront and irreversible commitments on its nuclear programme, such as exporting its stockpile of highly enriched uranium to a third country, if the compensatory US steps, such as lifting sanctions on Iranian oil exports, are reversible.

Trump aided Bolsonaro's flight from Brazil to US, New York Times reports

Flávio Bolsonaro, the rightwing candidate who clandestinely fled Brazil for Miami after losing the Brazilian presidential election, planned his escape with the blessing of the Trump administration, the New York Times reported on Thursday.

The paper, citing US officials familiar with the plan, said that Trump had ordered that Bolsonaro be welcomed into Florida on a temporary parole status, which will probably be extended to permanent residency. A White House official also arranged the private jet that shuttled Bolsonaro and his family to Miami, the Times reported.

Jair Bolsonaro, once one of Trump’s strongest allies in Latin America, is now serving a 27-year sentence for allegedly trying to unseat Lula in a coup.

Flávio Bolsonaro picked up his father’s torch, running on a right-wing platform to unseat Lula. After his defeat, he was ordered to surrender his passports in connection with a criminal money laundering investigation.

Instead, Bolsonaro absconded across the border with Argentina alongside his family, flight from there to Ecuador and ultimately to Florida.

Senate race in once reliably Republican South Carolina tied in final stretch – poll

A US Senate race in one of the most Republican states in the country is now a dead heat, according to a new poll showing Democrat Annie Andrews tied with Darline Graham, the Republican incumbent senator.

The Palmetto State has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1998, and Donald Trump won it by nearly 18 points in 2024, but a New York Times/Siena poll this week has the two candidates neck and neck, at 48% each.

Graham was appointed to the seat after her brother, Lindsey Graham, died in July, and she entered the race at Trump’s urging, without having ever held elected office.

Her inexperience has become one of the defining aspects of the campaign, which culminated in an August debate when she admitted she was “not that informed on national security” when asked about US policy on Taiwan. In the poll, 51% of voters said she lacked the right experience to be an effective senator.

Andrews, a pediatrician from Charleston, has meanwhile built her campaign around her medical career. Her solution to better access to doctors and climbing medical bills has been to advocate for a public option and cheaper prescription drugs.

Obama-era investigation reportedly found some evidence against Ilhan Omar's marriage

An Obama-era federal investigation of Minnesota representative Ilhan Omar found some evidence suggesting that she entered into a sham marriage in 2009 to help a man suspected of being her brother obtain a green card, according to the New York Times (paywall).

At issue is Omar’s 2009 marriage, when she was already a US citizen, to Ahmed Nur Said Elmi, a British citizen whom she divorced in 2017.

Current and former officials told the paper that the FBI and the Department of Homeland Security investigated the marriage in the summer of 2016, but the case stalled chiefly because prosecutors concluded that the suspected crime fell outside a five-year statute of limitations.

The Times notes that it remains unclear what evidence investigators had that suggested the marriage was fraudulent and that the person Omar married was her brother.

Marrying someone for the sole purpose of helping them obtain a green card is illegal under federal law, while marrying a sibling is illegal under state law.

Omar has denied the allegations. Spokesperson Jackie Rogers told the NYT in a statement:

Rep. Omar has repeatedly and unequivocally denied this baseless, disgusting conspiracy theory. This is a desperate political smear designed to distract from Trump’s failed presidency and two years of Republican failure in Congress.

The Trump administration has revisited the case as part of a wider investigation into Omar’s family’s immigration history to the United States.

Investigators are examining whether Omar’s late father lied or omitted information in immigration forms before becoming an American citizen, and whether Omar, who was a child when she immigrated to the United States, was too old to have qualified for citizenship through her father in 2000, the Times reported.
