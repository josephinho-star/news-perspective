Russia's Putin vows a response 'far more painful' after Ukraine attacks prized bridge in Crimea

2026-11-19 17:48

Russian President Vladimir Putin vowed Thursday to respond to Ukraine in a "far more painful" manner after Volodymyr Zelenskyy's forces heavily damaged the Kerch Strait bridge that has become a symbol of Russia's annexation of Crimea.

The warning was issued as Ukraine's president said he arrived in Berlin, Germany for talks with more than 40 partners aimed at ending the war, including with President Donald Trump's son-in-law and advisor Jared Kushner and U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff.

"We will respond to these strikes. And I want everyone to know this: I believe our response will be far more painful than what the Kyiv regime did to the bridge," Putin said after meeting with President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping for trilateral talks on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Summit in Shenzhen, China, according to Reuters.

"We will, of course, restore the bridge. The road section suffered serious damage," Putin reportedly added.

Ukraine attacked the bridge for the third time this week prior to Putin making his remarks, Reuters reported.

A Russian official told The Associated Press that the latest strike on the bridge linking Russia to Crimea halted rail traffic.

On Tuesday, the Security Service of Ukraine released videos showing the aftermath of a drone strike on the bridge, which destroyed one of its two arches and a roadway underneath it.

Putin said Thursday that he discussed with Russia's Security Council a potential stoppage of attacks at sea and against energy facilities, and "asked everyone to give it further thought and come back to it, hoping that perhaps some kind of agreement could be reached," according to Reuters.

"But after the strikes on the Crimean Bridge, there will be no agreements," Putin added.

Zelenskyy has described the bridge as the "main military crossing from Russian territory into our Ukrainian Crimea."

Ahead of Thursday's Coalition of the Willing meeting in Germany, Zelenskyy said "Ukraine’s priorities are strengthening our air defense, energy and financial support, the diplomatic track, and the next steps toward ending the war and guaranteeing security.

"Bilateral meetings with European partners are also planned, and, of course, with Chancellor Friedrich Merz. There will also be a format involving the American side and European national security advisors," he added.

Kushner said on X that the U.S. priorities for the trip "are to further strengthen our close coordination with Ukraine and Europe, work toward a unified position on the key elements of a lasting peace, and to jointly prepare the agenda and proposed format for the next round of multilateral negotiations."

Kushner declared that Trump is "determined to end this terrible war and create a lasting peace that makes all of Europe safer."
