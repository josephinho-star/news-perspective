Ravens silenced Marlon Humphrey for his words but what they'll hear after another loss will pale in comparison

2026-11-22 23:21

Days after silencing Marlon Humphrey for his disparaging words about their city, the Baltimore Ravens are going to wish they could silence a significant portion of their fans.

Days after one of their coaches addressed the Humphrey issue, saying there is "freedom of speech but not freedom of consequences," the Ravens are staring at the consequences of being a bad football team.

Days after basically not accepting Humphrey's apology for what he said on his podcast, the Ravens owe Baltimore an apology for their play on the field.

Baltimore Ravens 33.

Welcome to the week that was and the season it has been for the Ravens.

In a season that has been a bitter disappointment and now includes six consecutive losses, the Ravens spent two days last week addressing and re-addressing what seemed like an otherwise manageable controversy that Humphrey started.

Humphrey on his podcast was asked what it was Baltimore is best at making?

"They're best at making criminals," he replied half-jokingly.

The disparaging words lit a firestorm that became a national story last Wednesday and Thursday while the Ravens were trying to salvage this season. And it is unfortunate for everyone involved because ...

- Humphrey has a right to say whatever he wants, but he surely did offend a lot of people.

- National crime statistics say he wasn't completely wrong.

- There's a racial aspect to all this because everyone understands a lot of the "criminals" Humphrey was referring to in Baltimore are Black and even the city's mayor said the remark cut deeper for "young Black boys who look to the Ravens as a beacon of light in their lives."

- The Ravens allowed this to become a social justice issue instead of relegating it to a football issue.

Let's handle that part first. Humphrey apologized immediately once this became a problem. And the Ravens seemed to accept that Wednesday as they prepared for Sunday's game against the Panthers.

But seeing the negative media attention and backlash from fans and politicians and on social media, the Ravens took an extra step by suspending Humphrey on Thursday.

The club in need of more talent, not less, sent one of its top four or five defensive backs home for the weekend and possibly longer.

The team could have as easily decided to make it clear that it believed Humphrey was wrong, he apologized, and it was onward to Carolina. But the club instead bowed to outside opinions.

All while making its secondary inside the team weaker.

Baltimore allowed 35 points against Carolina. No, that doesn't imply Baltimore would have been better on defense if Humphrey played. He's not the same player he was four or five years ago.

But the fact is his replacement Chidobe Awuzie gave up a touchdown pass.

And Baltimore allowed 35 points or more for only the third time this season while Carolina quarterback Bryce Young delivered his fourth-best passer rating of the season.

"At the end of the day we had a lead, we were on the field with a lead," coach Jesse Minter said. "We didn't hold it."

Humphrey's suspension surely changed the subject about this season for the Ravens for a couple of days last week. But the team that could not abide Humphrey's words better get used to much uglier things said about them in the coming days.

Some fringe (I think) Ravens fans spent time on social media both wondering about and calling for Minter to be fired after only one season. His agreement to suspend Humphrey didn't protect the coach from fans that care more about good results than good intentions.

Considering this was a team expected to compete for a postseason berth when the season began, this is now one of the club's most disappointing seasons in recent memory.

If Baltimore and the Ravens were offended by Humphrey's words, they surely should be hurting by that.
