‘The Simpsons’ predicted a rumored global blackout — and it’s supposed to take place today

2026-11-17 15:30

The Simpsons has a strange and storied track record of accurately predicting world events, including Donald Trump being elected president, Lady Gaga’s Super Bowl performance, Nobel Prize winners, and the advent of smartwatch technology.

Now, the denizens of Springfield may have predicted the global Nov. 17 blackout that has sent people into full panic and straight to buying solar generators.

Here’s how The Simpsons, those animated augurs, saw it coming.

In a 2023 episode titled “It’s a Blunderful Life”, a future, older Simpson family, sans Homer, gathers to celebrate Thanksgiving. Reflecting on years past, Lisa Simpson writes in her diary about a blackout that occurred on, you guessed it, November 17.

In her diary entry, Lisa writes that a candle is now lit in honor of that fated day, “in remembrance of the great blackout that almost destroyed our town, when we actually had to write things by hand”.

Rumors of a major blackout on Nov. 17, 2026, have been circulating online, with some posts warning of widespread power and internet disruptions. The speculation has gained traction amid recent technology outages and renewed conversations about America’s critical infrastructure.

Customers of Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo were experiencing outages, with many reporting trouble accessing their accounts and checking balances. The disruptions came just days after major technical problems affected Microsoft services and nationwide air travel.

However, there’s an important distinction between online predictions and confirmed events. There is no verified announcement of a nationwide power shutdown tomorrow.

What is confirmed is that FEMA and the Federal Communications Commission have scheduled a nationwide Emergency Alert System test for Nov. 17 at approximately 2:20 p.m. Eastern Time. The test will briefly interrupt radio and television programming, but it is not a warning that the electricity grid will be shut down.

Still, unexpected local outages can happen because of severe weather, equipment failures, and other emergencies. Having a backup power plan can provide valuable peace of mind.

Beyond blackouts, Homer and the gang have foreseen all manner of future events.

In a 2012 episode titled “Them, Robot,” AI starts to take over the jobs of the people in Springfield as dubious Mr. Burns replaces his employees with robots, who turn on him.

IRL, a 2025 analysis found that artificial intelligence and automation could wipe out nearly 100 million jobs in the US over the next decade, including white- and blue-collar roles.

The beloved animated show, which first aired in 1989, has also shown what it would be like to live in a smart house.

In a 2002 episode titled “Treehouse of Horror XII,” the Simpson family moves into a voice-controlled house that manages their day-to-day lives. But the longer they live there, the more threatening the smart house becomes.
