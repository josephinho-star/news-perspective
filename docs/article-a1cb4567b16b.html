10-year Treasury yield slides despite sticky inflation data as investors buy bonds for protection

2026-11-25 16:20

U.S. Treasury yields fell on Wednesday, with investors fleeing to bonds for protection after October's personal consumption expenditures price index showed that inflation continued to accelerate.

The 10-year Treasury note yield — the main benchmark for mortgages, auto loans and credit card debt — was more than 9 basis points lower at 4.809%.

The yield on the 2-year Treasury note, which typically moves in line with short-term Federal Reserve interest rate decisions, was down more than 8 basis points at 4.588%.

The longer-dated 30-year Treasury yield, which tends to track geopolitical events, was more than 7 basis points lower at 5.213%.

One basis point equals 0.01%, or 1/100th of 1%, and yields and prices move inversely to one another.

The personal consumption expenditures price index — the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge of underlying inflation — rose 3.8% year over year for October, in line with the Dow Jones consensus estimate. Separately, economic growth came in much stronger for the third quarter. Gross domestic product rose at a 4.3% annualized rate, according to a revised Commerce Department estimate.

The data reinforced expectations for another Fed rate hike in December.

"[I]t is more urgent than ever that Fed officials step up the pace of interest rate hikes to cool off economic demand," said Chris Rupkey, chief economist at FWDBONDS. "Rate hikes are coming. Lots of them. Bet on it. The economy and inflation are literally on fire."

Wednesday's drop comes after yields steadied on Tuesday, with traders digesting softer demand for a 5-year Treasury auction.

Trading is shortened this week due to the Thanksgiving holiday. Bond markets will be closed all day Thursday for Thanksgiving, and will close at 2 p.m. E.T. on Friday.

— CNBC's Jeff Cox contributed reporting.
