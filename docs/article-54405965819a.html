Trump to confront Newsom over plan to spare 548 death row inmates, including notorious child killers

2026-11-23 11:00

FIRST ON FOX — President Donald Trump will confront California Gov. Gavin Newsom over his sweeping plan to spare 548 death row inmates from execution, including a man who murdered nine members of his own family, the killer of 12-year-old Polly Klaas and the man convicted of torturing 8-year-old Gabriel Fernandez to death.

Trump is expected to appear alongside Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel at the Department of Justice (DOJ) on Monday afternoon to condemn the Democratic governor's proposed commutations, the White House confirmed to Fox News Digital.

Families of murder victims are expected to attend the event, where their stories will be highlighted as the Trump administration challenges Newsom's effort to replace every California death sentence with life imprisonment without the possibility of parole.

"Governor Newsom is failing the victims and their families by refusing to carry out justice against these barbaric child killers and serial killers. These murderers raped, tortured and terrorized innocent victims. Governor Newsom must stop playing politics with public safety," White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis told Fox News Digital.

Newsom announced Nov. 5 that he intended to commute all 548 California death sentences to life imprisonment without parole, arguing that capital punishment is incompatible with the state's values.

Asked by Fox News Digital about the proposed commutations, Newsom's office did not directly address the individual murder cases highlighted by the White House. Instead, the governor's office referred Fox News Digital to his previously published letter defending the decision.

"California voters have voted to keep the death penalty on the books, and I respect that," Newsom wrote. "But the constitution also gave every governor clemency authority, and that power exists for moments when law as applied cannot be squared with justice. For me, this is that moment."

Newsom also acknowledged that some victims' families strongly oppose his decision, while his office pointed to other survivors who support eliminating capital punishment.

The White House provided Fox News Digital with a list of 20 notorious convicted killers who would benefit from Newsom's proposed clemency, detailing crimes involving murdered children, sexual violence, torture and serial killings.

Among them is Marcus Wesson, who was convicted of murdering nine members of his family in Fresno, including children and grandchildren. Wesson was also convicted of rape and sexual abuse involving his daughters and nieces, whom he subjected to years of abuse within a cult-like family structure.

Richard Allen Davis was sentenced to death for kidnapping and murdering 12-year-old Polly Klaas, who was abducted from a sleepover at her Petaluma home in 1993. The case shocked the nation and helped galvanize support for California's three-strikes sentencing law.

The White House also highlighted Isauro Aguirre, convicted of the torture and murder of 8-year-old Gabriel Fernandez in Los Angeles County. Gabriel suffered prolonged abuse before his death, a case that drew national outrage and later became the subject of a documentary series examining the violence he endured and failures to protect him.

Other offenders identified by the White House include Charles Ng, convicted of 11 murders involving captivity, torture and sexual violence, and Randy Kraft, the so-called "Scorecard Killer," convicted of murdering 16 men in crimes involving sexual assault and mutilation.

Chester Turner, convicted of murdering 14 women in Los Angeles, and Cary Stayner, convicted of four murders near Yosemite National Park, were also named.

Some of the cases involved the slaughter of entire families. Ramon Salcido was convicted of killing seven people during a Sonoma County rampage, including his wife and two young daughters, while former school administrator Vincent Brothers was convicted of murdering his wife, three children and mother-in-law in Bakersfield.

The White House's intervention comes as Newsom faces mounting opposition from California prosecutors and victims' advocates who argue that his sweeping clemency plan disregards the verdicts of juries and the suffering of families who spent years pursuing justice.

Although the condemned inmates would remain behind bars for life without the possibility of parole, the commutations would permanently remove the prospect of execution, regardless of the wishes of families who continue to seek that outcome.

"I was just sickened, frankly, for the crime victims in California who were slapped in the face by our governor," San Luis Obispo County District Attorney Dan Dow previously told Fox News Digital.

Orange County District Attorney Todd Spitzer previously accused Newsom of extending "mercy for the wolves … while inflicting the ultimate act of cruelty on the sheep."

The proposed commutations have not yet taken effect. Under California law, 382 requires a recommendation from the state Supreme Court because the inmates have multiple felony convictions. Newsom's administration has said it will not proceed with any of the commutations until the court acts.

The legal battle intensified last week when a coalition of California prosecutors and victims' advocates asked the state Supreme Court to halt the plan, arguing that Newsom failed to provide victims and their families an opportunity to be heard before pursuing the sweeping clemency action.

The court has invited additional briefs through Tuesday, Nov. 24, as it considers the governor's request. Newsom has asked the justices to act before his term ends in January.

California voters rejected efforts to abolish the death penalty in 2012 and 2016. The state has not carried out an execution since 2006, and Newsom imposed a formal moratorium on capital punishment shortly after taking office in 2019.

Trump has taken the opposite approach, directing the Justice Department on his first day back in office to prioritize seeking and implementing lawful death sentences. In April, the department announced additional measures to strengthen federal capital punishment procedures, including reinstating its previous lethal injection protocol and expanding available execution methods.
