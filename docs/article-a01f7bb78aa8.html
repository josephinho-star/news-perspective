Turkey’s ruling party, nationalist ally block inquiry into lawmakers’ ties to fund scandal

2026-11-25 16:16

Turkey’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) and its ally, the Nationalist Movement Party (MHP), voted Tuesday to block a parliamentary inquiry into lawmakers’ ties to an investment fund scandal that has affected nearly half a million investors, the ANKA news agency reported.

The vote was on whether to advance a motion from the opposition İYİ (Good) Party to examine whether lawmakers from any party had invested in the funds, made profits or used their political influence to help others make trades. The proposed inquiry would also seek to establish whether lists said to identify people who profited from the funds exist and make any evidence of corruption public.

Uğur Poyraz, the lawmaker who submitted the motion, told parliament a committee could establish who had money in the funds, who withdrew it and who lost savings. Durmuş Ali Keskinkılıç, speaking for the AKP, defended the criminal investigation and pledged that those responsible would be held to account.

Lawmakers also rejected a New Path Party motion to debate regulatory failures and complaints about repayments. A separate motion from the main opposition New Party to investigate investor losses and regulators’ conduct did not come to a vote because too few lawmakers were present at two quorum checks. The AKP and the MHP had blocked earlier attempts to open a parliamentary inquiry in October.

New Party leader Özgür Özel called on President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan to release the names of people who profited from the funds and disputed the government’s account of how much investors had recovered.

The crisis began in September when some funds could no longer meet withdrawal requests. Turkey’s securities regulator, the Capital Markets Board (SPK), ordered 131 funds run by seven firms to sell off their assets. Prosecutors suspect the funds were used to inflate share prices so people with advance knowledge could profit.

New arrest follows AKP deputy chair’s resignation

Hayati Yazıcı, a former trade minister and AKP lawmaker, resigned as a party deputy chair Tuesday after prosecutors questioned his son, Mustafa Yazıcı, and a court barred the younger Yazıcı from leaving Turkey. Hayati Yazıcı denied wrongdoing and said his son made no money from the funds.

Earlier Tuesday a court released former family minister and former AKP deputy chair Fatma Betül Sayan Kaya to house arrest. Investigators are examining share sales she made just before the funds were ordered to close. She and her husband have denied any wrongdoing.

An İstanbul court jailed Burhan Tunç Özkan pending trial on Wednesday, the Demirören news agency reported. Financial investigators traced a payment of 1 billion lira ($19.8 million) to Özkan from London-based brokerage Gildencrest Capital, formerly registered as Tera Europe, according to a prosecutors’ statement cited by the state-run Anadolu news agency. Özkan was detained Tuesday in the same operation as Kemal Akkaya, chairman of Turkish brokerage Bulls Yatırım.

Prosecutors are examining whether several connected groups manipulated share prices, with investigators examining separate groups believed to operate around Tera Yatırım and Pusula Yatırım, the pro-government Yeni Şafak daily reported on Wednesday. Justice Minister Akın Gürlek put the number of suspects at 632 on Sunday and said courts had appointed trustees to run 91 companies.

Interim payments and proposed reforms

The government aims to finish the current round of interim payments by Friday, November 27. Payments are capped at 1 million lira ($19,800) per investor in each eligible fund. Investors could still be owed about 184 billion lira ($3.6 billion).

The government also said it had finished drafting changes to the securities law that would let regulators intervene earlier, raise penalties for insider trading and market manipulation and recover money for investors from those responsible for losses. The draft is ready to go to the ruling party’s lawmakers.
