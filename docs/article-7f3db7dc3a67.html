Pilot who stabbed co-pilot on Israel-bound plane may have tried to crash it, says Netanyahu

2026-09-30 22:30

One of the pilots of a plane flying from the United Arab Emirates to Israel stabbed his co-pilot and appeared to try to crash the aircraft, the Israeli prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, has said, as Israeli officials said they were investigating whether the incident was an attempted terror attack on Israelis.

Eyewitnesses reported chaos onboard the flydubai flight, as the attacker tried to barricade himself and the wounded co-pilot in the cockpit, and then attempted to disable the controls, forcing the plane to descend 17,000 feet in just two minutes.

Passenger Yaniv Hayoun later recounted how he and other passengers heard screaming and felt pressure as the plane dived before forcing their way into the cockpit and finding the attacker at the instrument panel.

“I grabbed the attacker, put him in a chokehold, and dragged him out first,” Hayoun said during a meeting with Netanyahu after the plane had landed safely and the passengers were flown to Israel. “He was trying to destroy the instruments to disable the plane.”

As others wrestled with the attacker, Hayoun said he grabbed the controls and pulled them the way he had seen on television. Then other crew members took over.

Another passenger, Miryam Shira Ohayon, told reporters via her mother’s phone at Tel Aviv airport that a pilot had attempted to “basically carry out a suicide attack on the plane”, saying: “It was really on the way to crashing.” She added that a doctor onboard had treated the wounded pilot.

The stabbed pilot was identified as Indian national Captain Smit Machchhar, who was said to have made the emergency call and opened the door to the cockpit, allowing others to rush in and subdue the attacker.

“Despite being stabbed and seriously injured, he fought back, resisted, opened the cockpit door, and enabled passengers and crew to overpower the attacker – preventing a catastrophic mid-air disaster,” Netanyahu said, crediting him with saving 174 people onboard.

The plane was forced to make an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia, with the two pilots wounded but other passengers safe, according to a statement from Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz international airport, more commonly known as Tabuk airport.

The pilot accused of carrying out the stabbing was being questioned by Saudi authorities, Netanyahu said. Israel’s transport ministry said it would carry out a joint investigation with the defence establishment.

It was not immediately clear what motivated the apparent attack.

The Israeli defence minister, Israel Katz, described the incident as an “attempted jihadist attack”. The foreign ministry later briefed Israeli diplomats around the world that the incident was an “attempted terrorist attack against Israelis, the United Arab Emirates, and the Abraham Accords,” referring to the deal that normalised relations between the two countries in 2020.

Donald Trump said he spoke to Netanyahu on Wednesday, but suggested that the motive behind the incident remained unclear.

“I did speak to Bibi Netanyahu at length about this event, and it seems as though the co-pilot was perhaps a terrorist or perhaps a whack-job, and he stabbed the pilot,” Trump told reporters in the Oval Office.

The passengers of the diverted flight landed in Israel on Wednesday evening on a replacement aircraft, eight hours after their emergency landing. Netanyahu praised the bravery of the crew members and passengers who subdued the attacker and landed the plane safely.

“They saved many lives. They prevented a heavy disaster,” he said.

Israeli officials, speaking on condition of anonymity to reporters, said the attacker was an Omani national and that Israel’s current assessment of the incident was that it was a terror attack. There was no comment from Oman or the UAE.

Two and a half hours after flight FZ 1073 took off from Dubai, the plane sent out an emergency signal – squawk code 7700, indicating a general emergency – before sending out another – code 7500, indicating a potential hijacking attempt. Passengers heard screams from the front of the plane, and the wounded pilot managed to open the latch on the cockpit door, leaving it ajar.

The plane also began to abruptly descend, falling about 17,000ft in two minutes. As the plane began to dive, passengers and a crew member pushed their way into the cockpit, subduing the attacker. Other crew members then stabilised the descent of the plane and began to land it safely in north-western Saudi Arabia.

Among those who intervened was an off-duty British pilot, according to a passenger, Almog Itali, who said the plane was carrying a pair of relief pilots who were meant to fly the next flight.

“The relief pilots are European, one of them British, who entered [the cockpit] and pulled the aircraft up, pulled the yoke, stabilised the plane and landed us in Saudi Arabia,” Itali said in a clip posted by the BBC.

Also among those who stopped the apparent attack were Israeli security forces and air marshals, the Associated Press reported, citing sources close to the Saudi investigation into the incident.

Videos of the incident showed passengers screaming as the plane descended, as well as a blood-covered man who appeared to be the pilot injured and on the floor.

Yasmin Abu Kaiss, whose daughter was on the flight, told Reuters her daughter said she had heard screaming and a man with a knife had put a pilot on the floor, and that there was blood everywhere.

Israeli officials initially scrambled fighter jets after the emergency signals were sent out, fearing an attack on Israel or that the plane would be diverted to a third country. A spokesperson for the Israeli prime minister’s office then said the incident was not a hijacking, as media reports circulated of a fight between the two pilots.

As more information emerged about the violent nature of the attack, Israeli and international media began to report officials anonymously saying an investigation had been opened into whether this was an attack on Israelis.

On Wednesday evening flydubai announced it was suspending flights to and from Israel while an investigation into the incident continued.

The head of the Israeli military, Lt Gen Eyal Zamir, has postponed a visit to the US over the incident, where he was meant to have a meeting with US joint chiefs of staff and the head of US Central Command.

The incident came just days after Netanyahu had warned there were indications an attack would be carried out during Israel’s October elections. Israel’s military, quoted by Israeli media, said it had no intelligence that an attack was being planned.

Yair Lapid, the opposition figure and former prime minister, cast doubt on the connection between the alleged attack and Netanyahu’s warning, saying a security briefing on Tuesday night gave no information about any potential incident similar to what happened on Wednesday.

Israel experienced a series of hijackings and attacks on planes by Palestinian militants in the 1960s and 1970s, but has not experienced anything similar in decades. The country maintains some of the strictest airport security procedures in the world, including security checks before passengers board Israel-bound flights.

Flights between Dubai and Israel started after the countries normalised ties in 2020. Israel does not have diplomatic relations with Saudi Arabia, but in 2025 landed a plane in Riyadh because of a medical emergency.

Flydubai confirmed that flight FZ 1073 en route to Ben Gurion airport experienced an incident and landed safely in Tabuk on Wednesday. “Our teams are working closely with the relevant authorities,” the carrier said. “Further updates will be issued as additional confirmed details become available.”

Israeli media reported that a later flydubai flight heading to Tel Aviv turned around in mid-air and headed back towards Dubai after the incident.
