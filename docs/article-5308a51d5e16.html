Ukraine blames Russia for deadly sinking of Turkish-owned ship in Black Sea

2026-10-05 13:26

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy blamed Russian drones for an attack on a Turkish-owned ship that caught fire and sank in the Black Sea on Monday, killing two crew members, according to Romania’s Interior Ministry.

“A horrific strike by two Russian drones on a civilian vessel in neutral waters carrying corn,” Zelenskyy said in a post on X, adding that Azerbaijani and Indian nationals were on board.

Romanian officials said two crew members died and 11 were rescued after the 139-meter (455-foot) Royad Mammadov caught fire. The Interior Ministry said it sank about 30 nautical miles south of the fishing port of Sfantu Gheorghe.

“At around 11:00, Coast Guard border police officers were informed that a fire had broken out on board a commercial vessel located within Romania’s exclusive economic zone,” the ministry said in a statement.

“Eleven crew members have been rescued. Two deceased persons have also been identified,” it added.

Zelenskyy said the ship’s captain was killed in the strike.

The Black Sea has seen increasing drone attacks during the Russia-Ukraine war. Both countries increasingly rely on drone warfare as Ukraine, which borders NATO member Romania, fights Russia’s invasion launched in 2022.

Turkey has expressed concern over attacks on Black Sea shipping, and Turkish seafarers have staged protests over deaths in the attacks.

Turkey’s Chamber of Maritime Commerce (IMEAK) said last month that 226 civilian and commercial vessels had been attacked in the Black Sea since the start of the war, the vast majority this year.

Turkey has expressed frustration to both sides, and President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan told the UN General Assembly the attacks were “unacceptable”.

“Ukraine has repeatedly proposed concrete steps to de-escalate the situation and ensure security in the Black Sea,” Zelenskyy said on Monday, adding that “pressure is needed to restore global food security.”

The injured crew members were rescued by helicopter, Romanian officials said.

“Three of the crew members have been injured, eight are in good condition,” Irina Puscasu, spokesperson for the Romanian Naval Authority, told Agence France-Presse.

War-related objects found in the Black Sea more than doubled in six months amid the conflict, maritime risk expert Ambrey said in a report last month.

At least a third carried live payloads. The objects included unexploded ordnance, drones and other unmanned vehicles found washed ashore or floating at sea, the report said.

The increase was concentrated in Romanian, Bulgarian and Turkish waters, and the trend was expected to continue.

In August a Romanian F-16 fighter jet destroyed a maritime drone near an offshore gas project in the Black Sea. Romanian President Nicusor Dan blamed Russia for the incident.

In June a Ukrainian maritime drone exploded in Romania’s Black Sea port of Constanta without causing casualties.

Kyiv said Russian electronic interference had knocked the drone off course.
