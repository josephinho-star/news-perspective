Three Kyiv bridges hit during major Russian missile attack

2026-11-20 10:20

Russia has targeted bridges in Kyiv and killed eight during one of the largest air attacks of the war so far, days after Ukraine inflicted heavy damage on the strategically important Crimean Bridge.

Two people were killed in Russian strikes on a railway station in the western city of Lviv.

Ballistic missiles hit three bridges over the Dnipro River, vital routes which people in Kyiv rely on to move around the city, with six deaths confirmed in the capital.

Russian President Vladimir Putin had threatened to retaliate for Ukraine's strike on the Crimean Bridge, which the Kremlin built to link Russia and the occupied peninsula.

The three Kyiv bridges targeted overnight - the Podilsko-Voskresensky Bridge, the Darnytskyi Bridge and the Southern Bridge - have been closed to traffic, adding to disruption created by retaliatory strikes on bridges earlier this week.

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko urged people to "avoid travelling into the city by private car unless absolutely necessary".

He added that an unspecified critical infrastructure facility was also damaged and seven medical personnel were injured during the night's assault.

Ukraine's major operation to take out the road section of the Crimean Bridge earlier this week was a tactical and symbolic blow for Moscow.

Since illegally annexing the Crimea in 2014, Moscow has housed troops and military equipment there, with occupying forces travelling through the peninsula towards the front line.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said an operation to weaken the bridge's defences and ultimately cause part of it to collapse had been weeks in the making.

Russian President Vladimir Putin - who personally opened the bridge in 2019 to international condemnation - said the Kremlin's retaliation for the strike would be "far more painful".

Air raid sirens lasted non-stop throughout the night and were ongoing into the morning.

Ukraine's air force says almost 100 missiles were launched by Russia in its overnight attack - one of the largest attacks since the invasion began in 2022. It also involved around 400 drones.

The attack was "one of the most powerful" yet, according to Yuriy Ignat, head of communications for the Ukrainian air force.

Prime Minister Serhiy Koretsky said 28 people were injured, with an ambulance hospital in Kharkiv among the targets of an attack which hit nine regions.

Two people were killed in a hit on the Sknyliv railway station in the south-west of Lviv, the city's Mayor Andriy Sadovyi said.

He shared a picture of the ruined remains of what he said was the station's ticket terminal.

Ten people were injured and a rescue operation at the site is ongoing.

A Russian projectile reportedly landed inside Poland, with the armed forces searching a site 45km (28 miles) from its border with Ukraine.

Polish jets were scrambled on Nato's eastern flank as Russia's attack on western Ukraine continued.

The attack began hours after European leaders including UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham, French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said they were ready for negotiations involving Moscow to end the war in Ukraine.

The Berlin meeting of the so-called coalition of the willing, an international alliance of Ukraine's allies committed to providing security support in the event of a peace deal, was attended by Zelensky and US President Donald Trump's envoys.

Jared Kushner said the US "wants to make a deal with Europe by his side" after fears of an agreement negotiated between the US and Russia were reignited by Trump's one-to-one talks with Putin earlier this week.

He claimed Russia was "comfortable" with Ukraine having security guarantees based on Nato's Article Five, which requires allies to come to a country's defence if it comes under attack.

On Monday, Russia's deputy foreign minister Sergei Ryabkov pushed back against that, saying "quasi-Nato" membership for Ukraine would be "unacceptable" to Russia. The Kremlin has long cited the prospect of Ukraine joining Nato as a key justification for its war effort.

The US president dialled into the Berlin talks from Air Force One on his way back from China, where he met his Russian counterpart and Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

He reportedly questioned the decision of European leaders to commit €70bn (£58.6bn; $76.bn) in military funding for Ukraine.
