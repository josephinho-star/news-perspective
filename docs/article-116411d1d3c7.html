Trump, Putin and Xi release 'peace declaration' after historic meeting

2026-11-19 17:38

US President Donald Trump and his Russian and Chinese counterparts Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping have issued a "joint declaration of peace" following historic trilateral talks.

The three leaders met for 90 minutes in China's south-eastern city of Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.

While the presidents have previously had one-on-one meetings, these are their first trilateral talks.

In the declaration, they said "nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and that the countries planned to increase the production and supply of petroleum. They also said they were "committed to reform the UN" to respond more effectively to global challenges.

Thursday's statement also flagged the issue of immunity enjoyed by state officials and said "they agreed that the International Criminal Court and other similar institutions must not abuse their jurisdiction".

Trump praised the trilateral meeting calling it "a great and highly consequential trip", in a social media post while flying back to Washington on Air Force One.

In the same post, the president announced he would host a news conference to talk about the trip on Friday at the White House.

While in China, Trump met Putin ahead of the trilateral, in their first meeting since August 2025, where the US president greeted his Russian counterpart as his "friend".

He also spoke with Xi in a separate conversation, describing the pair's relationship as "very special".

Beyond the warmth and shared niceties, however, the three men had different goals and motivations for the sit-down.

The opening remarks of the trilateral gave some indication about what was to be discussed during the closed-door meeting.

Before giving the floor to his fellow leaders, Xi said that "positive interaction among major powers is not a matter of choice, but a mandatory requirement".

Putin followed this by highlighting a shared interest in ending the Ukraine war and said the conversation would also touch on food and energy security.

China has officially remained neutral since Putin launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, rejecting accusations from Kyiv and its allies that it has been tacitly supporting Moscow, including by exporting dual-use technology used by the Russian military.

Beijing has also repeatedly called for negotiations and a political settlement while avoiding condemnation of Russia's aggression.

Meanwhile, Trump closed by saying the presidents of three of the world's largest countries have a "special responsibility" to maintain productive dialogue.

"Too often past leaders have avoided talking to nations with whom they had disagreements," he said, adding: "We feel the opposite."

Trump referenced the three countries' nuclear capabilities and said they are "uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth".

It is "vital" that they co-operate to "combat the threat of nuclear proliferation in other countries around the world", he said, adding: "That's what we're doing in Iran."

"We're not going to let Iran have a nuclear weapon".

Trump then turned to Ukraine and said he and Putin have had a "long discussion" on the conflict there already.

He added that Putin "and I hope the other side, would like to see an end to that war".

Putin addressed the media following the trilateral meeting and described it as an "important chapter in the history of diplomacy".

He said Ukraine and Europe were not yet ready to make compromises to resolve the conflict in Ukraine - but he said Russia had invited EU representatives to Moscow to better understand Europe's position.

As the talks were under way in China, and even as Putin was holding his press conference claiming to be ready for peace, Ukraine's capital Kyiv was under attack from Russian drones.

Putin has repeatedly rejected all ceasefire offers from Ukraine, insisting on his maximalist demands which Kyiv and its European allies say are tantamount to de facto capitulation of Ukraine.

In Shenzhen, the Kremlin leader also vowed a "far more painful" retaliation in response to Ukrainian attacks on the bridge connecting Russia to Ukraine's occupied peninsula of Crimea. A missile and drone attack on Tuesday destroyed its central road section.

There was no mention from Putin of almost five years of Russian attacks on power plants, bridges and residential buildings in Ukraine.

The Russian president also directly addressed comparisons between his meeting with Trump and Xi and the 1945 Yalta Conference, where the leaders of the Soviet Union, Britain and the US met towards the end of World War Two.

"Nobody divided up the world at Yalta. It simply codified the realities that had emerged after WW2.

"And when it comes to today's conflicts, the realities on the ground will have to be accepted."

The Kremlin released a video showing Putin leaving the summit venue and saying goodbye to Xi.

"Thank you for organising this," Putin said. "I don't think anyone even expected everything to go so well."

Meanwhile, China's president described his meeting with Putin and Trump as "positive, constructive, and highly productive", according to the country's state broadcaster CCTV.

It added that Xi recalled "the history of fighting side-by-side during WW2", as the three leaders emphasised the importance of "cherishing the hard-won peace".

Thursday's trilateral meeting was reportedly the idea of Putin.

Some Russian newspapers called it a "diplomatic victory" as after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine many Western leaders were trying to isolate him.

The summit also marked the first time since Russia's war in Ukraine began that Putin took part in an international gathering attended by leaders from countries including Canada, Japan and Australia.

US lawmakers responded with frustration to Trump's warmth towards America's geopolitical adversaries.

"I was encouraged by some of the commitments that came out of the President's meetings in Asia, particularly the recognition that nuclear war must never be fought," Republican Senator John Curtis said in an emailed statement to the BBC.

But Curtis also said he was concerned about Trump's approach to the foreign leaders, adding that "Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping are not our friends".

"We should not treat them as though they share our values or interests," Curtis, who sits on the Senate foreign relations committee, added.

"Doing so risks undermining our national security, weakening trust with our allies, and sending the wrong message about where America stands."

Jeanne Shaheen, a ranking Democratic member on the same committee, chose harsher words in her comment.

"President Trump's 'friend' is a murderous war criminal," she wrote on X.
