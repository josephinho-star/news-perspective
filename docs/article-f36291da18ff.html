Mass casualty declared as multiple cars crash sending one spiraling down cliff in California

2026-11-28 00:09

California firefighters staged a dramatic rope rescue after a three-car crash sent a vehicle tumbling 50 feet down an embankment and hospitalized three people.

Four people were involved in the collision in Santiago Canyon before Orange County Fire Authority firefighters responded to the scene at 6:45 p.m. on Wednesday.

“With four patients involved, the incident was declared a Level 1 Mass Casualty Incident. OCFA’s Technical Rescue Truck (TRT) resources were utilized to stabilize the vehicle, extricate and package the patient, and establish a specialized rope rigging system to safely bring the patient back up to the roadway,” OCFA said.

“The complex rescue operation took more than an hour to complete, with three of the four patients transported to local hospitals. Incidents like these demonstrate the importance of our specialized rescue capabilities and the extensive training our firefighters undergo to respond to even the most challenging emergencies,” they added on.

A Level 1 MCI is the lowest tier among the five-level scale that is used by fire departments and paramedics. It typically involves 5 to 10 patients and is declared when an incident is “overwhelmed by the number and severity of casualties.”

This comes after one person was injured on 241 Toll Road near Santiago Canyon on Nov. 18. An SUV collided with a truck in a wrong-way crash and left large amounts of debris scattered around the road.

Two people were also killed following a crash at the 241 Freeway in Lake Forest near Santiago Canyon in mid-September. A 57-year-old woman slammed head-on into a 39-year-old man carrying a 7-year-old girl passenger.

The young girl survived but the other two tragically lost their lives.
