Drones hit 2 Turkish-owned ships off Bulgaria, 11 Turkish crew among those rescued

2026-10-06 12:28

Drones struck two Turkish-owned ships in the Black Sea off Bulgaria on Tuesday, sinking one and setting the other on fire, as rescuers searched for missing crew members and evacuated 18 sailors, including 11 Turkish nationals.

The Togolese-flagged Alfa Watan sank, while all 18 crew members — 11 Turkish and seven Indian — were rescued from the Palau-flagged Able. Two were hospitalized, one with burns and the other with a head injury.

Both vessels are Turkish owned, according to the Equasis merchant shipping database.

The attacks came a day after another Turkish-owned cargo ship caught fire and sank off Romania in what Ukraine said was a Russian drone attack, underscoring the growing risks to commercial shipping from the Russia-Ukraine war.

Bulgaria launched a search operation about 70 nautical miles east of the coastal town of Byala. The Maritime.bg shipping news website identified the sunken vessel as the Alfa Watan.

A Bulgarian ferry that witnessed the explosion and rushed to the scene found overturned life rafts and life jackets near the sunken ship, but no crew members, Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev told reporters. He did not specify how many people were missing.

The attack “constitutes a flagrant violation of maritime law and, more broadly, of the rules of navigation,” Radev said.

“Such an attack within Bulgaria’s exclusive economic zone is absolutely unacceptable,” he said.

According to the available information, the Able was carrying wheat and heading south, while the Alfa Watan was sailing north, Radev said.

“The situation significantly complicates navigation in the Black Sea, drives up insurance premiums and makes maritime traffic extremely difficult,” he said before convening an emergency meeting of ministers.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen also called attacks on civilian shipping in the Black Sea “unacceptable.”

“While the exact circumstances of these incidents are still being established, the developments are very concerning,” she wrote online.

Another Turkish-owned ship sank off Romania

Romania reported on Monday that a grain ship had caught fire and sank in the Black Sea. Ukraine blamed Russia for the attack on the Turkish-owned Royad Mammadov.

The Azerbaijani captain died and another Azerbaijani crew member is missing, according to Azerbaijan’s Foreign Ministry, as cited by Russia’s RIA Novosti news agency.

Drone incidents have become increasingly frequent across Eastern Europe in recent months as the effects of Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine spread. Attacks have also extended across the eastern Black Sea.

At least 45 merchant vessels were attacked over the past 12 months in areas of the Black Sea previously not listed as high risk, maritime risk consultancy Ambrey said last month.

No strikes were recorded in those areas during the preceding 12 months, Ambrey said, adding that the increase was driven by attacks farther from the Russian and Ukrainian coasts.

Turkey’s Chamber of Maritime Commerce (İMEAK) said in August that 226 civilian and commercial vessels had been attacked in the Black Sea since the war began, the vast majority this year.

Tuesday’s attacks were the first such incident off Bulgaria.

In August a drone exploded near the Trans-Balkan gas pipeline linking Turkey to Ukraine, close to Bulgaria’s border with Romania.

Ukraine denied targeting Bulgaria after the Bulgarian Defense Ministry said the drone was of a type “widely used by the Ukrainian military.”

Agence France-Presse, with reporting from Turkish Minute
