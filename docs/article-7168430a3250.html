Ford opposes EU plan to exclude Türkiye-made vehicles

2026-11-23 10:51

Ford Europe President Jim Baumbick criticized the exclusion of vehicles made in Türkiye under the EU’s proposed “Made in Europe” rules, saying the move would penalize Ford for investments it made under the rules in place at the time.

In an interview published on Nov. 23 by German business daily Handelsblatt, Baumbick said the proposal would make Ford Transit vans assembled in Türkiye ineligible for vehicle purchase incentives and public contracts.

Under the planned rules, those benefits would be reserved for vehicles produced entirely in the EU, the newspaper reported.

Baumbick said excluding vehicles made in Türkiye would weaken automakers operating in Europe. He urged EU policymakers to focus on helping those manufacturers compete with their main rivals in China.

He said half of the Transit’s parts are produced in Europe and that the vans were developed and tested in Germany, the United Kingdom and Belgium in line with EU rules at the time.

Ford invested heavily in production in Türkiye, a major EU trading partner, under those rules, Baumbick said. He called on the EU to protect existing investments.

Asked whether Ford could move Transit production to its plant in Cologne, Germany, Baumbick said the investment in Türkiye was only 3 years old and that the company had yet to recoup it.

Moving assembly now would cut the vehicle’s product cycle short and could reduce manufacturing capacity, he said. Relocating production would add costs without improving the van or making Ford more competitive, he added.

Baumbick said automakers make investment decisions over horizons of 10, 20 or 30 years. He urged policymakers to use incentives to guide future investment and give companies enough certainty to plan production over those periods.
