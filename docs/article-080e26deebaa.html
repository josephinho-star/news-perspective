Trump blasts interest rate uptick and claims ‘hostile’ Federal Reserve board made decision ‘only for political reasons’ – live

2026-09-16 23:47

President denied that his hand-picked Federal Reserve chair, Kevin Warsh, had not defied him by voting to raise interest rates, claiming he advised Warsh to vote as he did

Trump claims he told Fed chair Kevin Warsh to vote with the rest of the board for rate hike

Speaking to reporters on his way to give a rally speech in North Carolina, Donald Trump denied that his hand-picked Federal Reserve chair, Kevin Warsh, had not defied him by voting to raise interest rates, claiming that he had advised Warsh to vote as he did.

Asked if he still has confidence in Warsh, despite the rate hike Trump deplored, the president said:

“I do. I mean, I’m relying on Kevin, but he’s got a very tough board. He’s got a board that was put there by a lot of other people. And the interest rates are too high. They’re not appropriate,” Trump replied. “I told Kevin, I said, ‘You might as well vote with the board because it’s just not going to matter.’ The board is very hostile. They’re very political. They’re doing the wrong thing. They’re a bunch of politicians. They are people put on by politicians.”

“We should be paying the lowest interest rate anywhere in the world because we have the strongest credit,” he added. “If we wanted to get rid of deficits, which we could do with the swipe of the pen with every country, we’d make a trillion and a half dollars a year. We’d pay off our debt. We’d do all sorts of things. But we haven’t chosen to do that. But at some point, we will. Interest rates are too high.”

Later, asked again about Warsh, Trump said: “It doesn’t matter because he doesn’t have the votes... No matter how good a job, he’s got a hostile board... They’re raising that only for political reasons, and that’s a raise against Trump.”

Trump says Kennedy Center will 'end up being ripped down' if he doesn't get his name on it

Asked by a reporter in North Carolina why it is so important to him to have his name inscribed on the Kennedy Center, a memorial to another president, Donald Trump said he deserves “recognition” for saving the center no one else seemed to think was in dire condition until he took office.

“It’s a mess,” Trump said. “The Kennedy Center for many years has lost tens of millions of dollars, even hundreds of millions of dollars ... For me to go and fix it – I’m quite capable of fixing it – and then have to subsidize it for years to come, because you will lose with the Kennedy Center – they were losing hundreds of millions of dollars and sometimes hundreds of billions – this is before I got involved. So, for the Trump administration, for me, to get involved and to take that on long term and to carry it or to raise money to carry it, I think that the Trump administration should certainly have recognition because, frankly, if we don’t do that, it’s going to close.

“It’ll end up being ripped down. It’s in very, very bad shape, very dangerous shape. You see steel falling down. You see plaster falling off ceilings. You see a lot of dangers. It’s been very badly maintained for 25 years,” he continued.

“I’d like to be able to save it, but it’s going to take a lot of subsidy into the future. And for us as an administration to want to do that, I think the Trump administration should be recognized.”

House passes Russia sanctions and tariffs bill despite concerns about giving Trump more tariff power

The US Congress has passed a sweeping bill allowing Donald Trump to raise tariffs on top buyers of Russian energy and issue other punishing sanctions against the Kremlin following a pitched battle between lawmakers backing Ukraine and sceptics worried the bill gives the president too much power.

The Lindsey O Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 passed in a 262-159 vote on the last day before Congress goes on recess ahead of the midterm elections. Graham, who died earlier this year, co-authored and submitted the bill more than a year and a half ago.

The long delay in the vote was widely considered to be scepticism from the Trump administration, which had opposed additional sanctions and then recently changed its stance to support submitting the bill as efforts to negotiate with the Kremlin to end the war have yielded few results.

However the bill faced last-minute hurdles in Congress as members of the Republican party’s isolationist wing were joined by Democrats who warned that Trump could use the bill’s new tariffs authority against allies instead.

The bill would mandate new sanctions on Russian political and military officials, and foreign networks helping to supply Russia’s war machine and evade existing sanctions. It would also target Russia’s so-called fleet of “shadow tankers”, which have allowed the country to continue to ship crude around the world despite sanctions on its energy exports.

It would also allow Trump to impose tariffs of up to 100% on the top purchasers of Russian energy, including China, as well as other countries helping the Kremlin evade sanctions.

Senior Democrats voiced opposition to the bill during a heated floor debate. “Why in the world would this Congress give this president additional authority to visit that kind of economic harm on the American people?” said Hakeem Jeffries, the leading Democrat in the House, during a speech on Wednesday, adding he would vote against the bill.

Pentagon chief technology officer downplays concerns over AI safety and calls to nationalize AI firms

The US Department of Defense’s chief technology officer, Emil Michael, dismissed safety concerns raised by the leaders of artificial intelligence companies in an interview with CNBC on Wednesday.

Michael, a former Uber executive, previously led the Pentagon’s efforts to press Anthropic to allow the defense department to use its Claude AI model for fully autonomous lethal weapons or domestic mass surveillance.

Michael repeatedly said he opposes increased government regulatory oversight of AI companies and claimed that the alarms raised by leading AI firms were part of a “coordinated campaign to scare people to make irrational decisions that benefit” those firms.

He also suggested, as he had in a Fox News interview on Monday, that “the fear-mongering” is driven by an ideology called effective altruism.

“The amount of dollars being spent in DC now, directly and indirectly by this effective altruist movement,” Michael said, “I don’t think the country has ever seen.

“I think in the next few years, you’re going to see more dollars poured into this sort of industry from a lobbying standpoint, and to specific congresspeople and senators, than we’ve ever seen before,” he added. “And the promoters of some of these extinction death-cult-like philosophies are part of that effort.”

When Michael was asked if he favored the Trump administration seeking to nationalize or take partial government stakes in AI firms, he replied: “I hope not.”

He pointed out that the AI giants could become some of the biggest companies “in the history of the world. In America, that’s a good thing. We want others to come up alongside them. We don’t want government to get in the middle. That doesn’t really go well, in terms of nationalization.”

Kennedy Center closes, and looks set for construction, in apparent violation of court order

In apparent violation of a federal court order, the Kennedy Center was closed to the public on Wednesday and fencing was installed as construction looked set to begin, according to the testimony of a legal assistant working for the firm that represents the congresswoman Joyce Beatty, the Ohio Democrat and Kennedy Center trustee who sued to stop Donald Trump from closing the performing arts venue and adding his name to its facade.

The sworn statement was submitted to the District of Columbia court overseeing the lawsuit by Bridget Kennedy-Bailey of the Washington Litigation Group, in support of Beatty’s motion for an emergency hearing on the performing arts venue’s apparent closure.

Judge Christopher Cooper, who is overseeing Beatty’s suit, had ordered that the institution could not close to the public without his approval.

According to Kennedy-Bailey, when she visited the venue on Wednesday morning, she found the doors locked and was told by Kennedy Center employees that the facility was closed.

She also saw crews installing black metal gates outside the center, blocking all access, and white and orange barriers that “looked like those used on construction sites”.

Just before noon, someone who works in security for the center told the legal assistant “that the gates are for ‘public safety’ because they are doing construction inside the building”.

Kennedy-Bailey also testified that someone who said he works in public relations for the Kennedy Center told her that the building is closed for “repairs, and that staff are not allowed in without a hard hat”.

“I asked him if the construction was occurring today,” Kennedy-Bailey said, “and he stated that he was not sure but that it ‘seems like it’”

At the end of her sworn statement, Kennedy-Bailey said: “I saw a forklift carrying what appeared to be a more permanent form of fencing drive over to the north side of the building. I saw the forklift run into and hit the pillars on the building several times.”

Mitch McConnell has to be prompted on how to vote in televised Senate committee meeting
