Democrats criticize Mike Johnson for sending lawmakers home for recess a day early – US politics live

2026-09-16 20:33

Democrats slam Johnson decision to send House lawmakers home early

Many Democrats are slamming the decision by Mike Johnson, the Republican House speaker, to send lower chamber lawmakers home a day early for the pre-election recess. The move means that votes scheduled for Thursday, including on a proposal to impeach Pete Hegseth, the defense secretary, have been canceled.

In a straight-to-camera video posted on X, Melanie Stansbury, a Democratic congresswoman from New Mexico, said the impeachment vote was the “only one explanation” to cancel the House’s legislative agenda on Thursday.

“The speaker obviously, and obviously the president does not want that, and so instead they’re just gonna send us home,” Stansbury said. “They don’t want to hold Hegseth accountable for his blatantly illegal war in the Middle East that is harming Americans, that has killed service members, that has killed thousands of civilians, that has dragged our country into an economic spiral and is literally harming every single American.”

Some Republicans also expressed frustration with the speaker’s decision.

“Canceling yet another day of votes is unacceptable,” said Ralph Norman, a GOP representative from South Carolina. “The House has now canceled at least 63 scheduled voting days this Congress, including nine days this month alone, and now we’re leaving Washington until after the election.”

Trump reacts to interest rate hike by demanding massive cut instead

Donald Trump responded to the Federal Reserve’s unanimous decision on Wednesday to raise its benchmark interest rate by a quarter-percentage point, to a range of 3.75% to 4%, by demanding a massive cut in rates.

Writing on his social media platform, the president also offered a bizarre argument that the United States economy would benefit “If we stopped Trading with every country that we have a Deficit with, which is most of them”.

Here is the president’s complete statement on the interest rate hike, taken to combat inflation driven at least in part by the rise in oil prices caused by the war on Iran he started and has not been able to end.

Interest Rates in the United States should be 1%, or less, because we are the Best Credit in the World — BY FAR. Our Country is BOOMING with new Investment! If we stopped Trading with every country that we have a Deficit with, which is most of them, we would make, at least, 1.5 Trillion Dollars a year. The word “Deficit” is nothing more than a fancy word for LOSS. We are “carrying” almost every country in the World, and that cannot go on any longer. LOWER THE INTEREST RATES FOR THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, AND FAST!

The central bank’s move is the first time the Fed has raised rates since July 2023 and clearly set Kevin Warsh, Trump’s pick to be the new Fed chair, on a collision course with the president.

“The plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long,” Warsh said on Wednesday. “This summer’s inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved.”

Mitch McConnell has to be prompted on how to vote in televised Senate committee meeting

Mitch McConnell, the former Republican Senate majority leader who returned to work this week after a months‑long absence, had to be prompted by colleagues as to how to vote near the start of a televised meeting of the Senate agriculture committee on Wednesday.

Video of the moment shows that when McConnell’s name was called by the clerk, in a procedural vote on the farm bill, he initially did not respond.

As colleagues and aides scrambled to help the 84-year-old, the senior Democrat on the panel, Amy Klobuchar, could be seen mouthing the word “aye” in McConnell’s direction. A moment later, as the clerk called his name again, the Republican committee chair, John Boozman, leaned in McConnell’s direction and audibly prompted him to say “aye”.

“Aye,” McConnell said in a raspy voice from his wheelchair.

Susie Wiles, the White House chief of staff, announced that she is “cancer free”, exactly six months after Donald Trumprevealed in March that she had been diagnosed with early-stage breast cancer.

Wiles said in a social media post on Wednesday that her “pathology results came back clear” after a visit to the Mayo Clinic this week.

Wiles, Trump’s 2024 campaign manager and the first woman to serve as White House chief of staff, initially addressed her diagnosis in a statement released hours after Trump first revealed it online, and then brought it up at a meeting of the Kennedy Center board of trustees in the White House East Room.

Trump told the media covering the event that day that Wiles had already begun treatment for a “minor difficulty” that she would overcome. “She had a diagnosis – you probably saw it – and she’s gonna take care of it immediately as opposed to waiting,” he told the gathering. “I said do it immediately because with that particular ailment, the faster the better.”

The House speaker, Mike Johnson, has cancelled votes currently scheduled for Thursday, meaning that lower chamber lawmakers will now head back to their districts for the midterm recess a day early. The change to schedule means that the House will avoid voting on Republican congressman Thomas Massie’s resolution to impeach the defense secretary, Pete Hegseth.

The move comes as dozens of Democratic lawmakers have asked Johnson to postpone the pre-election recess to address the deluge of callsfor Congress to regulate the development of artificial intelligence. Lawmakers from both parties slammed the decision to cancel votes. Many Democrats said the “only one explanation” was to avoid the Hegseth impeachment vote. And some Republicans, including Massie, insisted: “When other people want to go home, I’m here to work”.

Johnson, however, insisted that“it’s time for our members to go home to their districts and make their case to the American people”. Outside his office, the speaker told reporters that the lower chamber “has done its work”. Johnson also addressed Massie’s impeachment resolution, calling it “a publicity stunt by someone who wants attention”.

The Federal Reserve decided to raise the interest rate today by a quarter of a percentage point, to a range of 3.75-4%. The decision was unanimous, with all 12 members of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) agreeing with the increase. The vote potentially sets Kevin Warsh, the current chair, on a collision course with Trump – who nominated Warsh under the expectation that he would cut rates. This comes after a federal judge blocked the Kennedy Center from both shutting down and for Trump to have his name emblazoned on the front of the building.

On Tuesday, the handpicked board voted again to close the institution’s performing arts building in Washington, citing safety and financial concerns. However, Judge Christopher Cooper had previously ruled that the center’s name cannot change, shy of congressional approval, and said that the institution could also not close to the public.

In her court filing, Beatty said that she had received reports that an announcement had been made over the loudspeakers at the Kennedy Center that it had been closed. This comes after Trump said on Tuesday that the venue would close “immediately” and threatened to cancel a planned renovation of the institution until the federal circuit court approves the formal name change. According to the administration’s lawyers this was reportedly an accident after “someone improperly triggered the fire alarm”. However, on Wednesday a staff member of the law firm representing Beatty said they sought to enter the center’s main building, but was “prevented from doing so and was informed by severals ecurity staff members that the building is currently closed to the public”.

“Given the unexplained and unlawful closure,” Beatty argues in her motion, it is possible that the Trump administration intends to “undertake immediate steps in order to make public access practically impossible for the foreseeable future, including by demolishing public avenues of access to the building, and thereby evade the Court’s order by fait accompli”.

Top foreign affairs committee Democrat says he will block Trump's $2.8bn arms sale to Israel

Representative Gregory Meeks, the top Democrat on the House foreign affairs committee, announced today he will not clear the Trump administration’s proposed $2.8bn sale of 2,000lb bombs to Israel, citing concerns that the arms would not be used in accordance with US and international law.

“I remain firmly committed to Israel’s security and its ability to defend itself against serious regional threats,” Meeks said in a statement, noting that the sale of 40,000 bombs “raises grave, unresolved concerns about how these munitions could be used in densely populated areas of Gaza and Lebanon”.

According to the Associated Press, Congress has been informally notified about the pending deal, most of which is to be paid for with foreign military financing – in which US taxpayer dollars are given to Israel to turn around and buy the weapons from the US.

Meeks added: “This decision does not diminish congressional support for Israel’s legitimate defense needs; it reflects Congress’s responsibility to ensure that US-funded weapons are used lawfully, responsibly and with meaningful safeguards for human life.”
