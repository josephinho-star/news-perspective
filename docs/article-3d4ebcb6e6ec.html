ANTA Group and UNHCR launch new partnership to continue supporting 300,000 displaced children and young people

2026-11-17 05:01

Earlier this month in Geneva, Switzerland, Chinese sportswear company ANTA Group and UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency, signed a new three-year global partnership, committing another $1.5 million and 1.2 million items of sportswear and equipment over the next three years to Moving for Change. This renewal signifies ANTA’s willingness to support education and sporting opportunities for displaced children and young people.

If you’re not familiar with ANTA Group, it is a global multi-brand sportswear group founded in 1991. The portfolio spans many brands including ANTA, FILA, DESCENTE, KOLON SPORT, JACK WOLFSKIN and MAIA ACTIVE to name a few. As part of its global philanthropy initiatives, ANTA Group has pledged to focus on turning athletic resources into educational access.

The partnership renewal was formalized by Wu Yonghua, Executive Director and Co-CEO of ANTA Group, and Tressa Rae Finerty, Deputy High Commissioner of UNHCR. “Young people forced to flee need more than safety. They need opportunities to learn, develop their talents and feel part of a community. By bringing together education and sport, our partnership with ANTA helps create those opportunities and supports young refugees to build their futures,” said Finerty. Ambassador Li Chijiang, Chargé d’Affaires a.i. of the Permanent Mission of China to the United Nations Office at Geneva and other International Organizations in Switzerland, Ding Shizhong, Board Chairman ANTA Group, and Olympic champion and ANTA brand ambassador Fan Zhendong were also in attendance for the signing.

A global brand gives back

Board Chairman of ANTA Group, Ding Shizhong told the Post, “ANTA Group entered into a global partnership with UNHCR and kicked off the program in 2023 to enhance the safety, education, and well-being of displaced children around the world. We aim to help them build resilience and hope for the future.”

A high-impact initial three-year rollout provided $1.5 million in funding and more than 1.2 million items of sportswear and equipment, benefiting 300,000 displaced young people in Africa. Children received financial assistance, material support, mental health support and other help so they could stay in school. Now the sportswear brand continues on its commitment to keep displaced kids learning, playing, and thriving by officially renewing its Moving for Change global partnership with UNHCR.

For ANTA Group the core philosophy behind its effort mirrors the discipline rooted in the company’s history. Back home in China, its sports-related programs support less‑developed regions across China and have benefited 10 million youths. The Group operates dual philanthropic platforms: corporate‑led giving and the founders’ family foundation. Through these platforms, it runs nearly 20 long‑term CSR programs covering diverse areas, with the total donations through these two philanthropic platforms exceeding RMB 4 billion.

Ding explains why the company is extending its practice into international humanitarian assistance: “Now we are extending our experience into international humanitarian assistance, so that what we leave around the world is not just our brands’ footprint, but a Chinese company’s journey of responsibility. We look forward to building on our new three-year partnership to help forcibly displaced young people rebuild their lives with confidence, integrate into their communities and move towards a brighter future through sport.”

As the leading sportswear company in China, ANTA spares no effort in R&D of sport technology. Over the past decade, its cumulative investment in innovation and R&D has exceeded RMB 20 billion, and it has continued to increase its annual investment in professional technology and product innovation. It consistently supports the development of Chinese sports. Across its portfolio of brands, the Group has provided competition, training and lifestyle gear to 40 Chinese national teams, and has applied the same technologies used by national teams to mass-market sports products. By embracing an all-round brand portfolio, ANTA Group aims to unlock the potential of both the mass and high-end sportswear markets. It is also actively expanding business into different international markets, including the fast-growing Southeast Asian market and the Middle East and African markets; as well as, the mature and highly competitive North American and European markets.

Sports transcend barriers

Christina Li, Vice President of ANTA Group, who led an ANTA delegation to refugee sites in Ethiopia to learn firsthand the impact of the program, said she was so moved when a girl told her what she wants is to return to a normal life.

She said, “We also saw children playing, laughing, and chasing balls with unbridled joy. The dusty pitch was alive with energy during a football match. For youth raised in displacement, access to sports and education is often limited. Yet, there they were, experiencing the pure, unifying power of play. Their radiant smiles were a testament to the positive change this initiative is creating, on and off the field.”

Over the next three years, the partnership will continue to focus on three core areas: primary education; sports for protection; ensuring education continuity during emergencies. ANTA Group will help students and teachers alike by building better learning hubs, upgrading sports facilities, and ensuring young refugees have the gear they need to compete, heal, and learn teamwork.

Ding also noted the Olympic spirit and the commitment to advancing human well‑being form people’s shared aspiration and bond.

“Looking further ahead, we will work hand‑in‑hand with UNHCR and all partners to ensure more children forced to flee from their homes can learn, play sports and grow alongside their peers. That is what Moving for Change is all about.”

The power of philanthropy

Ding hopes the initiative sets a benchmark for sustainable, outcome-focused humanitarian corporate partnerships. “We believe sport can transcend linguistic and cultural divides and bring people of diverse backgrounds together. Sport helps forcibly displaced children heal from trauma and find joy.”
