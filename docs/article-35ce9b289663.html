LSU spent $150 million on Lane Kiffin and a new roster, it finished worse than under Brian Kelly | Ian Miller

2026-11-23 11:27

In the real world, early September was not so long ago. In the world of modern college football, it's been an eternity.

Just a few months ago, the UCLA Bruins were a punchline, not a contender to win the Big Ten Conference and potentially secure a first round bye in the College Football Playoff. The Ohio State Buckeyes were widely viewed as the best team in the country, thanks to quarterback Julian Sayin and elite talent on defense.

Then, there's the LSU Tigers.

The Tigers made the biggest splash of the 2025-2026 offseason by hiring Lane Kiffin from Ole Miss to revitalize their program after firing Brian Kelly. Kiffin commanded an exorbitant salary, with nearly $100 million in guarantees. He hired the most expensive support staff in the country, with over $14 million in assistant salaries.

They revamped the roster too, putting together massive NIL deals and the No. 1 transfer portal class, with an estimated cost of around $40-50 million.

Then, week one. And boy, did it look like their decision-making had panned out. The Tigers beat Clemson 51-10 in a game that wasn't even that close. It was a complete demolition, and seemed to validate hiring Kiffin and their roster-building process. Kiffin even gave a typically sarcastic comment after the game, referencing the SEC's push to stop them from rostering players who'd formerly gone pro.

With a 31-3 lead at halftime, Kiffin told ESPN's Holly Rowe, "Imagine if we'd had pro players."

Essentially, "look how good we are already, we didn't even need them."

Fast-forward two months, and it turns out LSU might have needed them. Badly.

The Tigers' loss to the Tennessee Volunteers in Knoxville put a final nail in the coffin of a dismal season. Yes, LSU will beat up on hapless Arkansas this weekend, but every single outcome that looked possible after that Clemson game has now officially fallen by the wayside.

LSU have had a difficult schedule, one of the toughest in the country, in fact, but managed to get the least out of it. There was the emotionally charged return to Oxford against Ole Miss, which it lost. It lost at Kentucky. Then it was blown out at home by Alabama. It had a late lead against Texas, only to give up a devastating touchdown to Arch Manning and Raleek Brown for another loss.

On Saturday, Tennessee simply dominated the LSU offense. A whopping eight sacks, 10 tackles for loss, and just 43 rushing yards allowed on 34 attempts. It was an ugly game with six combined turnovers, and Tennessee was fortunate to cross 100 passing yards, but once again, the Tigers came up on the wrong side of what should have been a competitive game.

Winning all five of these games against solid teams might have been too much to ask for any team, but elite programs could and would expect to win some of them. LSU won none. Just 3-5 in the SEC, with the three wins coming over a woeful Texas A&M team, Mississippi State without Kamario Taylor, and Auburn.

After all that talk, all the money, all the swagger and arrogance, they sit here in late November at 6-5 with one of the most disappointing seasons by any team in recent memory. And maybe even more frustrating, it's worse off than any full season under Brian Kelly.

Kelly went 10-4 in 2022, 6-2 in the SEC. He went 10-3 in 2023, once again 6-2 in the SEC. He was 9-4 in 2024 and 5-3 in the SEC. Then was 5-3 when fired during the 2025 season. So in three full seasons in Baton Rouge, Kelly lost seven games, total, in the SEC, with a 17-7 overall record. Kiffin's lost five of his first eight.

$150 million, plus a $54 million buyout to Kelly, and they lost more SEC games in eight weeks than Kelly lost in two seasons.

Maybe even more frustrating, and damning for Kiffin, is that the Tigers, by computer models based on offensive, defensive and special teams efficiency, would still be expected to play like one of the top-15 teams in the country. Their losses, at least some of them, have been a result of not making plays when it matters, or getting hurt by costly mistakes. Like, say, 16 penalties for 98 yards against Tennessee. Some of that falls on the $150 million coaching staff.

Hard to get worse than that.

What's more incredible, even, is that this is who the Tigers are, and what the program has been. After the 2019 national championship winning team with Joe Burrow, it has been comparable to a mid-tier Big Ten program by most metrics: Minnesota.

One Golden Gophers-focused account on X put together a table showing the similarity in outcomes between the two teams in recent years.

And yeah, that's really close. Despite Minnesota having far fewer resources available to them.

Some of this comes down to LSU playing tougher schedules; it's had 29 games against AP ranked teams compared to 19 for Minnesota, but it's won a lower percentage of those matchups, implying either a talent or coaching gap between them and their opponents.

It's hard to believe, but LSU just hasn't been the type of program that they, or many fans, think they are. Maybe Kiffin's second season in Baton Rouge will be different; they'll still have a big recruiting class, transfer portal group, and plenty of money. But spending $200 million, between coaches, players, and buyouts, and finishing 6-5? Even with underlying performance suggesting a top-15 team?

That's about as bad as it gets.
