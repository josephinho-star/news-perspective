Malaysia’s air quality turns hazardous as Indonesian wildfires burn

2026-08-31 07:10

Pollution index hits 476 near the border, forcing school closures and triggering emergency cloud-seeding plans in Sarawak

Serian in Sarawak, closest to the frontier with Indonesia’s West Kalimantan province, recorded the highest reading of 476 as of Monday morning.

Kuching, Samarahan and Sri Aman all logged readings above 300 as of 10am, according to Malaysia’s Environment Department.

The API scale classifies anything above 300 as hazardous; readings of 201-300 are “very unhealthy”, 101-200 “unhealthy”, 51-100 “moderate” and 0-50 “good”.

Malaysia has activated its National Open Burning Action Plan and National Haze Action Plan in response, mobilising government agencies to tackle both the fires and the health risks posed by the smoke.
