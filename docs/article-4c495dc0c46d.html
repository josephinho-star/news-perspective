Öcalan to join panel overseeing PKK’s laying down of arms: adviser

2026-09-17 14:34

Jailed Kurdistan Workers’ Party (PKK) leader Abdullah Öcalan will join a panel overseeing the PKK’s laying down of its weapons as part of a peace process aimed at ending decades of deadly violence with Turkey, a senior presidential adviser said on Thursday.

Turkey’s parliament passed a law in August offering limited amnesty to PKK militants, marking the first legislative step toward ending the conflict.

As part of the process, four subcommittees were established to oversee practical aspects of the PKK’s decision to disband following a call by Öcalan, with one focusing on the laying down of arms.

The new law did not specify what would happen to Öcalan, 77, who has spent the past 27 years in prison on İmralı Island near İstanbul.

Speaking to the Turkish Press Federation, Mehmet Uçum, a senior adviser to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, said Öcalan would take part in the subcommittee focusing on the laying down of arms.

However, he made clear that the arrangement did not mean Öcalan would be released.

Uçum said the arrangement had been reached to allow Öcalan to play a more effective role in the process, according to remarks carried by private broadcaster NTV.

“This is a dialogue process being conducted with Öcalan. The objective is a difficult one: to ensure the dissolution of the militant group,” he said.

Öcalan has played a key role from his prison cell on İmralı in advancing efforts to end the conflict, with the PKK leadership repeatedly demanding his release as part of the process.
