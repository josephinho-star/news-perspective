Hundreds of Thanksgiving flights cancelled and train schedules slashed as nor’easter touches down

2026-11-26 20:39

Surviving awkward family conversations and Thanksgiving dinner with the in-laws is usually the most stressful part of the holiday — but this year, simply getting to the dinner table might be a bigger nightmare.

Mother Nature is serving up an extra helping of holiday travel hell.

Hundreds of flights have been canceled, thousands more delayed, MTA train schedules slashed, and railway tracks submerged in floodwaters as a powerful nor’easter batters the East Coast, wreaking havoc during one of the year’s busiest travel periods.

As of 3:06 p.m. Thursday, 419 flights to, from, or within the United States were canceled and another 1,763 were delayed, according to FlightAware.

For Americans who gambled on flying out on Thanksgiving morning, a few hours could have made all the difference.

One 34-year-old New Yorker who flew from Newark to Houston Thursday morning told The Post she made it to Texas without a hitch.

“Oh yeah, we landed no problems,” she said. “It felt like a completely normal travel day.”

But as the day wore on and weather conditions deteriorated across the Northeast, other travelers weren’t nearly as lucky.

Turns out, the early bird doesn’t just get the worm — it might also make it home in time for turkey.

Thanksgiving flights scheduled after 9 a.m. face double the risk of a major delay, The Post previously reported.

Major airlines, including Delta, United and American, have issued travel waivers allowing eligible passengers to rearrange their flights.

Meanwhile, those who thought they’d outsmart the storm and traveled by land were essentially traveling by sea.

In New Jersey, train tracks at Hoboken station were submerged in floodwaters, while NJ Transit scrapped select additional trains originally scheduled to accommodate crowds attending the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade — which was itself postponed until Saturday.

New Yorkers relying on the MTA were also dealing with schedule changes making it nearly impossible to navigate the city.

“The usual MTA changes in one word. F$CKED.,” wrote one disgruntled city-dweller on X.

And for some Americans fortunate enough to make it home, the biggest Thanksgiving dilemma wasn’t whether the turkey would be dry — it was whether it would get cooked at all.

Widespread power outages were reported across the East Coast, including in New Jersey.

The travel chaos comes as a record 81.5 million Americans were expected to venture at least 50 miles from home over the seven-day holiday travel period, including nearly 6 million domestic air travelers, according to AAA.

And they’re paying handsomely for the experience.

The average domestic round-trip flight booked for Thanksgiving this year cost $800 — a whopping 17% increase from last year, according to AAA.

Making matters worse for stranded travelers is the impending post-Thanksgiving travel rush.

Last year, the Sunday after Thanksgiving set a record for airport security screenings, with more than 3.1 million passengers passing through TSA checkpoints in a single day.

This year, the agency anticipates screening more than 17.6 million passengers over the holiday travel period, with more than 3 million expected to descend on airports on Sunday alone.

And while weather conditions are expected to improve across the Northeast heading into Friday, travelers scrambling to rebook their canceled flights could find themselves competing for coveted seats with millions of Americans heading home after the holiday.
