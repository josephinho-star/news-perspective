Turkey detains 33 in renewed probe into nationalist leader’s 2009 death

2026-09-25 13:16

Turkish police detained 33 people on Friday in a second round of raids in a renewed investigation into the 2009 helicopter crash that killed nationalist politician Muhsin Yazıcıoğlu and five others, Justice Minister Akın Gürlek announced.

Prosecutors opened proceedings against 47 people suspected of taking part in premeditated murder in connection with the activities of the faith-based Gülen movement and of trying to prevent the circumstances of the crash from coming to light, Gürlek said in a post.

The Gülen movement is a civic network inspired by the late Muslim cleric Fethullah Gülen whose participants have run schools, charities and other social initiatives in Turkey and abroad. Turkey labels it a terrorist organization, but other governments or major international bodies do not designate the movement as such.

Police searched 45 locations in 16 provinces in an operation coordinated from Ankara, Gürlek said. Ten of the 47 people were already in prison, while police were seeking the other four.

Investigators are examining allegations that the search for the helicopter was misdirected, false information notes were prepared and witnesses were manipulated, Gürlek said. He did not identify the people targeted in Friday’s operation, describe their alleged roles or disclose evidence connecting the movement to the crash.

In July a Turkish court jailed 19 people pending trial in the first round of the renewed investigation and released eight others under judicial supervision. Prosecutors had transferred the case from the Kahramanmaraş Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office to Ankara on June 12.

Yazıcıoğlu, the founder and leader of the nationalist Grand Unity Party (BBP), was traveling between campaign events ahead of Turkey’s March 2009 local elections when his chartered helicopter crashed near Göksun in the southern province of Kahramanmaraş on March 25, 2009.

The crash killed Yazıcıoğlu, three BBP officials, pilot Kaya İstektepe and İhlas News Agency reporter İsmail Güneş.

The case has been the subject of controversy for more than 17 years due to failures in the search-and-rescue operation, the removal of GPS navigation equipment from the wreckage and allegations that public officials interfered in the subsequent investigation.

Güneş contacted emergency services after the crash and said he was seriously injured, but rescue teams did not locate the wreckage for approximately 47 hours. His body was found separately five days later.

Yazıcıoğlu’s family and political associates have long argued that his death should be investigated as a possible assassination rather than treated solely as an accident. No court has conclusively established that the crash was deliberately caused.

Several public officials have previously been prosecuted over failures in the rescue operation, the handling of evidence and alleged interference in the investigation. Separate proceedings examined the removal of navigation equipment from the wreckage and allegations that Gülen-linked officials attempted to steer the inquiry through irregular procedures and fabricated evidence.

Erdoğan began targeting the movement after corruption investigations in December 2013 implicated him, members of his family and his inner circle, and then stepped up the crackdown after a failed coup in 2016.

Gülen, who died in the US in 2024, denied any involvement in the coup or violence and called for an independent international investigation.

A 2017 inquiry by the UK Parliament found no evidence that the movement directed the coup.

The United States never approved requests made by Turkey to extradite Gülen because Ankara’s evidence did not meet US legal standards.

The movement also denies involvement in Yazıcıoğlu’s death.

More than 126,000 people have been convicted of alleged Gülen links and 11,085 are in prison, while at least 134,000 public servants were dismissed, often through emergency decrees and without individual review.

United Nations bodies have documented arbitrary detention, denial of due process and torture in post-coup cases, while the European Court of Human Rights had found violations affecting 3,554 applicants by June under rulings that faulted Turkish courts for treating association and lawful acts as proof of terrorism.
