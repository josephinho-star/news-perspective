Joy Behar, 84, makes very intimate confession about her sex life, leaves ‘The View’ hosts in stitches

2026-11-20 19:09

“All I know is my vagina’s on life support,” Behar quipped during the conversation, as her co-hosts erupted in a fit of laughter.

Earlier in the discussion, the comedian — who has been married to husband Steve Janowitz since 2011 — joked that you “can’t wait that long” to get pregnant, or else you’ll be “sharing diapers with your baby.”

The other women — Alyssa Farah Griffin, Ana Navarro, Sara Haines, and Sunny Hostin — generally seemed to respect the choice, but wouldn’t have a baby at a later age themselves.

“I’m 58, and I’ve got a 20-year-old and I’m exhausted,” Hostin said. “I have a 24-year-old, I can’t imagine starting that journey at my age.”

However, they did speak about the double standard that happens when a man has a child at an older age, like Al Pacino having a baby when he was 83 in 2023.

Navarro joked that she would have to be taken out on a “stretcher” if her 85-year-old father called her to tell her that he was expecting.

Interestingly enough, this isn’t the first time that Behar has spoken about her vagina this week during “The View.”

On Wednesday’s episode of the show, the women spoke about a viral clip from the “Real Housewives of London” Season 2 reunion, in which Karen Loderick-Peace tells her co-star Amanda Cronin that she has a “low-budget vagina.”

“I have a low-rent vagina, but mine has a senior discount,” Behar joked to her co-hosts during the show.

The joke left Whoopi Goldberg embarrassed, as she hid her head on the “Hot Topics” table.

Haines also got in on the fun, as she joked that her vagina was more of a “small business” that has “inconsistent hours and a very tired owner.”

In the past, the women have been open about their sex lives on the daytime program.

During a Feb. 27 episode, she joked that the best place that she’s had sex is “wherever vacation is,” due to the fact that they have three young children at home.

“We don’t have a lot of screen time, so we will literally give them sugar and video games, put them in a corner, and they need us immediately,” she explained about her children.

“You can’t even get there mentally. You’re like, ‘Ahh!’ It’s annoying. It’s a turnoff. It’s like your parents calling when you settle in for a night,” she said.
