Trump’s ballroom construction can resume at White House, supreme court rules – as it happened

2026-09-01 01:58

The Supreme Court issued a decision on Monday that gives Donald Trump the ability to proceed with his plans for construction of a new White House ballroom.

Trump’s plans for a White House ballroom have been an ongoing point of contention and legal action, with opponents arguing that Trump has no legal authority to pursue the project without congressional approval. In a 5-4 decision, the court granted the Trump administration’s request to block a lower court ruling that would have prevented work from being done on the ballroom.

The Supreme Court also issued a temporary order earlier this month which allowed work to continue on the ballroom, resulting in Democratic Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer accusing the court of “rubber stamping Trump’s vanity-driven, gilded corruption”.

Providers are seeing more patients in need of abortions later in pregnancy as abortion bans and restrictions proliferate in US states, all while access to insurance falls.

Those bans and restrictions have also meant that later abortion care access has grown over the past decade in a handful of haven states and cities.

Patients are showing up “later and sicker” in need of reproductive healthcare, said Diane Horvath, a co-founder of Partners in Abortion Care in Maryland.

About 41 states have abortion bans or significant restrictions at some point in pregnancy. More than 20 million people have lost Medicaid coverage since 2023, while between 3 and 5 million people have lost or dropped their coverage through Affordable Care Act exchanges in recent months.

“People are getting chucked off of Medicaid. The premiums are all going up,” Horvath said. Instead of seeking care early in pregnancy, she said: “They’re just not going in. There’s no place to go.”

The cost of later abortion – which is loosely defined as anything past the first trimester, the time during which the vast majority of abortions occur – tends to rise the later it happens in a pregnancy.

“Sometimes you’re looking at a $22,000 gap with maybe three days to pull together funding,” said Alisha Dingus, executive director of the DC Abortion Fund (DCAF), one of the largest abortion funds in the country. Rising costs and decreasing availability “may create substantial barriers to accessing later abortion care”, a recent Jama study found.

Pentagon plays down report of Hegseth regularly overruling military advice

Pete Hegseth regularly receives formal objections from senior commanders and proceeds anyway, the Pentagon’s top spokesman has said, as he tried to discredit a Washington Post report that four generals and admirals had advised against prolonging the war against Iran.

“Of course, we demand candid military advice,” Sean Parnellwrote on X. “Non-concurs are standard. The Secretary sees them constantly. That’s how you run a real military.”

Parnell was responding to the Post’s account of four four-star commanders who had lodged formal “non-concurs” against a plan to keep more than 50,000 US troops in the Middle East through September, with some deployments potentially running into 2027.

In dismissing the story, Parnell did not dispute that the objections had been filed, or that Hegseth fields such dissent on an ongoing basis.

“Publishing highly classified assessments from the Secretary’s orders book is a crime,” he wrote. “It’s a betrayal of the force. And much of what they’re publishing is inaccurate … We are in great shape around the world. This story isn’t about readiness. It’s TDS and PDS – they hate President Trump and Secretary Hegseth more than they love America.”

It was unclear which parts of the story the Pentagon deems to be inaccurate. A community note attached to the X post noted that, while leaking classified material is illegal for government employees, the US supreme court ruled in 1971 that the first amendment protects the press’s right to publish classified information of public interest.

Kalshi permanently bans George Santos after he bet on his State of the Union plans

Prediction market platform Kalshi has issued its first lifetime ban on none other than the disgraced former Republican congressman George Santos, who bet against his own announced plans to attend Donald Trump’s State of the Union address.

In a filing, Kalshi said of the affair: “The Compliance Department found that Santos made these statements with the intent to manipulate the price of the Yes or No contracts that he intended to purchase. Ultimately, these statements did in fact manipulate the price of said contracts.

“The Compliance Department has issued a penalty permanently suspending Santos from direct or indirect access to Kalshi and has imposed a penalty of $71,356.”

The company said he had made $17,839.57 from his wagers.

Santos responded in a post on X this morning: “Hey @Kalshi thanks for the lifetime ban from your gambling platform. Let’s see how much longer you guys are around for.”

Santos was released from prison in New Jersey in October after Donald Trump commuted his seven-year, three-month sentence for wire fraud and aggravated identity theft.

The former congressman was revealed to have fabricated much about his life during his congressional campaign and was expelled from Congress while under federal investigation for fraud in 2023.

Following his release, Santos returned to X in February to tell his millions of followers that he planned to attend Trump’s speech. “I’m gonna be in the gallery,” he wrote.

But he didn’t attend the event. “I guess people lost money,” he said on his podcast in March. “Some people made unexpected money. That’s to show you how fragile these markets are.”

He was also fined $35,000 and received a three-year trading ban from the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission last month for alleged market manipulation over the incident.
