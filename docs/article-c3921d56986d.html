[UPDATE] 11 students injured, 2 seriously, in shooting outside high school in western Turkey

2026-09-22 08:17

A student opened fire outside a high school in western Turkey on Tuesday, wounding 11 students, two of them seriously, before police detained him, government officials said.

The shooting occurred at around 8 a.m. (0500 GMT) near İnci Üzmez Vocational and Technical Anatolian High School in Turgutlu, a district of Manisa province near the western city of İzmir, the Manisa Governor’s Office said.

School shootings are rare in Turkey, although two attacks on consecutive days in southern Turkey in April sparked widespread anger.

Several media outlets, including the İHA news agency and private broadcaster NTV, initially said eight people had been injured. Police and emergency crews rushed to the scene.

The DHA news agency broadcast security camera footage of the alleged attacker, a young person wearing black jeans, a black jacket and a green top, walking along a street with what appeared to be a rifle.

İHA published a similar image apparently showing the same person running with a gun.

Footage from outside the school showed police wrestling a person in a white shirt to the ground before leading him away. A woman could be heard screaming as people pressed against the school fence and filmed with their mobile phones.

Turkish media identified the suspect as a ninth-grade student at the school. His name has not been released because he is a minor.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek said the Turgutlu Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office had opened an investigation and assigned two prosecutors to the case, according to state-run TRT Haber.

The suspect’s mother, father and grandfather were also detained. Investigators are examining how the child obtained the gun, how it had been stored and whether his parents had fulfilled their duty of supervision, Gürlek said. Claims that the student had experienced bullying before the attack are also being investigated and have not been established as a motive.

The Education Ministry sent three inspectors to Turgutlu, suspended classes at the school for the day and deployed teams to provide psychological support to students, teachers and parents.

The shooting came five months after two attacks on consecutive days raised concerns about security in Turkish schools.

In April a 14-year-old opened fire at a school in the southern province of Kahramanmaraş, killing a teacher and nine students aged 10 and 11. The attacker died at the scene.

A day earlier another teenager opened fire at his former high school in the southeastern province of Şanlıurfa, wounding 16 people before taking his own life when confronted by police.

Following the attacks, President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan dismissed a deputy education minister and pledged tighter controls on gun ownership.

He said school safety would be one of the government’s top priorities.

“When we look at similar attacks around the world, especially in the United States, we see one of the perpetrators’ aims is to terrorize society,” he said.

Tuesday’s shooting came a day after Education Minister Yusuf Tekin and Interior Minister Mustafa Çiftçi signed a protocol setting out security measures for the 2026-2027 academic year, which began September 14.

Çiftçi said school security had been strengthened following the April shootings.

“To ensure such pain is never experienced again, I would like to remind everyone that we must be extremely vigilant this year,” he said.

He said 31,000 school grounds supervisors had been deployed for the first time.

The Interior Ministry also conducted a new security assessment and assigned police officers to remain permanently at schools classified as first- or second-degree risks, Çiftçi said.

Schools classified as lower risk have been assigned police liaison officers who work with school administrators.

Police responsible for security, public order, narcotics and juvenile issues will conduct regular patrols around schools, he said.

The new measures also include inspections of school buses and reviews of abandoned buildings near schools.

Çiftçi and Tekin were traveling to Manisa following Tuesday’s attack, according to Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz.

Agence France-Presse, with reporting from Turkish Minute
