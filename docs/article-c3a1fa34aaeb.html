Christa Pike remains in heavily guarded hospital room, ‘may not fully recover’ after botched Tennessee execution: attorney

2026-10-04 22:48

Christa Pike, the convicted murderer who survived a botched execution attempt last week, may never fully recover, her lawyers revealed, as she remains unconscious in a heavily guarded hospital room.

Pike, the first woman to be put to death by Tennessee in over 200 years, has been hooked up to a ventilator since last Wednesday after shockingly surviving two rounds of a lethal injection she was sentenced to for the sadistic 1995 murder of her classmate.

She is the first death row inmate to survive a successful injection of pentobarbital — which had left her snoring and unconscious but still breathing while strapped to a gurney at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution death chamber on Wednesday night.

“We don’t know right now, and we may never know, what sort of torture she endured during that process,” one of Pike’s lawyers, Stephen Ferrell, told Jake Tapper on CNN Sunday.

While Pike, 50, dodged death, her condition is still not much better, having failed to regain consciousness or say anything since the attempted execution.

She remains “critically ill,” Ferrell said. “We don’t really know the prognosis yet and may not know for several days.”

“It remains to be seen what the future is,” he continued. “I think one of the main things that will leave this up in the air for a while is the fact that we don’t know what the prognosis is — as in, the long-term prognosis. If she recovers — she may not fully recover.”

“And there may be issues about her competence. We just don’t know any of this,” the attorney said.

Pike’s legal team has been unable to see her as she languishes in the hospital, where she is “guarded,” he added.

Pike was sentenced to death in 1996 for the brutal murder of her 19-year-old classmate Colleen Slemmer in a jealous rage.

Ferrell has requested that Tennessee Gov. Bill Lee commute Pike’s sentence to life in prison in light of the failed execution.

Unless Pike fully recovers, it’s unlikely the state will try to execute her again, according to legal experts.

“She can’t be executed if she is brain injured and doesn’t understand the proceedings,” Professor Deborah Denno of the Fordham School of Law told The Post.

“The United States Supreme Court recognized in a case involving a dementia patient in 2019 that you can’t execute someone who’s not aware of the punishment that they’re going to receive.”
