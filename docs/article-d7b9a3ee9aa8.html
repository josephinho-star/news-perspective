Merz says ‘shock’ AfD victory in key German state ‘is an election result we cannot simply move past’ – Europe live

2026-09-07 11:49

Merz concedes 'most severe election defeat' in 'years, or decades'

Merz begins by saying “we are all deeply shocked” by the result in Saxony-Anchalt.

“We did not expect it to turn out this way,” he says.

He says the vote “will have repercussions” and says even a glance at who congratulated the AfD shows “the level of international attention this result has garnered.”

He says that “when nearly 60% of voters … cast their ballots for political parties that fundamentally call into question our country’s democratic institutions … it is an election result we cannot simply move past as if nothing happened.”

He concedes this is “the most severe election defeat the CDU has suffered in years, indeed in decades.”

AfD win celebrated '2,500 kilometers east from here,' Merz says

In further comments, Merz comes fairly close to drawing a link betweeen AfD and Russia, as he says “2,500 kilometres in the east, the election results were being celebrated.”

“That comes as no surprise, given who tried to foster and bring about this outcome. In that sense, we know exactly who we are dealing with here.”

Merz says he wants to focus on this in the coming days and weeks, and says he is aware of “our shared responsibility for our country.”

“I am aware of my personal responsibility for our country. I am determined to live up to this responsibility,” he says, seemingly dismissing any talk he could choose to step down from the post.

Merz says Germany needs “fundamental reforms” in response to the result, a challenge he says he is ready to face.

“It is a matter of nothing less than the future of our country and the opportunities available to our children and grandchildren – opportunities we must not squander,” he says.

He says the government needs to get better at explaining its actions and rationale behind them.

Merz says it is “the very essence of democracy” that people’s attitudes shift, but he says “there is one thing that is not up for a vote: the rules by which we live together and the values enshrined” in the constitution.

He says Germany “bears a special responsibility due to its history,” and will “always uphold our political order.”

Far-right AfD win in Germany is ‘grave moment’ for Europe, politicians warn

Mainstream politicians around Europe have expressed alarm after the far-right Alternative für Deutschland (AfD) swept to a historic state election victory in Germany, while nationalist figures cheered the result as a mandate for change.

While falling just short of an absolute majority, the AfD surged into first place in the Saxony-Anhalt state election on Sunday, exit polls showed, putting the far right within reach of power at the state level for the first time since the second world war.

'We cannot simply carry on as before,' Merz reportedly told party bosses, as he prepares to respond to AfD win
