Arthritis drug restores 100% of hair on some patients with severe alopecia in global trial

2026-08-30 12:00

Tests at hospitals in the US, Europe and China discover medication can help to reverse hair loss caused by the autoimmune disease

The condition is called alopecia areata. It is an autoimmune disease where the body’s own immune system mistakenly attacks hair follicles, causing unpredictable hair loss. Sometimes it is just a few patches but in severe cases, the entire scalp, eyebrows and even body hair can vanish. Until now, there has been no reliably effective treatment.

Enter upadacitinib. This once-daily oral medication was previously approved to tame inflammation in various forms of arthritis. But scientists suspected it might also help with alopecia areata by calming the same kind of immune overreaction that destroys hair follicles.

On average, the volunteers had lost about 84 per cent of their scalp hair. Some had not seen any regrowth for at least six months.

Participants were randomly assigned to take either a 15mg dose, a 30mg dose, or a placebo pill every day. The results, published in JAMA Dermatology on August 12, were striking.

Within just 24 weeks, roughly half of those taking upadacitinib – both doses combined – saw at least 80 per cent of their scalp hair grow back.
