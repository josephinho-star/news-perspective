Senate Republicans block Democratic effort to end US war with Iran

Senate Republicans on Thursday blocked a Democratic effort to end the US war with Iran, voting 49-50 against a House-passed war powers resolution as the conflict passed the 200-day mark, with 19 American service members killed.

The measure passed the House earlier with bipartisan support. Senate Democrats, led by the minority leader, Chuck Schumer, and the senator Andy Kim of New Jersey, forced the vote under the War Powers Resolution, which allows lawmakers to bring such measures to the floor.

In June, the Senate adopted a similar House-passed resolution 50-48, with four Republicans – Bill Cassidy, Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski and Rand Paul – voting in favor. But a month later, after the ceasefire collapsed, the chamber voted 47-49 against advancing a legally binding joint resolution, with Collins the only Republican in support.

After the vote, Ron Wyden, the Democratic senator from Oregon, acknowledged the uphill fight. “We’re going to stay at it until we win,” he said. Wyden said the biggest challenge was senators who were “pretty cautious and looking at the constitutional authority”.

Democratic senator Tim Kaine from Virginia echoed that sentiment, saying: “We’re going to keep doing them every week because we see Republican candidates out in the field now starting to change their position.”

“We started with one Republican vote, and we have five now,” he said. “So we’ll keep at it.”

Still, not all Republicans are convinced on the reasons to end the war. Dan Sullivan, the Republican senator from Alaska, said: “The war powers vote is about removing American forces from the region, and when there are troops in contact, I don’t believe in doing it.”

Speaking before the vote, Kim, a former state department official who served in the Middle East, said Americans were “sick and tired of this reckless and expensive war”.

“Democrats have taken action. We’ve come to this chamber 11 times in the past 208 days to try to bring this war to an end,” Kim said.

He cited a congressional budget office report estimating the war cost nearly $40bn in its first four months, and said at least 19 US service members had been killed and more than 750 injured. Kim also said estimates showed higher petrol prices had cost American households about $900 each since the war began.

“Chaos is not a negotiating tactic,” Kim said. “Trump is simply over his head here.”

Melania Trump and Peng Liyuan toured Smithsonian during Trump and Xi's meeting

After leaving their husbands at the White House earlier, Melania Trump and Peng Liyuan toured one of the largest Asian art museums in the US, celebrating cultural ties between the US and China during a high-profile diplomatic visit to Washington.

The Associated Press reports that the first ladies visited the Smithsonian’s National Museum of Asian Art in a display of soft power that united two figures known for fusing style and diplomacy. It was the first public meeting in nearly a decade between the spouses, one a former fashion model and the other a famed soprano.

They arrived to crowds gathered on sidewalks across from the museum, many waving Chinese and American flags.

Inside the museum, the first ladies toured the Peacock Room, a gilded green, blue and gold dining room that was once housed in a London mansion before opening to the public in Washington in 1923. The room was created by American artist James McNeill Whistler.

They also paused to look at a stone disk from ancient China, along with “Princess from the Land of Porcelain,” an oil painting by Whistler hanging in the Peacock Room and featuring a kimono-clad woman standing and holding a fan. Later, youngsters from Hope Chinese School serenaded the women with renditions of famous Chinese folk songs, “Song and Smile” and “Jasmine Flower.”

Palestinian president Mahmoud Abbas calls on Trump to "prevent the annexation" of Palestinian land in Gaza, West Bank

Speaking remotely to the United Nations General Assembly’s annual gathering of world leaders, Mahmoud Abbas, the longtime president of the Palestinian Authority, pressured Donald Trump to follow through on a peace plan for Gaza that would prevent the Israeli takeover of Palestinian land.

“We call upon President Trump to fulfill the commitments he made to the leaders of the Arab and Islamic states last September to prevent the annexation of Palestinian land and to reject displacement.”

Abbas said that while he welcomed Trump’s 20-point peace plan for Gaza, he warned that its implementation was falling short and called for the next phase to be accelerated, with a clear timetable for a lasting political settlement:

“Regretfully, we have not seen any of this materialise on the ground,” Abbas said. “The Israeli killing and aggression continue to claim the lives of men, women and children in Gaza. Nearly a year has passed since the adoption of [the peace plan] and the establishment of the Board of Peace. And only one year remains of the designated transitional period before the end of 2027.”

“Therefore, we called for accelerating the implementation of the second phase rather than wasting any more time. The transitional period must not be allowed to become a permanent or open-ended situation without any clear time-bound political horizon.”

“Rather, it must lead to the reunification of the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, under the authority of the State of Palestine and its legitimate institutions, in accordance with the principle of one state, one government.”

Abbas delivered his remarks via a pre-recorded video for the second year in a row, after the Trump administration refused to grant him a visa.

The State Department said in a statement that the Palestinian Authority and the Palestine Liberation Organization had “failed to live up to their commitments”, accusing the groups of “taking actions to internationalize the Israeli-Palestinian conflict including through the International Criminal Court and the International Court of Justice” and of “glorifying terrorism,” among other allegations.

Abbas pushed back on those allegations, saying: “We reject these unlawful punitive measures, and we reject the accusations and justifications on which they were based.”

The Palestinian Authority president painted a bleak picture of the situation in both Gaza and the West Bank, but concluded with a vow, directed at the Palestinian people themselves, that they would not yield to efforts to remove them from their land.

“We will not leave our homeland, we will not accept displacement, and we will not allow anyone to decide our future on our behalf,” Abbas said to conclude the speech.

Xi pushed US to oppose Taiwanese independence, according to Chinese readout of meeting

The subject of Taiwan came up during a bilateral meeting in which Xi Jinping urged the US to oppose “Taiwan independence,” according to a readout from the official Chinese news agency Xinhua.

The White House did not immediately provide its own readout of the discussion.

Xi had been expected to press Trump this week to halt arms sales to Taiwan, the Associated Press reported. After visiting China in May, the US president said he was suspending an arms package to Taiwan worth about $14 billion and called it a “very good negotiating chip.”

The move left some US allies in Asia and even some Republicans concerned that Trump may be tempted to soften Washington’s support for Taipei in exchange for economic wins with China.

China has long asserted territorial ownership of Taiwan, a democratically governed island that relies on US power to counter Beijing’s threats. Despite backing Taiwan, the US has traditionally avoided taking an official stance on its independence.

Trump's pick to lead FDA bucks president on vaccines in hearing

Heidi Overton, the White House adviser who Trump has nominated to lead the Food and Drug Administration, publicly broke with the president on the subject of vaccine safety during Senate nomination hearing – though she tried not to say that in so many words.

Facing the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee, which holds the keys to her confirmation, Overton was pressed on the subject directly by committee chairman Sen. Bill Cassidy, a Republican and physician from Louisiana who has frequently pushed back on vaccine-skeptics in the Trump administration.

“You were behind the president in the Oval Office when he signed an executive order to break up the vaccine schedule into more doses,” Cassidy said to Overton as he opened the hearing.

He continued: “The order is based on zero scientific evidence. At the same time, the president called the MMR vaccine ‘quite lethal.’ There ain’t no scientific evidence of that. And, by the way, this comes as we see Americans dying from measles for the first time in decades.”

Later, questioned directly whether the MMR vaccine was safe and effective, Overton affirmed that “the MMR vaccine is not lethal” and that it is “safe and effective.”

But when pressed to say outright whether she disagreed with the president, or detail specifically what she would do to counter any anti-vaccine information being peddled to Americans, Overton was more vague.

“So you disagree with the President with that?” Cassidy said.

“The MMR vaccine is currently licensed and approved by the FDA,” Overton replied, avoiding any mention of the president. “It is safe and effective. And it is our best took right now in the public health response to the measles outbreak.”

She later added: “Every vaccine on the US market right now are safe and effective according to the FDA.”

Democrats demand answers after man dies in car crash during ICE traffic stop in Michigan

Leading Democrats and immigration advocates in Michigan are demanding transparency from federal authorities after a man died in a fiery car crash as he attempted to avoid agents from Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

Mario David Coronado Juarez, a Guatemalan national, was killed Sunday in Grand Rapids when his vehicle struck a tree at high speed and burst into flames shortly after the ICE officers tried to conduct a traffic stop.

His death came the same day as two other incidents of ICE violence, as critics say officers are pursuing increasingly aggressive tactics in support of Donald Trump’s immigration crackdown.

In Austin, Texas, an agent shot and wounded a Venezuelan man, Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, who was working as a Door Dash delivery driver. And in Evanston, Illinois, a US citizen needed hospital treatment for injuries sustained when two ICE agents attempted to arrest him in an apparent case of mistaken identity.

A Washington Post analysis in July found that at least 17 motorists had been shot by federal immigration agents during enforcement operations, and six of those died.

Gretchen Whitmer, the Democratic Michigan governor, said she had ordered a review of the Grand Rapids incident.

Joseph Trigg, chief of the Grand Rapids police department, asked the Michigan state police (MSP) to join the investigation, noting in a statement that his own traffic detectives had been “limited in their ability” to obtain information from federal agencies.

“MSP, under the direction of the governor and attorney general, may be in a better position to determine the timeframe and any other potentially relevant circumstances, and any next steps that may be appropriate,” Trigg’s statement said.

According to the immigration advocacy group Movimiento Cosecha in Grand Rapids, Coronado, 36, was from the Guatemalan city of San Marcos, and came to the US alone to work as a construction worker and send money home for his wife and three young children.

Hillary Scholten, a Democratic Michigan congresswoman, said she had asked ICE for an explanation of its agents’ actions prior to the accident.

“No traffic stop should end with a fatality. Something went wrong, and we need to know what and how,” she said in a statement that requested “a transparent investigation into facts we don’t yet know.”

ICE and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), its parent agency, have not yet commented on the incident.

Trump and Xi, speaking to reporters, make friendly overtures in White House remarks

Shortly after Xi Jinping’s arrival at the White House, Donald Trump gave remarks alongside the visiting Chinese president, in which the two expressed optimism for deepening the testy US-China relationship.

Trump, speaking, noted that Xi is the first Chinese leader to make a second formal visit to the White House, and said the two have “forged a truly great friendship” since Trump was first elected in 2016.

Trump went on to say that he’s “very proud” of the US-China relationship, and says the two countries are working towards a “more balanced trade relationship.” He said the two leaders will also discuss “pressing issues” concerning security, technology and artificial intelligence.

“The decisions we make in these areas today can promote peace and prosperity for decades and decades to come,” Trump said.

Taking to the podium after him, Xi said both China and the United States are “great countries” with “great peoples,” and that he came to Washington to “carry on our friendship, expand co-operation and promote the wellbeing of both our peoples”.

He added that China and the US “shoulder a responsibility to promote human progress” and said he is willing to work with Trump to “steer the ship of China-US ties”.

“The interests of China and the United States are deeply intertwined,” Xi said, adding that it would be hard to solve many of the world’s problems without the two countries co-operating with one another.

Xi then issued an invitation for 100,000 American students to visit China in the next five years, and announced that two pandas – Ping Ping and Fu Shuang – will settle into their new home at the Atlanta zoo in the coming days. The move is reminiscent of China’s gift of two giant pandas to the Smithsonian national zoo in 1972, following Richard Nixon’s historic visit to Beijing in the detente era of the Cold War.

To the blare of trumpets, Donald Trump and first lady Melania Trump walked out of the White House residence onto the South Lawn, where they greeted Xi Jinping and his wife Peng Liyuan as they stepped out of a black vehicle and onto the red carpet.

They warmly shook hands and then posed for the cameras.

Anti-Beijing and pro-Beijing protesters gathered outside the White House ahead of Xi’s arrival, the Associated Press notes.

“Down with Xi Jinping,” chanted those condemning Beijing’s brutal policy in Tibet. They also chanted, “Shame on Xi Jinping.”

Another group has gathered about a block away with banners welcoming the Chinese leader.

The two camps are separated and there has been no direct clash.
