Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologizes to employees, says he is in therapy: reports

2026-11-24 19:08

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang reportedly apologized to his employees Tuesday and said he is in therapy.

The disclosure came a day after his stunning return as CEO, just over a week after he resigned amid revelations of his infidelity.

“I made some terrible mistakes,” he said during a company livestream, according to the New York Times. “I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and of course, I hurt all of you.”

The exec also shared he has been going to therapy “every day for an hour and a half” and that the process “been helping me a lot,” according to Wired.

Huang also sought to reassure female employees, touting Nvidia’s flexible work policies that allow women to balance their careers and family lives.

“If I did anything that put that in jeopardy, I cannot tell you how sad I am, and I’ll do everything I can to fix it,” he was quoted as saying.

Huang reportedly insisted that Nvidia would not tolerate violations of its code of conduct, but cautioned that employees who run afoul of the rules would not necessarily be fired.

That “does not mean, the moment you violate a code of conduct, we walk you out the door,” he said, according to Wired, adding that “facts, circumstances, a lot of assessments” would factor into such decisions.

Huang reportedly insisted he didn’t come back because he’s a “tech bro” who’s “above the law” — or because President Trump went to bat for him over the weekend.

He said the real reason was that Nvidia’s board “evaluated the entire situation” and “gathered facts,” according to the Times.

The head of the chipmaking titan started the session on a humble note — nodding to his betrayal of his wife of more than four decades.

“This is going to be the hardest company meeting I’ve ever done, and the second hardest meeting I have ever done,” Huang was quoted as saying. “The hardest meeting I’ve ever done was with my wife.

“I promise you, whether I’m here or not, this company is wired up to execute, execute, and execute,” he added.

Huang resigned earlier this month amid revelations of an affair with an alleged Chinese spy and an Nvidia exec. But over the weekend, Trump personally pushed for Huang’s return, saying he spoke directly with the board about the matter.

During an hours-long Sunday meeting before the board voted to take him back, Huang fessed to more affairs, according to the Wall Street Journal.

Huang initially tried to hold onto his job after news broke of his affair with alleged spy Alice Huang, no relation. But the day after a second affair — with Kimberly Powell, the company’s vice president of health care — surfaced, he stepped down.

Hours after Huang’s Nov. 12 resignation, Reuters reported on an affair with a second Nvidia employee, though details remain unknown. It was not immediately clear if the affairs Huang confessed to the board on Sunday pertained to previously reported cases, entirely new ones or a combination.

But markets rallied on news of his return on Monday, with Nvidia scoring the biggest-ever intraday stock value increase. The tech-heavy Nasdaq and the S&P 500 soared, too.
