Vivek Ramaswamy campaign ad for Ohio ‘shameful, disgusting’, says Republican congresswoman – US politics live

2026-10-22 13:49

Vivek Ramaswamy campaign ad for Ohio ‘shameful, disgusting’, says Republican congresswoman

Good morning and welcome to our live coverage of US politics. We’re less than two weeks out from the midterm vote and the campaigns are getting even more nasty.

Condemnation continues of Republican Vivek Ramaswamy’s campaign in the Ohio governor race after his team put out an attack ad, featuring the estranged mother of his Democratic rival Amy Acton criticising her daughter and calling her a liar.

Acton’s campaign has called it a despicable ad after putting out the clarification that Acton was sexually abused by her stepfather and removed from her mother’s custody at age 12.

Ramaswamy’s team, however, has dug in behind the ad, and were backed by JD Vance on Wednesday, who condemned Republican criticism. He said Ramaswamy “had the audacity to run a TV commercial which calls it out like it is”.

However, the South Carolina congresswoman Nancy Mace joined the chorus of Republican critics, saying:

This ad is going to go down as the most shameful, disgusting, offensive ad this election season.

She said she was stunned, adding:

I just can’t believe that this is what passes for decency in this election.

Today, we’ll also bring you the latest from other states where nominees are getting some help from big names in the final weeks of campaigning.

In Maine, Troy Jackson, the Democratic nominee for US Senate, will get support from progressive firebrand Alexandria Ocasio-Cortzez, the congresswoman from New York. The two will hold a rally in Portland as Jackson attempts to oust Susan Collins, the moderate Republican, from the Senate seat she’s held on to for almost 30 years.

Meanwhile, in Nebraska, Vice-President JD Vance will stump for Pete Ricketts, the GOP senator who is facing a re-election battle against the independent contender Dan Osborn. A reminder that the Democratic nominee, Cindy Burbank, withdrew after the primary earlier this year, in an attempt to shore up more support for Osborn.

Meanwhile, my colleagues Sam Levine and George Chidi, who have spent years covering voting rights, joined the Guardian’s Stateside with Kai and Carter for an episode all about the Trump administration’s persistent efforts to interfere with the midterm elections – including the justice department charging 28 green card holders in California over alleged voter fraud earlier this week.

As the president attempts to crackdown on non-citizen voting, which experts insist is an extremely rare phenomenon, Sam and George explain how this is part of a wider tacticpush to throw the results of the midterms into doubt.

Guardian reporters answer your questions about Trump's mass deportation agenda and the midterms

Also today, my colleagues Maanvi Singh, José Olivares and Sam Levin will take your questions about how Trump’s mass deportation agenda will shape the upcoming midterm elections.

We’re also tracking the fallout over a controversial ad from Vivek Ramaswamy – the Republican gubernatorial nominee in Ohio – which featured the estranged mother of his Democratic opponent, Amy Acton.

In the campaign spot her mother, Donna Arthur, was critical of Acton and called her a “liar”.

On Wednesday, Acton responded to the controversy and told a group of voters said Ramaswamy shouldn’t have “exploited” her mother.

A reminder that Acton and her brother were removed from their mother’s care when Acton was 12, after their stepfather was arrested and charged for raping her. However Arthur remained married to her husband, and they fled the state.

Acton went on to describe the recent advert as “another sign of this time, where enough is enough … do anything, say anything just to get ahead. And I think Ohioans are smarter than that.”

Donald Trump will begin his day in Washington before traveling to Sioux City, Iowa, for another campaign rally, as part of his week-long midterm blitz.

He’ll stump for Ashley Hinson, the Republican congresswoman and nominee for the state’s open US Senate, who is trailing in the polls against her opponent, the Democratic state legislator Josh Turek.

The president will also campaign on behalf of Zach Lahn, a businessman and the GOP nominee for governor, who is facing a tough competition against the Democratic state auditor Rob Sand in November.

Iowa has become an unexpected battleground this midterm cycle, with voters particularly frustrated about the impact on the war in Iran – namely the high cost of fuel and fertiliser. Democrats are hoping for an upset in a state where they haven’t won a US Senate set since 2008.

Trump will take the stage at 7pm ET, and we’ll bring you the latest lines.

New polls shows the super-tight race in Texas where Democratic candidate James Talarico is challenging Republican Ken Paxton in the US Senate race. Earlier this week, Talarico also won the endorsement of George W Bush’s brother.

New polling from Emerson College shows Talarico at 48%, just in front of Paxton at 46% – with about 5% undecided.

Neil Bush, a son of former president George HW Bush and a lifelong Republican, announced on Tuesday that he crossed party lines to vote for Talarico.

In a Facebook post, Neil Bush said he chose to vote early for Talarico over Ken Paxton, the current Texas attorney general, because he was “deeply troubled” by Paxton’s conduct and was “enormously impressed” by Talarico’s principles.

As a moderate Republican, Neil Bush’s statement on the competitive Senate race in Texas reflects views from a faction of the party that often disagrees with Donald Trump while holding traditional conservative views.

I have been deeply troubled by Paxton’s conduct as Texas attorney general, particularly his decision to challenge the results of the 2020 presidential election in other states in an effort to help Donald Trump remain in power.

I have been a Republican for most of my life, and I still believe deeply in many traditional Republican principles. But character matters more than party affiliation. Our democracy depends on leaders who respect our institutions, tell the truth, accept accountability, and seek to unite rather than divide.

Trump praised supporters for punching protesters at rally

Multiple protesters were removed from Donald Trump’s rally at South Carolina’s Clemson University on Wednesday, including one who was apparently punched by a rally attender – to the US president’s delight.

“That was great, man. Wow. Man, I loved that. I watched that,” Trump said. “That was incredible. Wow. That was unbelievable. Is everybody OK?

“Who’s the guy that threw that punch?” he continued. “You know, you reminded me of Evander, the real deal, Holyfield,” referring to the former heavyweight champion, who was also in attendance at the rally.

“You gotta give them credit. It takes courage to do that at a Trump rally,” he said. “He got whacked.”

The president was interrupted at least nine times by hecklers. One protester who was removed by security was followed by a rally attendee, who appeared to slap the demonstrator on the head.

Trump was in South Carolina to support Darline Graham, the sister of late senator Lindsey Graham who died suddenly in July from a heart condition. Graham, appointed to serve the remainder of her brother’s term, is facing Democratic challenger Annie Andrews in her bid for a full six-year term.

Responding to the ad on Wednesday, Amy Acton told a group of voters that her mother was not well and Ramaswamy shouldn’t have “exploited her”.

Earlier in the week, her brother had called Vivek Ramaswamy’s attack ad a “despicable low” in a statement released by Acton’s campaign:

It’s not surprising that Vivek Ramaswamy has stooped to such a despicable low as to attempt to turn the sexual abuse Amy endured at twelve years old from our birth mother’s husband into a political talking point.

After he was arrested and Amy and I were removed from their custody, our birth mother stayed married to Amy’s rapist until he died. This desperate, disgusting attack reveals exactly who Vivek Ramaswamy is at his core – a pathetic excuse for a man who is willing to hurt anyone as long as it gets him what he wants.”

The ad, which had circulated among conservatives on social media, featured Acton’s mother, Donna Arthur, urging Ohioans to not vote for her daughter.

The Ohio gubernatorial campaign between Acton and Ramaswamy is regarded as one of the uglier contests in the 3 November midterm elections.

Acton has refused to debate Ramaswamy, who she has called a “chronic liar” and has also criticized him for airing an ad that features a woman married to a registered sex offender.
