Trump defends Ohio Democrat’s estranged mother after Ramaswamy’s widely criticized campaign ad – live

2026-10-22 23:07

Trump defends Amy Acton’s estranged mother, who stayed with her husband charged with raping her daughter, and touts Vivek Ramaswamy’s ‘well coordinated’ attack ad

Trump says 'the press has ganged up on' Amy Acton's estranged mother who stayed with husband charged with raping her daughter

At the White House on Thursday, Donald Trump blamed the media for, in his eyes, unfairly attacking the estranged mother of Amy Acton, the Democratic nominee for Ohio governor, after the candidate’s mother recorded a campaign ad for Vivek Ramaswamy, the Republican nominee, in which she called the daughter she has not seen since she was 12 “a liar”.

The ad has been widely denounced this week by Republicans and Democrats because Acton’s mother lost custody of her children in 1978 after her husband, Acton’s stepfather, was charged with sexually assaulting his stepdaughter, who was 12 at the time.

Trump, who was informed on Wednesday by Karen Travers of ABC News that Acton had “suffered sexual abuse by her mother’s husband” as a child, offered his full support for the ad earlier on Thursday, without acknowledging that Acton’s mother was estranged because she had stayed with the man charged with assaulting her daughter.

The president was confronted with that fact by Kaitlan Collins of CNN as he left the White House on Thursday for a campaign rally in Iowa. “Why did you make no mention of the sexual abuse allegations that Amy Acton made against her stepfather who stayed married to her mother?” the CNN correspondent asked.

Trump again professed total ignorance of the sexual assault allegation and attacked the questioner instead. “What happened is the the press has ganged up on a woman making a horrible statement about her daughter. And I don’t know why she should be shielded, or the daughter should be-” Trump said.

“She accused her stepfather of child rape,” Collins explained.

“Wait a minute,” the president continued. “The mother made a horrible statement about the daughter. Said she was a dirty liar.”

Trump then complained to Collins that if his late mother had said made statements like that about him, “you’d be the first one to be broadcasting and you would have found nothing wrong with them”.

The president ignored Collins when she said “but the child abuse allegation is different”.

“The press has ganged up because you hate it, because that one ad should really win,” Trump went on.

“Look, if what the mother said about the daughter is half right, she has no right even running for the governor of Ohio,” the president, who has a long record of documented lies, said – suddenly claiming that making false statement should be disqualifying for a public official.

“What about what the daughter said about her mother?” Collins asked.

“I don’t know what the daughter said,” Trump replied, clinging to his ignorance like a life preserver. “I don’t know.”

“Her mother remained married her stepfather who sexually abused her when she was 12 years old,” Collins repeated.

“Well, when did she make that statement? Was it just recently?” Trump replied, apparently trying to cast doubt on the allegation that was, in fact, made in 1978.

After Collins and another reporter said Acton made the allegation a sa child in the 1970s, Trump said.

“You know, I have no idea. All I know is Amy Acton’s mother said horrible things about Amy Acton, calling her a liar and other things,” the president said. “And I say that she’s fair game.”

“You’re trying to help Amy Acton get elected – the press has teamed up to help Amy Acton get elected,” Trump repeated. “And that’s all it is.”

As Trump travels to Iowa, Republican Senate candidate Ashley Hinson denounced Ohio ad he supports

Donald Trump is expected to speak soon at a rally in Sioux City, Iowa in support of Republican Senate candidate Ashley Hinson, who broke with the president on Thursday by denouncing an attack ad he has supported in the Ohio governor race.

As Trump defended the ad, from Republican candidate Vivek Ramaswamy, in which the estranged mother of the Democratic candidate, Dr Amy Acton, calls her daughter “a liar”, Hinson joined the bipartisan backlash against the ad triggered by the fact that Acton was removed from her mother’s custody at age 12, after her stepfather was charged with raping her.

In an interview ahead of today’s rally, Hinson was asked by Jonathan Karl of ABC News about the ad from Ramaswamy’s campaign. “It’s terrible,” Hinson said. “I think about it as a mom, and you think about kid surviving childhood sexual abuse and having to relive that trauma on TV again. I think it’s absolutely terrible, and I think they should take the ad down.”

Hinson also called the comment from vice-president JD Vance, that he was disgusted by Republican criticism of the ad, “ridiculous”.

Student IDs will not be accepted at New Hampshire polls, Supreme Court rules

The US supreme court ruled on Thursday that New Hampshire students cannot use their school-issued ID cards as proof of identity to vote, handing a victory to the state’s Republicans.

The high court cited a previous ruling, “lower federal courts should ordinarily not alter the election rules on the eve of an election,” in its decision to keep intact an April state law that struck high school and college IDs from a list of acceptable voter documents. House Bill 323, signed by New Hampshire’s Republican governor, Kelly Ayotte, requires government-issued IDs, such as a passport or driver license.

The Coalition for Open Democracy, a New Hampshire-based nonprofit, had sued to block the legislation, arguing that it raised unconstitutional barriers for the state’s young voting bloc.

Republican legislators nationwide have embraced stricter voter ID laws, framing them as a safeguard against alleged voter fraud. Voting rights groups have argued that the narrow range of acceptable forms of identification disenfranchises millions of voters.

According to the Brennan Center, “more than 49 million American adults don’t possess an unexpired driver’s license with their current name and address. Seniors, people of color, people with disabilities, low-income voters, and students are especially likely to lack such documents.”

Trump's DoJ investigating Corey Lewandowski over role as Kristi Noem adviser – reports

The Department of Justice has reportedly opened a criminal investigation of Donald Trump’s first campaign manager, Corey Lewandowski, over allegations that he illegally profited from his role as an adviser to the former homeland security secretary Kristi Noem, according to reporting from the Wall Street Journal and the New York Times.

The investigation was triggered by a referral from the inspector general for the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) unnamed officials told both newspapers.

Lewandowski’s lawyer, Adam Trigg, denied the allegatinos in a statement to the Times. “Mr. Lewandowski has not been contacted by the Justice Department regarding any investigation, and he denies any wrongdoing when he was proudly serving his country in a voluntary capacity,” Trigg said.

Noem, who was fired by Trump in March, subsequently filed for divorce from her husband, citing “irreconcilable differences”, amid speculation that she was in a relationship with Lewandowski.

At a congressional hearing in March, a Democratic member of Congress asked Noem whether she had had sexual relations with Lewandowski.

Answering under oath, Noem – who was accompanied to the hearing by her then-husband – dismissed the question as “tabloid garbage” but did not say no.

State department reportedly asked career officials up for promotions about voting history

The state department asked foreign service officials who are up whether they voted for Donald Trump in 2016, 2020 and 2024, according to a report by the Washington Sun.

Citing several former and current state department officials familiar with the matter, the Washington sun reports that top brass at the state department made clear that only those who voted for Trump would be eligible for the mid-to-upper level foreign service roles. They also reportedly told employees that their donation history would be examined and any donation to Democrats would be disqualifying.

However, politicized personnel decisions are prohibited under federal law when it comes to career civil servants.

The report notes that senior Democrats have been aware of allegations about political affiliations being a factor in career development decisions. “These are critical national security positions and individuals should be chosen for their experience and ability, not their political loyalty. We are working with the House and other relevant committees to thoroughly investigate all such claims,” Senator Jeanne Shaheen, the outgoing Democrat from New Hampshire, told the Washington Sun in a statement.

A spokesperson for the state department declined to comment on the voting history questions from the Sun, but emphasized the importance of “fidelity” in a statement to the outlet.

“Fidelity means upholding the Constitution and the law and executing policy with loyalty to the American people and their interests,” the spokesperson said. “It emphasizes the critical importance of service, integrity, and policy alignment across the Foreign Service. It is not about partisan behavior but rather about executing the president’s vision for foreign policy.”

Susan Collins’ deciding vote to confirm conservative supreme court justice Brett Kavanaugh has become a flashpoint in the Senate race in Maine.

This midterm election is the first time Collins is on the ballot since the 2022 Dobbs decision, which overturned the constitutional right to an abortion.

“I want to say, for every woman in Maine, if we want to be able to deliver our children safely … the most important thing that we can do for women in Maine is to vote for Troy Jackson for the United States Senate,” said representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, the Democratic congresswoman stumping with Jackson in Portland today.

The Guardian was the first to report that the one of the country’s oldest abortion rights groups, Reproductive Freedom for All, would endorse Jackson in this year’s Senate race.

Speaking to the crowd in Portland, Troy Jackson said that – if elected – his “day one agenda” in the Senate would involve repealing the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Donald Trump’s domestic policy legislation that was signed into law last year.

“I know will be a big uphill deal. So we can get some semblance of our healthcare back,” Jackson said. “I mean, our healthcare wasn’t good before, but you know, trying to get that back so that we can work on Medicare for all. I believe strongly in that, and that’s my top priority.”

Discussing the immigration crackdown in Maine that saw a 26-year-old man in Biddeford killed at the hands of a federal immigration agent, Ocasio-Cortez noted how the Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) presence in Maine has been disproportionate.

She added that tech companies which have substantial contracts with the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) are “going out to lunch” on the $70bn funding bill for federal immigration enforcement that Susan Collins, the five-term Republican incumbent, “helped rubber stamp”.
