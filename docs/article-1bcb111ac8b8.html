Abdul El-Sayed makes history as 1st Muslim elected to US Senate

2026-11-04 08:25

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed won Michigan’s open U.S. Senate seat Tuesday, becoming the first Muslim elected to the chamber and retaining a critical seat for his party.

El-Sayed’s victory in the closely contested election marked a breakthrough for the Democratic Party’s progressive wing and denied Republicans a chance to flip the seat of retiring Democratic Sen. Gary Peters.

El-Sayed, 42, a former public health official, focused on lowering costs for working families and reducing corporate influence in politics.

His platform included Medicare for All, a government-funded universal health care program championed by progressives in his party.

Republicans held a 53-47 Senate majority going into Tuesday’s midterm elections, with Democrats needing a net gain of four seats to take control.

His opponent Mike Rogers, 63, a former congressman and FBI agent, entered the race with strong backing from Trump and national Republicans.

He had sought to cast El-Sayed as a radical candidate sympathetic to Islamic extremism through alleged ties to the Muslim Brotherhood, claims that El-Sayed denies and that remain unsupported by evidence. Democrats said the attacks on El-Sayed relied on Islamophobic tropes.

Rogers also devoted significant effort to courting Jewish voters who may have been uneasy with El-Sayed's opposition to U.S. military aid for Israel and his condemnation of Israel's Gaza genocide.

But Rogers struggled to overcome voter concerns about the economy and Trump's low ratings. In the closing weeks of the race, he broke with the president on the war in Iran and the U.S. trade dispute with Canada, arguing that ending both was necessary to help bring down costs for businesses and consumers.
