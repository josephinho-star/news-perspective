Arrests over France protests rise to 1,800, with whole streets in Paris ‘devastated’ – Europe live

2026-10-18 12:14

More than 1,800 people have been arrested in France over protests on Saturday, the country’s interior minister Laurent Nunez has said.

Nunez, in a post on X, said “the Republic will not yield” as more than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters have been injured in the demonstrations.

“The material damage is considerable,” Nunez added, pointing to damaged shops.

He blamed thousands of “far-left” protesters along with “criminals who looted and ransacked” buildings across France.

“The Republic will yield nothing to those who seek to impose disorder and violence,” he said.

Causing disorder through violence and in a coordinated manner is not.

Attacking our law enforcement officers, targeting our institutions, damaging public buildings, breaking into prefectures or town halls, and looting businesses – none of this can be justified.

Following the events of 17 October – which, in some cities, continued late into the night-I wish to express my sincere gratitude and full support to the police officers, gendarmes, and firefighters who intervened under extreme conditions all across France.

By midday – and as early as the morning in Marseille – thousands of far-left individuals, intent on attacking public buildings and confronting our law enforcement, began committing acts of violence.

They were subsequently joined by criminals who looted and ransacked numerous businesses in several French cities, including Paris, Marseille, Lyon, Bordeaux, Rennes, Strasbourg, Grenoble, Nice, Limoges, and many others.

As of now, 1,800 people have been arrested, and investigations are already under way to identify the perpetrators so that they may be held accountable before the justice system.

At this stage, more than 500 police officers, gendarmes, and firefighters have been injured amid this violence.

Sadly, this casualty count is only provisional. The material damage is substantial, affecting, among others, hundreds of business owners.

The Republic will not yield to those who seek to impose disorder and violence.

Elsewhere in Europe, violent protests are also continuing Albania, where thousands have been calling for the prime minister to step down as they rally against poor university conditions, high tuition fees and corruption.

Albanian’s capital saw protests on Sunday for a sixth day in a row as the prime minister, Edi Rama, ruled out resigning, the main demand of the protesters.

Hundreds of protesters gathered again in Tirana on Sunday at noon in front of the education ministry chanting “Rama, resign” in a tense standoff with the police, who later used water cannons and tear gas to hold back the crowds.

Shops and institutions in the centre of the city have secured their doors and windows with metal shutters, while city workers removed paving stones to prevent their use by protesters.

In an over-an-hour long video broadcast earlier in the day, Rama ruled out resigning, stating that the protests are the “work of an outside hand”.

Writer and activist Lura Elezi, 38, told AFP that she is protesting for “a better life, rule of law and against crime and corruption”.

“I love my country, but my government does not respect us - it defies us. Every citizen must take responsibility for their country, taking on the duty to lead, to love, and not to destroy one’s own nation,” she said.

Tirana saw its harshest violence on Saturday when protesters threw rocks, Molotov cocktails, metal objects and pyrotechnic devices at police, and torched cars and litter bins, using them as blazing barricades to stop police advancing.

Macron will sit down with members of his cabinet on Sunday, the Elysee has said, ahead of his televised speech to the French public this evening.

It was to come as France counts the cost of Saturday’s protests, which saw clashes, arson and widespread looting.

A dispatch here from Sam Jones, our reporter in Madrid, as protests have spread to Italy and Portugal

The angry protests that have convulsed Spain and France in recent weeks have spilled over to Italy and Portugal, with rioters clashing with police and setting property on fire in Milan, and a tent encampment springing up in Lisbon over the weekend.

A march in central Milan on Saturday to protest against rising living costs began peacefully but ended with masked rioters throwing stones, paving blocks and other objects at riot police and setting fire to barricades made from bar tables.

Police used water cannon and teargas to disperse the crowds, and images of the aftermath showed a severely damaged carabinieri car.

At least two police officers were injured and about 50 demonstrators, including many minors, were detained, the LaPresse news agency reported.

Italy’s prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, said that while demonstrating was a right, “setting our cities ablaze is not”. In a social media post, she said “hooligans” had exploited the protests “to sow chaos, attack those responsible for ensuring security and to hold entire cities hostage”.

Emmanuel Macron will give a televised address to the French public this evening on the protests and unrest on Saturday.

The French President will speak at 8pm French time (6pm GMT), after more than 1,800 people were arrested on Saturday in connection with violence that spread from protests against the cost of living.

Paris police said about 110,000 people took place in a protest march on Saturday against the cost of living and school conditions.

Numbers have differed for those nationwide, with authorities saying it was about 550,000 but organisers claiming it was higher at about 800,000.

One person died of a heart attack, Paris police said. French media reported a protester suffered a medical emergency in the street during the march.

Thirteen police and gendarmerie officers and two firefighters were injured, the statement said, according to Associated Press.

The mayors of France’s three largest cities, Paris, Marseille and Lyon have requested a meeting with the prime minister, Sébastien Lecornu, in the coming days.

Around Paris’ Place de la République on Sunday morning, dislodged cobblestones, shattered storefronts and broken glass marked the aftermath of the events.

Looting and damage were reported around the square and in some other tourist and shopping areas in the city centre.

Police said groups numbering from several hundred to more than 1,000 people formed unauthorised marches across central Paris on Saturday.

“By late afternoon, a new phase began with the arrival of large numbers of people whose sole aim was to steal and loot, taking advantage of the unrest,” police said.

It’s important to note that protests have also been taking place across Europe.

In Italy rioters clashed with police, looted stores and set property on fire in central Milan on Saturday.

The demonstration escalated after a march against wars and rising living costs began peacefully.

However masked protesters threw stones, paving blocks and other objects at riot police and set fire to barricades made from bar tables. Images of the aftermath showed a severely damaged carabinieri car.

Police used water cannons and teargas to disperse the crowds.

At least two police officers were injured and about 50 demonstrators, including many minors, were detained, the LaPresse news agency reported.

Meanwhile more peaceful protests took place in Portugal, with activists making calls for action to stop rising housing and living costs.

Hundreds of people gathered at Rossio Square in its capital, Lisbon, after a march that had seen thousands take to the streets across the country.

Reports from Agence France-Presse said the group in Lisbon vowed to stay overnight as they gathered around a statue of King Pedro IV in the square.

“The Portuguese social class that pays taxes, that which keeps this country going, is under attack and is losing its rights, notably its right to housing,” said Elena Costa, a 46-year-old social assistant.
