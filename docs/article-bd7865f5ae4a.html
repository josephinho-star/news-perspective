Two killed in İstanbul building collapse, search continues for survivors

2026-10-08 09:10

Two people died and rescuers were trying to reach two others after the lower three floors of a seven-story building collapsed in İstanbul early Thursday, with the risk of further collapse complicating the search.

Ten people, including three children, had been rescued from the rubble, according to Turkish media reports citing the İstanbul Governor’s Office. Residents of the upper floors were evacuated.

The building in Maltepe’s Cevizli neighborhood, on the city’s Asian side, partially collapsed at about 5:40 a.m. Firefighters, medical teams and personnel from Turkey’s Disaster and Emergency Management Authority (AFAD) were dispatched to the scene.

İstanbul Governor Davut Gül warned that the remaining structure could also collapse, forcing rescuers to proceed cautiously. Nearby buildings were evacuated as a precaution, The Associated Press reported.

The cause of the collapse had not been established.

Maltepe Mayor Esin Köymen said municipal archive records showed the building’s top two floors had been added without permits after an occupancy permit was issued. She said the municipality had received no previous complaints about the building.

According to the governor’s office, the building was constructed in 1987 and contained a ground-floor business and 12 apartments. Population records listed 33 residents, with 14 registered in the four apartments affected by the collapse.

Tolga Gürpınar, a resident rescued from the building, said it had cracks and that neighbors had heard cracking noises before the collapse. Residents had discussed redevelopment with contractors but had not reached an agreement, he said.

Prosecutors on İstanbul’s Asian side opened an investigation, assigning a deputy chief prosecutor and two prosecutors to the case.

The Interior Ministry also appointed two inspectors to examine whether public officials bore responsibility for negligence or failures during the building’s planning, construction or alterations, according to the DHA news agency.

İstanbul and other parts of Turkey are undergoing an urban redevelopment program intended to replace aging buildings vulnerable to earthquakes with safer housing.
