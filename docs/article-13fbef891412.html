Alarming new poll shows gap widening even further between Steve Hilton and Xavier Becerra

2026-09-24 17:53

California Democratic candidate Xavier Becerra has cemented his lead by seven more percentage points over Republican rival Steven Hilton in the governor’s race, according to a new poll by the UC Berkeley Institute of Governmental Studies.

About 58% of 4,512 likely voters surveyed from Sept. 15 to 20 said they supported Becerra, a former Health and Human Services secretary under former President Joe Biden, over the 33% who backed Hilton, who is endorsed by President Donald Trump.

The Californians surveyed cited the state’s steep cost of living — with some of the highest housing and fuel costs in the nation — as their top concern.

However, the Dem frontrunner’s lead has grown since a May poll by the same UC Berkeley institute, which found 55% support for Becerra versus Hilton’s 37%.

Hilton has dismissed any worries that his campaign is falling behind.

“Californians are desperate for change and we have momentum,” Hilton told The Post. “Becerra is a career politician and electing him is four more years of the one-party rule of the last 16.”

Experts say the part of the gap could be explained by the fact that 37% of likely voters named fighting the Trump administration’s policies as their highest concern, second only to the 44% of voters who named the high cost of living.

The Hilton campaign has tapped deeply into cost-of-living issues during his general election campaigning — such as $3 gas and no taxes on income below $150,000 — but experts said the odds of a Republican in a deep-blue state like California are too high to overcome.

“The difficult thing is that Steve Hilton’s a Republican in California,” Tim Rosales, a Republican strategist, told the Los Angeles Times, which sponsored the poll. Democrats “understand the cost of living, and they feel it. They just feel like they need a new Democrat to deal with it.”

Rosales said that Hilton’s hope is to convert a lot more independents to his side, but the poll showed more than half of likely voters not registered with either political party supporting Becerra. Hilton had 30% of them.

Institute director Eric Schickler told the Times that many voters are motivated by the opportunity to deal a blow to Trump in the midterm elections.

“With 2026 shaping up to be a strong Democratic year nationally, the headwinds facing Hilton in Democratic-leaning California are even stronger than usual,” he said.

The November election is only a little more than a month away, but Hilton may be counting on a last-minute change of heart from Californians fed up with the status quo.

The two candidates are scheduled to meet in a Sept. 30 nationally televised debate in Burbank hosted by CNN.
