Melbourne finishes second-last at world tram driving championships

Despite having one of the largest tram networks in the world, Melbourne tram drivers finished second last, just ahead of London, at the 12th European TramDriver Championship held in Warsaw yesterday.

One child dead in Blue Mountains stabbing, two children and woman in critical condition

A child has died in a stabbing attack in the Blue Mountains today, with two other children and a woman in hospital in critical condition, New South Wales police say.

In a statement this afternoon, police said emergency services responded to reports of three children and a woman with stab wounds in a vehicle on Waratah Road, Valley Heights. They said:

Officers from Blue Mountains Police Area Command have been informed that several members of the public performed first aid on the woman and three children whilst contacting Triple Zero.

One of the children was located deceased in the vehicle.

Police said NSW Ambulance treated the two children, who are believed to be under five, and the woman, believed to be in her 40s, at the scene before they were taken to hospital, where all three are listed as being in a critical condition.

“A crime scene has been established, and police are not looking for any other people at this time,” they said. Police are urging anyone with information or mobile phone or dashcam footage of the incident to contact Crime Stoppers.

Plibersek says ‘we owe these women more than our sorrow’ after deaths in NSW

The social services minister, Tanya Plibersek, has made a statement after the deaths of three women in alleged domestic violence-related murders in separate incidents in a 12-hour period in NSW.

Three men have now been charged with domestic violence-related murders. They include a 37-year-old man charged today after the body of a woman in her 70s was found at Mole River in the state’s north yesterday evening.

In a statement, Plibersek said her thoughts were with the families and loved ones of the women:

We owe these women more than our sorrow.

Every level of government, every community organisation, our schools and workplaces, every family – all of us need to work together to keep women and children safe.

Treasurer warns of ‘confronting’ 40-year economic outlook

Australia’s 40-year outlook will be “confronting”, treasurer Jim Chalmers says, but not a “pessimistic story” as AI looks to transform the global economy.

Chalmers will present the seventh intergenerational report tomorrow afternoon, detailing forecasts for the impact of new technologies on society and the economy, along with more warnings about the country’s ageing population and declining fertility rates.

It comes as the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, is visiting the United States this week to discuss a “global framework” for the rollout of AI, urging the “big two giants” – the US and China – to work in the interest of humanity.

At a press conference this morning, the treasurer warned the report will show Australia is “dealing with serious risks to our economy and serious pressures on the budget” but he believed the government’s policies were well-equipped to handle it.

The intergenerational report is a really important way that we plan for the future to make sure that Australians are beneficiaries and not victims of all this accelerating change that’s come at us from around the world.

AI will be the most “transformative influence on our society and our economy over the next 40 years”, Chalmers said.

Artificial intelligence does present us with very substantial risks that we will manage. I’m very confident that we can maximise the economic upside of technological change at the same time as we minimise the very substantial risks and concerns that people legitimately have about how this part of our economy is changing.

Damaging winds expected throughout Tasmania and parts of Victoria today

The Bureau of Meteorology has issued severe weather warnings in both states with north-westerly winds expected to increase on Sunday ahead of a strong cold front.

Peak wind gusts around 90-100km/h were forecast, and already recorded in some locations. A 109km/h wind gust was recorded at kunanyi/Mount Wellington in Hobart earlier this morning. While in Victoria, a 95km/h wind gust was recorded at Mount Gellibrand, about 130km south-west of Melbourne.

Maximum temperatures on Sunday were expected to reach 8C to 15C above average across south-east Australia, the BoM said.

By midday, temperatures had already exceeded 35C at several locations across the country, including Oodnadatta in South Australia, Birdsville in Queensland and at Roebourne airport west of Port Hedland, in WA.

Ten historic Sydney train stations ready for metro extension opening

The stations are now ready to reopen as metro stations ahead of the extension of the wildly popular line later this year, the NSW government says.

The ten stations – Marrickville, Dulwich Hill, Hurlstone Park, Canterbury, Campsie, Belmore, Lakemba, Wiley Park, Punchbowl, and Bankstown – used to form part of the 130‑year‑old heavy rail line. They have been closed since September 2024 in favour of rail replacement buses.

The government says the now complete metro refurbishment has required the installation of 360 automated platform screen doors, and more than 1,300 gap fillers to fill the spaces between the trains and curved platforms.

The NSW transport minister, John Graham, says:

These 19th-century stations have transformed to meet the standards of the 21st century public transport.

These station upgrades are the culmination of years of hard work, investment and patience from their communities. Along with every person living in this corridor, I can’t wait to see these stations in full swing.

The government has declined to provide a state date for the Sydenham to Bankstown extension before the national rail safety regulator approves the line. The Sydney Morning Herald has reported that Sydney Metrois targeting a public opening date in mid-October.

Sloane says says women being failed after three deaths hours apart

The NSW opposition leader, Kellie Sloane, says women are being failed “across our country and [by] every level of government” after the deaths of three women just hours apart in separate incidents in NSW.

They include the death of a woman in her 90s, who was found with stab wounds inside her unit at Sydney’s Bondi beach at about 5.15pm yesterday. A 47-year-old man has been charged with murder.

In a statement this morning Sloane said she was horrified by the deaths.

These deaths are not isolated. According to the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare, a woman was killed every 11 days in 2024-25 by her intimate partner. Even more women lose their lives when you include other family members …

Because these matters are before the courts, I won’t comment on the circumstances. But we cannot treat this as background noise. We are failing women across our country and every level of government, and every one of us, has a responsibility to act.

In a statement today, the premier, Chris Minns, said “these are shocking crimes and families are today dealing with unimaginable grief”.

My thoughts are with the families and loved ones of the women who have died, and the communities affected by these events.

South Australian algal bloom caused sea dragon mass mortality event

Thousands of sea dragons, pipefishes and seahorses died during South Australia’s devastating algal bloom, in what ecologists say is the worst mass mortality event for the fish family globally.

More than 1,600 weedy and leafy sea dragons, 1,400 pipefishes and 160 seahorses washed up as the harmful algae spread along the state’s coastline, according to analysis of citizen scientist records by Dragon Search SA.

The state is home to more than 30 species of Syngnathidae, a family that includes sea dragons, seahorses, pipefishes and more. At least 13 were affected by the toxic bloom, which began in March 2025 and still lingers at one site in southern Spencer gulf.

Weedy and leafy sea dragons, found only in Australia, were often some of the first mortalities observed, as the algal bloom spread up and down the coast.

Opposition leader says treasurer should ‘get on board’ with his tax cut plans

Angus Taylor has continued to spruik his double tax cut policy this morning, which he claims will lower petrol prices and put the budget in better shape.

On 2GB radio, the opposition leader said the treasurer, Jim Chalmers, should adopt the policy to halve the fuel excise when the two-week average price of oil exceeds US$100 a barrel and cut the tobacco excise to take back cigarette sales from the black market.

Chalmers told News24 this morning the government had no plans to again halve the fuel excise as oil prices continue to rise in response to the US’s protracted conflict with Iran.

Let’s just lock it in, and let’s stop this silly carry on. And the treasurer should just get on board. He should just get on board, and this is a treasurer who’s in denial about the state of the economy.

Taylor insists fuel price shield policy is ‘anti-inflationary’ after Chalmers labels it ‘uncapped, unfunded’

The federal opposition leader, Angus Taylor, has insisted his “fuel price shield” policy is “anti-inflationary” after criticism by the federal treasurer, Jim Chalmers.

As we reported earlier, the opposition’s policy would see the federal fuel excise halved when the two-week average price of oil exceeds US$100 a barrel.

Asked what he thought of the idea on News24 earlier, Chalmers accused Taylor of coming up with an “an uncapped, unfunded policy”.

Angus Taylor’s not trying to save Australian motorists from higher petrol prices. He’s trying to save the Liberal party from One Nation.

At a press conference this morning, Taylor rejected Chalmers’ comments, saying the opposition would fund the policy through increased revenue created by controversial proposed cuts to the federal tobacco excise.

We can bring down taxes, fully fund it through the budget, and ensure that we provide the relief Australians want at the bowser: $15 a tank, 27 cents a litre … So this is anti-inflationary, and it’s been done in a way which is responsible for the budget.

The opposition has estimated the policy would cost around $950m each month, and would have been triggered twice in the past five years, during oil price shocks in early 2022 and March this year.

Albanese says One Nation migration policy would be ‘diabolical’ for economy

The prime minister has defended the government’s proposed immigration enforcement rules, as One Nation and the Coalition call for a tougher crackdown on net migration.

The government has announced long-awaited changes to immigration rules, under which foreign nationals without a valid visa face new measures to force them to leave the country. It has promised to bring down net overseas migration to 245,000 this financial year, and 225,000 in 2027-28.

One Nation says it is targeting net-negative arrivals over three years, while the federal opposition leader, Angus Taylor, told the NSW Liberal state council yesterday he would introduce one of the “biggest immigration cuts in Australian history”.

Albanese told the ABC’s Insiders One Nation’s policy would be “be diabolical for our economy, and have real consequences for issues such as aged care, healthcare, the hospitality sector, the agricultural sector”.

A poll by The Sydney Morning Herald suggested 40% of voters thought the government’s 2027-28 target was still too high. Asked about recent polling on immigration, Albanese said:

We will put forward the case, for example, of our farmers need the areas to come in if they’re going to have their crops picked. That is just something that the farming sector are telling us, and it’s something that’s a reality, as well as [in] a range of other areas as well, including in the care sector.
