First Thing: Mississippi turns blue as Scott Colom unseats Republican Senate incumbent

2026-11-12 13:38

Good morning. In a stunning upset after more than a week of vote counting in one of the country’s most reliably red states, the Democrat Scott Colom has defeated Cindy Hyde-Smith, the incumbent Republican senator, in Mississippi. He becomes the first Black senator elected in the state since Reconstruction.

Colom’s election capped a national wave of anger at Donald Trump’s administration, sweeping away Republicans in places where Democrats had not been competitive in decades. With the addition of Colom, Democrats will hold at least 55 seats in the Senate, the party’s largest midterm gain since 1986.

What did Colom say about his victory? Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney, said there had been times when his long-shot campaign felt defeated but “I had to have faith”. He told supporters: “My commitment to you, each and every one of you, is that first I’m going to treat your children like I want my children to be treated. I’m going to treat your parents the way I want my parents to be treated. I’m gonna treat your future the way I want my future to be.”

How have Democrats responded to a White House threat? Robert Garcia, the ranking member of the oversight committee in the House of Representatives said it appeared Donald Trump had “something to hide” after the White House threatened criminal investigations over the party’s planned scrutiny of the administration. Garcia said in a statement that Congress’s authority came from article I of the constitution, “not from the permission of the White House”.

Democrats lambast absent peers after Republicans block Hegseth impeachment attempt

Democratic lawmakers expressed outrage at nearly a dozen of their colleagues for missing a vote Tuesday evening to impeach the defense secretary, Pete Hegseth, allowing Republicans to defeat the effort. Eleven Democrats missed the key procedural vote on the impeachment effort, which failed by just seven votes. “You should show up and do your job,” Abdul El-Sayed, the senator-elect of Michigan, said on CNN, while Richard Ojeda, the representative-elect of North Carolina, said: “Shame on them for not showing up … they had an opportunity to finally get rid of this buffoonery.”
