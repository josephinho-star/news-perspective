Former Penn State frat brother indicted by federal grand jury in cocaine trafficking scheme

2026-11-18 19:13

The alleged leader of a cocaine-trafficking ring at Penn State University has been indicted on drug distribution charges by a federal grand jury, adding to the state charges he is already facing.

Agostino Abbatiello, 24, of Westbury, New York, was a "major supplier" of cocaine to at least two separate fraternities — Sigma Chi and Delta Upsilon — at the university in State College, Pennsylvania, between January 2023 and December 2024, according to federal prosecutors.

Abbatiello, a former Sigma Chi member, frequently drove to Philadelphia and New York to buy cocaine before bringing his hauls back to campus and enlisting his fellow fraternity brothers and pledges to package the drugs for resale, according to federal and state authorities.

He'd allegedly return with as much as a kilogram each time, which factors heavily into the sheer number of charges he's facing.

Abbatiello, who graduated in spring 2024, now faces one count of conspiracy to distribute cocaine and six counts of distribution of cocaine; these charges could land him in prison for the rest of his life if he's convicted.

"College should be a marketplace of ideas and not illegal drugs," said U.S. Attorney Brian D. Miller for the Middle District of Pennsylvania in a statement. "Our office will continue working with the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s office, and with our law enforcement partners to identify, investigate, and hold accountable those who profit from illegal drug activity in our communities."

Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday, a Republican and former York County district attorney, announced state charges against Abbatiello and his alleged co-conspirators in August following a joint investigation. In all, 14 people were charged at the state level.

Thomas Robinson, who Pennsylvania authorities said made regular trips to Philadelphia and New York to buy cocaine with Abbatiello, has not been indicted in federal court.

Another state defendant was Robinson's father, Paul Robinson, who state prosecutors said tried to conceal evidence in the case, including a safe containing drugs and cash.

Sunday described Abbatiello as "the top rung of the ladder for a high-level drug trafficking ring." Robinson was a "rung below that on the ladder," he added.

When he announced the state charges in August, Sunday said the cocaine was sold among Penn State students, often at parties. Investigators also found that packaging the drugs was used as an initiation ritual for fraternity pledges.

"We found a pattern of events that showed cutting and bagging cocaine was for some pledges an indoctrination into the Delta Upsilon and Sigma Chi fraternities," Sunday said. "

A variety of different agencies have investigated the case, including Homeland Security Investigations, the State College Police Department and a narcotics unit within the Pennsylvania Attorney General's Office.
