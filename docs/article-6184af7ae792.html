Lutnick agrees to testify before Congress amid bipartisan concerns

2026-11-13 13:52

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has agreed to testify before Congress amid bipartisan concerns about the Nvidia spy scandal and the agency’s enforcement of chip export controls.

Lutnick has “proactively agreed to appear voluntarily” before the Senate Banking Committee, spokesperson Kristen Eichamer said in a statement.

Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott (R-S.C.) sent letters to both Lutnick and former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang on Thursday, asking them to appear before his committee in early December.

“Given the Department of Commerce’s central role in issuing and enforcing export control regulations, your testimony before the Committee will be essential to our oversight of these efforts,” Scott wrote.

“Recent developments underscore the significant national security and economic interests at stake and the need for timely, substantive engagement with Congress,” he added.

Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on Thursday following a series of affair scandals, including one involving a woman whom U.S. officials suspected of passing information to China.

The revelation about Huang’s involvement with an alleged Chinese spy immediately drew calls from lawmakers on both sides of the aisle for investigations into the national security implications, given Nvidia’s central role in the AI boom and the tech mogul’s close ties to President Trump.

But Lutnick is also facing scrutiny following a series of reports about the Commerce Department’s enforcement of export controls on Nvidia’s chips.

Several outlets reported the agency paused an investigation last year into Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company that was suspected of helping to smuggle Nvidia chips to China.

A former Megaspeed executive, Alice Huang, is the woman with whom the ex-Nvidia CEO admitted to having an affair, and U.S. officials concluded she was a Chinese intelligence asset.

Reports said the Commerce Department passed the probe off to the Department of Justice (DOJ), although neither agency confirmed this development.

Axios first reported earlier this week that Lutnick stalled an authorization from Trump and other agency heads to crack down on firms suspected of diverting Nvidia’s chips to China.

Amid these reports, Sen. Jim Banks (R-Ind.) on Thursday called for Lutnick to appear before Congress. Banks, a member of the Senate Banking Committee, has long pushed for stricter chip export rules.

“They [Nvidia] opposed other export control laws, and now we know that the Department of Commerce Secretary Lutnick, it appears, used loopholes to allow some of these chip sales to occur, even though President Trump has directed his administration and the Department of Commerce not to allow that to happen,” Banks told Fox News.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren (Mass.), the top Democrat on the Banking Committee, also argued Thursday that Lutnick and Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler “must explain why they have not closed export control loopholes and act today to ensure our advanced technology is not fueling China’s military.”

Kessler heads up the Bureau of Industry and Security, which is tasked with overseeing U.S. export controls.
