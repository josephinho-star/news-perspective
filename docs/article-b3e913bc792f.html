Man triggers relationship crisis by grabbing first class, leaving woman behind in coach

2026-10-06 12:00

A woman's uncomfortable travel experience with a now-ex-boyfriend is fueling a heated debate about airplane etiquette and expectations between couples as a psychotherapist pointed out the larger issues involved.

In a recent TikTok video, the woman said she and her then-boyfriend were flying to visit his family when he received a seat upgrade — leaving her stuck back in the economy section.

The woman said she did not have airline status, while her boyfriend frequently traveled for work and regularly received upgrades and points, according to the video.

The woman said she expected him to suggest switching seats — but that he took the upgrade himself.

She said sitting in economy was not the problem; it was his actions that crushed her.

"It's not the seat, it's the principle," she said — describing the experience as "the beginning of the end" of their relationship.

Jonathan Alpert, a New York-based psychotherapist and author of "Therapy Nation," told Fox News Digital that offering the woman the upgraded seat would have been a considerate gesture on the man's part. But the disagreement appeared to represent a larger issue in the relationship, he said.

"I think this situation probably isn't really about him giving up his seat, but it's probably about him or his lack of selflessness," Alpert said, adding the issue is less about gender and more about "kindness and thoughtfulness."

Still, Alpert said he would not consider the seat decision alone a reason to end a relationship. He suggested there were likely other issues between the couple and that the incident may have been "the straw that broke the camel's back."

Travel can bring those underlying differences to the surface, Alpert said.

He described it as a "stress test" that can reveal how partners handle pressure, adapt to unexpected situations and compromise with each other.

For couples faced with a similar upgrade, Alpert said "selflessness is the name of the game," suggesting a traveler should offer the better seat to a partner or turn down the upgrade completely in order to sit together.

"Any real man would let his wife have first class."

"At the end of the day, I would think that a couple wants to sit together and not apart," he said.

Many social media users sided with the woman, saying they, too, would have expected a partner to offer the better seat or decline the upgrade altogether.

"Personally I'd rather just reject the upgrade altogether so we could sit together," one person wrote.

One commenter who identified herself as a flight attendant agreed, writing, "I'm a flight attendant and if a man ever says his wife is in the back, I always call him out."

Another person said, "Any real man would let his wife have first class."

Others pushed back on the expectation that the boyfriend should have surrendered his seat.

"Why does the man always have to suffer?" one person asked.

"It's called chivalry," another responded.
