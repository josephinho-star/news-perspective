Trump country cracked: 5 races that saw double-digit Trump territory fall to Democrats in 2026

2026-11-26 11:00

Donald Trump carried Mississippi by nearly 23 points in 2024. Two years later, Democrat Scott Colom won the state’s Senate race, ending a Republican hold that had lasted more than four decades.

Colom’s victory was part of a string of Democratic breakthroughs during the midterms in places Trump had carried by double digits. Democrats also won Senate races in Kansas and South Carolina, captured the Texas governor’s office and flipped a Republican-held House seat deep in Trump country in Indiana that had not elected a Democrat since 1990.

The results stand out for more than the presidential margins as well — Democrats broke Senate streaks that had lasted decades and defeated Republican incumbents in races many expected the GOP to hold, including one where the Democratic challenger spent next to nothing on television advertisements.

Here are five of the year’s most striking upsets:

Scott Colom breaks Mississippi Democrats' 44-year Senate drought

Trump carried the state by nearly 23 points in 2024, and a Democrat had not won a U.S. Senate race in Mississippi since 1982. Several major election forecasters rated the contest Solid Republican for much of the cycle, before shifting it to Likely Republican as the race tightened.

Colom, a district attorney, defeated Republican incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, R-Miss., by less than one percentage point, 49.36% to 48.54%, according to Associated Press election results. Hyde-Smith had Trump’s backing, and the president rallied for her in the campaign’s final days. Meanwhile, the Senate Leadership Fund, a super PAC aligned with Senate GOP leadership, made a seven-figure late-campaign ad buy, as previously reported by Fox News Digital.

The Associated Press did not call the race until Nov. 11, more than a week after Election Day. Colom will also become the first Black senator to represent Mississippi since Reconstruction.

The margin was razor-thin, but the political barrier was enormous — a Trump +23 state, a Trump-backed incumbent and a 44-year drought in Democratic Senate victories. But Democrats pulled off the upset.

Gina Hinojosa ends Texas' 36-year Democratic losing streak

Hinojosa, a Democratic state representative, defeated three-term Republican Gov. Greg Abbott, R-Texas, as he sought an unprecedented fourth term, ending a 36-year Democratic losing streak in Texas governor’s races. Trump had carried the state by 13.7 points in 2024, according to Associated Press election data, and an October Fox News poll showed Abbott ahead leading up to the Nov. 3 election. Hinojosa, however, ultimately won with 52.8% of the vote to Abbott’s 45.7%, according to Associated Press election results.

Her victory was a major break from Texas’ recent political history and a miss by many major polls. It also made Hinojosa the state’s first Latina governor. Democrats had not won the governor’s office since Ann Richards’ 1990 victory.

Hinojosa’s win was one of two Democratic statewide breakthroughs in Texas, as Democratic state Rep. James Talarico also defeated Republican Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton in the Senate race.

Annie Andrews breaks through in South Carolina

South Carolina had not elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1998, when Fritz Hollings won his final term, and Trump carried the state by nearly 18 points in 2024, according to Associated Press election data. Andrews, a Democratic pediatrician who had never held elected office before, defeated appointed Sen. Darline Graham, R-S.C., the sister of the late Sen. Lindsey Graham, by nearly five points.

Graham had Trump’s endorsement and the president also helped her with late campaign appearances on the trail. She had been appointed to serve out her brother’s term after his death, then won the Republican nomination for a full six-year term.

Andrews’ win broke a nearly three-decade Republican hold on the state’s Senate seat and became a rare moment when a Trump-backed candidate lost in a state Republicans had long counted on.

Adam Hamilton ends Kansas’ 94-year streak

Kansas had not sent a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1932, when George McGill won a full term. Hamilton, a Democrat and the founding pastor of a large United Methodist church, defeated first-term Sen. Roger Marshall, R-Kan., in a state Trump carried by 16 points in 2024, according to election data from the Associated Press.

The Kansas race was long viewed as a Republican hold, but major election forecasters moved it into toss-up territory late in the campaign as Marshall faced scrutiny over his residency and lawsuits his former medical practice filed against patients.

Trump and Vice President JD Vance both campaigned for Marshall, and Republican-aligned groups spent heavily to keep the seat, including more than $26 million committed to ads supporting him after the August primary — more than was spent on Marshall during his first race in 2020, according to AdImpact data cited by the Associated Press.

Hamilton’s win ended a 94-year Republican Senate streak.

J.D. Ford flips a longtime GOP district

Democrat J.D. Ford defeated three-term Rep. Victoria Spartz, R-Ind., in Indiana’s 5th Congressional District, which Trump carried by about 17 points, according to Associated Press election results from 2024. Spartz had won re-election there by 18.6 points in 2024, and the district had not elected a Democrat since 1990, when Jim Jontz won.

Ford, a Democratic state senator, won 51.7% to Spartz’s 48.3%, according to the Associated Press results after the midterms. His victory was the only Indiana House seat to change parties, making it a sharp reversal in a state that Trump carried by 19 points statewide in 2024.

Democrats also flipped several other Republican-held House seats in districts Trump carried by double digits, including in North Carolina. Democrat Richard Ojeda defeated Rep. Richard Hudson, R-N.C., who chaired the National Republican Congressional Committee, the House GOP’s campaign and fundraising arm. Hudson was charged with helping protect the Republican majority, and he had raised $4.2 million to Ojeda’s $2.9 million and won the district by nearly 19 points in 2024, while Trump had carried it by 16 points.

In North Carolina’s 6th District, Democrat Cyril Jefferson defeated first-term Rep. Addison McDowell, R-N.C., in a district Trump carried by nearly 17 points, and Democrat Zach Dembo also flipped an open, Republican-held seat in Kentucky’s 6th District, beating Republican Ralph Alvarado by 9.4 points in a district Trump had carried by roughly 15 points in 2024.

Meanwhile, a razor-thin margin in Tennessee’s 4th District underscores the challenges Republicans faced across several traditionally safe districts. Democrat Victoria Broderick, who spent under $100,000 and had no record of airing any paid TV ads, leads incumbent Rep. Scott DesJarlais, R-Tenn., by 277 votes in the latest count, and DesJarlais conceded the race on Nov. 16. Fox News Digital previously reported that Broderick spent under $100,000 compared to DesJarlais’ more than $500,000. The Associated Press has yet to formally call the race with 99% of the vote counted so far.
