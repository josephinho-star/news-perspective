Trump, Xi, and Putin Are Having a Trilateral Summit. But What’s on the Agenda?

2026-11-18 13:44

The first-ever trilateral summit between U.S. President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese leader Xi Jinping is set for November 19 in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. The agenda is unclear, though the White House claims the lead topic will be nuclear proliferation while Putin seems to want to discuss Ukraine.

Putin originally floated the idea of a trilateral meeting during a phone call with Trump in October. He sought the meeting to discuss ways to end the war in Ukraine, which is possibly suggestive of the effectiveness of Ukraine’s deep strikes within Russia hitting energy and other critical infrastructure. To underscore the point, Ukraine appears to have destroyed a significant portion of the Kerch bridge, which connects the disputed Crimea to Russian territory, in the last couple of days.

Meanwhile, the White House stated that Trump will use the trilateral meeting to focus on “the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world, whose proliferation President Trump believes is very dangerous.”

The three countries are meeting nine months after the New START, the last of the bilateral strategic nuclear arms control agreement between the United States and Russia, expired in February this year. The Trump administration let the treaty expire rather than renew or replace it because Trump is keen on a broader pact that would also include China. Even during his first term, Trump had called for trilateral discussions on nuclear issues.

China has so far rejected the offer of nuclear talks, arguing that it has a considerably smaller nuclear arsenal compared to Russia and the U.S. and that it poses no threat. Most recently, China’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson said, “China’s nuclear strength is by no means at the same level with that of the U.S. and Russia. It is neither fair, reasonable nor realistic to ask China to join the so-called trilateral nuclear disarmament negotiations at this stage.”

China undoubtedly has the fastest growing nuclear arsenal in the world and any effort at bringing China into an arms control process should be a welcome step. Therefore, if Trump is successful in having a focused discussion on nuclear issues in Shenzhen, it could be a good start.

But we should not expect any kind of agreement or statement regarding nuclear issues to come out of this trilateral. Normally, summits such as this are the culmination of significant negotiations, with leaders coming to sign agreements already reached between their respective bureaucracies. That has not happened, which suggests that this will be more of a photo-opportunity than a momentous diplomatic moment. That said, the trilateral could serve to kick-off negotiations, which itself would be a significant achievement. However, there is no indication that Xi has changed his mind on joining such negotiations.

Any progress on nuclear arms control talks will be in Australia’s interests too because China’s nuclear build-up, especially with no transparency or limits, could potentially trigger a nuclear arms race in the Pacific.

For its part, China has yet to indicate any issues of interest for the trilateral and therefore it is difficult to see what Beijing seeks to gain from this meeting. The trilateral summit will be more than a bit concerning if the three leaders end up discussing ideas like spheres of influence, though no such outcome will be lasting.

If the three countries were to reach a deal on Ukraine or Taiwan, that could also be potentially detrimental to regional and global security. Any such agreement would mostly end up being in favor of Russia and China, ignoring the basic tenets of international law. Reaching a deal on Ukraine without Kyiv being part of the discussions may lead to disastrous outcomes for both Ukraine and Europe, but also for the broader democratic community. It would set a bad precedent for China and other authoritarian states in carrying forward their regional territorial ambitions. Even though Trump indicated that he is not keen for the three to discuss Ukraine, media reports suggest that Trump will, in fact, talk Ukraine with Xi and Putin. One can understand how stressful a prospect that must be for Ukraine considering that Trump had earlier said he was fine with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy not being present at a potential meeting with Putin about the war.

On Taiwan, specifically, Trump needs to maintain U.S. commitments and move ahead with arms sales. Sacrificing Taiwan for a trade truce with China would be a horrible mistake. It would only embolden China to pursue such high-handedness while dealing with the U.S., Australia, and others in the rest of Indo-Pacific. China has already many times used economic coercion as leverage to achieve its strategic goals but it hasn’t often succeeded. It would be dangerous to alter that trend.

The best outcome for Australia, and the rest of the international community, will be that this trilateral summit is simply a photo-opportunity to let Trump have a sense of some achievement on the global scene, after a few bad weeks at home.
