Jets offensive coordinator Frank Reich takes blame for ugly touchdown drought

2026-11-20 01:34

It has been a long nine quarters for the Jets offense, which hasn’t scored a touchdown since the Nov. 1 loss to the Raiders in Las Vegas.

tRY IT NOWFew have felt the impact of the drought more than offensive coordinator Frank Reich.

“It starts with me,’’ Reich said Thursday. “I’m the offensive coordinator. I — and we as a staff — have to put players in position to do what they do. We’ve got a very talented group. We need to do a better job coaching [and] the players need to do their part in executing and making plays.”

The Jets have been held to eight Jason Sanders field goals since finding the end zone against the Raiders.

“We know we need to put more points on the board, whether it’s in the red zone or whether we’re farther out, we still want to be explosive,’’ Reich said. “It’s been nine quarters. Believe me, I feel every play of that nine quarters.”

Among the criticism of the Jets offense has been an inability to get the team’s playmakers involved enough on offense.

“When your record is what it is and you go nine quarters without scoring a touchdown, that’s what we’re here all hours of the day trying to get right,’’ Reich said. “We’re working hard to get that right, and I’m confident we will.”

Geno Smith (ankle) was limited in practice Thursday, but was on the field during warmups and has said he expects to play.

If Smith can’t go, Cade Klubnik would be called upon again after filling in for Smith in Sunday’s loss to the Bills.

Klubnik — who went 11-for-20 for 137 yards and one interception and was sacked three times in his NFL debut — was asked what he thought after having some time to watch film.

“There were some good things,’’ Klubnik said. “I made some good throws with confidence, and I was happy with some of my checks and my presence was calm.”

Perhaps most importantly, he didn’t feel overmatched. His teammates agreed he looked the part, which tells the fourth-round pick out of Clemson that he’s headed in the right direction.

“I think it showed my preparation has been good,’’ Klubnik said. “I’ll be able to keep that going, whether I start or not [this week]. It feels good because I didn’t finish the game and come home and say the next morning, ‘I wish I did something completely different.’ The process was good, but always needs to be fine-tuned.”

Add Demario Davis to the chorus of people praising rookie David Bailey, who has 7 ½ sacks and five forced fumbles just nine games into his career.

“He’s had unreal progression,’’ Davis said Thursday of the No. 2 overall pick. “To be at the top of the league in sacks and pressures is impressive.’’

And Davis has also liked what he’s seen in other aspects of Bailey’s game, like his ability to affect attempted screens and deflect passes.

“He’s able to do all facets,’’ Davis said. “You wouldn’t know he was a rookie.”

Andrew Beck, a fullback, has kicked off 10 times as the Jets look for an advantage in their coverage game, using him as a potential added tackler.

Special teams coach Chris Banjo noted that since teams are still adjusting to the new kickoff rules, “the challenge as coaches is to find that extra edge …”

Wide receivers Omar Cooper Jr. (ankle/hip) and Isaiah Williams (ribs) didn’t practice.
