‘War’ Episode 5 Recap: Chat, Is This Bad?

2026-10-30 17:30

“Please give it up for the beautiful, the inevitable, the sublime Ms. Carla Duval!” Her features transform while she walks from green room to stage on the set of The Jack Jeffries Show, a kind of Graham Norton chatfest for the media universe as it exists inside War on HBO. Carla is the famous actress, greeting handlers and staffers politely as she glides by. She is her whole self, all of the dramas of her divorce and risks of appearing publicly while it’s happening, crossing like the shadow of a storm across the Channel. And the instant her intro is over and the cue hits, she becomes locked in and camera-ready. So many performances happening inside Carla all the time.

Which is probably what she wanted to get across, when Carla impulsively lashes out on the show. But it doesn’t go like she meant. “This is a talk show! Let me bloody talk! He’s the one who’s acting!” And to a heckling audience, “You lot can fuck off!” It goes on like that. Carla, set off by a photo taken on the Milliner red carpet seconds before she got served, immediately becomes trending public enemy #1 on the socials, and “Car Crash Duval” headlines splash across every tabloid in the UK. As she cowers from the paparazzi in seclusion, the braintrust at Cathcarts deliver metaphorical high-fives. For them, Carla’s outburst is a colossal own goal.

“As much as it renders your whole case unwinnable, it’s actually not the very worst situation to be in.” The spin at Taylor & Byrne, from a crisis PR expert, is that people are at least still talking about Carla. “When the hating ends, that’s when the world has made up its mind.” The rhythms of this media world War has created feel so recognizable. Carla and Morgan’s dispute, and the tactics of their respective teams, the whole thing becoming some larger beast with many more mouths and limbs; the Blake Lively and Justin Baldoni stuff comes to mind.

An influence-shifting podcast is also part of this War media landscape, and who says what when they hop on the Every Little Thing She Does pod has the power to change conversations. Carla’s appearance on the show does exactly that — she is able to harness hearts and likes for being blindsided by Morgan and his lawyers — and so expectations shift back to the Henderson side and Cathcarts, who never ever run out of dirty tricks. Morgan’s subsequent, public peacemaking dinner with Gabriel isn’t the salve he thinks it is, as he tries to tell the kid nothing about their relationship will change. It’s actually a false flag sympathy op arranged for the flashing pap cameras by St. John Smallwood. It’s a PR war they’re in, where the battles are fought in the media space.

At least before all of this heads to court. Remember, this whole thing is still barrelling toward litigation. Cathcarts’ other ratfucker tactic is to bury Taylor & Byrne in paperwork while Johnny Warren, their man on the inside, continues to feed them intel. At night, Johnny snoops in Serena’s office. He takes spy photos of a list of Carla’s personal assets. It’s full of legitimate things she owns — and things she declared as a lark, like a Ferrari Testarossa. But Johnny doesn’t know he’s taking photos of the first version, before Serena made Molly make the proper corrections and re-type the document. When his bad data gets baked into the hard copy that Cathcarts then sends back to Taylor & Byrne, Johnny the Spy will be cooked.

So why not agree to meet Carla after hours before that happens? When the paparazzi firestorm hit, she told Johnny and his I’m-there-for-you texts to buzz off. But later, when public perception has turned back to her, Carla is more comfortable in her skin. She calls Johnny, gives him directions to sneak in through the back gate, and when he arrives, they agree it’s all a bad idea. Which part, the furious making out on the floor of her flat, or him spending the night?

Johnny wakes up in her bed the next morning to the usual flurry of texts and missed calls from his girlfriend Bella, and Carla wakes up to another media world worm-turn. Splashy photos of Morgan hugging Gabriel outside a restaurant, and the headline “Son Sides With Stepfather.” Carla is the bad actress, the bad mother, the bad wife, the bad person, the bad woman — again. So many performances within her.

It’s come down to this. Carla is with Serena and Nick, crying, trying to back out of going to court, because she’s realized the gravity of this as it relates to Gabe. It’s all gotten so rotten, she says, so putrid in public. Why couldn’t her relationship with Morgan had stayed like it was in those idyllic months they spent on the beach in Normandy? She’s emotional, wistful, forgetting everything she did to put this rotten thing in motion, when she muses over the poetry where she and Morgan found a bond. “To hail soft Zephyr, as she goes…”

“Either your ex comes after your business, or I do. And I can do it in a way that keeps you rich.” Michael Ancker is in the building. Actor David Menkin does a terrific job immediately selling us on Ancker as the billion candlewatt-arrogant tech oligarch of the War media world. But when he pitches Morgan on a two-billion-dollar sale of Zephyr, with a massive chunk guaranteed safe from his wife’s lawyers, Morgan tells the guy to fuck right off. Maybe he still believes there is something to be solved equitably, and recovered amicably.

In that same moment, Carla is revealing in her memory the kernel of what Taylor & Byrne could take. With her memory of the poem, and its inspiration for the Zephyr platform’s name, Nick sees the courtroom opening to their victory. “We take that one fact, and we get you half.” Let the next performance begin.

Discovery for War Season 1 Episode 5 (“Every Little Thing She Does”):

- War jams beating the drum. When Carla calls Johnny and he sidles through the back gate, the sounds of Devendra Banhart (“Bad Girl”) waft through her flat. And later, when the Testarossa File arrives at Taylor & Byrne, and Johnny looks like he just swallowed a bug, Bob Dylan cranks up the “Trouble.”

- A hit UK-based podcast called Every Little Thing She Does? Name’s gotta be a reference to this 1981 Ghost in the Machine highlight from The Police, right?

- “To hail soft Zephyr, as she goes/To fan the dew-drop from the rose;/To shelter from the scorching beam,/And muse beside the rippling stream.“

- The poem Carla remembers reading with Morgan, during the first blush of their love at her cottage in Normandy, is “Ode to the Muse,” a 1791 composition by English poet Mary Darby Robinson.
