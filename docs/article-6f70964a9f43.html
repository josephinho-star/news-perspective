France to deploy 70,000 officers as it braces for weekend of protests – Europe live

2026-10-23 13:53

70,000 police officers and gendarmes to be deployed

The French interior minister says the government is mobilising 70,000 police officers and gendarmes- military officers who serve in the national police force – to city streets this weekend.

Riot squads and other elite, specialised units will be deployed. Even police trainees are being drafted in as support.

Laurent Nuñez said the government was aiming for an “extremely mobile, agile and effective” force. Some units will be stationed specifically outside government and public buildings as protection.

As I’ve noted earlier, several museums and tourist sites are closing this weekend – including Paris’s major museums the Louvre and Orsay – while shops and businesses are also boarding up.

‘Hell on earth’: last residents eat weeds to survive in Russian-occupied Oleshky

The White House says the first lady has led the charge in negotiating this evacuation corridor for civilians in Oleshky; we’ll have more details soon.

My colleagues Peter Beaumont and Artem Mazhulin however had this report last month on just how dire the situation has become in the war-ravaged town:

Oleshky, across the Dnipro River from the southern city of Kherson, has become “a hell on earth”, according to Ukrainians familiar with conditions there.

Food had finally run out for the 2,000 or so civilians, including 50 children, remaining in the city and its surrounding villages.

In messages shared with the Guardian, residents said they were down to one meal a day: soup without potato, made with courgette grown in what once were the flower boxes of the city’s apartment houses, sometimes with the addition of foraged purslane, an edible weed.

“We’re running out of everything,” said a resident early in the summer. “By August, the situation will be critical for us. And come autumn, we’ll definitely die. I’m already dying. I’m getting weaker by the day.”

“We’re in Oleshky [and] we can’t get out. We’re going hungry … Right now, I’ve got absolutely nothing to eat,” read another message from a woman in the besieged city who was waiting to be evacuated by the occupying forces. Before the war, she had weighed 110kg (17st); now she weighed just 67kg, she wrote.

In September, military authorities in Kherson carried out a large-scale airdrop of emergency food, water and medical supplies into the city and surrounding areas, using large drones, after Russia reportedly again rejected a ceasefire to evacuate civilians.

The Ukrainian foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, raised the situation in Oleshky at the UN general assembly in New York in September. He told those gathered: “Russia is deliberately making life there impossible. They are starving them to death.”

Melania Trump helps broker ceasefire in Russian-occupied town where residents were starving to death

The White House has issued a statement saying Melania Trump has helped broker a ‘ceasefire’ in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky, where residents have been enduring a worsening humanitarian crisis due to the lack of food, electricity and other resources.

The small town in southern Kherson has been the centre of an increasingly desperate humanitarian crisis under Russian occupation.

Since May, Russia has failed to deliver food, medicine or other essential goods to the area leading to deaths from starvation and disease. There has also been problems with the burial of bodies and sanitation, as the local morgue has been destroyed and there is no running water.

Russia and Ukraine have accused the other of blocking supplies to the population of the town which Ukrainian forces want to retake.

Ukraine has previously appealed for a ceasefire to evacuate the estimated 2,000 civilians left in the town – but Russia hadn’t made any commitments.

Earlier this week, Pope Leo called for an intervention while the Kremlin said Putin had discussed the situation with Donald Trump.

The White House now say both countries have agreed to pause military action “to allow for humanitarian aid to enter and to prepare a civilian evacuation through a designated safe passageway.

A ceasefire is set to come into effect on 29 October.

“The situation in Oleshky is dire. Oleshky is a front-line war city on the brink of disaster. Oleshky’s remaining population is comprised primarily of elderly residents who urgently need medical attention, medicine, food, and clean water,” Melania Trump said in the statement.

The Guardian’s international correspondents are looking into the statement and attempting to corroborate it with their sources.

France and UK stage joint exercise to conduct a 'simulated nuclear strike'

The French and British governments have issued a joint statement confirming that their air forces have for the first time staged a joint exercise simulating a nuclear strike, AFP reports.

UK defence minister Wes Streeting was quoted as saying:

This exercise demonstrates the depth of our partnership, the credibility of our deterrents, and our determination to remain united in the face of emerging threats.

France’s statement said that “possessing independent nuclear forces does not preclude coordination.”

The exercise consisted of French Rafales taking off from a base in northern France escorted by British RAF Typhoons.

Von der Leyen 'deeply alarmed' by drone attack in Black Sea

President of the European CommissionUrsula von der Leyen said Friday she was “deeply alarmed” after Russia was blamed for a new drone attack that sank a ship in the Black Sea near Romania.

Posting on social media, AFP report she said:

Deeply alarmed by yet another Russian escalation in the Black Sea. A civilian vessel carrying Ukrainian and Azerbaijani crew has been struck off the coast of Romania. These reckless attacks target innocent lives and openly defy international law.

President Emmanuel Macron has commented on a shooting earlier today in the town of Trappes, west of Paris, which has left one person dead and wounded four others, including a 12-year-old girl.

On social media the French president said:

We would like to thank our law enforcement agencies, which have already taken the suspect into custody. The National Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor’s Office has taken over the case.

France protest bans criticised by the left and civil rights groups

It’s all a bit of a moveable feast at the moment in terms of where and when protests can or cannot be held this weekend.

Initially authorities in cities like Marseille and Lyon had issued blanket bans on any unauthorised demonstrations.

That prompted 1.) a rush by trade unions and student groups to file their protest plans with officials and negotiate march routes and

2.) last-minute legal challenges with plaintiffs arguing their constitutional right to protest is being suppressed by the government.

Trade unionists, lawyers, students, public intellectuals, and rights activists have all come out criticising the curbs on protest. The government says it’s trying to prevent a repeat of the looting, fires and violence seen last weekend.

Left-wing politicians also denounced the “headlong authoritarian rush” of the government. Far-left leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon’s party put out this statement this morning:

By deciding to ban demonstrations, to choose who has the right to organize them, to put in place curfews on the scale of a department or a city, to encourage traders to use private security, the executive is crossing one by one the limits that separate a democracy from an authoritarian and police state.

Furthermore, bans on demonstrations are foolish decisions that are likely to generate chaos. Indeed, these bans will not quell anger against the government’s policies, nor will they quell the desire to express it in the streets.

Meanwhile co-president of the Association of Criminal Lawyers, Romain Boulet, slammed the thousands of arrests that have occurred this week calling it: “The most repressive week in the history of the Fifth Republic.”

Never before in France have there been so many arrests and so many people brought before the courts as this week.

The Justice Minister has just given an updated on sentencings- saying 232 people have been sentenced to prison since Saturday.

Elite units like the GIGN – the National Gendarmerie Intervention Group- and RAID (Research, Assistance, Intervention and Deterrence) are being activated tomorrow in the protests.

These units more commonly specialise in counter-terrorism events, hostage rescue, protecting senior state officials.

Nuñez defended their involvement in policing these street demonstrations of students, workers and civil servants. He noted calls on social media “from the far-left” to target public buildings.

“We are not talking about a peaceful, friendly demonstration,” Nunez told reporters. “We are ready tomorrow to be extremely firm and not tolerate anything.“

Laurent Nuñez said the government was aiming for an “extremely mobile, agile and effective” force. Some units will be stationed specifically outside government and public buildings as protection.

As I’ve noted earlier, several museums and tourist sites are closing this weekend – including Paris’s major museums the Louvre and Orsay – while shops and businesses are also boarding up.

Nuñez again condemned the vandalism and looting seen on the sidelines of protests last weekend – saying authorities would be “uncompromising” with “the vandals, with the looters and with all those who will commit atrocities”.

He also again repeated that some of this had been stoked by groups “on the far-left and radical extreme left” – but didn’t provide details.

“What happened on Octobe 17 is totally unacceptable. We will be fully in control to prevent these lootings, these attacks on public buildings” from occurring again, he said.

More than 200 people have already been jailed over last weekend’s violence.

French interior minister Laurent Nuñez is speaking now following a meeting of the Defence Council this morning, chaired by President Macron.

He begins by mentioning the shooting this morning in Trappes before going on to defending some of the blanket bans on demonstrations that have been issued in different regions across the country- saying each prefecture has the best idea of the security situation.

For example, until an hour ago, Marseille authorities had banned all demonstrations but the prefecture there has now allowed a trade union march to go ahead tomorrow.

Several local protest bans are also being challenged in last-minute court hearings today.

Nuñez will also be travelling to Orleans tonight to speak with law enforcement there ahead of the National Rally congress and counter-protests expected in the city tomorrow.
