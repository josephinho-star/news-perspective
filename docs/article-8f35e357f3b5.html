Hurricane Isaias updates: at least four people dead and hundreds of thousands without power – as it happened

2026-10-10 22:18

In Florida, one person was killed after a tree fell on their home, the state’s governor, Ron DeSantis, said at an afternoon news conference on Saturday. Another individual was found dead in their garage with the generator on.

In Alabama, two people are dead, according to that state’s governor, Kay Ivey.

Earlier reports indicated one casualty due to a downed tree hitting a home, but details around the second death remain unknown.

After beginning as a category 2 hurricane, Isaias weakened to a post-tropical cyclone early on Saturday after it made landfall in Florida and lashed the US Gulf coast with heavy rain and fierce winds.

Customers are slowly getting power back as the total number of power outages across the US has trended down after hitting a peak of more than 850,000 earlier in the day. As of Saturday evening, it is hovering at about 630,000, according to poweroutage.com.

Trump previously approved emergency declarations for Florida and Alabama ahead of the storm’s landfall.

The National Hurricane Center said parts of Georgia and the Carolinas could see up to 10in of rain, though totals are likely to be 3 to 6in. The center added that there is a possibility of tornadoes across eastern Georgia and the Carolinas.

Isaias weakens to post-tropical cyclone over central Alabama

Isaias, which has been downgraded from a hurricane to a post-tropical cyclone, is rapidly weakening over central Alabama, but its remnants are continuing to unleash severe weather across the eastern US with torrential rain and damaging winds.

Approximately 10 million residents from Florida northward to southern Virginia remain under flood watches and warnings, with several advisories extended through Sunday afternoon.

As the system shifts toward the mid-Atlantic and north-east, an additional 1-3in of rain is expected, with localized rainfall totals exceeding 5in in areas hit by training storms.

A massive swath of the eastern seaboard, encompassing roughly 22 million people from Louisiana up into Pennsylvania, remains under wind alerts. Wind gusts up to 60mph threaten to cause widespread power outages, down trees and disrupt travel through the remainder of the weekend.

The Blue Cross NC 300 at Charlotte Motor Speedway has been postponed to Sunday at 8pm due to heavy rain and weather conditions from the remnants of Hurricane Isaias.

The race will follow the Nascar Cup Series Bank of America 400 at the track in Concord, North Carolina.

Between 3 and 6in of rain is predicted in parts of Georgia and North and South Carolina due to Isaias, with up to 10in possible in some areas.

Shell, the oil and gas mega-company, said they are actively monitoring the “improving offshore conditions” in the wake of Hurricane Isaias. As conditions continue to improve today, the company says they are “beginning the process of redeploying personnel to our Mars, Olympus, Ursa, Vito and Appomattox assets”.

Energy department approves 4m barrels of emergency oil

The US Department of Energy said on Saturday it authorized an emergency exchange of up to 4m barrels of crude oil from the strategic petroleum reserve to address supply disruptions due to Hurricane Isaias.

“With this emergency exchange, the energy department is acting swiftly to help maintain refinery operations, limit disruptions to fuel supplies, and ensure American families and businesses continue to have access to affordable, reliable, and secure energy,” the US secretary of energy, Chris Wright, said in a statement to Reuters.

The center of Isaias has moved out of Florida, but storm hazards persist.

State officials advised residents to exercise caution if they go outside.

“There could be downed power lines,” governor Ron DeSantis said early Saturday at a news conference. “There could be floodwaters. Stay out of those.”

Jared Perdue, secretary of Florida’s transportation department, urged the public to avoid unnecessary travel, as utility crews, debris removal teams and first responders are at work.

“Remember vehicles can be swept away in an instance in water that is not very deep,” Perdue said.

In an update on social media, the Florida national guard shared photos of troops pulling people to safety after a truck became submerged in a flooded ditch.

“En route from Tallahassee, the Soldiers stopped, swam to the vehicle and pulled its occupants to safety,” the post said.
