Cousins emotional after win in Vikings return, reflects on 'tough three years'

2026-11-16 00:24

GREEN BAY, Wis. -- Kirk Cousins managed to hold back tears Sunday morning when he arrived at Lambeau Field. He didn't even try on the way out.

After the Minnesota Vikings' 30-10 victory over the Green Bay Packers, Cousins' first game since the Vikings reacquired him at Tuesday's trade deadline, Cousins let it all out.

"It was emotional coming to the stadium," Cousins told ESPN's Lindsey Thiry, his voice catching several times before he headed into the locker room. "Three years ago, tearing my Achilles here. It's been a tough three years. I was just grateful for the opportunity to play here today, and grateful for my teammates and what they did to make this outcome possible."

Cousins' six-season tenure as the Vikings' quarterback effectively ended Oct. 29, 2023, when he tore his right Achilles tendon during a 24-10 victory over the Packers. He signed that offseason with the Atlanta Falcons, only to see the Falcons use the No. 8 pick of the 2024 draft on quarterback Michael Penix Jr. After two years in limbo, Cousins signed last spring with the Las Vegas Raiders, who then selected quarterback Fernando Mendoza with the No. 1 pick.

The Vikings, however, needed him to do nothing short of rescuing their season when they traded a 2027 third-round pick Tuesday to bring him back. Week 1 starter Kyler Murray is nursing a strained hamstring, and in Week 9, backup Carson Wentz had the worst outing by QBR of any NFL quarterback in a game this season. The Vikings had lost consecutive games after a 6-0 start.

On Sunday, Cousins became the first quarterback since at least 1950, when the NFL began tracking quarterback snaps, to start games for different teams in consecutive weeks. He completed 21 of 31 passes for 207 yards and two touchdowns, including a season-high nine completions to receiver Justin Jefferson, and was particularly effective in critical moments of the NFC North matchup.

Cousins completed all eight pass attempts during a fourth-quarter drive that gave the Vikings a three-score lead. On third downs, he completed 9 of 12 passes for 110 yards and seven first-down conversions. Jefferson said afterward that with a defense that is playing as well as the Vikings' -- it sacked Packers quarterback Jordan Love eight times and forced four turnovers -- the Vikings' offense needs only to be "mediocre" to do its part."

"He did what we needed him to do," Jefferson said, "and that was just pitch and catch, just kind of getting it to the open guys and just letting them be the playmakers. It wasn't really too much of big shots down the field, but it was just dumping it off and getting those extra 3, 4 yards after the catch. So it was definitely great for us. And his composure, his confidence in the huddle, all of that, it definitely created a positive vibe in the huddle."

Cousins arrived in Minnesota on Tuesday night, attended his first meeting Wednesday morning and participated in two practices Thursday and Friday. Right tackle Brian O'Neill said the Vikings changed most of their cadences to accommodate Cousins' preferences during the short week, and Cousins said that on a couple of occasions he let coach Kevin O'Connell know what playcalls he was most and least comfortable with.

"He's always been mature," O'Connell said. "He's always been meticulous in his preparation. He's always done everything in his power to prepare himself for games. But there was a level of poise. It never felt like at any point from the time we talked on the phone [before the trade] all the way through to giving him a game ball today, where it was anything other than just rock solid, consistent."

Said O'Neill: "I can't say enough about what he did all week to prepare. I mean, he has been gone for a couple years, learned a language in Atlanta, learned a language in Vegas, and then in three days switched back to the language we talk about here. And he was running the show and he was in command. It was so awesome to see that. ... [and] a little bit of full circle to have a good feeling again in this locker room, but we got a lot of work to do and we'll keep going."

O'Neill was referencing the morose atmosphere in the visitors locker room after their game at Lambeau in 2023, knowing that Cousins would be lost for the season. The Vikings tiptoed around that coincidence this week, and O'Connell said he knew Cousins well enough to avoid the topic entirely.

"Kirk's not really wired like that," O'Connell said.

Cousins, however, had clearly thought about it himself.

"There's so much football up ahead," Cousins said, "but certainly it's gratifying to be able to come back here with the purple on. I think to come back here with another team isn't quite the same. To come back here with the purple on, similar time of year, three years later, it's a blessing, to be able to have a win like we had today.

"But there's just so much football ahead, and the story is going to get written much later."
