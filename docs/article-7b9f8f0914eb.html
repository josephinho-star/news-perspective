Welsh first minister accuses Burnham of ‘casually’ rejecting devolution calls

2026-11-26 11:09

The Welsh first minister has launched a fierce attack on Andy Burnham, accusing him of “casually” rejecting calls to hand over more powers to the Welsh government.

In an interview with the Guardian, Rhun ap Iorwerth said Burnham was treating Wales with less respect than he was showing to English regions, had little understanding of how devolution worked in Wales and had “let himself down”.

Calling for an urgent face-to-face meeting, the Plaid Cymru leader said he would try to rally Welsh Labour members and other Welsh politicians behind him to force the prime minister to think again.

In a letter to ap Iorwerth, Burnham said he was “not persuaded” by the Plaid-led Welsh government’s case to devolve policing and justice, the crown estate and rail infrastructure to Wales.

Ap Iorwerth said the content and “abrupt” and “dismissive” tone of the letter was a surprise given Burnham’s rhetoric on devolution. He said he felt a deep frustration and disappointment at what he characterised as a “casual casting of Wales’ aspirations aside”.

Ap Iorwerth said: “It’s clear that when Andy Burnham talks of the positivity of devolution, he is not talking in the same respectful terms towards Wales as he is towards regions of England. I believe the prime minister has let himself down … people were expecting more.”

He said the case for more policy areas to be devolved to Wales was not a “whim” and had been built for years by independent experts as well as Plaid and senior Welsh Labour politicians. He said: “This is cross-party. This is non-party political. This is independent evidence gathered over many years about taking Wales forward.”

When he came to power, Burnham declared: “The days of Whitehall fighting the devolution of power into the regions and nations are over, for good.”

Ap Iorwerth said he thought the prime minister genuinely believed in devolution. “But his understanding of devolution and the merits of it are seen very much from an English perspective. He clearly wants to drive forward devolution in England and he’s trying to see Wales as another England. He is trying to fit a round Wales peg into a square English hole here.

“To hear him casually declare that on those fundamental next stages of the devolution story – policing, justice, the crown estate and rail infrastructure – he was not persuaded and therefore it would not happen ran contrary to all the rhetoric that we have heard from him.”

Ap Iorweth claimed there had long been a split within Welsh Labour between those who backed more significant powers for Cardiff and those who were more London-focused: “Within part of the Labour party there is hostility to empowering Wales as a nation.”

Labour’s disastrous results in the Senedd elections this year – its worst ever – meant the “Welsh-facing” element of the party was weakened, ap Iorwerth said. Burnham had “outsourced” his decision on the extra powers to the element that “don’t believe in taking Wales forward”, he added.

“We’re asking good people in the Labour party and in the wider Labour movement to work with us to make sure that Wales is not forgotten here by the UK government,” the first minister said. “He [Burnham] needs to learn about Wales because he’s never had any interaction with Wales politically before.”

Plaid, which ultimately wants independence for Wales, believes its win in the Senedd elections means it has a strong mandate to secure more powers. But in the letter, Burnham said: “Devolution should not simply mean transferring powers from one parliament to another. It should put more power in the hands of places and communities.”

He said an integrated England and Wales policing system was essential to maintaining “seamless cross-border working” and a “shared England and Wales justice system” was preferred.

On the crown estate, he said the “join up” with England was crucial for investors in floating offshore wind projects, and managing rail infrastructure required “consistency on both sides of the border”.

The UK government is worried that the changes Plaid wants will take many years to enact, and distract from the fundamental job of improving prosperity in Wales.

Stephen Kinnock, the secretary of state for Wales, said: “The prime minister has written to the first minister setting out an offer for how he wants to work in partnership to deliver good growth and jobs in every Welsh postcode, reindustrialise Wales and empower Welsh communities through devolution.

“The prime minister’s offer reflects our view that devolution must be about more than transferring powers from one government to another and that he instead wants each government to consider, alongside local authority partners, how we can empower Welsh regions to use both reserved and devolved powers to drive economic growth.”

The pro-independence movement YesCymru is planning an “urgent protest” in Cardiff this weekend on the issue. Rob Hughes, the organisation’s chair, said: “We can’t put up with this any more – Andy Burnham is treating Wales with total disrespect.”
