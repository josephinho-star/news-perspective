Picking up the pieces after Week 12 and what it all means for Rivalry Week

2026-11-23 22:20

In Friday's preview, I basically hinted at chaos on the horizon. Both of the Big Ten's favorites were playing on the road, as was every team ranked between No. 10 and No. 16 in the College Football Playoff rankings. Some teams were going to lose, even if we didn't know who.

I was hopeful, but Saturday exceeded expectations. Both Big Ten favorites lost. So did No. 14 Ohio State and No. 16 Kentucky. No. 10 BYU flirted seriously with the idea. No. 17 Boise State blew a late lead in shocking fashion, throwing the race for the Group of 6's CFP berth into question. Seemingly every Big 12 game featured a solid comeback, overtime or both, and place-kickers were deciding games from coast to coast.

Now we enter Rivalry Week with both uncertainty that we expected and uncertainty we did not know to look for (namely, Big Ten tiebreaker scenarios). Let's relive a wacky week by spinning it forward. What happened on Saturday, and how does it apply to what we'll see this coming weekend?

All times below are Eastern.

The CFP committee always manages to surprise me in some form or fashion on Tuesday nights, but I feel comfortable making this broad generalization: At this point, six to seven teams -- Utah (11-0), Georgia (10-1), Notre Dame (10-1), Miami (10-1), Alabama (9-2), Texas (9-2) and quite possibly Tennessee (9-2) -- have to feel secure about losing a Rivalry Week game but remaining in the projected playoff field. In some order, they should make up the top seven in the new rankings.

Factoring in the Group of 6's guaranteed spot (which we'll talk about in a bit), that basically leaves either eight to nine teams fighting for the last four to five spots: Wake Forest (10-1), UCLA (10-1), Texas Tech (10-1), BYU (9-2), Indiana (9-2), Iowa (9-2), Oregon (8-3) and USC (8-3). For this exercise, we'll include Tennessee in this batch, though the Vols really could be safe by now. (Note: I'm using updated AP poll rankings below because the CFP rankings aren't out until Tuesday.)

No. 15 USC at No. 10 UCLA (Saturday, 3:30 p.m., CBS) -- SP+ win probability: UCLA, 70%

This one's far more interesting than it was a couple of days ago because UCLA will drop, perhaps quite a bit, due to Saturday's overtime loss at Michigan. The Bruins are likely in "win and you're in, lose and you're out" status against USC, and the Trojans technically aren't out of the CFP hunt if they win either, though they'll need loads of help.

We've known for a while what UCLA's fatal flaw would likely be -- the Bruins were pretty mediocre throwing the ball before Nico Iamaleava's injury, and while Ty Dieffenbach had developed a nice rapport with deep-ball master Aidan Mizell, it remained likely that passing troubles would be at the heart of the Bruins either missing the CFP or losing once they got there. Sure enough, Dieffenbach took four sacks, threw an interception and lost an unforced fumble against an awesome Michigan defense. Then, to make matters even worse, he was carted off with injury in overtime. Third-stringer Madden Iamaleava was 0-for-1 passing and rushed twice for minus-1 yard. Dieffenbach's status is uncertain moving forward, and Madden Iamaleava seems likely to start Saturday.

The other seven teams in this tier are all favored to win their rivalry games, but per SP+ there's only a 28% chance that all of them do. Iowa will host Nebraska as only a marginal favorite, Texas Tech faces a short week and an awkward Thursday night kickoff against TCU, and Oregon will take on a suddenly torrid Washington on Saturday night. We should see at least a couple of upsets.

Nebraska at No. 13 Iowa (Friday, noon, CBS) -- SP+ win probability: Iowa, 61%

Cincinnati at No. 11 BYU (Saturday, 3:30 p.m., Fox) -- SP+ win probability: BYU, 88%

Duke at No. 8 Wake Forest (Saturday, 3:30 p.m., ESPN) -- SP+ win probability: Wake, 85%

No. 7 Tennessee at Vanderbilt (Saturday, 7 p.m., ESPN) -- SP+ win probability: Tennessee, 93%

Purdue at No. 12 Indiana (Saturday, 7 p.m., FS1) -- SP+ win probability: Indiana, 97%

Washington at No. 14 Oregon (Saturday, 7:30 p.m., NBC) -- SP+ win probability: Oregon, 79%

For this group, it's all about surviving and advancing. BYU didn't exactly look the part of a surefire playoff team in Saturday afternoon's triple-overtime win over Kansas, but the Cougars indeed survived, and their marquee win over Notre Dame should keep them on the right side of the in-or-out line.

Wake, meanwhile, has been wrecking shop at an almost unprecedented level for the past two months but will likely remain barely in the field because of the CFP committee's overemphasis on schedule strength. (Watch the games, guys! Wake is amazing right now!) With Saturday's 50-29 romp over Georgia Tech -- a game the Demon Deacons led by as many as 36 before Tech began scoring in garbage time (and a game in which the most drama came from the stands) -- they have outscored their last seven opponents by an average of 52-10 and have led their past five by an average of 36-1 at halftime. But if they trip up against Duke this coming weekend, they're likely out.

Four of the games above (Nebraska-Iowa, USC-UCLA, Purdue-Indiana and Washington-Oregon) will also define how the final Big Ten title game race plays out. And they could actually create some bid thievery. Because what seemed like a pretty straightforward situation heading into the weekend -- UCLA was 7-0, Indiana was 6-1, and everyone else was 5-2 or worse -- suddenly got incredibly messy.

I mentioned above that UCLA's fatal flaw was pretty clear; the same could be said for Indiana. Because of quarterback inconsistency and spotty place-kicking, the Hoosiers had become close-game addicts of late -- five of their last six games had been decided by one score -- and both of those factors bit them in Saturday's 27-24 overtime loss to Washington. Grant Wilson, who replaced a struggling Josh Hoover in the lineup a couple of weeks earlier, was replaced by Hoover early in the third quarter, and both Nico Radicic (third quarter) and long-distance specialist Paddy McAteer (overtime) had field goals blocked.

Throw in a batch of spotty defense from a previously impeccable unit -- UW's Demond Williams Jr. threw for 227 yards and gained 114 yards in seven non-sack carries -- and Indiana finds itself in serious trouble in both the CFP hunt and Big Ten race.

After the upsets, UCLA is 7-1 in conference play, while Oregon, Indiana and Iowa are all 6-2. Thanks to a perfect record against common opponents, Oregon would win a tiebreaker against either Indiana or Iowa, so if the Ducks and UCLA both win, they'll play each other in, of all places, Indianapolis. If Oregon wins and UCLA loses, the tiebreakers go Iowa's way. If Oregon loses, then it could be either Indiana or Iowa depending on other factors. And then there's my favorite scenario: If USC beats UCLA and Oregon, Iowa and Indiana all lose ... we get an immediate USC-UCLA rematch. Got all that?

This will play out in slow motion, starting early Friday in Iowa City. That will clarify some of the stakes before USC-UCLA on Saturday afternoon, then the two Saturday evening games.

Second spots in the SEC and Big 12 title games

The Big Ten's race is the most convoluted of the power conferences, but while we already know we're getting Miami-Wake Forest in the ACC championship game, only half of the matchups are set in the Big 12 (where Utah has clinched a berth) and SEC (Georgia is in).

In the SEC, it will take four games to separate three 6-2 teams.

Mississippi State (5-6) at No. 23 Ole Miss (Friday, noon, ABC) -- SP+ win probability: Ole Miss, 82%

No. 5 Texas at Texas A&M (Friday, 7:30 p.m., ABC) -- SP+ win probability: Texas, 87%

No. 7 Tennessee at Vanderbilt (Saturday, 7 p.m., ESPN) -- SP+ win probability: Tennessee, 93%

Auburn at No. 6 Alabama (Saturday, 7:30 p.m., ABC) -- SP+ win probability: Alabama, 88%

If Ole Miss beats Mississippi State in the Egg Bowl, that will, strangely enough, mean that Texas' conference opponents have a better overall conference record than Alabama's opponents, giving the Longhorns a key, potential tiebreaker advantage. If MSU pulls an upset -- and that indeed would be a hell of an upset -- then Bama will have that advantage. If all the favorites win, Texas will play Georgia in the final SEC title game. It wouldn't take many upsets for either Bama or Tennessee to end up with the edge, but as you see above, any upset will be a pretty significant one. All of the favorites have a projected advantage of at least two touchdowns.

In the Big 12, either 7-1 Texas Tech or 6-2 BYU will face Utah, and we'll know by Thanksgiving night. If Tech handles TCU as projected, the Red Raiders are in. If TCU rides a fired-coach bump to an upset, BYU would play its way into a Holy War rematch with a win Saturday.

TCU at No. 9 Texas Tech (Thursday, 8 p.m., ESPN) -- SP+ win probability: Tech, 86%

Cincinnati at No. 11 BYU (Saturday, 3:30 p.m., Fox) -- SP+ win probability: BYU, 88%

Two comments about Georgia and Utah, by the way:

1. Maybe the worst thing that could have possibly happened to any other team in the country was Georgia falling asleep and blowing a 14-point lead against Auburn a few weeks ago. The Bulldogs were already the best team in the country in my opinion, but since that game they have adopted an utterly sadistic streak. They scored early-round knockouts against Florida, Ole Miss and Missouri, and on Saturday they unleashed a downright mean surprise onside kick early in the second half of a comfortable win against South Carolina.

The Bulldogs were settling for field goals and therefore hadn't yet landed the knockout punch against the Gamecocks, and since they punted after this kick, it didn't knock Carolina out either. But this pretty clearly revealed where Kirby Smart's mindset is. And it's a pretty scary thing for everyone else.

2. Though Utah doesn't blow teams out like Georgia does, you could say the Utes learned a similar lesson a while back. Since nearly blowing a comfortable second-half lead against Colorado, the Utes have played in four competitive games, and they've won the fourth quarter in those games by a combined 58-14.

They trailed Houston heading into the fourth but put the game away with a pair of scores. They went up eight early in the fourth against BYU and locked things down on defense. They fell behind Arizona late in Tucson but almost immediately responded with a game-winning, 70-yard touchdown pass from Devon Dampier to Creed Whittemore. And in a tricky game against TCU on Saturday night, they were tied with six minutes left but scored, forced a punt, scored on a 91-yard Dampier-to-Larry Simmons pass, picked off a pass and scored again, all in about four minutes. If Georgia is mastering early-round knockouts, Utah is mastering late-round knockouts.

This one's really blurry. Because the CFP committee is forever uninterested in ranking mid-major teams (and because there's no sort of "receiving votes" column in which we see who else was considered for a spot), Boise State is the only Group of 6 team that has had a CFP ranking this season. The Broncos suffered a shocking collapse late against San Diego State, however, and might fall out of the rankings this week. Meanwhile, we have no idea what the committee might think about fellow 9-2 teams Louisiana Tech, UTSA, New Mexico, Air Force or North Dakota State or a 10-1 Liberty team that has been romping of late but has played against a particularly weak schedule.

When I say BSU's collapse was shocking, I mean it was shocking.

The Broncos had a chance to go up two scores with less than five minutes to play but had to settle for a field goal and a 20-13 lead after a first-and-goal, and SDSU made it 20-19 with 1:51 left but missed a 2-point conversion. Needing a first down to run out the clock, Maddux Madsen threw a short pass to Keawe Browne on third-and-8, but he gained only 5 yards and was pushed out of bounds, stopping the clock with 1:31 left. Spencer Danielson elected to go for it on fourth down to ice the game, but Madsen got hemmed in for a loss. Given a bonus possession and about 40 bonus seconds, SDSU advanced the ball 30 yards, and Cooper DiLeva nailed a 42-yard game winner. The Aztecs clinched the other spot in the Pac-12 championship game and will return to Boise in two weeks.

For now, I'm betting Boise State still gets the nod as the top G6 team, but there's a sudden air of mystery here, and if the Broncos lose again to SDSU, we don't know who's second in line. But hey, that gives us more to pay attention to during Rivalry Week!

North Dakota State at San José State (Friday, 3:30 p.m., CBSSN) -- SP+ projection: NDSU, 91%

Air Force at New Mexico (Friday, 4:30 p.m., The CW) -- SP+ projection: New Mexico, 74%

No. 22 Boise State at Colorado State (Saturday, 6 p.m., USA) -- SP+ projection: BSU, 93%

Liberty is 10-1 and an overwhelming favorite to beat both Missouri State and then Jacksonville State in the Conference USA title game. But all of the Flames' wins have come against teams ranked 95th or worse in SP+, and they didn't play a power-conference opponent. The committee usually punishes teams for that. Meanwhile, there's a huge batch of 9-2 teams with pretty similar résumés. My bet is that either New Mexico (because of an ultra-competitive loss to Oklahoma) or UTSA (because of American Conference residence) would be most well regarded by the committee in case of another Boise State loss, but it's hard to feel confident in that.

Title games are set in Conference USA (Liberty vs. Jacksonville State) and the Pac-12 (Boise State vs. San Diego State), and Louisiana Tech has locked up a spot in the Sun Belt title game, but plenty remains up for grabs in the Group of 6, notwithstanding that we don't know how the hunt for a CFP berth will play out.

Louisiana Tech at Georgia Southern (Saturday, noon, ESPNU) -- SP+ projection: Georgia Southern, 53%

Coastal Carolina at James Madison (Saturday, 2 p.m., ESPN+) - SP+ projection: JMU, 92%

We'll start with the easiest one to lay out. Georgia Southern is 6-1 in conference and needs only a win against Louisiana Tech to earn an immediate Tech rematch, but the Eagles lost head-to-head to 5-2 JMU, which is heavily favored over Coastal. If Tech and JMU both win, they'll play each other.

Air Force at New Mexico (Friday, 4:30 p.m., The CW) -- SP+ projection: New Mexico, 74%

Nevada at UNLV (Saturday, 9 p.m., CBSSN) -- SP+ projection: UNLV, 57%

Last year, the Mountain West needed to go to the computers -- a mix that includes my SP+ ratings -- to break a four-way tie and determine its title game. Unless Nevada upsets UNLV, they'll be looking to the computers again. New Mexico is 6-1, Air Force and UNLV are 5-2, and both North Dakota State and Wyoming are in the clubhouse at 6-2. If New Mexico beats Air Force, the Lobos are in, and if Nevada pulls an upset, that will clinch a title game berth for NDSU. If UNLV beats Nevada instead, the computers will decide among NDSU, UNLV and Wyoming. And if both UNLV and Air Force prevail, computers will decide both title game spots. Messy!

Miami (Ohio) at Western Michigan (Tuesday, 7 p.m., ESPN2) -- SP+ projection: WMU, 65%

Kent State at Eastern Michigan (Tuesday, 7 p.m., ESPN+) -- SP+ projection: EMU, 69%

Buffalo at Akron (Friday, noon, CBSSN) -- SP+ projection: Buffalo, 83%

Toledo at Ohio (Friday, noon, ESPNU) -- SP+ projection: Toledo, 61%

We are emerging from November's midweek MACtion slate with an absolute mess on our hands. Five teams -- 6-1 Buffalo, Ohio, Toledo and Western Michigan and 5-2 Miami (Ohio) -- are all still eligible for the title game. Luckily we get two head-to-head matchups among those teams this week, and it appears that other tiebreakers such as "conference opponent win percentage" will break any tie before computers have to get involved. If the favorites all win, we'll get either WMU vs. Toledo or WMU vs. Buffalo, depending on whether Kent State or EMU wins Tuesday night. (If it's EMU, Toledo makes it due to opponent win percentage; if it's Kent State, Buffalo makes it.)

I know bowls aren't what they used to be, I know five-win teams can sometimes earn bowl bids because of empty slots, and I know there are plenty of fans of frustrating 5-6 power-conference teams that would almost prefer their team fall short of a bowl so they can move on with their lives (looking at you, Florida State fans).

The pursuit of a 6-6 record is therefore huge for some teams and a nuisance for others. But for most, making a bowl is still better than not making one, and lots of teams enter Rivalry Week with 5-6 records (or, in one case, 4-6). And in one rivalry game, two 5-6 teams will square off.

NC State (5-6) at North Carolina (5-6) (Saturday, noon, ACCN) -- SP+ win probability: UNC, 62%

Bill Belichick's UNC team began the season 4-2 with encouraging wins over TCU, Pitt and Duke, but the Tar Heels have lost four of five following Saturday's humiliating 41-24 loss to 5-5 Virginia. Freshman quarterback Travis Burgess played reasonably well in his first career start, but the once-solid UNC defense gave up 464 yards (7.9 per play) to the Cavaliers, and now a rivalry loss -- against an NC State team that just thumped Florida State as a 10.5-point underdog to move to 5-6 itself -- would mean two seasons and no bowls for Belichick.

The rest of the 5-6 list includes some teams that are very unlikely to win -- three play top-10 opponents, and two more play ranked teams -- but a retooling team like Iowa State or mid-majors such as UMass, Georgia State, Nevada and Kennesaw State (which has won four in a row after a 1-6 start) could definitely denote a bowl bid as a sure sign of progress.

Poor UMass has lost six of seven since a 4-0 start and would be at the back of the line for a bowl bid because of its two wins over FCS teams, but after a heartbreaking 34-33 MACtion loss to Akron, the Minutemen would still be incredibly well served to end the regular season on a happy note.

My 20 favorite games of the weekend

1. Washington 27, No. 8 Indiana 24. A three-game losing streak completely wrecked Washington's ambitions, but the Huskies haven't ever stopped fighting. They've now won four of five.

2. Nebraska 31, No. 14 Ohio State 28. Like Washington, Nebraska never stopped scrapping. The Huskers lost four games in a row, and all were tight until the very end, but given another chance to both win a close game and finally take down a ranked opponent (after 31 straight losses against them), they got it done. This may be the worst Ohio State team in 15 years, but catharsis is catharsis.

5. NAIA: No. 19 Ottawa (Kansas) 31, No. 14 Baker 28 (OT). The early rounds of the small-school playoffs typically aren't overloaded with close games, but NAIA gave us a classic. Down 18 in the second quarter and down 10 late, Ottawa scored touchdowns of 63, 63 and 37 yards to charge back, tied the game in the final minute, picked off a pass to start overtime and advanced to the round of 16 with a 20-yard Ike Prengel field goal.

6. FCS: No. 15 Lehigh 51, Lafayette 48 (OT). Throw out the records in the most-played rivalry of them all. Lafayette (4-6) led by 10 early in the second half, but Lehigh (9-2) charged ahead and led 48-40 late in regulation. Lafayette's Eddie Jordan found Carson Persing for a touchdown and Ethan Hosak for a game-tying 2-point conversion, but Jack Simonetta missed a field goal to start OT, and Lehigh's Connor Poole knocked in a 30-yarder for the win.

7. Rice 31, Temple 27. After trailing by 10 with less than two minutes left, Rice was driving for the game-tying field goal, but Patrick Crayton Jr. and Jacurri Brown said, "Why don't we go for the win instead?"
