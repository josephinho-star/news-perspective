Turkey to host first meeting of Saudi-Pakistani defense pact committee

2026-08-30 23:48

Turkey will host senior officials from Saudi Arabia and Pakistan in İstanbul on Monday for the first meeting of a committee established under a new trilateral defense pact, the Turkish Foreign Ministry has announced.

The inaugural meeting of the Strategic Political and Defense Committee will bring together the three countries’ foreign and defense ministers and top military commanders.

Turkey will be represented by Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Defense Minister Yaşar Güler and Chief of General Staff Gen. Selçuk Bayraktaroğlu, according to the ministry.

Pakistan’s delegation will include Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar, Defense Minister Khawaja Asif and Chief of Defense Forces and army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir.

Saudi Arabia will be represented by its foreign and defense ministers and chief of general staff, Turkish diplomatic sources said.

The committee was created under the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, signed by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in the Saudi city on August 7.

The agreement includes a collective defense provision under which an attack on one signatory is considered an attack on all three. It brings together Turkey, which has NATO’s second-largest military, Pakistan, the only Muslim-majority country with nuclear weapons, and Saudi Arabia, a major Gulf economic and military power.

Monday’s meeting is expected to provide strategic direction for cooperation under the pact and address international security, the development of defense capabilities and greater interoperability among the three countries’ armed forces.

Officials are also expected to discuss defense industry cooperation, including joint production and research and development, as well as closer coordination in counterterrorism.

The agreement was signed amid heightened regional tensions stemming from the war involving the United States and Iran and attacks by Iran-backed Houthi rebels on shipping and other targets in the region.

Turkish and Pakistani officials have said the pact is defensive and is not directed against Iran or any other country. Islamabad has also said the agreement is open to other states willing to accept its principles and resolve disputes peacefully.

The three countries already have extensive bilateral defense ties. Turkey and Pakistan have long cooperated on military training and defense production, while Pakistan and Saudi Arabia maintain close security relations that include training and advisory support.
