Row breaks out between Tories and Reform at Piddington press conference

A news conference in Piddington has descended into a shouting match between Reform’s Zia Yusuf and the Conservative’s Chris Philp.

The two men, who are both home affairs spokespeople for their respective parties, appeared alongside Piddington parish council leader Tim McNally at a media event designed to outline local opposition to government plans to for a local accommodation site for asylum seekers.

But the united front did not last long and soon ended up with the pair bickering in front of the cameras. Yusuf asked Philp:

Would you like to actually apologise for the things that you did in government to bring hundreds of thousands of people into this country illegally?

Are you going to take the opportunity to apologise to the people of Piddington and to the country?

Philp responded by saying that the residents of the military base would be asylum seekers who had arrived recently.

He said that “rather than engaging in point scoring”, Yusuf should “read the room”. However, Yusuf said the Tories failed to “secure our borders” during their 14 years of government.

Before that, McNally had accused prime minister Andy Burnham of “failing to step up”. He said:

[Burnham hasn’t] extended to us as simple as courtesy of consideration of what we are going to have to consider, to receive here as our new incumbent guests that you have targeted for us - 1,250 men.

'Public safety our top priority,' says Downing Street on Piddington asylum base plans

Meanwhile, Downing Street has defended plans to convert the site at MoD Bicester near Piddington into asylum seeker accommodation.

Responding to questions around public safety, a spokesperson said such sites are “designed to be self-contained with 24/7 security and CCTV to minimise the impact on the local community”, the Press Association reported.

The spokesperson added: “As the prime minister himself has said, we do not want a situation where it’s only the poorest communities in the country that receive all of the dispersal of refugees and asylum seekers who believe all parties to play their part.”

The spokesperson said the safety of the public “will always be our top priority”.

The Home Office underspent its Prevent counter-terror scheme budget by nearly a third last year, despite the number of referrals reaching record levels, the public spending watchdog has said.

The National Audit Office (NAO) said the Home Office underspent its £23.4 million Prevent budget by 28% for 2025-26, continuing a six-year pattern in which it underspent by an average of £5.6 million (17%) a year in real term, PA reported.

The government-led, multi-agency programme aims to stop individuals becoming terrorists. It has faced significant criticism after it failed to intervene in the case of Southport attacker Axel Rudakubana, who had been referred three times.

Demand for Prevent has grown significantly with referrals increasing year on year since 2020, and reaching a record 10,293 in the year ending September 2025.

Officials believe the referral spike might be down to more awareness of Prevent after the Southport Attack in July 2024.

Planned asylum centre near Oxfordshire village to face legal challenge

Ministers will face a legal challenge over plans to convert a Ministry of Defence site near the Oxfordshire village of Piddington into accommodation for more than 1,200 detained asylum seekers.

It emerged on Thursday that Angela Rayner, the housing and communities secretary, has approved full planning permission, a decision welcomed by the Home Office, who want to end the use of asylum hotels.

Tim McNally, the local parish council chair, told BBC Radio 4’s Today Programme there would be “many actions” by villagers, and that ministers should “absolutely” be braced for a legal challenge, which would need to be lodged within the next six weeks.

Ministers have faced opposition over the plans from villagers, who even made a symbolic attempt to declare the village independent from the rest of the country after a referendum of its 350 residents. Asked how the village would respond, McNally said: “There’s going to be many actions, you will see. We will disclose that as we move forward.”

Asked if ministers should be braced for a legal challenge, he said: “Absolutely, absolutely.”

UK-EU deal will lead to 'growing pressure' for parliament to scrutinise legislation, committee warns

Hello and welcome to the UK politics live blog.

There will be a “growing pressure” for parliament to scrutinise EU legislation under new deals on food and emissions to be sealed ahead of the UK’s summit with the bloc next week, a Lords’ committee has warned.

Prime minister Andy Burnham is due to meet EU leaders in Brussels next week and sign agreements that he says could raise GDP by 0.4%. This includes a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal on food and farming, as well as one on emissions trading and another for a youth mobility scheme.

A deal is also expected to be struck on a health security agreement to help address shortages of drugs such as antibiotics and ADHD medication, which have got worse since Brexit, according to a draft text seen by the I newspaper.

It will mark a significant shift in the Britain’s post-Brexit relationship with the EU as they will require dynamic alignment, the process of matching laws to the bloc’s evolving legislation, the House of Lords European Affairs Committee said in a new report.

The committee of peers says this will mean parliament needs to set up a “robust, consistent and adequately resourced cross-departmental system” to manage the UK’s dynamic alignment commitments. See the published report here.

The committee chairman, Jock Stirrup, said:

Although important details remain to be determined of the legal and institutional form that dynamic alignment will take, its introduction will mark a major change to the nature of the UK-EU relationship as it exists under the Trade and Cooperation Agreement.

As the UK aligns with more EU law, parliament will face growing pressure to scrutinise significant volumes of EU legislation.

The food and farming deal alone could generate at least 780 pieces of in-scope EU law every year, the committee heard.

Meanwhile, the I reports that the government is close to securing a deal with Europe to help tackle post-Brexit medicine shortages, including antibiotics, ADHD medication and epilepsy drugs.

Experts on post-Brexit health policy have reportedly described the deal as “significant’” and a “good first step” in addressing shortages that have hit both sides in recent years.

The prime minister was meeting fellow leaders at the European Political Community summit in Dublin, ahead of the summit next week, where he hopes to bring Britain closer to Europe.
