US bans use of China's DeepSeek by govt agencies

2026-10-20 06:28

The U.S. government's Department of Homeland Security has banned the use of DeepSeek artificial intelligence tools in its agencies. (AFP Photo)

The U.S. government has ordered its agencies to stop using artificial intelligence tools from Chinese startup DeepSeek, as the countries race for dominance in the fast-moving sector.

A notice from the Department of Homeland Security announced the ban on products developed by DeepSeek, and tools created using DeepSeek by third parties, saying they must be removed from IT devices.

The department can issue direction to civilian agencies, but not the military or intelligence services.

"DeepSeek's models present multiple threat vectors," said a document attached to the notice.

Use of DeepSeek AI tools "may result in the transfer of sensitive data to [China] if used by U.S. government employees or vendors," the department warned.

Its servers are located in China, where the legal system permits the government "extensive authority" to access private company infrastructure, it said.

DeepSeek, one of China's top AI labs, shook up the industry in January 2025 with a low-cost, high-performance chatbot, challenging assumptions of U.S. dominance.

The firm released a long-awaited new model, V4, in April, and this month updated a faster, more lightweight version of V4.

The United States has accused DeepSeek and other top Chinese AI firms of systematically stealing the capabilities of cutting-edge models developed by American rivals.

Despite their fierce competition in the field, China and the United States in September agreed to set up a "communication channel" for AI incidents.

The topic is expected to arise again when the countries' leaders meet next month at a summit in the Chinese city of Shenzhen.
