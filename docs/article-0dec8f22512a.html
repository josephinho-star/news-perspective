Meta whistleblower says gag order reminiscent of ‘1984’

2026-11-27 23:08

Sarah Wynn-Williams, a former Facebook employee whose book warned about the company’s power, said a gag order preventing her from speaking about the tech giant was akin to the reality in George Orwell’s “1984” novel.

“Control what people are allowed to remember or what they are allowed to know, and you control what they are able to imagine,” she wrote in a Guardian opinion piece published Friday. “Control what they can imagine, and you have already chosen their future for them.”

Facebook’s parent company Meta and other corporations, she wrote, have “mastered corporate lawfare” and can let “slip the dogs of law on individuals who don’t have millions of pounds to defend themselves against” what she called “attacks” by those companies.

Wynn-Williams worked at Facebook from 2011-17, rising to the social media company’s global public policy director. The company fired her in 2017, a move Wynn-Williams has argued was retaliation for reporting her boss, Joel Kaplan, for sexual harassment.

Wynn-Williams released a book about experience at Facebook in 2025. But Meta, led by CEO Mark Zuckerberg, secured an emergency ruling by the American Arbitration Association in March of that year, which barred Wynn-Williams from publicly promoting her memoir, titled, “Careless People: A Cautionary Tale of Power, Greed and Lost Idealism.”

During testimony before Senate lawmakers in April 2025, Wynn-Williams said Meta executives “lied about what they were doing with the Chinese Communist Party to employees, shareholders, Congress and the American public.” Meta has pushed back on those claims.

Wynn-Williams wrote in the Guardian that it is “hard to explain” living under a gag order, again comparing it to an Orwellian situation.

“Orwell gave us the thoughtcrime: you don’t have to act against the Party to be guilty,” she wrote. “You just have to think the wrong thing. That’s what it feels like. To worry you might think the wrong thing and it could slip out.”

The former Facebook employee sued Meta in June, with her lawyers arguing the company is “punishing” her for “disclosing its illegal and indefensible workplace conditions” and corporate misconduct.

“Meta has engaged in surveillance to monitor Ms. Wynn-Williams’s speech and associations,” lawyers for Wynn-Williams wrote in a 57-page filing in U.S. District Court in Northern California.

“It has used the results of that surveillance to seek sanctions against Ms. Wynn-Williams for a wide range of public appearances and, in recent months, for nearly every public appearance she has made, even though she has never spoken about her book or about Meta at any appearance,” her attorneys added.

In her Guardian piece, Wynn-Williams wrote “it has felt as if certain sectors of society, including technology companies, act with impunity.”

She added, “I worry about all the powerful people who continue to benefit from silence and deprive us, the public, of information that can lead to warnings and ideally accountability. I worry we will only be allowed the truth about these companies when it no longer matters.”
