Strict rules KJ Biermann must follow after jail release for sodomy and sexual battery charges

2026-09-16 18:01

Fulton County Superior Court Judge Melynee Leftridge amended KJ’s bond order to require him to surrender his passport to his pretrial officer before he can be released from custody, according to a Sept. 15 filing exclusively obtained by Page Six.

As Page Six previously reported, KJ was ordered to abstain from using his cellphone or any form of social media. He must also stay at his mom’s home and attend school virtually.

The teen is also banned from having direct or indirect contact with the alleged victim, her family or associates, and he is barred from being in contact with anyone under the age of 16.

KJ is subject to electronic monitoring upon his release.

The update came after sexual assault case expert and former prosecutor Lindsay Richards, who is not involved in the case, exclusively told Page Six earlier this month that KJ would face strict stipulations if he were to be released from jail.

“I think there’s a likelihood he’ll be released and he will have a lot of conditions of his release,” she told us at the time. “I think he’ll be on a GPS. He’ll probably be on house arrest, having to do school at home.”

KJ had allegedly been under court supervision since October 2025 following the alleged incident with the family member, which prosecutors said was reported in April 2023.

Earlier this month, KJ was formally indicted on charges of aggravated child molestation, aggravated sodomy, aggravated sexual battery, false imprisonment and three counts of aggravated sexual battery against a child under 16 stemming from an alleged incident in Alpharetta, Georgia, earlier this year.
