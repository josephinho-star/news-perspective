Bruce Springsteen fan club — the ‘Spring-Nuts’ — shut down after infighting over politics, El-Sayed endorsement

2026-11-15 23:31

A Bruce Springsteen fan club was shut down by its administrators after reaching a “breaking point” with constant fighting over the singer’s political stances and his endorsement of Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed.

The “Spring-Nuts,” a Facebook group with nearly 20,000 members, survived for more than a decade by abiding by a “NO DRAMA” and “No infighting” rule that expected members to “respect everyone’s choices and opinions,” Asbury Park Press reported.

But The Boss’s anti-Trump politics have spilled into the Spring-Nuts, causing the otherwise apolitical fan club focused on raising funds for charities, including WhyHunger, to descend into chaos.

“As the world grew increasingly divisive, hostile, those tensions inevitably spilled into our little corner of the E Street world,” founders Julie and Howie Chaz said in a statement posted last Sunday, according to the outlet.

“What should have been respectful differences of opinion too often deteriorated into personal attacks, bullying, insults, and some genuinely vile and hateful rhetoric.”

Howie Chaz told the outlet that Springsteen’s “outspoken” politics ultimately forced the Spring-Nuts into a “difficult balancing act” that led the admin to shut it down.

Springsteen virtually greeted the Spring-Nuts at a 2022 event at the Stone Pony in Asbury Park, the outlet reported.

“Spring-Nuts! Spring-Nuts! I thank you for gathering here at the Pony for this momentous occasion in support of WhyHunger,” Springsteen said. “Everybody have a great night!”

The 77-year-old rock legend has ramped up his discussion of political beliefs on tour and TV appearances. Springsteen’s 20-date “Land of Hope and Dreams” tour kicked off this spring with the slogan “No Kings,” taking a strong stance against Trump and ICE.

“As Bruce became more outspoken about his politics, something we understood and respected as his absolute right, we found ourselves facing a difficult balancing act,” Howie Chaz said.

“How could we be a Bruce Springsteen fan community trying to protect that harmony and camaraderie and ignore messages and statements coming directly from the very artist who brought us all together?” the co-founder added.

“The breaking point,” according to Chaz, “was Springsteen’s endorsement of El-Sayed in the Michigan Senate race.”

The Boss endorsed Democrat El-Sayed earlier this month ahead of the highly contentious Michigan race, calling him a “fighter for our American Constitution.”

“Dr. El-Sayed is a fighter for our American Constitution, he’s a fighter for our American Democracy, and he’s a fighter against the corrupt, lawless, and anti-American leadership currently in the White House. That’s why I’m in his corner 100%,” Springsteen wrote.

Springsteen’s “endorsement was deeply disappointing to us, given our concerns as proud Jews,” Julie and Howie said.

“We certainly don’t believe Bruce intended for his message to make fans feel unwelcome. But very unfortunately, some of his fans appointed themselves the gatekeepers of who was and wasn’t worthy of belonging in the E Street community.”
