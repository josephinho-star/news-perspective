2 men jailed for up to 38 months over pro-independence group’s secession plot

2026-09-10 07:25

Judge says pair, acting as advisers, devised ‘full plan’ to advance goals of Taiwan-based group calling for Hong Kong’s independence

Hong Kong’s District Court has sentenced two men with autism to up to three years and two months in prison for their involvement in a pro-independence group seeking to separate the city from Chinese sovereignty.

District Judge Ernest Lin Kam-hung on Thursday found that courier Chan Tai-sum, 27, and waiter Ng Chi-tung, 25, had been deeply involved in the Taiwan-based Hong Kong Democratic Independence Union between November 2024 and July 2025.

He said the pair had jointly devised a “full plan” to advance the group’s objectives, although it had not been carried out.

Founded by a fugitive, the union advocates Hong Kong’s “liberation” from mainland Chinese rule, seeks international recognition for a self-proclaimed “Hong Kong Parliament” and calls for the establishment of an army to “restore and defend” the city.

Both defendants had earlier pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit secession.

Judge Lin said the pair had served as advisers within the organisation. Chan was responsible for designing promotional materials, establishing ties with foreign organisations and compiling personal data on police officers and their families.
