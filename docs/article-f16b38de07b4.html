NATO member Estonia to close Russia border after attempted 'sabotage' of drone maker

2026-11-16 09:30

NATO member Estonia says it is preparing to close road border crossings with Russia from Dec. 1, as it blames Moscow for an attempted "sabotage" of one of its defense companies.

Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said on Sunday that Russia's military intelligence service GRU had ordered an arson attack against Estonian drone maker Threod Systems in the early hours of Saturday morning.

Authorities said the attempted attack did not cause significant damage and eight people were detained in connection with it.

Estonia, which shares a 294-kilometer (183-mile) border with Russia, said closing the crossings would help to reduce Moscow's ability to conduct hostile activities against it and mitigate risks related to sanctions circumvention.

Tallinn has frequently raised the alarm over sabotage attacks against NATO countries that have been supporting Ukraine since Russia launched the full-scale invasion of its neighbor in February 2022.

"This weekend's attempted sabotage against an Estonian defence company, attributed by our security authorities to Russia's GRU, is yet another reminder that Russia's hostile activities extend far beyond Ukraine," Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said Sunday via social media.

"Estonia is preparing to close its road border crossings with Russia in response. Hostile actions must have consequences."

Tsahkna said he had informed his Finnish, Lithuanian and Latvian counterparts of the decision.

Russia's foreign ministry said it would assess Estonia's plans to close its border crossings, adding that if these plans were to come into force, it would be seen as "yet another bout of aggressive and unbridled Russophobia," according to state news agency Tass.

It did not directly comment on the accusation that Moscow is responsible for the attack on Threod Systems.

Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said the attack was part of Russia's broader "hybrid war" against Europe.

"We will not be intimidated. Estonia is preparing for the possibility of closing its border with Russia to reduce the risks to our people and national security," Michal said Sunday via social media.

The latest incident follows an arson attack in mid-August against Milrem Robotics, an Estonian defense company that supplies Ukraine with unmanned military vehicles. Estonian authorities later blamed Russia for the attack. Moscow denied the allegation.

European security officials and lawmakers have previously warned about Russia's hybrid warfare. In September, Danish intelligence officials said Russia could launch a "limited" military attack against a NATO member in the coming months, saying the Kremlin appears more willing to take risks after more than 4½ years of war against Ukraine.

The Danish Defence Intelligence Service, or DDIS, identified a "low but growing risk" that Russia could carry out isolated strikes with long-range weapons on infrastructure that is critical to the support of Ukraine.
