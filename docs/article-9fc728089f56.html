Israeli strikes kill 16 schoolchildren so far this month, Gaza ministry says

2026-11-26 20:33

Israeli strikes have killed at least 16 schoolchildren in Gaza since the start of November, as young people across the territory face intensifying bombardment and a struggle to find food, clean water and shelter.

On Wednesday, Gaza’s ministry of education said: “Palestinian students had been targeted for the third time in less than 24 hours, in an escalation reflecting the continued Israeli violations against children in the Gaza Strip. The number of schoolchildren killed since the beginning of November has now risen to 16.”

The statement follows the death of two sisters, Yusra, 15, and Jana Mohammed Abu al-Ouf, 13, in the early hours of Wednesday in an airstrike targeting a residential apartment in western Gaza City.

Yusra was a pupil at Kafr Qasim school, while her sister Jana attended al-Manar private school.

The ministry added: “The girls’ father, Mohammed Majed Abu Al-Ouf, and their mother, Noura Ezzat Abu Al-Ouf, were also killed in the strike which injured several other Palestinians.” The father was a director of internal security services for Hamas in northern Gaza.

Earlier this year, an independent UN inquiry found that Israel continued to commit genocide by deliberately targeting Palestinian children in Gaza.

On 6 November, Mohammed Nidal Barbakh, 15, was shot by Israeli forces in the Bir 19 area of al-Mawasi, Khan Younis, and later died from his injuries.

He was in the 10th grade at the Muscat school and dreamed of becoming a professional football player.

“That day, after lunch, Mohammed went to the sports academy at 1.30pm to take part in training,” his father, Dr Nidal Mohammed Barbakh, said. “An Israeli tank had advanced and positioned itself on a nearby hill before firing two rounds towards the pitch. One of them ricocheted after hitting a metal object, striking Mohammed in the back and exiting through his neck.

“He was talented and an excellent student. He was killed at the age of 15, on a sports pitch in al-Mawasi, an area where people had been told to go because it was considered safe. I do not absolve the Israeli army of responsibility for what happened. It kills every ambition within us and buries our children’s dreams before they have a chance to come true.”

On 13 November, Israeli artillery shelling killed three people in Deir al-Balah, central Gaza, including a father and his 12-year-old son, Ahmed Abu Daher, as well as a woman.

Ahmed dreamed of becoming an Arabic language teacher, like his grandfather Saleh, said his mother, Walaa Abu Daher.

“That day, my husband was heading to a condolence gathering in the al-Qastal area, east of Deir al-Balah,” Daher said. “Ahmed asked his father if he could accompany him. While they were at the gathering, they were targeted by Israeli artillery fire.

“Ahmed ran to ask the people attending the gathering for help. He then rushed back towards his father when a second shell was fired. Shrapnel struck Ahmed in his legs, abdomen and head. I cannot describe how deeply their loss has affected me. I feel as though my life stopped that day.”

Thousands of school-age children and hundreds of education workers have been killed in Israel’s war in Gaza, while tens of thousands of children have been unable to attend school regularly.

On Thursday, Israeli attacks killed at least nine Palestinians across Gaza, including Khaled Abu Nada, the undersecretary of Gaza’s ministry of education, and his wife, according to health officials, as attacks continued despite the ceasefire in the territory.

A few days ago, the ministry of education said Israel’s attacks against students, civilian leaders and teachers were “part of a systematic assault on the Palestinian education system”.

According to Unicef, 98% of school buildings in Gaza have sustained damage, with more than 93% requiring major rehabilitation or full reconstruction, while approximately 81% were directly hit.

Young Palestinians in Gaza face a fourth winter in overcrowded tents and makeshift shelters, with limited access to basic services as the humanitarian and public health crisis deepens.

Children and young people are among those most vulnerable to meningitis, for which Gaza’s health ministry issued an epidemic alert on Tuesday after a sharp rise in infections, with more than 270 new cases reported in October.

Health organisations attribute the rise in infections to the destruction of infrastructure, overcrowded shelters and the contamination of drinking water with sewage – a crisis made worse by winter flooding and restrictions on humanitarian aid.

At least 74,746 Palestinians have been killed in Israel’s war in Gaza over the last three years, according to the territory’s health ministry. More than 20,000 children have been reported killed by Israeli strikes.

The Israel Defense Forces said there was not enough information to comment on the incidents involving Ahmed and Mohammed.

It said that on the day Ahmed died it was carrying out operations that resulted in the killing of “10 terrorists” and “prior to the strikes, measures were taken to mitigate harm to civilians”.
