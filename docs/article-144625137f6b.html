Duffy vows to modernize aging air traffic system after nationwide ground stop

2026-11-17 05:50

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy on Monday said he plans to modernize America’s aging air traffic control system and build new architecture that is "worthy of America" following a nationwide ground stop on Friday that stranded more than a million passengers.

During an appearance on Fox News' "Hannity," Duffy said he wants to build a brand-new modern architecture "of a resilient telecom network."

"Congress did give me $12.5 billion. That’s for radar and radios. I am well on my way to deploying all that money. You can’t buy this off the shelf, so I’ve ordered it. As it is delivered, we pay for it, and we deploy it into the NAS. But what’s really important is the next phase of the investment," he told host Sean Hannity on Monday.

The $12.5 billion in congressional funding is part of a broader effort to modernize the nation's air traffic control system, including communications, surveillance and other infrastructure.

"So you saw a telecom issue on Friday. We’re replacing old copper wires with fiber, but this is an old architecture of a network. You’ve seen Newark go down. You’ve seen Dallas go down. You saw all of New York go down just a couple of months ago because of telecom issues. I want to build a brand-new architecture of a resilient telecom network that’s worthy of 2026 and worthy of America," he continued.

Duffy added that the department needs new software.

"The software we use to manage our airplanes in the airspace was built in the 1990s. It works. It works well. But if we’re going to drive more safety and more efficiency, we need brand-new software," he explained.

The secretary also said intelligence agencies found no evidence that a cyberattack forced the ground stop on Friday and that the Federal Aviation Administration was investigating whether a network problem, router issue or installation error caused the system failure.

The FAA is working with contractor L3Harris, which installed the router in question and is assisting with the investigation at its Florida laboratory.

"We had all of our intelligence agencies, at Donald Trump’s direction, talk on Friday night," Duffy said. "No one was seeing any kind of hack into our system. And we’ve now secured the router."

"We’re doing an analysis in Florida with L3Harris and the FAA. I actually asked the FBI to come in just to make sure, to put people at ease, that this was not a hack. So I think it was a network problem or a router problem or an installation error," he continued.

The roughly five-hour ground stop affected more than 1.5 million passengers whose flights were scheduled to depart on Friday afternoon and evening. More than 12,000 flights were canceled that day, according to the FAA, while delays and cancellations continued through the weekend.
