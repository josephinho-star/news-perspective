Netanyahu calls Erdoğan a ‘tyrant’ at UN, Ankara rejects accusations

2026-09-25 06:06

Turkey on Friday rejected Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s “baseless accusations” after he called President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan a “tyrant” and accused Ankara of harboring Hamas leaders.

Netanyahu’s address to the UN General Assembly on Thursday sparked an uproar in the chamber, with dozens of envoys walking out, some jeering and booing, while his delegation stood and applauded.

“The latest country to become a super spreader of antisemitic lies (is Turkey),” Netanyahu said.

“Erdogan is a tyrant. He also hosts the Hamas terror chiefs.”

Erdoğan, in his own address to the General Assembly on Tuesday, urged world leaders to “exert greater pressure on Israel” over the war in Gaza and called on countries to recognize Palestine as an independent state.

Burhanettin Duran, Turkey’s presidential communications director, said Netanyahu had used the UN General Assembly podium to make “baseless accusations” against Turkey and Erdoğan, adding that the remarks were incompatible with the UN’s “ideals of peace, law and dialogue.”

Netanyahu’s words could not “make his massacres, the humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza and the proceedings before international law invisible,” Duran added.

Israel’s military campaign in Gaza, launched after the Hamas-led October 7, 2023 attacks, has killed 73,910 Palestinians and injured 174,935, according to Gaza’s Health Ministry.

Even after a ceasefire was announced in October 2025, Israeli strikes and other attacks have continued to cause casualties, while the UN says civilians still face widespread destruction, repeated displacement and severe shortages of essential supplies.

Israeli actions in Gaza have also been the subject of proceedings before international courts, including South Africa’s genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice.
