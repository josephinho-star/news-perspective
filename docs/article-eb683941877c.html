Poland’s justice minister says investigation into Kraków shooting will aim to establish if the 24-year-old acted alone – Europe live

2026-11-09 10:10

Investigation into Kraków shooting will aim to establish if suspect acted alone, minister says

We are restarting our live coverage of European news after the mass shooting in Kraków, Poland’s second-largest city, over the weekend in which six people were killed.

In an update this morning, the authorities said the 24-year-old Polish attacker, who had been injured in a shootout with police, has died in hospital.

Polish prosecutors had opened a terrorism investigation after the man, from the town of Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski in southeastern Poland, opened fire on Kraków’s popular Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon, killing six people, including a Briton, and injuring 17 others.

On the first of two days of national mourning declared after the rare mass shooting, police confirmed yesterday that three people found dead at the gunman’s home after the attack were his father, 53, mother, 52, and sister, 21.

Five of the injured are foreign nationals from Britain, Austria, Germany, Portugal and Russia. Two police officers were also among the injured, one of whom was in a serious condition.

The interior minister, Marcin Kierwiński, said the suspect was fascinated with far-right ideologies and held “very radical” and “ultra-nationalistic” views.

But officials appeared to backtrack from these comments as nationalist opposition politicians questioned the grounds on which they were based.

“Until the matter is fully clarified and the authorities are in possession of complete information, it is best to refrain from making comments, even if there are some initial indications,” government spokesperson Adam Szlapka told TVN24.

Meanwhile, the justice minister, Waldemar Zurek, said despite the attacker’s death, the investigation will continue, aiming to establish if he acted alone. Main Market Square reopened on Sunday evening after a security cordon was lifted.

At least 20 people have died over the weekend trying to reach Crete, authorities say

At least 20 people have died over the weekend trying to reach Greece’s island of Crete, authorities confirmed earlier today.

Eight bodies were found on one boat and 12 on another, while an estimated 40 people were rescued off both, according to the Greek coast guard.

Greece receives a large number of undocumented migrants through the Mediterranean, often from nearby Turkey and countries in northern Africa.

Last month, Frontex, the EU’s border agency, said that the eastern Mediterranean was the busiest route for migrants attempting to enter the EU, although irregular border crossings into the bloc were down by a third so far this year.

Greece has been accused by human rights groups of forcibly returning migrants and asylum seekers on its sea and land borders, a practice it denies.

Many of the people making these dangerous journeys are trying to escape rapid climate change, famine, oppressive governments and war, and are often not eligible to apply for asylum from their home countries.

Kremlin denies that Europeans will participate in upcoming Abu Dhabi talks

The US secretary of state, Marco Rubio, has said some progress had been made on what the “security infrastructure” of Ukraine and Europe could look like after the war with Russia.

Rubio’s comments on Sunday come as Russia-Ukraine negotiations under US mediation have been set for Tuesday and Wednesday in Abu Dhabi, with the talks expected to focus on postwar security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts and steps to end the war.

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, who is also Trump’s son-in-law, will represent the US during the talks. The Kremlin insisted this morning that no European officials will be there, rebuffing France’s foreign minister, Jean-Noel Barrot, who said French representatives would be present.

In an interview with CBS’s “60 Minutes” broadcast on Sunday, the US president, Donald Trump, said he believed the war would end soon and suggested Ukraine could emerge from a settlement with altered borders. “I think we’re gonna have it done, and I think it’s gonna happen and they’re gonna end up with a little bit different shape Ukraine,” Trump said.

Despite Trump’s apparent optimism, no major breakthrough is expected at the talks as Vladimir Putin has consistently been sticking to his maximalist positions and both sides remain far apart, particularly on territory. But the US hopes the Russian president may be more willing to compromise after Trump struck a deal in October with the Kremlin to resume diesel exports to international markets.

A Ukrainian drone attack has hit Russia’s second-largest oil refinery in Kirishi, in the Leningrad region, the regional governor, Aleksandr Drozdenko, said.

A fire broke out at the refinery after the overnight Ukrainian drone strike, but has since been extinguished, he wrote on Telegram, adding that one woman had been killed by falling drone debris.

Ukraine has been targeting Russia’s oil industry, from pipelines and ports to refineries and tankers, in an effort to undermine Russia’s war economy.

The Kirishi refinery, about 800km (500 miles) from the Ukrainian border, has suffered several Ukrainian drone attacks this year.

The facility, one of the key suppliers of diesel to the domestic market and for export, is reported to produce more than 6% of Russia’s total refined oil.
