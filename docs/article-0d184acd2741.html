Cowboys coach says he considered walking away from team after crushing demotion

2026-11-20 00:59

Christian Parker was about to put on his cowboy hat and ride off into the not-so-sunny sunset and head out of Dallas.

The Dallas Cowboys stripped play-calling duties from the beleaguered defensive coordinator on Wednesday after Parker's defense got gashed for 584 yards in the team's 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers on Sunday. Mike Pettine will now call the defensive plays.

Parker said there was consideration of him stepping away from his role and the team following his demotion, but he decided to stay.

"I think, you know, initially it's going to sting, right? Initially in your mind, you're thinking about all the things and in my opinion, that would have been running from it. At the end of the day, I got a job to do and my job is to be the defensive coordinator of the Dallas Cowboys," Parker told reporters on Thursday.

When asked about his future with the club, he responded, "We’ll cross that bridge when we get to it." The 34-year-old is the youngest defensive coordinator in Cowboys history and acknowledged the harsh reality of being demoted.

"Not an ideal situation for me personally," Parker said. "But it’s what’s best for the team at this current point."

Parker said he has told players a lot about having a reduced role, and it would be hypocritical of him not to put the team first when the same was asked of him.

"I’ve had conversations with players a million times where you ask them to take a reduced role, they might lose their starting job or whatever," Parker said. "And the communication and the expectation for them is always to continue business as usual and then put the team first. So it would be very hypocritical of me to explain that to different players over the course of my career and not take that advice myself."

Parker's unit has struggled mightily this season. They have allowed 28.2 points per game (30th in the NFL) and 393.8 yards per game (last in the NFL), yielding 239.7 passing yards per game (26th in the NFL) and 154.1 yards per game (last in the NFL). They also don't force turnovers, as their 0.8 takeaways a game are 30th in the NFL.

The rest of the NFC East has been ravaged by injuries and underperformance, yet the Cowboys, despite remaining largely healthy, have been unable to take advantage. The New York Giants lead the division at 5-3-1 despite having started three different quarterbacks after Jaxson Dart's injury.

The Philadelphia Eagles have lost seven games in a row, and Jalen Hurts is out for the season because of blood clots. The Washington Commanders lost Jayden Daniels for some time, among other key contributors for the season.

Yet the Cowboys are 4-6 because of their woeful defense. Parker will hope to see the unit improve with Pettine play-calling.
