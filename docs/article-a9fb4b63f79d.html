Confirmed case of bird flu in endangered Australian sea lion

South Australia has confirmed its first H5 bird flu case in an endangered Australian sea lion.

The sea lion was found dead by a state government ranger at Seal Bay at Kangaroo Island on Friday.

The animal had sustained a shark wound three months prior and was already being monitored. The government said it was confident the shark wound was not the cause of death and tests had returned a strong positive result for H5 bird flu.

The government said there had been no other known endangered sea lion deaths at seal bay but it was monitoring the population closely.

Seal Bay has an estimated 800 resident Australian sea lions, the third largest single colony for the species and about 5% of the total population. About three quarters of Australia’s sea lion population lives in South Australia.

Scientists had warned of the potential “carnage” in the Australian sea lion population if bird flu takes hold, after the virus had already impacted seal populations in South America and sub-Antarctica.

John Woinarski, a leading mammal scientist at Charles Darwin University, said at the time he feared the virus could quickly start affecting more seals and sea lions.

“The carnage may be substantial and represent a serious conservation impact,” he said.

“I fear for Australian sea lions in particular because of their small population size.”

Comments by senior Reserve Bank of Australia officials will be closely monitored as anticipation builds towards another interest rate hike later this month, AAP has reported.

The central bank’s chief economist, Sarah Hunter, will participate in a fireside chat at a Regional Australia Institute summit in Canberra on Monday.

Then, RBA governor Michele Bullock will appear before a parliamentary committee on Friday.

Both are expected to echo recent comments from bank officials reiterating that inflation is too high and leaving the door open to further interest rate hikes, following the three the bank has handed down so far in 2026.

Dr Hunter and RBA deputy governor Andrew Hauser sent bonds markets into a spin in separate hawkish speaking appearances last week.

Markets are now pricing in about a four in five chance the RBA will lift the cash rate by 25 basis points on September 29.

Hanson faces bipartisan attack over resurfaced comments on Albanese’s mother

Pauline Hanson has come under fire from Liberal and Labor MPs for “appalling” comments attacking Anthony Albanese’s mother that have resurfaced from an old podcast interview.

The One Nation leader criticised Albanese’s mother, Maryanne Ellery, for staying in public housing for several decades, in a podcast with Karl Stefanovic from February this year.

Andrew Hastie, who has had his own fight with Hanson, this morning criticised Hanson and told News24 she should “leave mums out of it”.

I don’t think you should be talking about the late mother of anyone in this game, and certainly not reflecting on circumstances over which she had no understanding … I remember in the schoolyard, if anyone said anything about your mum, mate, it was on. So let’s leave mums out of it.”

The trade minister, Don Farrell, also berated Hanson for the remarks this morning, telling News24:

Anthony loves his mother, as I’m sure you do, I certainly loved my mother … To have them attacked publicly this way is just appalling. There’s a certain bitterness in One Nation’s approach to the world that I find quite un-Australian, actually. I think Ms Hanson should apologise.”

People in western NSW developing MND at four times global average

People across a vast swathe of Australia are developing a deadly disease at more than four times the global average, AAP has reported.

Researchers found a motor neurone disease (MND) incidence rate of 3.72 cases per 100,000 people annually across western NSW.

In some areas, the prevalence of MND was up to seven times higher than expected, with several higher-incidence regions overlapping with areas of more intensive agricultural activity.

The global burden of MND is increasing, and in 2021 the age-standardised prevalence and incidence were estimated at 0.77 cases per 100,000 people.

MND was also observed to be occurring at higher rates in women than in other areas, and the incidence of bulbar-onset MND was unusually high.
