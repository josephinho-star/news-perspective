Mexico’s cartel ‘godfather’ convicted in American DEA agent’s murder dies at age 80

2026-11-16 09:24

Stepheny Price , Fox News

Published Nov. 16, 2026, 4:24 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

The Mexican cartel boss who helped build a drug empire supplying the United States and was convicted in the murder of Drug Enforcement Administration agent Enrique “Kiki” Camarena has died at 80.

Miguel Angel Felix Gallardo, known as the “boss of bosses,” died Sunday in a Guadalajara hospital after suffering multiple organ failure, The Associated Press reported, citing Jalisco’s security ministry. He had been transferred from prison Friday.

Felix Gallardo had been behind bars since 1989, according to The AP.

In 2017, a Mexican federal judge gave Felix Gallardo a 37-year prison term for the murders of Camarena and Mexican pilot Alfredo Zavala, Excélsior reported. The judge also ordered more than 20 million pesos in compensation for the victims’ families, roughly $1.18 million at the time.

Camarena was heading to meet his wife for lunch on Feb. 7, 1985, when five men abducted him as he left the U.S. Consulate in Guadalajara, according to the DEA. One of the attackers identified himself as a Mexican law enforcement official.

The 37-year-old agent was never seen alive again.

The DEA says Camarena was believed to have been tortured for two days before dying from a crushed skull. His body was discovered on March 5, 1985.

His killing triggered Operation Leyenda, which the agency describes as the largest homicide investigation it had ever undertaken. Felix Gallardo was among the major Mexican crime figures arrested in connection with Camarena’s torture and murder, according to the DEA.

The agent, a Marine Corps veteran who joined the DEA in 1974, left behind his wife and three children.

Felix Gallardo’s reach helped reshape organized crime in Mexico. A 2023 report released by Sen. Chuck Grassley’s office described the former police officer as having brought traffickers together into a large organization that became a blueprint for many of Mexico’s major criminal groups.

The Guadalajara cartel was among the first Mexican trafficking organizations to work with Colombian cocaine suppliers, according to a historical DEA publication. The agency said Felix Gallardo and Rafael Caro Quintero formed the cartel to ship heroin and marijuana into the United States before it prospered from the cocaine trade.

Camarena’s legacy, meanwhile, lives on through the red ribbons worn each October in his memory as part of drug prevention efforts, according to the DEA.

Fox News Digital reached out to Jalisco’s security ministry and the DEA for confirmation and comment.
