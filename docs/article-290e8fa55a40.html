Erdoğan tells Zelenskyy Black Sea ship attacks are indefensible

2026-09-24 11:19

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan told Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in New York on Wednesday that attacks on commercial ships in the Black Sea could not be justified, following strikes on Turkish vessels that killed and injured crew members and raised fears for a vital grain route.

Erdoğan urged Russia and Ukraine to act responsibly and called for steps to protect navigation, according to the Turkish presidency’s account of the meeting at the Turkish House during the United Nations General Assembly. The statement did not identify who carried out particular attacks.

Turkish crews have been among the casualties. On August 3, drones reportedly struck the Turkish-owned vessels Yaşar and Nadezhda after they left Russia’s Novorossiysk port, injuring crew members including Turkish nationals, Turkey’s foreign ministry said. The ministry warned that continued escalation could also harm food security.

A June strike on the Turkish fishing boat Duru 67 off Crimea killed its captain. One survivor said coast guard personnel told the crew that a Ukrainian drone had hit the boat by mistake, but the coast guard’s public statement did not identify the attacker. A July drone strike on the coal carrier Reyhan Sarı killed a Turkish crew member. The shipowner and crew later accused Ukraine in a complaint.

Tamer Kıran, head of Turkey’s Chamber of Maritime Commerce, said in August that 22 Turkish-owned vessels had been attacked in the preceding two months.

Earlier Wednesday, Zelenskyy told a summit on Crimea that Ukraine was ready for a Black Sea maritime truce based on proposals from Turkey, Egypt and India if Russia took concrete steps to de-escalate, Reuters reported.

After meeting Erdoğan, Zelenskyy said Russia must stop striking Ukrainian infrastructure before Kyiv would halt its long-range retaliatory attacks. He said the leaders discussed restoring Ukraine’s maritime export corridor, which supplies food to countries around the world, according to his office.

Erdoğan said Turkey would keep trying to revive direct negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv. Turkey and the United Nations brokered a Black Sea grain deal in 2022, but Russia ended its participation in July 2023.
