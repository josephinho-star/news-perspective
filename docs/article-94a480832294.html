Anthropic's stock has been a clunker post-IPO. Why that matters for the AI trade

2026-11-25 16:05

Anthropic's rough start to its life as a public company isn't just a headache for the Claude creator's new shareholders. It's also problematic for OpenAI's future fundraising plans and creates a new overhang for the entire artificial intelligence trade. What we had hoped would be a clearing event for the AI trade is proving to be anything but. In just their fifth trading day, Anthropic shares are trading well below their initial public offering price of $83 — down 3% Wednesday to roughly $79 apiece. The stock lost 6.5% yesterday, closing at $81.90. Wednesday's decline puts Anthropic even further below its opening price of $111.50 during Thursday's debut session and its intraday peak of $115.10. Anthropic shares didn't even rally on Monday, when most stocks in the AI trade ripped higher on news of Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia as CEO. The negative reaction to Anthropic's debut muddies OpenAI's path to becoming a public company next year, forcing investors to reconsider their assumptions on the ChatGPT maker's ability to raise mountains of cash to pay its data center bills. In turn, this raises questions about the future earnings estimates for a host of AI-related companies, from chipmakers to cloud-computing giants and the industrial picks-and-shovels providers. That's the main cloud over the AI trade stemming from Anthropic's stock price struggles. But a secondary layer involves Anthropic's own capacity to fund future spending. Its public stock is a form of currency that it can use to fund its operations. So the worse it trades, the less valuable that currency becomes in potential future equity sales needed to fund operations. Of course, Anthropic already has its record-breaking IPO haul — $177.6 billion, including both the "greenshoe" allotment released by underwriters and its $26 billion private placement. Anthropic securing this windfall has all sorts of near-term benefits , both directly and indirectly, for companies levered to the AI infrastructure buildout. Still, investors must also remain mindful of Anthropic's ability to meet its massive financial commitments further down the road. To be sure, it remains very early in Anthropic's public-company journey and it is far from the only mega-IPO to struggle to find its footing. All but five of the 20 largest traditional U.S. IPOs since 1990 have dropped below their offer price within the first year of trading, according to HB Wealth . Considering that history, Anthropic's poor price action may just be a function of coming public, and not a harsh judgment on the AI trade more broadly. It may simply be there's a price-discovery process that cannot be completed in just four days. Nevertheless, investors are understandably trying to draw conclusions about the implications because a financially strong Anthropic is not enough on its own to keep the AI trade afloat. OpenAI is another crucial leg to the AI funding stool, and it looks wobblier by virtue of the suboptimal performance of Anthropic's stock. Jim Cramer touched on this relationship in his Sunday column, and Tuesday's sell-off in Anthropic makes it all the more relevant to consider. Whether due to poor pricing on the part of lead underwriter Goldman Sachs, or poor allocation choices made by Anthropic's leadership, the action in Anthropic "might doom all of OpenAI's attempts to raise money and lead to a down IPO for ChatGPT maker — a catastrophic outcome, at least in that world," Jim wrote. Consider this: At its IPO price of $83, Anthropic was valued at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, which includes existing shares and those that could be exercised in the future due to stock options, warrants, and convertible notes. OpenAI, meanwhile, was valued at $1.25 trillion during a recent funding round, CNBC reported on Nov. 12 , up from about $852 billion in the round prior. The date is key: that's before the Anthropic IPO was completed. OpenAI has signaled plans to go public next year, though the timing is uncertain. In August, CFO Sarah Friar told employees that the AI lab "will be a public company in 2027" or sooner, CNBC reported. At that time, an IPO in 2026 was still thought to be on the table. In September, CEO Sam Altma n ruled out a 2026 debut amid an intensifying debate around AI safety. That didn't stop its chief rival Anthropic from marching on. The problem is that if Anthropic continues to get a cold shoulder from public-market investors, it makes it harder for OpenAI to pursue an IPO at a valuation that would make all its existing private-market shareholders happy. In the eyes of Wall Street, Anthropic is often seen as the more secure bet, at least in terms of revenue durability, thanks to greater enterprise exposure. So if Wall Street isn't loving Anthropic, how would they treat publicly listed shares of OpenAI? This brings us to the secondary reason why Anthropic's stumbles out of the gate create an overhang on the broader AI trade: Anthropic's own funding needs further down the road. We feel better about this than before its IPO, but simply being public doesn't mean it's out of the woods either. While the company is sitting on well over $200 billion in cash now — having had $132 billion in cash and short-term investments at the end of September, even before the IPO — the company has more than $900 billion in future financial commitments, according to its IPO prospectus. Obviously, cash on hand at Anthropic is a key factor to being able to pay its future compute bills. But so, too, is the ability to raise additional capital in the future. Being free cash flow positive — as Anthropic was in the first nine months of 2026 — certainly helps. However, as we see with the hyperscale players, the buildout requires more than just internally generated cash. In this way, the post-IPO price action is problematic for Anthropic's ability to fund its future obligations. Remember, its stock is a currency. And right now, that currency is losing its value. Taken together, that may mean that the AI trade is rangebound until we get more clarity on the AI labs' ability to raise funds. It's difficult to predict when that catalyst will arrive, or what it may be. It could be a slow grind as investors get more comfortable with Anthropic's finances and its stock trades better. Maybe it's as simple as one good quarter that sees Anthropic stock surge on the back of a revenue and/or profitability acceleration, in turn casting a positive halo on OpenAI. We also know sentiment can turn around quickly, going from positive one day to negative the next, and vice versa. Bigger picture, the silver lining is that demand for AI is still outstripping supply. While funding abilities for these two key players remain less certain than we would like, sustained demand means we can't turn too negative on the trade. MySpace fizzled, but that didn't mean social media demand went away. Most of it just went to Facebook. The demand for AI compute is about far more than two leading model providers being able to fund their own portion of the buildout. (Jim Cramer's Charitable Trust is long AMZN.
