Judge sides against Democrats on taxpayer-funded pro-Trump ads

2026-11-26 03:09

A federal judge sided against the Democratic National Committee (DNC) in their case regarding the administration’s taxpayer-funded pro-President Trump advertisements that played earlier this year, according to new court filings.

The DNC previously argued that the ads would affect the outcome of November’s midterm elections. A federal judge issued a temporary restraining order that required the administration to stop airing the commercials and cancel any remaining ad placements until Nov. 8.

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan, a Biden-era appointee, ruled Wednesday night that the DNC failed to demonstrate “irreparable harm” that the ads would pose after the election concluded, even with Louisiana’s runoff House elections in December and the 2028 presidential election.

“At this juncture and on this record, the court is unpersuaded,” she wrote in her 25-page ruling.

The nearly $20 million ad campaign has been the subject of litigation. It included at least five commercials promoting Trump and his policies, with one 30-second spot featuring the president’s voice denouncing communism, socialism and Marxism set to the song “Love Me” by artist JMSN, who says the White House did not obtain his permission to use the music.

Each ad included a disclaimer tag stating it was “paid for by the U.S. government.”

AliKhan previously blocked the administration from airing the ad until Wednesday, while U.S. District Judge Jennifer Rochon in New York temporarily blocked the ads from airing in a case brought by Common Cause, unions and an Alabama state House candidate.

It drew almost immediate scrutiny, with the DNC filing a lawsuit to block the ad campaign.

A spokesperson for the DNC said in a statement to The Hill that the committee was “disappointed” by the judge’s decision. The spokesperson continued that the DNC would “fight on to put an end to the Trump administration’s attempts to undermine our democracy.”

The White House did not respond immediately to The Hill’s request for comment.

Trump previously defended the ads as a “rather standard thing to do” but pledged to pay for them himself or by using outside groups.

“The Radical Left is upset with the fact that I am taking Ads, which I consider to be a positive promotion for our Great U.S.A., and paying for them with U.S.A. money,” the president wrote on Truth Social last month. “Although nothing will make them happy, I have decided to do the Patriotic Ads, among others, and pay for them myself, and with money I raised for MAGA, Inc.”
