Australia news live: Gallagher says final budget outcome will shave $6bn off deficit; RBA interest rate hike looms

2026-09-27 23:51

Mike Baird to step down as chair of Cricket Australia

Former NSW premier Mike Baird will step down as the chair of Cricket Australia at the body’s next annual general meeting in October.

Baird has been chair since early 2023 and led Cricket Australia’s push to privatize the Big Bash Leagues clubs. Both NSW and Queensland have opposed the plan.

In a statement, Baird said:

I believe Australian cricket continues to be well led by the CA Board. Their mix of significant experience and passion for the game gives me great confidence for the future. …

I have been incredibly proud to play a role in the oversight of cricket on the CNSW Board and, over the past six years, the CA Board, including almost four as chair.

David Boon, a former Test batter, will take over as interim chair on 28 October as a process to find a new full-time chair begins.

Paramedics to take the lead in Victoria mental health call outs

Paramedics will take the lead in mental health emergency callouts – not police – under a new agreement announced by Victoria’s health minister, Ingrid Stitt, this morning.

Under the new mental health crisis response protocols, signed by Ambulance Victoria, Victoria police and the health department, these callouts will be treated as a “health-led” response rather than a law-and-order matter.

Victoria’s mental health royal commission recommended in 2021 that paramedics become first responders to Triple Zero calls for mental health crises. In May, the New South Wales government accepted a similar recomendation by both a parliamentary inquiry and an internal police review.

Under the Victorian agreement, police and protective service officers will attend mental health callouts to support paramedics when needed rather than leading the initial response.

People in mental health crisis will also be transferred to hospitals by family members, mental health services, non-emergency patient transport or ambulances, rather than in the back of a police car when it is safe to do so, under the new arrangements.

When someone is experiencing a mental health crisis, they deserve a response that puts their health and wellbeing first while keeping them and the community safe.

RBA expected to hike cash rate to 4.6%, its highest level since 2011

Australia’s key interest rate is expected to hit its highest level since 2011, drag down house prices and add more than $100 to typical monthly mortgage repayments.

The Reserve Bank board is poised to hike the cash rate from 4.35% to 4.6% on Tuesday, which would be the fourth increase in 2026 and push typical home loan interest rates to 6.5%.

For someone with an average-sized new mortgage of $731,000, now paying a rate of 6.2%, an increase would add about $119 to their $4,477 monthly repayments. That would take the total rise in repayments since January to nearly $480.

Double murder trial of Beau Lamarre-Condon to resume

A police officer’s double-murder trial is set to resume with a close confidant continuing in the witness box, AAP reports.

Beau Lamarre-Condon, 31, has pleaded not guilty to the murders of Jesse Baird and Luke Davies, suggesting the two men died after two separate struggles in Baird’s house.

Close friend Georgia Cook will give evidence when the trial enters its second week on Monday. On Friday, Cook recounted a dinner the pair had in May 2023, when Lamarre-Condon propositioned her.

“What would you do if I told you I killed somebody?” Cook told the jury he said to her. “Would you help me hide the body?”

As well as contesting the two murder charges, Lamarre-Condon denies breaking into Baird’s home in August 2023 while disguised.

Lamarre-Condon’s barrister has conceded his client did some “stupid things” but disputes he exhibited threatening behaviours before the fatal incident in the Paddington terrace.

More than 100 witnesses are expected to be called during the 12-week trial, including dozens of police officers and friends and family of the three men. More than 20 were called in the trial’s opening week.

Victoria government delays level crossing removals, promises to duplicate Upfield line

The Victorian Labor government will delay the removal of eight level crossings in Brunswick, saving the budget $686m, while promising to duplicate the train line and increase services if re-elected in November.

The premier, Ben Carroll, and treasurer, Colin Brooks, will today announce the plan to defer the eight level crossing removals on the Upfield line, to coincide with track duplication.

Former premier Daniel Andrews had promised to remove the level crossings in 2022, with a completion date of 2027 that was later pushed back to 2030. Now, the government says they won’t be complete until 2033.

If re-elected in November, Labor will use some of the savings to begin work on building a second track on the train line between Gowrie and Upfield stations.

Currently, the 4km section of the line runs on a single track that the government says creates bottlenecks and delays for passengers. While commuters on most other metropolitan train lines wait less than 10 minutes, those of on the Upfield line can wait for up to 20 minutes even during the mornings and afternoons.

Carroll said duplicating the line will allow for turn-up-and-go services:

Fifty percent more capacity on the line, for turn-up-and-go services – that’s a huge change for the northern suburbs.

Doing it in one go reduces costs and minimises disruption for passengers. It’s common sense.

A dog needing a fence: Medicare breach reveals AI issue

Nobody should be surprised when an artificial intelligence agent goes off the rails, because it was never really on them in the first place, an AI expert says.

AAP reports an OpenAI breach of a Medicare statistics website made global headlines when the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, disclosed it on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York.

The incident has sent Canberra scrambling to fortify digital infrastructure, but something more fundamental needs to change in AI behaviour, Dr Chetan Arora, a cybersecurity expert, told AAP.

He compared autonomous AI models to dogs being trained not to leave a yard.

“…I can train my dog by means of punishment and reward for not crossing the boundary of my home [or the] the second way is actually putting the fence and deterring the dog,” Arora said.

What was missing in these systems is this engineering approach of building the fence.

OpenAI has stressed it did not direct its agent to infiltrate the website in order to perform the innocuous research task it had been set.

The bot interacted normally with three Australian federal and state government sites, but broke into the Medicare statistics portal when offered resistance.

Policing autonomous agents did not require a human watching a model’s every move, but there needed to be a clear framework with flesh-and-blood input and stringent reporting obligations, Arora said.

A task force set up after the breach to examine AI reporting obligations and Australia’s cyber-safety and legal avenues for punishing OpenAI is expected to report in a matter of weeks.

Bogong moths descend along NSW coast during annual migration

Australians along the east coast are reporting many sightings of endangered bogong moths as the insects continue their long migration towards the country’s alpine regions.

Bogong moths, about the size of a bottle cap with a distinctive dark stripe that runs down each wing behind two white spots, used to be incredibly abundant. But populations collapsed by up to 99.5% in the two years before 2019.

Zoos Victoria has a citizen science initiative called moth tracker where you can upload sightings and track the migration. Reports currently show the moths travelling from north of Newcastle all the way down the NSW south coast towards the Victorian border. They can be blown towards the coast by westerly winds and can be drawn inside homes by lights, the Australian Museum says.

Wildflower Nursery in Erina, NSW along the Central Coast, said they’ve noticed bogong moths “everywhere”.

“They’re endangered for a start, and they’re really essential nocturnal pollinators,” Natalie Sanderson, the owner of the nursery, said on social media, noting the moths have been blown off course towards the coastline en route to the Australian alps. “If we want to help them get back on track, and back to where they were headed, is turn off any non-essential nightlights around our properties and that will help them find their way back.”

People are encouraged not to kill the moths to aid population recovery.

“Noted ✍️ add ‘moth plague’ to warnings in future,” NSW SES wrote on social media.

Gallagher says final budget outcome will show $6bn improvement

Katy Gallagher and the treasurer, Jim Chalmers, are set to present the final budget outcome today, which she said will show about a $6bn improvement since the federal budget. The estimated deficit for 2025-26 in May was $28.3bn.

We do everything we can to make sure that the budget is in as good a shape as it can, acknowledging that we are having to find room to invest in all these areas that the Australian people care about deeply and want to see improved services in.

OpenAI breach signals a ‘new world’: Katy Gallagher

Katy Gallagher, who is also the minister for government services, is speaking this morning about the OpenAI breach of Medicare, saying we are essentially “in a new world” with the brisk advancement of technology.

She told Radio National breakfast this morning:

I think this incident really adds weight to the arguments that the PM that has been putting forward about needing to legislate, to provide some protections, some guardrails, and also, you know, have an escalation pathway.

I mean, the idea that something happened in June 18th and we weren’t aware of it until the second week of September is just not acceptable.

Gallagher said mandatory reporting requirements should be imposed “at a minimum”. She said there should be consequences while acknowledging that OpenAI had been helping in the investigation after the incident.

I think we have to have a look at this incident and deal with it on its merits, but the broader question of how we deal with these in the future.

Interest rate hike looms as inflation stubbornly high

Rising unemployment, stubbornly high inflation and a looming interest rate hike are piling misery onto Australian consumers, but Labor insists better days are on the horizon, Australian Associated Press reports.

Central bankers in the US, Japan and Europe have all pushed interest rates up since the RBA’s board met in August, all crediting their decisions to rising oil and fuel prices resulting from the increasingly intractable US-Iran war.

On Sunday the deputy prime minister, RichardMarles, said the government knew households were “doing it tough” and was working to put downward pressure on inflation.

Average fuel prices in Australia’s five largest cities had risen to $2.37 per litre for petrol and $2.69 for diesel as of Wednesday, but Labor has rejected calls for it to reinstate a previous cut to fuel taxes to help ease the burden on road-users.

Inflation, which came in hotter than expected in August, could rise from 3.5 to 4% when the Australian Bureau of Statistics publishes its latest figures on Wednesday.

Chalmers and Gallagher to present final budget outcome

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, and the finance minister, Katy Gallagher, will present the final budget outcome on Monday, the updated accounting measure for the 2025-26 financial year.

Despite challenges facing the government, Chalmers is expected to say the figures show the budget deficit is smaller than originally forecast.

He will argue the improvements are worth billions of dollars to the budget.
