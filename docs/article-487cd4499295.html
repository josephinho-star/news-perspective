Former capital markets chief faces scrutiny in Turkey fund scandal

2026-10-04 20:26

Turkish prosecutors have sought permission to investigate the former head of the country’s capital markets regulator on suspicion of abuse of office as a major investment fund scandal continues to shake financial markets, media reports said Sunday.

Turkey has been grappling with the fallout from the scandal since mid-September, when prosecutors began investigating allegations of widespread market manipulation.

On Saturday, an İstanbul court ordered another 20 suspects jailed pending trial, bringing the total number held in custody in related investigations to 85.

The scandal has already led to the resignation of senior ruling party figure and former minister Fatma Betül Sayan Kaya amid allegations that she and her husband profited from share transactions linked to the crisis.

In a letter to the Treasury and Finance Ministry dated September 30, prosecutors sought permission to investigate İbrahim Ömer Gönül, the former head of the Capital Markets Board (SPK), for alleged abuse of office. The ministry has since assigned its Inspection Board to conduct a preliminary review.

Prosecutors said there were “clear and sufficient indications” that Gönül failed to carry out his inspection and oversight responsibilities during his four-year term, which ended in April.

Gönül had already given a statement to prosecutors as a suspect in the wider fund investigation earlier this week.

The SPK is responsible for protecting investors and ensuring that Turkey’s capital markets operate fairly, transparently and efficiently.

The crisis erupted when several investment funds were unable to meet redemption requests during a market selloff in mid-September, triggering a sharp fall in İstanbul’s main stock index.

The SPK subsequently ordered the liquidation of 131 funds managed by seven portfolio companies. The regulator said the funds had 455,758 individual investors, while Treasury and Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek has valued their assets at about $18.3 billion.

“Our top priority is to prevent harm to the Turkish economy,” President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan said on Saturday.

“Our second priority is to ensure that anyone who has obtained unjust gains is held accountable by the justice system. We are working to resolve this issue swiftly on the basis of justice and fairness, without anyone’s rights being compromised,” he said.
