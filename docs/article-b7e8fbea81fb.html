2 killed in separate fires, bringing NYC’s fire-related death toll to nearly double this point in 2025

2026-11-21 01:02

Two people died in separate city blazes Thursday, bringing this year’s fire-related death toll to 90 – nearly double the number at this point in 2025, according to the FDNY.

The first fire erupted around 10:15 a.m. on the first floor of a four-story apartment building on Lincoln Place near Classon Avenue in Crown Heights, fire officials said.

A 57-year-old woman – who was not immediately identified – was rushed with critical injuries to Brooklyn Methodist Hospital, where she was died, according to the NYPD and FDNY.

Three more victims suffered minor injuries in the blaze, but refused medical attention, officials said.

The flames were knocked down within 45 minutes, and the cause of the blaze remains under investigation.

The second inferno broke out around 12:25 p.m. on the second floor of a private house on Metcalf Avenue near East 172nd Street in the Soundview section of the Bronx, officials said.

Zaidan Haruna – a 22-year-old who lived in the home – suffered smoke inhalation and was rushed to Jacobi Medical Center, where she also died, police said.

Another person was hospitalized for minor injuries, according to the FDNY.

A 7-year-old child was also inside the building at the time of the blaze, but wasn’t harmed, authorities confirmed.

There have been 90 fire-related deaths – a result of 62 blazes – across the Big Apple this year, including Thursday’s victims. That marks a significant spike from the 51 fatalities at this point in 2025, according to the FDNY.

Fire officials attribute the death surge, in part, to the “extremely cold” weather to start 2026.

“We not only had a significant number of fires and multiple alarm fires, but we started the year with 11 fire fatalities in January alone,” the department said.

“Unfortunately, we have had a consistent number of fatal fires as the year has gone on.”

Nineteen of those have been incendiary fires – “whether suicide, self-immolation, or the intentional ignition of flammable liquids, cigarette into cardboard boxes” – and 18 were electrical blazes, marking the two leading causes of fatal fires so far this year.

Just weeks ago, six people were killed when a massive fire ripped through a Sunset Park home in Brooklyn.

Nine others were also injured in the blaze, including three firefighters and two cops.

Four residents inside the building died at the scene, while two others were rushed to local hospitals and later died.

Days earlier in the Bronx, four people were killed in an inferno in the kitchen of an 11th-floor apartment at NYCHA’s Patterson Houses.

The cause of that fire was determined to be a lithium-ion battery from a flaming e-bike that trapped residents inside, blocking their only escape route, according to the FDNY.

E-bike batteries have resulted in nine deaths so far this year, the department said.
