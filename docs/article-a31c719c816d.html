Turkey captures suspect in deadly 2013 Reyhanlı bombing

2026-10-01 08:52

Turkey’s intelligence agency has captured a Syrian suspect wanted in connection with a deadly bombing in 2013 in the southern border town of Reyhanlı as he was preparing to flee to Europe by sea, state media reported Thursday, citing security sources.

The twin car bombings in Reyhanlı, near the Syrian border, killed 53 people and became one of the deadliest attacks in Turkey linked to spillover from Syria’s civil war.

Omar al-Khatib, a Syrian national wanted under an INTERPOL Red Notice, is accused of playing a role in planning and organizing the attack and coordinating between the perpetrators and intelligence officials loyal to then-Syrian president Bashar al-Assad, according to the state-run Anadolu news agency.

Turkey’s National Intelligence Organization (MİT) captured al-Khatib as he was preparing to leave for Europe by sea and brought him to Turkey, the reports said, without specifying where the operation took place.

According to information attributed to MİT, al-Khatib and another suspect, Yusuf Nazik, entered Turkey from Syria in February 2013 to identify potential attack locations before returning to the Syrian province of Latakia, where the plot was allegedly coordinated with Syrian military intelligence official Kifah Melhem.

Nazik was captured by MİT in Syria in 2018 and later sentenced to 53 aggravated life terms for his role in the attack.

A Turkish court in June 2026 also sentenced three other defendants — Memet Gezer, Temir Dükancı and Cengiz Sertel — to 53 aggravated life terms each over the bombings. Al-Khatib’s case had been separated because he remained at large.

Ankara blamed groups allegedly backed by the Syrian government for the Reyhanlı attack, accusations Damascus denied at the time.

Turkey was a strong supporter of Syrian rebels seeking to overthrow Assad and hosted millions of Syrians who fled the conflict.

Since Assad’s ouster in late 2024, Ankara has developed close ties with Syria’s new leadership under President Ahmed al-Sharaa.
