Tributes for Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor after his death aged 65

2026-11-26 12:33

Simon Le Bon, Sir Rod Stewart and Gary Kemp are among the pop stars who have paid tribute to Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor following his death at the age of 65.

Taylor co-wrote some of the group's biggest hits including Save A Prayer, Rio and Girls On Film.

Le Bon and his bandmates paid tribute, saying "he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music", while Sir Rod remembered his "dear pal" and Spandau Ballet's Kemp called him "a great player who set the tone for the 80s".

Taylor's wife Tracey called him "the rock of our family", adding: "We will miss him every single day." He died after "a long and courageous battle with prostate cancer", Duran Duran said in a statement on Wednesday.

He died "peacefully at home, surrounded by his family, on Monday 23rd November", a statement said.

Paying tribute, his Duran Duran bandmates said: "From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music.

"His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed."

They said their thoughts were with his wife, children and grandson.

Le Bon said there was a "a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart".

"Andy never took the easy musical route. His overdrive overdrove us into being the only funk/rock/punk band in the New Romantic movement, with songs that still survive, decades later."

Sir Rod, who wrote five songs with Taylor for the 1988 album Out of Order, recalled them making "some good music together" and having "some outrageous fun".

Kemp said Taylor "influenced many of us along the way with his sparse staccato style", adding: "He brought shameless rock to the mix."

In a band tribute, Spandau Ballet called him "a hugely talented musician and an important part of the British music scene".

US guitarist Nile Rodgers, the co-founder of Chic, recalled "countless great days and nights" with his "dear friend".

Belinda Carlisle said she was "blessed" to have worked with Taylor, who played the guitar solo and was in the video for her song Mad About You.

Former Led Zeppelin frontman Robert Plant called Taylor "a tough fighter", while Then Jerico's Mark Shaw remembered him as "one of the greatest musicians and songwriters I've ever heard and had the privilege to work with".

Taylor's wife Tracey, a hairstylist he met during the early days of Duran Duran, and whom he married in 1982, said: "For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first for Taylor, adding: "Nothing made him happier than having us all together."

She said he faced the last few years "with so much courage and determination" and said they were "grateful" for the "kindness and support shown to him by fans throughout his illness", saying it brought him "immense comfort and strength".

The son of a fisherman, Taylor taught himself guitar, bass, and drums and began playing professionally at age 13.

He played with several bands around the UK before answering a music paper advertisement for a "live-wire guitarist", which led to his audition for Duran Duran in Birmingham in April 1980.

The group went on to achieve global fame through the New Romantic movement, which combined slick, melodic synth-pop with aspirational music videos and chiselled good looks.

Taylor's guitar lines - full of glassy, powerful riffs - were often a highlight of their songs, from the choppy opening of Girls on Film to the sleazy scuzz of Hungry Like The Wolf.

Duran Duran were one of the biggest pop acts of the 1980s, and its five members became global hearththrobs.

Their indelible hooks and airbrushed music videos found a natural home on the newly launched MTV, giving their career a huge boost in the US.

They became so popular in the US that the press labeled them the leaders of a "second British invasion" of the charts - alongside groups like Spandau Ballet, Human League and Eurythmics.

During a hiatus in 1984, Andy and John Taylor enlisted Robert Palmer for their successful but short-lived side project the Power Station.

The following year, the guitarist decided to quit Duran Duran after a miserable experience performing at Live Aid.

"We'd reached rock bottom and we had absolutely nothing to say to each other," he wrote in his autobiography, Wild Boy.

"We drove to the venue through streets packed with excited rock fans, but inside the bus the atmosphere was as if we were on a way to a funeral."

He went on to play on Palmer's Addicted To Love, and released a solo album, Thunder, in 1987.

After a period writing and producing albums for other artists, he rejoined Duran Duran in 2000, lasting for just one album (2004's Astronaut) before parting ways again.

In 2022, when the band were inducted to the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, Taylor was notably absent from the ceremony, despite public plans for a reunion.

When the group took the stage, Le Bon read a letter from the guitarist, in which he revealed his diagnosis with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer.

Nonetheless, he contributed to the following year's Halloween-themed album Danse Macabre, marking his final collaboration with the band.

Duran Duran at the BBC, on BBC Four on Friday 27 November at 21:30 GMT

Sounds of the 80s, on Radio 2 on Saturday 28 November at at 18:00 GMT
