Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor dies aged 65

2026-11-26 05:19

Andy Taylor, the guitarist for the pop group Duran Duran, has died at the age of 65.

Born in Tynemouth, he co-wrote some of the group's biggest hits, including Save A Prayer, Rio and Girls On Film; and also worked with artists like Robert Palmer, Rod Stewart and Belinda Carlisle.

In a statement, his bandmates said Taylor had fought "a long and courageous battle with prostate cancer" and "passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by his family, on Monday 23 November".

His wife, Tracey, said the musician "was the rock of our family", adding: "We will miss him every single day".

The son of a fisherman, Taylor taught himself guitar, bass, and drums and began playing professionally at age 13.

He played with several bands around the UK before answering a music paper advertisement for a "live-wire guitarist", which led to his audition for Duran Duran in Birmingham in April 1980.

The group went on to achieve global fame through the New Romantic movement, which combined slick, melodic synth-pop with aspirational music videos and chiselled good looks.

Taylor's guitar lines - full of glassy, powerful riffs - were often a highlight of their songs, from the choppy opening of Girls on Film to the sleazy scuzz of Hungry Like The Wolf.

"Andy was there at the very beginning of our story," said his bandmates Simon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes, John Taylor and Roger Taylor (his bandmates were unrelated, despite sharing the same last name).

"From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music.

"His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed.

"Our thoughts are with his wife Tracey, his children Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew, and grandson Albie. They all loved him deeply and we ask that their privacy be respected at this very difficult time."

Sir Rod Stewart, who wrote five songs with Taylor for the 1988 album Out of Order, paid tribute to his "dear pal".

"We made some good music together and at the same time had some outrageous fun," Sir Rod posted on Instagram.

Duran Duran were one of the biggest pop acts of the 1980s, and its five members became global hearththrobs.

Their indelible hooks and airbrushed music videos found a natural home on the newly launched MTV, giving their career a huge boost in the US.

They became so popular in the States that the press labeled them the leaders of a "Second British Invasion" of the charts - alongside groups like Spandau Ballet, Human League and Eurythmics.

During a hiatus in 1984, Andy and John Taylor enlisted Robert Palmer for their successful but short-lived side project the Power Station.

The following year, the guitarist decided to quit Duran Duran, after a miserable experience performing at Live Aid.

"We'd reached rock bottom and we had absolutely nothing to say to each other," he wrote in his autobiography, Wild Boy.

"We drove to the venue through streets packed with excited rock fans, but inside the bus the atmosphere was as if we were on a way to a funeral."

He went on to play on Robert Palmer's Addicted To Love, and released a solo album, Thunder, in 1987.

After a period writing and producing albums for other artists, he rejoined Duran Duran in 2000, lasting for just one album (2004's Astronaut) before parting ways again.

In 2022, when the band were inducted to the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, Taylor was notably absent from the ceremony, despite public plans for a reunion.

When the group took the stage, singer Simon Le Bon read a letter from the guitarist, in which he revealed his diagnosis with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer.

Nonetheless, he contributed to the following year's Halloween-themed album Danse Macabre, marking his final collaboration with the band.

"Andy never took the easy musical route," said Le Bon in a personal statement marking his friend's death.

"His overdrive, overdrove us into being the only funk/rock/punk band in the New Romantic movement, with songs that still survive, decades later.

"There's a massive Andy Taylor shaped hole in my heart. It'll never close up; I won't let it. I will miss him & love him for all my days."

Taylor is survived by his wife, Tracey, a hairstylist he met during the early days of Duran Duran, and whom he married in 1982.

"For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first," she wrote in her statement.

"Nothing made him happier than having us all together, and no matter how far music took him, his heart was always at home."

She continued: "Andy faced the last few years with so much courage and determination. He never gave up, fighting until the very end, never losing his love of life.

"The kindness and support shown to him by fans from all over the world throughout his illness brought him immense comfort and strength, and for that we will always be grateful."
