Politics live: Kevin Rudd urges more multilateral diplomacy; UK hopeful Australia will join West Bank trade ban

2026-09-09 20:50

Australia should ‘put shoulder to the wheel’ on multilateral diplomacy: Rudd

Former prime minister Kevin Rudd is in Canberra for a round of high-profile speeches this week, marking his return to Australia after serving as ambassador to Washington DC.

Rudd spoke at the Australian Institute of International Affairs on Wednesday night and will be at the National Press Club today.

He said Australia must continue to push multilateral diplomacy, even as Donald Trump withdraws the US from a range of important global institutions. Australia, as a leading middle power, should promote the United Nations system, which has support across Asia and Europe, according to Rudd.

There remains an opportunity for Australia, in partnership with other middle powers around the world, to put our shoulders to the wheel in supporting those UN multilateral institutions which now find themselves under financial and institutional pressure as a result of the US withdrawing.

Australia remains active in the UN, the IMF and the World Bank. We remain active in the G20 and in APEC.

We remain active in major regional institutions, such as the Pacific Islands Forum, where the government has once again registered a remarkable series of foreign policy achievements.

Rudd said this kind of serious engagement would “put flesh on the bones” of the plan outlined by the Canadian prime minister, Mark Carney, at Davos earlier this year.

And it would mark a deeply practical contribution to sustaining the multilateral rules-based system as America resolves its internal domestic debate on its future posture within it.

Victoria has highest rate of car theft and home burglary claims in ICA data

Melbourne recorded more insurance claims for stolen and broken-into cars than all other Australian capital cities combined, according to data analysis by the Insurance Council of Australia.

Property crime data released by the ICA on Wednesday night showed Victoria had the highest rates of car theft and home burglary claims in the country in the last financial year. Analysis of Insurance Statistics Australia (ISA) data showed that there were more than 12,300 claims – costing insurers $244m – lodged in the state for cars stolen or broken into.

In the same time period, every other state and territory recorded a decline.

The ICA’s chief executive, Andrew Hall, said in Victoria a car was stolen or broken into every 43 minutes while a home is broken into every two hours.

The insurance data shows Victoria has a crime problem, and it is being felt directly by households, businesses and communities.

Before the November state election, the ICA called for the next state government to reduce car theft and property crime through a statewide strategy and better intelligence sharing, improve disaster recovery by establishing centrally coordinated clean-up arrangements, and ease cost-of-living pressures by abolishing stamp duty on business, home and contents insurance products.

It also called for improving insurance affordability for healthcare professionals, community organisations and live music venues by “modernising civil liability laws”.

It was ‘hoped’ Australia would join UK trade ban on Israeli settlements in West Bank: Emily Thornberry

The head of the UK’s foreign affairs select committee says there was hope Australia would join the UK’s trade ban on Israeli settlements in the West Bank.

Australia is preparing another round of sanctions against Israel but has stopped short of joining the UK’s trade ban. On Tuesday, the UK government announced it would ban imported goods from illegal settlements in the occupied territories.

Speaking to the ABC’s 7.30, Emily Thornberry, the chair of the UK’s foreign affairs select committee, was asked whether she hoped Australia would join the UK’s trade ban decision:

I think it was hoped that they might but, you know, all countries have their own decisions to make. We all want the same thing and nobody says this is easy.

Thornberry said she had a “a lot of time” for the foreign affairs minister, Penny Wong:

I don’t know the basis upon which she’s made a decision not to be involved with us, but I’m sure that you know she believes that what she’s doing is in the interests of Palestinians and in the interests of justice.

I’m always happy to talk to my Australian friends and maybe even persuade them.

On Wednesday, Wong said she was concerned a blanket ban could have “unintended consequences” for Australian businesses, Palestinians and Israelis and reiterated the country would continue to protect the prospect of a two-state solution.
