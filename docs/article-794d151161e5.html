Australia politics live: Albanese faces more questions over $6m golf club grant; Chalmers says Coalition was ‘doing better under Sussan Ley’

2026-09-10 05:40

PM on the defensive over Marrickville golf club grant

The prime minister is again on the back foot, defending a $6m grant given to the Marrickville golf club, which he was an honorary member of.

Yesterday Anthony Albanese said the money was to stop sewerage from the club ending up in the Cooks River. Liberal frontbencher Tony Pasin asks if the PM mislead parliament because $1m was used on stormwater works, but $4.55m was spent on a clubhouse precinct.

Albanese provides a pretty short answer, and doesn’t address the substance of the question, but basically does confirm that part of the funding, not all of it, was for the sewerage works.

There were three commitments in my electorate. One for a women’s health centre, the one for a St Vincent de Paul facility, a refuge, for women and children escaping domestic violence, and one for the upgrade of the inner west council–owned Marrickville golf club. Included in that was funding to fix the sewerage because it goes into the river and causes a problem when it floods.

Victoria’s WFH bill effectively delayed until after state election

Australian-first laws that guarantee the right to work from home two days a week are unlikely to pass Victorian parliament before the November state election, with the Coalition and crossbench teaming up to refer the bill to an inquiry.

The laws were first announced in 2025 by the then premier Jacinta Allan in a bid to capitalise on the backlash to the federal Coalition’s plan to force public servants back into the office full-time, which contributed to their election loss.

The Victorian Coalition, though, had said it would support the bill and expected it would pass parliament on Thursday.

But in a shock development, the Coalition has joined with crossbench MPs, including Legalise Cannabis, David Limbrick from the Libertarians, former Liberal MP Moira Deeming and One Nation’s Rikkie-Lee Tyrrell to refer the bill to an inquiry that effectively stalls it until after the election.

The inquiry was put forward by Shooters, Fishers and Farmers MP Jeff Bourman and passed 18 votes to 17.

Independents Sophie Scamps and Helen Haines asked the government why Australia won’t join partners including the UK in implementing a trade ban on Israeli settlements in the West Bank – Albanese and Richard Marles repeated the same lines that Penny Wong gave yesterday.

Two MPs – Nationals MP Kevin Hogan and Labor backbencher Basem Abdo – were kicked out in the middle of a question to Jim Chalmers about government debt bonds.

Department denies dereliction of duties over alleged AI hallucinations in submission on social media ban

Last month, the federal infrastructure department was quizzed in a Senate inquiry over claims made in a submission to the social media ban amendment bill that alleged citation errors identified in the age assurance technology trial report were AI hallucinations.

It was aired just before we published our story identifying several errors in the report.

At the time, officials relayed the claims by ACCS - the age verification organisation that undertook the trial - that the errors were due to links breaking in the intervening time between the report publication and the hearing.

Senator Fatima Payman queried in her dissenting report of the inquiry whether officials had been derelict in their duty in not independently verifying the advice provided by ACCS at the time.

The department’s secretary, Jim Betts, said in a letter to the committee chair, tabled this week, that officials are not failing in their duties if they give evidence “based on advice provided to them that, at face value, can be accepted as accurate”.

The representations made by officials were based on information available to them at the time and were honestly and reasonably held.

Betts said the department had continued to investigate since then. The department said ACCS had since provided new citations to replace the erroneous ones and would correct the report online.

ACCS has denied the citation errors were AI hallucinations.

The department declined to answer further questions on the report.

Coalition tries to pin Albanese on golf club membership

Take two – Anthony Albanese is asked again how the Daily Telegraph knew about his honorary membership in 2019 but he didn’t, and asks if it’s just “another case of you falling off a stage?”

The inference there is that Albanese is lying about the membership and doesn’t want to own up to it (like when he tried to deny that he fell off a stage during the last election campaign).

Albanese once again does his absolute best to totally avoid the question.

He says the article was related to a 2019 campaign to save the golf course from future development, and does a little copy and paste from a previous answer where he said that of the Coalition’s 2025 election promises, not one was for Sydney’s inner west.

I certainly supported it as did people throughout the area of the inner west either side of the Cooks River, because this is an absolutely vital and precious piece of the inner west, of Sydney.

The manager of opposition business, Dan Tehan, tries to table the article to parliament, but leader of the House, Tony Burke rejects the attempt, and says it’s already online.

Richard Marles offers no further insight on position over Israeli settlements

The independent MP Helen Haines asks: what will it take for Australia to join our allies in imposing trade bans on Israeli settlements in the West Bank?

This time the deputy PM Richard Marles takes the question and sheds no further light on the issue than when Anthony Albanese was asked about it a short time ago.

Marles says the Australian government supports a two-state solution, and illegal settlements are hindering that, but that targeted sanctions on individuals and entities are still the answer.

Because of the positions that we have taken, we have put in place sanctions and bans in respect of those that have been participating in the settler – settlements – in the territories, and that has included Israeli ministers themselves. We will continue to look at further targeted measures, working collaboratively with our allies and partners around the world in respect of this issue.

Albanese dodges question on 2019 report of honorary membership in golf club

The opposition has come with receipts, asking how a Daily Telegraph article from 2019 reported that Anthony Albanese was an honorary member of the Marrickville golf club but the prime minister himself didn’t know until now.

A Coalition MP shouts asking if Toto is a member too, to which Albanese replies, smiling: “Don’t attack Toto, you’ve got to draw the line somewhere.”

The prime minister doesn’t really answer the question.

He goes off on a bit of a tangent, saying that the golf club wasn’t even in his electorate in 2019, and that he has the second-smallest electorate in the country.

There is very little green space in my electorate, and I defends every inch of it. In 2019, the Marrickville golf course was not in my electorate. It was actually in the electorate of Barton.

Albanese defends not declaring golf club membership

Liberal frontbencher Angie Bell is next and asks the prime minister why he hasn’t declared his honorary membership of the Marrickville golf club when he did declare honorary membership of the Royal Sydney golf club.

The former received $6m under an invite-only grants program that gave the majority of funding to safe, marginal or target Labor seats.

Spoiler alert: the opposition intervenes three times because they’re not happy with the answer.

Anthony Albanese says it’s because he knew about one (Royal Sydney) but didn’t know about the other, and doesn’t even have a card for the Marrickville club.

Albanese then tries to turn the attention back to the Coalition, and says he took a look at all the opposition’s 2025 election funding promises (which some staff member would have spent many hours trying to pour through).

I looked through the list of Coalition election commitments from 2025 and there were over 1,000 projects worth $1.2bn. I looked for anything in Grayndler and of the thousand projects, there wasn’t a single one in the inner west, not one.
