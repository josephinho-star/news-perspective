How to think about the ‘AI bubble’ that supposedly threatens the stock market

2026-11-23 11:00

Now? On Nov. 2 just before Election Day, 23 of 35 financial firms’ market outlooks I reviewed cited midterms’ historical bullishness. Media coverage likewise hailed the Goldilocks effect of gridlock.

On Nov. 4, Reuters wrote the election results bullishly limit the scope for sharp policy shifts. Inc. Magazine declared, “a divided government is historically positive for the stock market.”

Of course, the bullishness isn’t universal. Many fear Democrats for many reasons – excess spending, new regulations, restraining AI, investigations and more. But the rising bullish gridlock tone raises the odds my long beloved “Miracle” is – sadly – now priced in.

In my 54-year career, many powerful concepts became priced in. So, I fear the Miracle’s power is fading. We shall see.

Regardless, other underappreciated reasons for bullishness abound. Among them: This bull market is actually far broader than commonly envisioned.

The belief that the post-2022 rally is primarily tech and AI is widespread. Many believe vast data center investment underpins all economic growth – that it is all chip stocks, circular AI investment and astronomical AI profitability projections – basically a “house of cards” inflated by relatively few stocks. Many now cast potential AI regulation and the Jensen Huang scandal as what collapses that house.

Yes, there is excess AI hype. That risks AI-related stocks underperforming. But a bubble? Real bubbles are exceedingly rare. And devilishly hard to spot before bursting. Remember when pundits swore the “Mag 7” was a bubble? That was three-plus years ago.

Earlier, “the FAANGs” supposedly propped up a narrow bubblicious rally. That term dates to 2013. That bull market peaked in 2020 – but from COVID, not de-FAANGing.

To see this bull market’s real and massive breadth, employ basic logic tests. The AI trade centers on tech, classic growth stocks. Yet through Friday, Russell’s 1000 Growth Index is down 13.3% in 2026. The 1000 Value? Up 16.1%. Even small caps show it: the Russell 2000 Value index trounces its growth counterpart, 8.4% to -2.1%. This market is value-led.

Or look regionally. America’s S&P 500 is 35.7% tech by market cap. Tech-like firms add another 11.2%. MSCI’s World Ex-USA Index is less than 10% tech and tech-like. Yet the two are just a fraction of one percent apart year-to-date. If this bull market was all AI, why isn’t America leading by a mile?

Or, review AI “winners.” Pure-play AI developers are still largely private or tiny. Publicly traded beneficiaries are largely tech, semiconductor firms and data-center related.

They mostly peaked in May or June. Semiconductor stocks overall tumbled 42.5% since! And that long before the Nvidia CEO drama. Yet the broader S&P 500 sits just 11.6% below Oct. 6’s record high. If chips and AI solely support the broad market, why isn’t it down much more? Yes, I know last week’s decline technically slams us into “correction territory” — meaning the S&P 500 is down more than 10%, just barely, but less than 20%. Yet corrections happen sharp and gone every few years.

But logic tests burst the belief this bull market is some AI bubble-like risk-o-rama. They reveal the brighter reality: A broader bull market with room to resume. Because stocks rise about three time periods for every one they fall, if you haven’t a good, valid reason to be bearish, you should be bullish.

And the basis for any valid reason to be bullish or bearish is knowing something important others don’t. Do you?

Ken Fisher is the founder and executive chairman of Fisher Investments, a four-time New York Times bestselling author, and regular columnist in 21 countries globally.
