Anthropic co-founder to make ‘large gift’ to Trump’s investment accounts for kids after record IPO: Bessent

2026-11-20 19:02

One of Anthropic’s newly minted billionaire co-founders is poised to share the wealth with President Trump’s signature investment program for children following the AI giant’s blockbuster initial public offering, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent revealed Friday.

“One of the Anthropic founders is going to make a large gift to Trump Accounts over the next few weeks,” Bessent told CNBC.

“We believe that it’s important for these great entrepreneurs to share their bounty,” the Treasury chief added.

Bessent did not identify the founder or disclose how much money would be donated to the program, which provides government-funded investment accounts for American children.

A source briefed on the matter told The Post that the Anthropic co-founder in question is Tom Brown.

Brown has emerged as Anthropic’s point man in Washington after his fellow co-founder, CEO Dario Amodei, reportedly rubbed Trump administration officials the wrong way.

The MIT-educated engineer was tapped to handle sensitive White House negotiations earlier this year after officials complained that Amodei was difficult to deal with, according to a June report by Wired.

“Tom Brown is not being a weirdo like Dario and can actually engage,” one person familiar with the discussions told Wired.

Still, Brown, 39, once helped launch a dating app partly because he had been painfully awkward growing up. His app matched groups of strangers for drinks before moving on to artificial intelligence.

More recently, Brown and Anthropic public policy chief Sarah Heck took leading roles in talks aimed at resolving restrictions on the company’s advanced AI models, according to the report.

Brown’s growing influence in Washington was underscored in September, when Bloomberg reported that he held discussions with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael amid continuing tensions over the company’s AI safety policies.

The Trump Accounts program is designed to encourage investing and financial literacy among young Americans.

Under the initiative, US citizens born between 2025 and 2028 are eligible for a government-funded investment account seeded with $1,000, which families can supplement with additional contributions.

Anthropic’s IPO bonanza has also sparked questions about whether some of its newly wealthy employees could cash out their shares and leave the company.

More than half of the company’s roughly 5,000 workers are estimated to hold equity worth at least $10 million, while approximately 600 non-founder employees have stakes valued at $100 million or more, according to estimates cited by The Post.

The company has warned investors that retaining top researchers and other technical talent is critical to its future success as it battles rivals including OpenAI and Google.

Meanwhile, Anthropic’s stock market honeymoon has already hit turbulence.

Shares plunged nearly 10% Friday morning, erasing much of the gains from Thursday’s debut amid a broader selloff in technology stocks.

The stock had surged as high as $111.50 on its first day of trading before closing at $97.17.

The selloff came amid investor concerns about the enormous sums needed to finance the AI industry’s expansion.

Anthropic has disclosed hundreds of billions of dollars in future computing and infrastructure commitments, even as Dario Amodei said Thursday that the company hopes eventually to tap the debt markets for additional financing.
