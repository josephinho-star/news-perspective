Howard Lutnick hails Rudy Giuliani’s 9/11 response ahead of Presidential Medal of Freedom ceremony

2026-11-24 06:25

Rudy Giuliani was feted Monday on the eve of his Presidential Medal of Freedom ceremony at the White House – with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick lauding the former New York City mayor’s leadership on 9/11.

Giuliani, known as “America’s Mayor,” is slated to become only the third person during President Trump’s second term to be bestowed the nation’s highest civilian honor.

“What an honor to be here with you,” Lutnick told Giuliani during a dinner hosted by the Italian ambassador to the US ahead of Tuesday’s Oval Office ceremony.

“The mayor and I have a bond that can never be broken,” the commerce secretary explained, “because together we suffered catastrophic loss.”

Lutnick lost his brother, his best friend and hundreds of employees at his firm, Cantor Fitzgerald, in the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks.

Meanwhile, Giuliani was faced with the deaths of hundreds of first responders among the more than 2,700 killed in the attacks on the World Trade Center.

“I lost all the people who worked for me. He lost all the people who worked for him … our hearts were broken,” Lutnick continued. “But we both chose a particular path, and that was to take care of the families of those we lost, to care for them, to let them know how much we cared for them, to rebuild and make sure we rebuilt with dignity and with power, so that everybody understood that you can’t take New York down, you can’t take the United States down: Those are just not things that happen.”

Lutnick said Giuliani “stood up for” the victims of the attack “brilliantly.”

“He’s shown a light to all the United States, and to the world, what it’s like to lead in times of the worst circumstances,” he added.

The Presidential Medal of Freedom is awarded to individuals who have made especially meritorious contributions to the security or national interests of the US, world peace, or cultural or other significant public or private endeavors.

The president has awarded the medal posthumously to two people in his second term: slain conservative political activist Charlie Kirk and Welles Crowther, an equities trader credited with saving more than a dozen people from the World Trade Center on 9/11.

Trump has also announced plans to bestow the honor on neurosurgeon and former HUD Secretary Ben Carson; 2026 Winter Olympics hockey gold medalist Connor Hellebuyck; and baseball great Roger Clemens.

The president announced his plan to honor Giuliani in September 2025, days after the former mayor was seriously injured in a car crash.

“As President of the United States of America, I am pleased to announce that Rudy Giuliani, the greatest Mayor in the history of New York City, and an equally great American Patriot, will receive THE PRESIDENTIAL MEDAL OF FREEDOM, our Country’s highest civilian honor,” Trump wrote on Truth Social at the time.

“There is no American more deserving of this honor,” Giuliani spokesman Ted Goodman wrote on X.

“Mayor Rudy Giuliani took down the Mafia, saved New York City, comforted the nation following 9/11, and served in countless other ways to improve the lives of others,” he added. “Thank you, President Trump, for honoring his life and legacy.”
