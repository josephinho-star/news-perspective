Putin scores ‘massive win’ at first trilateral meeting, rebuilding standing as Ukraine war grinds on

2026-11-20 09:26

Russian President Vladimir Putin came away from China stronger than he arrived, standing as an equal beside the leaders of the U.S. and China, both far more powerful than Russia, even as he remains bogged down in a war in Ukraine now in its fifth year.

Putin stood shoulder to shoulder with U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping on a red-carpeted stage Thursday after the first-ever meeting between the three leaders. Trump publicly praised Putin as "my friend" as the two entered a separate bilateral meeting.

Putin was greeted at the APEC summit with smiles and handshakes, and he held bilateral meetings with at least seven world leaders over the two days. This came with the Ukraine war showing no signs of ending, more than four years after Russia invaded its neighbor.

"It's a massive win for Putin," said Joerg Wuttke, an expert at DGA-Albright Stonebridge Group. It's "as if the world has forgiven."

Hours before the three-way meeting, Ukrainian forces struck the bridge linking Russia to Crimea, a peninsula annexed by Russia in 2014, for the third time this week. Russia retaliated with a major attack on Kyiv, killing several people and damaging Ukrainian infrastructure.

The two-day summit appeared to be a symbolic triumph, even as Moscow escalated the war and took a hard line during the European Union leaders' renewed push to end the conflict.

Despite few concrete outcomes, the first-ever three-way summit is "arguably a diplomatic victory for Moscow," said Andrius Tursa, an advisor for Central and Eastern Europe and Russia at Teneo. Putin will likely seek to institutionalize the format, Tursa said.

During the meeting and afterward, Putin and Trump railed against the International Criminal Court, which has had an arrest warrant out for Putin since March 2023. Trump called the court, which his administration has sanctioned, an out-of-control "rogue globalist organization." Putin said he would consider attending the December G20 meeting in Miami after discussing it with Trump.

The three leaders issued a joint declaration of peace after the trilateral talks. Beijing has repeatedly called for negotiations and a political settlement while avoiding condemnation of Russia's aggression.

Putin later spent much of a press briefing after the trilateral meeting defending Moscow's decision to invade Ukraine, insisting that its root causes lie in NATO expansion and the alleged illegitimacy of Ukraine's leadership.

He also blamed the continued conflict on Ukraine's reluctance to accept terms. "If they truly want to end the conflict, they should agree to the compromises ... We accepted those terms; now, it is up to Ukraine."

This stance leaves "little scope for a ceasefire, let alone a durable settlement, absent a material shift in the military balance or political leadership in Moscow or Kyiv," Tursa said.

Russia lags both the U.S. and China in economic clout, population size, and most other major metrics of influence. Russian media, however, portrayed Putin's participation in discussions on global issues alongside the leaders of two major economic and military powers as a recognition of Russia's global power status and a setback for Western isolation efforts.

Putin said that all three leaders agreed that it's a "useful format," and such meetings could take place again.

"A key Kremlin objective will be to institutionalize such a great-power format," said Tursa. No concrete agreements on Ukraine were announced following the summit, and it remains unclear what, if anything, was settled with neither Kyiv nor European officials in the room.

One signal is whether the three leaders meet again in the near future, with the upcoming G20 summit in Miami a potential next opportunity, Tursa said.

Ukraine and European governments have said repeatedly that they must have a say in any talks on their security. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said ahead of the summit that big powers tend to assume they can decide the world's future, which he called a "huge mistake."
