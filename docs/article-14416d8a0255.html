Ken Griffin sells NYC duplex to Jets owner for $58M — months after Mamdani’s pied-à-terre tax stunt

2026-11-17 23:45

Hedge fund billionaire Ken Griffin has sold a sprawling Park Avenue duplex to New York Jets owner Woody Johnson for $58 million — just months after Mayor Zohran Mamdani targeted the Citadel founder in a creepy video touting the lefty’s new pied-à-terre tax.

The move may save Griffin from having to pay at least some of the pied-à-terre tax, as his primary residence is in Florida. Earlier this year, Business Insider calculated that Griffin would owe an additional $1.3 million to $1.4 million in taxes because of the second residence levy.

Johnson, an heir to the Johnson & Johnson fortune, snapped up the roughly 8,500-square-foot residence at 740 Park Ave., one of the Upper East Side’s most exclusive addresses, in an off-market deal.

The new transaction represents a roughly 29% increase over Griffin’s purchase price, before accounting for any transaction costs or improvements. Griffin paid $13 million less for the pad just last year.

“I wish Woody and his family many happy years in one of New York’s great residences,” Griffin told The Post on Tuesday.

The sale comes after the billionaire clashed publicly with Mamdani over the democratic socialist’s push to tax the city’s wealthiest property owners.

In April, Mamdani filmed a Tax Day video outside Griffin’s $238 million penthouse at 220 Central Park South, which the hedge fund mogul purchased in 2019 in what was then the most expensive residential real estate transaction in US history. Griffin still owns that property.

The mayor singled out Griffin’s apartment while promoting a proposed tax on luxury second homes valued at $5 million or more.

Griffin blasted the stunt as a “personal attack” and a “profound lack of judgment,” while Citadel subsequently raised questions about its planned multibillion-dollar office tower at 350 Park Ave.

Speaking at the Milken Institute Global Conference in May, Griffin said the tower remained “a point of discussion internally” and that his firm needed to “double down on our bet in Miami.”

The Park Avenue duplex, which was previously marketed with five bedrooms, was never publicly listed for sale. Few details about its interior finishes have been disclosed.

The purchase gives Johnson, the former US ambassador to Britain, a new foothold in Manhattan’s ultra-exclusive co-op market more than a decade after he sold his duplex at nearby 834 Fifth Ave. to billionaire Leonard Blavatnik for $77.5 million.

Johnson also owns a sprawling estate in Bedminster, NJ, which he originally purchased for $3.3 million in 1984, as well as a Palm Beach, Fla., residence acquired in 2020 for approximately $33 million.

The limestone building at 740 Park Ave., developed around 1930 by real estate investor James T. Lee, the grandfather of Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis, contains roughly 30 apartments.

It has continued to command eye-popping prices despite softer demand for luxury Manhattan co-ops.

Despite unloading his latest Park Avenue property, Griffin isn’t abandoning the prestigious address.
