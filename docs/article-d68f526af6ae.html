Nvidia hires major New York law firm to investigate Jensen Huang affair

2026-11-10 21:06

Nvidia has hired New York law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to investigate the affair between its chief executive and an alleged Chinese spy.

Jensen Huang, a co-founder of Nvidia who has led it to become one of the most valuable companies in the world, admitted to having a romantic relationship with a woman outside of his marriage.

The woman, Alice Huang, is alleged to be a spy for Beijing. She was an executive at a Singaporean company Megaspeed, which has been a customer of Nvidia.

Nvidia said on Tuesday that Wachtell attorney Joon Kim was set to lead "a thorough and independent investigation" into Jensen Huang's affair. The FBI is also conducting its own probe.

Nvidia is widely considered one of the most important companies behind the US artificial intelligence (AI) boom given it is the sole manufacturer of a crucial computer chip required for successfully operating AI tools.

Shares in the company fell to a near-yearly low on Tuesday, wiping more than $500bn (£375bn) off Nvidia's stock market value since Friday, even as tech stocks have recovered since last week's sell-off.

Jensen Huang's relationship with an executive of an Nvidia customer, who is also believed by the US government to be spying for China, has business and national security implications.

According to the New York Times, Secretary of State Marco Rubio sent a memo to top officials in Washington in spring of this year warning that the Nvidia boss was in a close relationship with a Chinese spy.

Beijing has rejected claims that Jensen Huang has been targeted, with its foreign ministry saying the allegations were "intended to smear and malign China".

US President Donald Trump said Monday that he had spoken to Jensen Huang and that the Nvidia boss told him "I had no idea she was a spy." Trump said he believed the him.

When announcing on Sunday that it would investigate the affair, Nvidia's board said it had no reason to believe the company's secrets or national security were compromised.

However, the relationship has drawn political attention. Congressional leaders of the House Committee on Foreign Affairs have asked Jensen Huang to appear next week for a closed door briefing.

The Nvidia boss has yet to say whether he will attend, a person familiar with the committee's demand told the BBC.

Committee chair Republican Brian Mast said he would subpoena Jensen Huang, effectively forcing him to appear before the committee, if necessary.

Joon Kim, who will lead Nvidia's investigation, is a former US Attorney for New York's Southern District and spent several years working as a government attorney focused on fraud and other criminal acts.

Wachtell is one of the largest law firms in the US and has several notable clients in the tech industry. Among them are OpenAI and Salesforce.

The investigation is expected to be treated as urgent, according to sources familiar with the sentiment inside Nvidia. It is unclear, however, if Wachtell will be able to interview Alice Huang, who is also known as Huang Le, and is not related to Jensen Huang.

Jensen Huang's public image is tightly intertwined with Nvidia. He has become a key public figure in the devleopment on AI, arguing the technology is a "new industrial revolution" and is on par with the advent of electricity.

A spokesperson for Nvidia declined to comment on Tuesday when asked if Jensen Huang had retained any additional of his own legal representation amid the company investigation and the separate one launched by the FBI.
