Jeffries brushes off opposition from far-left Dems who voted no: 'We’re going to work together'

2026-11-18 19:35

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., brushed off opposition from far-left Democrats during Wednesday's caucus vote, which overwhelmingly nominated him to become the next House speaker.

Speaking after the vote, Jeffries said he expects the incoming Democratic majority to "work together."

Only 11 Democrats voted against Jeffries' nomination. Among those who opposed him — or intended to — were Reps.-elect Darializa Avila Chevalier, Adam Hamawy, Frederick D. Haynes III, Melat Kiros, Donavan McKinney, Chris Rabb and Claire Valdez.

In a joint statement, the group noted that discussions with Jeffries regarding their legislative priorities remain "ongoing."

"Whether on Medicare-for-all, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these issues," the incoming lawmakers said in their statement. "We have worked to secure commitments from Leader Jeffries in good-faith conversations."

When asked about the dissenting votes, Jeffries said the caucus' election results spoke for themselves.

He declined to say whether he would adopt the far left's policy demands, which include abolishing U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and ending military aid to Israel.

"It's an honor to be serving with a majority of 260 members from all across the country, people who have different perspectives, and we embrace that," Jeffries said.

"But we're going to work together to find the highest common denominator in order to make life better for the American people. That's our commitment."

Jeffries emphasized that the incoming Congress' primary focus should be to "fight MAGA extremism, not each other."

"To fight MAGA extremism and to get big things done for the American people," he added. "And that is what we are going to accomplish together, beginning on Jan. 3."

Rep.-elect Frederick D. Haynes III, who will succeed Rep. Jasmine Crockett, D-Texas, explained that he woke up unexpectedly ill Wednesday morning and was unable to vote.

"He attempted to make it to the caucus meeting in time to cast his ballot but ultimately narrowly missed the vote," his office said in a statement.

"His intended vote was no, as per the released statement, and he looks forward to a quick recovery and continuing to work with his colleagues and Leader Jeffries to win the agenda Americans gave Democrats the mandate to deliver: Medicare-for-all, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics and ending the genocide in Gaza."
