Inside the low-security prison where Russell Brand is serving 10-year rape sentence: Yoga, bowling and more

2026-11-15 17:59

Russell Brand has reportedly been moved to a low-security prison, with access to his own cell, yoga, bowling and more, after he was sentenced to more than 10 years in prison for rape and sexual assault.

The actor was transferred to HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, England, on Friday, which primarily holds an estimated 1,200 male sexual offenders, the Sun reported Saturday.

An insider told the outlet that Brand “looked like he was s–tting himself” as guards escorted him to a single-person cell.

“He was heard asking for blankets on his first night, and he’s had extra officers looking after him since he arrived,” the source said, adding that the “Forgetting Sarah Marshall” star, 51, has yet to interact with the other prisoners.

Per the outlet, Littlehey is a safer prison for Brand than the high-security HMP Wakefield facility he was reportedly almost transferred to.

Littlehey offers Brand the choice to participate in a football league, board games, origami classes and choir sessions. The prison also features indoor bowling, two gyms and a few sports halls.

The “Get Him to the Greek” actor’s wife, Laura, 39, can also visit him once a week, but conjugal visits are prohibited. The couple shares three kids.

Former Metropolitan Police detective Mick Neville told the Sun that Brand was likely transferred to Littlehey because Wakefield, which is dubbed a “Monster Mansion” prison, was too unsafe.

“People will see Littlehey as Brand given a soft option,” Neville explained. “People would expect him to spend time in a jail such as Monster Mansion after the appalling things he did.”

“But the Prison Service have clearly decided that putting him into Monster Mansion would be a real risk to him and soak up massive resources to keep him safe,” Neville continued, saying that Brand would’ve likely been “at risk” following the murder of Ian Watkins at Wakefield in 2025.

Brand remains behind bars after he was sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison on Nov. 9, following a nearly four-week trial, which saw him being convicted of rape and sexual assault.

The “Rock of Ages” star’s conviction stems from incidents in the 2000s.

Throughout the trial, Brand denied any wrongdoing.

Last Thursday, six additional women accused the former comedian of sexual misconduct, with the alleged incidents occurring around the same time as the prior misconduct he was convicted of.

A rep for Brand has yet to address the new allegations.
