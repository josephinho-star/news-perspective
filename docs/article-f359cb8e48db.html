Bullying mayor ousted as Japan’s voters reject political abuse

2026-09-01 00:00

The fall of Yokohama’s mayor suggests the end of an era for local Japanese leaders treating staff and public funds with contempt

Takeharu Yamanaka reportedly called his staff “human trash” and told a senior official to “commit seppuku” (ritual suicide) if the city failed to win a bid for an international conference.

He stepped down on Friday after a prolonged investigation found he had repeatedly verbally abused city employees and humiliated subordinates.

“Public attitudes have changed a lot in recent years,” said Hiromi Murakami, a political-science professor at Temple University’s Tokyo branch.

“This sort of behaviour has been going on for years in local governments, companies and organisations in Japan but there is far greater awareness about bullying now and people are less willing to tolerate it.”
