Turkey, 7 other countries condemn Israeli ministers’ Gaza expulsion plans

2026-09-07 09:02

Turkey and seven other Arab and Muslim-majority countries have condemned proposals by two Israeli government ministers to expel Palestinians from the Gaza Strip, warning that they threaten a US-backed peace plan for the territory.

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said Wednesday that Israel had drawn up plans to remove Palestinians from Gaza but was waiting for US approval and for countries willing to receive the displaced population.

A day later, National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir outlined a proposal to remove 250,000 Palestinians from Gaza in the first year, followed by the rest of the territory’s roughly 2 million residents over the next six years.

The foreign ministers of Turkey, Egypt, Indonesia, Jordan, Pakistan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates said in a joint statement on Sunday that they condemned “in the strongest terms” the plans and mechanisms proposed by Katz and Ben-Gvir to forcibly remove Palestinians from Gaza.

The ministers said the proposals violated international law and posed a direct threat to the “legitimate and inalienable rights” of the Palestinian people.

“Such measures directly challenge international efforts to achieve peace, particularly President Donald Trump’s Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict, which includes a firm rejection of forced displacement,” the statement said, referring to the US-backed peace plan agreed in October 2025.

Although Israeli officials have described the proposed departures as voluntary, the eight countries rejected attempts to displace Palestinians either within or outside the occupied Palestinian territories, regardless of the justification given.

The ministers said Gaza was an integral part of the occupied Palestinian territories and called for the territorial unity of Gaza and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, to be preserved.

They also urged the international community, particularly the UN Security Council, to oppose attempts to create a new demographic or geographic reality in the Palestinian territories.

The statement reiterated the countries’ support for a two-state solution involving the establishment of an independent Palestinian state along the borders that existed before the 1967 Arab-Israeli war, with East Jerusalem as its capital.

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee, a strong supporter of Israel and Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, denied Saturday that Israel had a plan to forcibly remove Palestinians from Gaza, saying options would be considered only for people who wanted to leave voluntarily.

Turkey has emerged as one of Israel’s most outspoken critics over the Gaza war. Ankara has accused the government of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of committing genocide, an allegation Israel denies, and joined South Africa’s genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice in 2024.

Turkey also halted direct trade with Israel in May 2024 and later closed its airspace to Israeli government aircraft and restricted the transit of weapons bound for Israel.

The war began with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on October 7, 2023, which killed about 1,200 people and resulted in roughly 250 people being taken hostage. Israel’s subsequent military campaign has killed more than 73,600 Palestinians, according to Gaza health officials, and devastated much of the territory.
