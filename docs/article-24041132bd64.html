Netanyahu orders ICC case preparation against Erdoğan over Kurds, Hamas: report

2026-09-27 19:57

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has ordered officials to prepare a possible International Criminal Court (ICC) case against Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan over alleged abuses against Kurds and claims that Ankara funds Hamas, Israeli news broadcaster i24NEWS reported Sunday.

According to i24NEWS, Netanyahu asked an interministerial team and the director of the government’s international law department to prepare the material.

The broadcaster said Israel might seek to submit it through another country and that officials hoped the prospect of a case would deter further moves by Ankara.

It cited unnamed sources; Netanyahu’s office and Israel’s Justice Ministry declined to comment. No ICC filing has been reported.

The report follows a sharp exchange at the UN General Assembly. In his speech Thursday, Netanyahu called Erdoğan a “tyrant” and said he hosted Hamas leaders. Turkish presidential communications director Burhanettin Duran rejected the remarks as “baseless accusations,” saying they could not divert attention from Israel’s actions in Gaza or the legal proceedings involving Netanyahu.

Erdoğan has repeatedly accused Netanyahu of committing genocide in Gaza and compared him to Adolf Hitler. The leaders exchanged similar accusations in June, when Netanyahu alleged that Erdoğan had committed genocide against Kurds.

Israel’s military campaign in Gaza, launched after the Hamas-led October 7, 2023, attack, has killed more than 74,000 Palestinians, according to Gaza’s Health Ministry and caused widespread destruction and displacement.

Erdoğan received a delegation led by a senior Hamas figure at the presidential complex in January 2025. Turkey does not designate Hamas as a terrorist organization, while the United States and European Union do.

Erdoğan has said he does not regard Hamas as a terrorist organization, describing it instead as “a resistance group.”

A possible ICC case would face a significant jurisdictional hurdle. Neither Turkey nor Israel is a party to the court’s founding treaty. Under the Rome Statute, submission by another country would not, by itself, give the court jurisdiction over alleged crimes committed in Turkey by a Turkish national.

The ICC issued an arrest warrant for Netanyahu in November 2024 over alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity in Gaza. Separately, Turkey has intervened in South Africa’s genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice (ICJ).

The ICC proceedings concern Netanyahu’s individual criminal responsibility; the ICJ case concerns the state of Israel.

In August, Turkey began seeking an INTERPOL Red Notice for Netanyahu in a Turkish criminal case concerning Israel’s interception of a Gaza-bound aid flotilla. There has been no confirmation that INTERPOL issued the notice. A Red Notice is a request to locate and provisionally arrest a person, not an international arrest warrant.

Netanyahu’s allegations about Kurds come as Turkey pursues a renewed peace process with the outlawed Kurdistan Workers’ Party (PKK). Parliament passed its first law under the initiative in August. Disputes over Kurdish political rights remain: the Council of Europe has urged Turkey to release jailed former leaders of a pro-Kurdish party in accordance with European Court of Human Rights rulings.

The confrontation comes a month before Israel’s October 27 parliamentary election, in which Netanyahu is seeking another term. His UN speech attacked Iran, Hamas and foreign critics of Israel as well as Erdoğan.

Turkey’s presidency has accused Netanyahu’s government of portraying Turkey as a threat for electoral gain. His Likud party also featured Erdoğan alongside other figures on a campaign billboard warning voters against a Netanyahu defeat.
