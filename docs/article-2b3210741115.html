Eagles erase 21-point deficit to stun Cowboys with biggest Thanksgiving comeback in NFL history

2026-11-27 02:13

Look, if you saw the 3-7 Philadelphia Eagles visiting the 5-6 Dallas Cowboys and figured it was time for your Thanksgiving nap, I get it. The NFL stuck them between Bears-Lions and Chiefs-Bills. When Dallas went up 31-10 late in the third quarter, it looked like you’d timed it perfectly.

Hopefully, someone in your family had the decency to wake you up for the fourth quarter. The Eagles took their first lead Thursday with two seconds left, when Jake Elliott drilled the game-winner from the Cowboys’ star at midfield. Philly’s 39-38 win was the biggest comeback in NFL Thanksgiving history.

If you did sneak in a nap, the first half was the time to do it. By the time the Eagles and Cowboys kicked off, you’d already watched Caleb Williams hit Rome Odunze with three seconds left to give Chicago a 39-35 win. The Bears and Lions came in tied atop the NFC North, and Chicago left Detroit with a one-game lead.

Besides, you had to save some energy for Josh Allen vs. Patrick Mahomes on Thanksgiving night. The 9-1 Buffalo Bills were hosting the 8-2 Kansas City Chiefs in arguably the biggest game of the regular season so far.

Javonte Williams scores three touchdowns before halftime

Philly had already seen what this Cowboys offense could do. Dallas’ 38-21 win in Philadelphia last month kicked off a five-game stretch in which the Cowboys averaged 37.2 points per game. Two Dallas possessions into the rematch, they were already up 14-0.

Javonte Williams capped the 11-play, 77-yard opening drive with a 1-yard touchdown run. Then, on the ensuing Eagles possession, Quinnen Williams stuffed Saquon Barkley on fourth-and-1. Five plays later, KaVontae Turpin caught a short pass from Dak Prescott and sprinted up the sideline for a 31-yard score.

The Eagles finally got on the board with a 48-yard Jake Elliott field goal on their third drive, but Dallas answered right back. Prescott hit CeeDee Lamb for gains of 10 and 19 yards before Javonte Williams ran it in from the 12 for his second touchdown.

Eagles quarterback Joe Milton found DeVonta Smith on a 50-yard bomb to cut the deficit to 21-10 with 4:06 left in the first half. Dallas answered again, and the officials got plenty of screen time.

Three Eagles penalties gave the Cowboys 44 yards, including back-to-back pass interference calls that moved Dallas from the Philly 40 to the 1. After two Eagles stops, an offensive pass interference call on CeeDee Lamb wiped out a touchdown pass to Brevyn Spann-Ford and made it third-and-goal from the 12. Javonte Williams caught Prescott’s pass at the 8, dragged an Eagles defender to the goal line and fell across it for his third touchdown of the half.

By halftime, it looked a lot like Sunday against Tennessee. For the second time in five days, the Cowboys scored four first-half touchdowns and took an 18-point lead into the locker room. They led the Titans 27-9 and the Eagles 28-10.

Eagles roar back in the second half

For most of the third quarter, it looked like the second half would go the same way, too. Tennessee never got closer than 11 after halftime, and Brandon Aubrey drilled a 59-yard field goal to put Dallas up 31-10 with 3:35 left in the third. Then came two plays worth waking up for.

Milton found Smith for another bomb, this one a 45-yard touchdown with 15 seconds left in the quarter. Just 89 seconds of game time later, Ty Robinson blocked Bryan Anger’s punt, and Kenny Moore II scooped it up at the Dallas 3 and ran it in. Suddenly, it was 31-24 with nearly the entire fourth quarter left.

Prescott answered by hitting Lamb for 39 yards on second-and-16 and another 12 on third-and-8 before scrambling for a 14-yard touchdown himself. That put Dallas up 38-24 with 10:11 left. The two-touchdown cushion lasted all of 62 seconds.

Britain Covey returned the kickoff 55 yards, Milton found Smith twice, and then the Eagles quarterback ran one in himself from the 15. The Eagles went for two after cutting the deficit to eight, but Milton’s pass to Zach Ertz fell incomplete.

Philly forced a three-and-out, and Covey returned the punt 23 yards. With 7:37 left, the Eagles had the ball at the Dallas 49 with a chance to tie.

But DeMarvion Overshown sacked Milton on second down, and on third-and-15, Cobie Durant picked off a pass intended for Makai Lemon and returned it to the Eagles’ 48.

Dallas drove to the Philly 22, where Aubrey had a chance to make it a two-score game. Naturally, the guy who drilled one from 59 in the third quarter pulled this one wide left from 40. With 3:27 left, the Eagles still trailed 38-30 and had another shot to tie.

Milton took the Eagles 70 yards in four throws, the last a 24-yard touchdown to Makai Lemon with 2:10 left. He went right back to Lemon for the tie. This time the pass fell incomplete, and Dallas still led 38-36.

Because Philadelphia had all three timeouts and the two-minute warning, the Dallas offense needed two first downs to seal the victory. They got the first on a 15-yard strike from Prescott to Brevyn Spann-Ford on a second-and-8 play.

But they couldn't get the second. Javonte Williams had three straight carries to gain nine yards, leaving the Cowboys with a fourth-and-1 on the Eagles' 41-yard line and 55 seconds remaining. Instead of attempting a long field goal or punting, the Cowboys elected to go for a first down and the win.

Ty Robinson, who’d already blocked a punt, sacked Prescott on fourth-and-1. The Eagles got the ball back near midfield with 50 seconds left.

With seven seconds left and the ball at the Dallas 41, Philly sent Elliott out to win it.

He drilled it.

Hopefully everyone was awake to see it.
