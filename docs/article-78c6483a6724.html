Malaysia seeks to resettle 10,000 Rohingya refugees in Turkey

2026-11-20 09:37

Malaysia’s cabinet has approved a proposal to resettle 10,000 Rohingya refugees in Turkey through a vocational training program, Deputy Prime Minister Ahmad Zahid Hamidi said Friday.

The announcement came a day after the International Court of Justice, the United Nations’ top court, ruled that Myanmar had committed genocide against the Rohingya, a persecuted, predominantly Muslim minority.

“Following my recent official working visit to the Republic of Turkiye, the Cabinet has agreed to a proposal to resettle 10,000 Rohingya refugees from Malaysia to Turkiye through a structured skills training program,” Ahmad Zahid said.

“I put forward this proposal as part of the government’s efforts to address the situation of Rohingya refugees in Malaysia in a more coordinated, effective and humane manner,” he said.

Under the proposal, the refugees would undergo three to six months of technical and vocational training in Turkey. The program would focus on practical skills sought by employers across several industries, Ahmad Zahid said.

Malaysia in late September sent back around 1,500 Myanmar nationals as it launched a scheme that rights groups say may endanger migrants by returning them to the war-torn country.

The Malaysian government said those returned had been held in detention centers and had agreed to go back voluntarily. The returns came amid rising anti-migrant sentiment in Malaysia.

In October, the government said it would seek clarification from Myanmar authorities over reports that hundreds of migrants had been detained after their return.

The Rohingya account for two-thirds of the 190,000 refugees and asylum seekers from Myanmar in Malaysia, according to the United Nations.
