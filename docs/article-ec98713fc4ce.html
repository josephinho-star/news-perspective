Aaron Judge’s imminent return is everything the Yankees have been waiting for

2026-09-07 23:55

Aaron Judge is primed to return to the Yankees as early as Tuesday against the Rockies in The Bronx — Aaron Boone did not rule it out — though it could be Wednesday or Thursday if they choose to give him a few more days of ramping up with facing live pitching and the Trajekt machine. Neither of those was available to him over the last week as he joined the team on its West Coast road trip to check some of the final boxes in his rehab.

But the long-awaited day is nearly here.

“Best player in the world,” manager Aaron Boone said over the weekend, summing up the potential impact of his captain’s imminent return.

The back-to-back AL MVP will jump right back into a playoff race. The Yankees trail the Rays by four games for the division — and are two games clear of the Red Sox for the top AL wild card — and survived his absence, entering Tuesday 45-39 since he last played despite dealing with other injuries during that span.

“We’re still in it,” Max Fried said Sunday. “Obviously we’d love to be playing better, [and] we’re motivated to keep going and fight to try to win the division. But anytime you get your best player back, it’s obviously a huge boost.”

On Monday’s off-day, Judge was set to face live pitching for the first time in his ramp-up, after spending the last week taking on-field batting practice, hitting off high-velocity pitching machines and standing in on bullpen sessions to track pitches.

All of that, plus having the Trajekt machine at his disposal back at Yankee Stadium, is likely to allow Judge to skip a rehab assignment in favor of being activated sooner, despite the lengthy absence. Because of that, the Yankees may ease him in over the next few days as he finishes off his buildup. That could mean a day off after his first game, playing five to seven innings in the field and/or serving as the DH.

But the Yankees will welcome him back in any form with open arms, after their lineup spent a large chunk of his IL stint struggling before finally starting to break out of it over the last few weeks.

“He’s our captain, best player in baseball,” Fried said. “To have him back in the lineup is obviously a huge boost. We’re definitely a better team when he’s in the lineup.”

The Yankees opened a spot on the active roster Sunday night, when they optioned Elmer Rodríguez to Triple-A, though it was unclear if it was for Judge. They will still need to open a spot on the 40-man roster to activate Judge off the 60-day IL (plus Clarke Schmidt at some point in the next week or two), which would make it surprising if they replaced Rodríguez with a pitcher who is not currently on the 40-man roster (i.e. Eli Morgan). But of their healthy 40-man pitchers in the minors, only Brendan Beck is eligible to be recalled Tuesday, which added to the Judge intrigue.

The last game Judge played was May 31, when the Yankees beat the Athletics to improve to 36-23. They started 10-5 without him before sputtering during a 4-14 stretch into July. But they began to break out of the malaise from there, even as they were still not scoring much, as they leaned on the strength of their pitching staff to weather the storm.

During that time, they have also withstood injuries to Cody Bellinger, Trent Grisham (twice), Austin Wells, Ryan McMahon, Max Fried, Carlos Rodón, Ryan Weathers and more, plus multiple setbacks for Giancarlo Stanton that have kept him out since late April.

From June 1 through Sunday, the Yankees lineup ranked 26th in runs per game (4.06, compared to the 5.17 they were scoring with him); 29th in batting average (.227, compared to .243 with him); 27th in OPS (.681, compared to .770 with him); and 29th in WRC+ (90, compared to 116 with him).

But now, they are on the verge of reintegrating one of the game’s most fearsome hitters at a critical time.

“I feel like we’ve played pretty well here over the last several weeks,” Boone said. “Certainly we’ve had long stretches where the offense has struggled, and I think a big part of that is not having your guy. But I also feel like that’s ticked up here in the last couple weeks where we’ve really started to play better offensively. Certainly look forward to getting our guy back.”
