William Shatner, 95, hits back at ‘Halloween’ director’s ‘piece of s–t’ criticism

2026-11-27 23:53

William Shatner and “Halloween” director John Carpenter are trading barbs over a run-in that apparently ended in horror.

Speaking to the “Dropping Names” podcast on Nov. 18, Carpenter, 78, alleged that he was once rudely snubbed by Shatner, 95, when he attempted to say hello in Orlando, Florida.

“This guy, he’s sitting in the green room, on his phone,” Carpenter recalled. “And I just thought, ‘Well, I’m going to go in and introduce myself.’ I said, ‘Hello, Mr. Shatner, I’m John Carpenter.’”

Per Carpenter, Shatner barely acknowledged him, simply saying “Pleasure” with no further engagement.

“Did not look up from the phone,” Carpenter claimed. “This piece of s–t did not look up from the phone. He’s an a–hole.”

Shatner apparently caught wind of this story, as he posted a politely snarky apology to Instagram on Friday, saying he is living “in infamy” for not recognizing the director or giving him due attention.

“I don’t know why I didn’t look up, but obviously I didn’t hear you. I was probably reading a great review of one of your movies and it occupied my attention to the point where I didn’t look up,” he wrote.

Shatner added that Carpenter is “obviously one of the great directors in film” and that he “would have relished the chance to talk” to him. He urged the filmmaker to approach him next time and make an introduction, because “I really don’t know what you look like and apparently you know what I look like.”

“This piece of s–t would confide in you my admiration,” the “Star Trek” icon concluded.

Carpenter, apparently not about to be outdone, responded with his own carefully worded post shortly after. “I said hello to you and introduced myself to you in Orlando,” he wrote. “You would not look me in the eye. Maybe it’s a Canadian thing.”

The director noted that he would indeed reintroduce himself at the next convention, adding, “After all, even Captain Kirk deserves a second chance at first contact.”

Fans of the two film staples left amused comments on both posts. “Never meet your heroes,” one joked.

Carpenter and Shatner share an unlikely Hollywood connection: Shatner’s likeness famously served as the basis for the iconic Michael Myers mask in Carpenter’s 1978 horror classic, “Halloween.”
