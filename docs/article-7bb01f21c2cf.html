LA homeowners slam city over unmaintained streets and blocked drains after ‘tidal wave’ of floodwater swamps houses

2026-11-23 23:02

Sherman Oaks residents are bracing for increased rainfall during El Niño after a debris-choked storm drain sent water pouring into their alley last week.

An intense video of the flood shows one woman running from her house to warn a neighbor as water surged toward the properties.

Susan Acacio, who lives on the street, told CBS News the runoff rushed down the alley toward a drain at its far end.

“The water just came barreling down our alley here, and went down to the drain at the end of the alley, and because the streets hadn’t been maintained here, debris piled up and clogged the drain and the water started backing up,” she said.

Neighbor Courtnee Draper said she was caught off guard when she opened her garage.

“I opened my garage, and it was just like a mini tidal wave of water and all my belongings, my kids’ artwork, just everything was flowing out onto the street. I was running after things,” she told CBS News.

Draper, a single mother of three, was driving her kids to school Nov. 19 when the flood and a mudslide hit her home. She said water ran calf-deep across the entire first floor.

The slide also loosened the hillside behind the house, which has left her worried about the next storm. Making the property safe again is expected to cost more than $100,000.

Draper has worked nearly 10 years in the Los Angeles County District Attorney’s Office as an advocate for crime victims. For the past four, she has focused on survivors of sexual assault and domestic violence.

Her friend Christina Fontana started a GoFundMe campaign to help the family pay for repairs.

“Money never mattered to her. Helping people in need did. And now I’m hoping to help her get the support she needs to get her family through this traumatic and extremely difficult time,” Fontana said in a statement on the fundraiser page.

Fontana added that Draper is uneasy about asking for help. She urged people to give what they can, even a few dollars, and asked anyone who knows a reliable remediation contractor to pass along a name.

Residents say they contacted city services within hours of the rainfall but are still waiting, CBS News reported.

Acacio said the leftover mess has made the alley hazardous. “We’re slipping and sliding, and it’s very dangerous,” she added.
