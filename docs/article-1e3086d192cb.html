10 missing after Turkish cargo ship sinks in collision off İstanbul

2026-09-02 08:09

A Turkish cargo ship sank in the Sea of Marmara near İstanbul after colliding with an empty oil tanker early Wednesday, leaving its 10 crew members missing, according to Turkish officials.

The Tuğberk İmamoğlu, which was carrying rolled steel, collided with the ALSU tanker before sinking rapidly, the Transport Ministry’s Directorate General of Maritime Affairs said on X.

“Due to the Tuğberk İmamoğlu not responding to any calls and the crew not answering their mobile phones … it is believed the cargo ship has sunk,” the directorate said.

The collision occurred about 18 nautical miles off Silivri, on the western edge of İstanbul province.

Transport Minister Abdulkadir Uraloğlu told reporters that the cargo ship appeared to have sunk within 11 minutes.

“We received the emergency signal at 3:26 a.m. If we accept the captain’s reported collision time of 3:15, we can say the sinking occurred very rapidly, within just 11 minutes,” he said.

The signal was transmitted by the ship’s emergency position-indicating radio beacon (EPIRB) through Turkey’s satellite-assisted search and rescue system.

“We spotted life buoys and a lifeboat, but it was empty. Unfortunately, no survivors have been found so far,” Uraloğlu said, adding that search and rescue operations were continuing.

The ship was carrying 10 people — the captain and nine other crew members — all of them Turkish nationals, he said.

The vessel sank in an area where the water is approximately 900 meters (2,950 feet) deep. Fourteen search and rescue vessels and a helicopter were initially deployed, according to the maritime directorate.

“Life-saving equipment, including a lifeboat belonging to the Tuğberk İmamoğlu, which had 10 crew members on board, has been found at sea. Unfortunately, the crew members were not inside,” it said.

No visible damage was detected on the ALSU, which was also participating in the search operation.

The cause of the collision was not immediately known.

Maritime engineer Sabri Gökhan told the private NTV broadcaster that human error was one possibility.

“The Sea of Marmara is a heavily trafficked and closely monitored area. It has all the characteristics of a maritime highway,” he said.

“Ships don’t sink easily there. It was likely a mistake by the crew,” he added.

There has been no official determination of responsibility for the collision.

According to the VesselFinder tracking website, the Tuğberk İmamoğlu was a Turkish-flagged cargo ship built in 1990 and measuring 90 meters (295 feet) in length.

It was traveling from İskenderun to Karadeniz Ereğli with 4,199 tons of rolled sheet steel and rebar when the collision occurred. The ALSU was sailing empty from Kocaeli to Aliağa.

The maritime directorate initially said the ALSU was carrying 3,000 tons of motor oil but later corrected the information, saying the tanker was empty, a detail also confirmed by the İstanbul Governor’s Office.

The accident occurred three days after a passenger ferry capsized and sank off northern Cyprus, killing eight Turkish nationals. Search teams are still looking for 20 people listed as missing.

In September 2008 the Hayat N ro-ro ferry sank shortly after leaving Bandırma on the southern coast of the Sea of Marmara, killing one person and leaving four others missing at the time.
