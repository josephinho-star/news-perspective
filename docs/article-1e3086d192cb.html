Cargo ship sinks off İstanbul after collision, 10 crew missing

2026-09-02 08:09

A cargo ship carrying 10 crew members sank in the Sea of Marmara near İstanbul early Wednesday after colliding with an oil tanker, prompting a large-scale search and rescue operation, according to Turkish officials.

The accident occurred before dawn when the Tuğberk İmamoğlu, which was carrying 4,200 tons of rolled sheet metal, collided with the ALSU, an empty oil tanker, according to the Transport Ministry’s Directorate General of Maritime Affairs.

The collision took place about 18 nautical miles off Silivri, on the western edge of İstanbul province, the İstanbul Governor’s Office said.

The captain of the ALSU reported the collision at 3:15 a.m. Search and rescue teams were unable to contact the Tuğberk İmamoğlu or any of its crew, leading officials to conclude that the vessel had sunk.

Fourteen search and rescue vessels and a helicopter were dispatched to the area.

“Life-saving equipment, including a lifeboat belonging to the Tuğberk İmamoğlu, which had 10 crew members on board, has been found at sea. Unfortunately, the crew members were not inside,” the maritime directorate said.

The ALSU was also taking part in the search for the missing crew.

Officials initially said the tanker was carrying 3,000 tons of motor oil but later corrected the statement, saying it was empty at the time of the collision.

The accident came three days after a passenger ferry capsized and sank off northern Cyprus, killing eight people. Search teams are still looking for 20 others listed as missing.
