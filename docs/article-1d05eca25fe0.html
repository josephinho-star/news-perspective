Canadian singer Joni Mitchell officially becomes American citizen after 62-year journey

2026-10-15 01:04

Joni Mitchell has officially become an American citizen at age 82, more than six decades after she first moved to the United States.

The music icon, who hails from Canada, announced the news on Instagram, sharing a photo of herself beaming while holding an American flag along with a video from her naturalization ceremony.

"A journey that began 62 years ago," Mitchell wrote in the caption of her post.

Mitchell wore a red and navy blue floral cardigan that had an American flag pin along with a red beaded necklace, a red beret and black pants with black boots while posing in between an American flag and a Department of Homeland Security flag.

In the video Mitchell uploaded, she was seated in the front row at the ceremony as the speaker announced, "And Joni Mitchell, Canada" to cheers and applause from the audience.

According to KSL, Mitchell will also retain her Canadian citizenship because the U.S. and Canada legally permit dual citizenship.

The legendary singer-songwriter was born Roberta Joan Anderson in Fort Macleod, Alberta, to Royal Canadian Air Force flight Lt. William Andrew Anderson and teacher Myrtle Marguerite.

Mitchell's family lived on several bases during World War II before eventually settling in Saskatoon, which the singer has often referred to as her hometown. She went on to attend the Alberta College of Art in Calgary before dropping out and relocating to Toronto, where she played gigs at coffee houses and folk clubs.

The "Both Sides Now" songstress moved to the United States in 1965 at age 21 after marrying American folk singer Chuck Mitchell. The former couple, who lived in Detroit, Michigan, performed together as a folk duo before divorcing in 1967.

After their divorce, Mitchell left Detroit and moved to New York City, where she became part of the Greenwich Village folk scene and performed along the East Coast.

By 1968, Mitchell moved to Los Angeles, settling in Laurel Canyon, which was becoming a hub for singer-songwriters including David Crosby, Graham Nash and others. In the mid-1970s, Mitchell moved to LA's Bel Air neighborhood, where she lived in a historic 1920s home for decades.

Mitchell built her legendary music career while living in California, where she recorded many of her most acclaimed albums, including "Blue," "Court and Spark" and "Ladies of the Canyon."

Mitchell has won 11 Grammy Awards and was inducted into both the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame and the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1997.

She was also recognized with the Kennedy Center Honors in 2021 and received the prestigious Library of Congress Gershwin Prize for Popular Song in 2023.

The musician lives in Santa Barbara, California.

Mitchell is an outspoken critic of President Donald Trump and expressed frustration that she couldn't vote in U.S. elections because she was Canadian.

During a performance at the Hollywood Bowl in LA in October 2024, Mitchell slammed Trump while performing her 1985 song "Dog Eat Dog," according to Billboard.

After singing a lyric about wealthy financiers, she added, "Like Donald Trump!"

Following her performance of the song, Mitchell responded to an audience member who shouted an anti-Trump remark.

"'F--- Donald Trump.' I love that song," she said, apparently referencing rapper YG's 2016 protest song "FDT (F--- Donald Trump)" featuring the late rapper and activist Nipsey Hussle.

She then encouraged Americans to participate in the 2024 U.S. presidential election, in which Trump ran against Democratic nominee former Vice President Kamala Harris.

"Everybody get out and vote. This is an important one. I wish I could vote . I'm Canadian. I'm one of those lousy immigrants," Mitchell said.

Months before becoming an American citizen, Mitchell criticized the political climate in the U.S. while accepting a lifetime achievement award at Canada's Juno Awards in March.

At the ceremony, Mitchell praised Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, saying, "I'm so glad to be back in Canada. This man is a blessing. You guys are so fortunate."

She also appeared to take a swipe at the Trump administration while contrasting Canada with the U.S.

"I'm living in the states, and you know what's happening there," she said.

After Mitchell's comments at the Juno Awards, the White House issued a dismissive response.

When asked about her remarks, White House spokesperson Davis Ingle told The Independent, "Who the hell is Joni Mitchell?"

Mitchell also appeared to express opposition to the Trump administration's immigration enforcement policies during the 2026 Grammy Awards.

While accepting the award for Best Historical Album, she wore an "ICE OUT" pin, joining other entertainers protesting U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
