Ukrainian intelligence says North Korea paused missile deliveries to Russia amid friction over South Korea

2026-11-26 19:10

North Korea has temporarily halted shipments of ballistic missiles and artillery shells to Russia following diplomatic friction between the two allies over Moscow's outreach to South Korea, Ukrainian military intelligence said Thursday.

According to Ukraine's GUR military intelligence agency, North Korean Foreign Minister Choe Son Hui announced the freeze on KN-23 ballistic missile and artillery deliveries last month after Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Andrey Rudenko suggested Moscow would help de-escalate tensions between Seoul and Pyongyang, Reuters reported.

Choe demanded that Russia clarify its policy toward South Korea, GUR said, though Reuters noted it could not independently verify the report or confirm public remarks from either official.

The reported pause also suspends Russian military flights over North Korea and stalls a Kremlin request to expand North Korea's troop deployment in Russia from roughly 8,500 to 50,000, according to the outlet.

Rudenko, who oversees Asian relations, said last month that Seoul had signaled interest in normalizing ties and resuming direct flights with Moscow once the Ukraine war ends.

Despite the reported freeze, North Korean troops remain active in Russia, with 11th Corps units serving in the Kursk region and a missile unit operating in Voronezh since mid-summer, GUR said. Russian President Vladimir Putin also hosted top Kim Jong Un aide Jo Yong Won at the Kremlin last week, where both men reportedly praised bilateral cooperation.

Meanwhile, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned on social media Thursday that Moscow is "completing preparations for a new massive strike" on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, logistics and critical services.

Crews are already working to restore power in six regions after overnight Russian missile and drone strikes damaged an apartment building, a hospital and businesses, according to Zelenskyy, who also renewed appeals for U.S.-made Patriot air defense interceptors.

In Washington, President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social Thursday that he urged Putin during talks in China to "pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe, in the hopes that a deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine, and made quickly."

U.S. envoy Jared Kushner, who is mediating talks with Steve Witkoff, said on the MD Meets podcast that Putin has "shown restraint" by agreeing not to use nuclear weapons, though negotiations remain deadlocked over Ukrainian territory.

Ukrainian forces also struck a Russian military airfield and an oil pumping facility overnight, while Russia claimed it intercepted 343 Ukrainian drones.

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
