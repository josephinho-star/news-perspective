The J.J. McCarthy improvement that could get Giants to the next level

2026-11-27 01:26

The deep balls were flying from the right arm of J.J. McCarthy, but the connections were tough to come by.

Deep to rookie Malachi Fields, incomplete.

Deep to Darnell Mooney, incomplete.

Another one deep to Fields, interception overturned upon review to an incomplete pass.

Deep to Mooney over the middle, incomplete.

Yet another deep shot to Mooney on third-and-17, not close, and some boos ensued from the crowd at MetLife Stadium.

“At the end of the day, I just missed them,” McCarthy said. “The big thing that kind of stood out to me was just put some more air on the ball and let the receivers be able to track it a little bit better, but that’s an easy fix and we’ll get it fixed.”

These down-the-field attempts from McCarthy were part of his 20 incomplete passes in the 23-18 upset victory over the Jaguars, showing that the Giants have to be really good, but not perfect, to beat a quality opponent. McCarthy has been a godsend since arriving in a trade with the Vikings — that 2027 fifth-round pick was draft capital well spent — and he takes a 3-1 record as the Giants starting quarterback into Sunday’s game with the Colts in Indianapolis.

Ever since his arrival Sept. 29, it has been a full-speed-ahead crash course for McCarthy, as far as learning a brand-new offense and trying to fast-forward his comfort with the unfamiliar players around him. Building chemistry with wide receivers and tight ends takes time, and that was not on McCarthy’s side.

The reputation for having a strong arm and a nice feel for the deep ball preceded McCarthy to his second NFL stop. That is the next bit of firepower to add to his arsenal as the Giants look to secure and build on their first-place lead in the NFC East.

“For me, personally, just having more time on task with my guys being able to hit the ones that are there, especially the deeper shots,” McCarthy said. “Just being able to get in here and work with the guys just constantly and just be able to get those reps over and over again.

“But I’m going to make those. Those are things that are going to come and have shown up, but sometimes you just miss. In this league, you can’t miss too many times. So just being able to be ready for that next opportunity and nail it.”

McCarthy has not pushed the ball into the end zone as often as the Giants would like. He has only two touchdown passes in his four starts. Some of this is happenstance — the Giants had four rushing touchdowns in McCarthy’s first start, a 27-21 overtime victory in Houston. Some of this is McCarthy at times still looking a bit uncertain within the offense, as far as going through his progressions.

Making the timely throw, and finding a way to win, though, are McCarthy hallmarks. That is how he went 27-1 at Michigan. His stats were often mid-level — the Wolverines were largely a running team during his time in Ann Arbor — but when a throw or a run was needed to keep a drive alive, McCarthy delivered with impressive regularity.

McCarthy was spraying the ball down the stretch against the Jaguars as the Giants fell into a second-half rut, with four consecutive possessions failing to pick up a first down. When it came time to close out the game or else hand it back over to Trevor Lawrence and the Jaguars offense, McCarthy came through with his most clutch throw. He wanted to find Nabers on the left side, but Nabers was shadowed by two defenders. No opening there. McCarthy instantly pivoted and fired a strike to Fields for a 19-yard completion. That alleviated a great deal of pressure. After two 5-yard runs from Cam Skattebo, McCarthy could kneel down three times to end it.

“That was my second read,’’ McCarthy said of the pass to Fields. “There are so many things that you could really look at from your perspective and everyone has — they’re entitled to their own perspective and their own opinion, but if you’re not really in the room and you don’t understand what the progressions are. Sometimes it’s zone-based or man-based coverage reads where it’s like, yeah, it might be your first progression, but it’s really the second in your thought process of what the defense has given you. It’s little things like that where you just can’t really focus on and just got to worry about doing your job as a quarterback, which is throwing to the open guy, and sometimes, a lot of the times, with good defenses, that is going through your reads.”

And, sometimes, those reads lead to deep throws down the field.

“Come back and make the next throw, and that’s a confident guy, and he’s been in a lot of huge games,” coach John Harbaugh said. “And we’re going to start hitting some of those deep passes. We wanted to take some shots, we wanted to go after them downfield and we did, and those are going to start happening. We’ll start making those plays for sure.”
