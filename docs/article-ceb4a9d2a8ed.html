The leaked Lenovo Legion Go Beam could be the affordable handheld we need

2026-11-18 19:11

For a while there, it seemed like Lenovo had given up on selling handheld gaming PCs, hiking the price of its Legion Go 2 and Legion Go S even higher than other RAM-starved competitors. A decent one can easily cost $1,000 if not $2,000 now. But it looks like Lenovo might be about to announce a relatively cheap handheld once again— the Legion Go Beam.

It looks comparatively cheap — and that might be a good thing in this economy.

It looks comparatively cheap — and that might be a good thing in this economy.

Windows Latest has leaked images of the handheld, and there’s no question it’s designed to be a value alternative to even the company’s least expensive Legion Go S variant. While it’s got the same big stippled grips and triggers for comfort, there’s only one USB-C port on the top instead of two, a comparatively cheap-looking D-pad, no touchpad, and only a single tiny intake and exhaust for its fan.

There’s no SteamOS logo where that would go, so it likely runs Windows instead… but then there’s that name, “Beam.” Perhaps you’ll “beam” your games to it because it’s a streaming and cloud gaming handheld, instead of one that primarily plays games on its own chip? Maybe that’s why it doesn’t need as much airflow as previous handhelds?

Perhaps there’s a partnership with Xbox Cloud Gaming specifically, and that’s why it’s all lit up in Xbox green?

But it might just be green because it looks nice with white, which has been the “value” color for handhelds so far (see: ROG Ally, Xbox Ally, and the Windows-based Legion Go S). And it might be “Beam” because there’s a literal beam of light on the bottom, even though the first thing I do with most handhelds is turn any and all distracting RGB lighting off. Or maybe it’s “Beam” just because it sounds like a friendly, fun, value-oriented name, instead of something “S” or “Pro.”

I won’t begin to speculate on the Legion Go Beam’s price, except to say that “cheap” may be relative. The Legion Go S was originally supposed to cost $499, but wound up costing far more except on the occasional sale. The Legion Go 2 was supposed to start at $1,099 but wound up costing $1,500 and up after RAMageddon. Streaming-first handhelds have been far cheaper, though, like the $249 Sony PlayStation Portal.

Lenovo has a history of announcing its new gaming devices at CES in January each year, including previous Legion Go handhelds, so I’m expecting to see an official announcement of the Lenovo Legion Go Beam then. Even if so, that doesn’t mean it’ll be available in January — there’s often a many-month lag.
