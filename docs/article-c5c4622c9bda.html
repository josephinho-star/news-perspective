Australian politics live: Blyth did not ‘feel safe’ in the Liberal party before One Nation defection; Canavan vows ‘more united’ comeback

2026-11-26 01:19

That AGM was where Alex Antic was elected the state party president, replacing Blyth who did not run but backed a different candidate.

I did not feel safe in the Liberal party, and I think it’s been well documented that private security was employed by the division for my own protection.

The energy minister has declared coal-fired power stations must be replaced, with the stations facing at least one breakdown everyday for three years.

Chris Bowen has told the House Australia’s coal power stations are about 40 years old, on average. He said:

While coal has played an important, a vital role empowering this nation, our coal fire power stations are ageing. … And as they become older, they become increasingly unreliable.

They must be replaced.

Bowen said coal power outages had occurred every day for the last three years and seen energy prices “skyrocket”.

When coal fire stations go down, your bills go up.

And there hasn’t been one day in the past three years – not one day, in three years – without a coal fired power station breaking down.

Australian homes have more solar panels than swimming pools, says Bowen

Chris Bowen is now addressing the House of Representatives with the annual climate change statement, talking up the “story of the clean energy transition in real life”.

Solar panel installation has sped up, he said.

There are now more solar panels on rooftops around the country than there are back yard swimming pools.

And newly installed rooftop solar capacity was 45% higher in the first three-quarters of this year than it was in the same period in 2025. So our transformation is gaining even more speed.

Bowen says the government’s cheaper home batteries program – at risk of being cut as Labor looks for budget savings – has helped households install more than 622,000 batteries, more than 75% of which were in outer suburban and regional areas.

That represents 17.35GWh of storage, increasing Australia’s home battery capacity by more than five times, Bowen said.

He is making the statement days after the climate negotiations broke down at Cop31 in Turkey, sharing leadership with Australia. The minister has said he was frustrated after many countries including the European Union refused to back a proposal that excluded mention of fossil fuels.

Nearly one in three 18- to 24-year-olds used alcohol at high-risk level in 2025, data shows

Gen Z are more likely than any other age group to use alcohol at high-risk levels, above national health guidelines, according to data published by the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare on Thursday.

More than 17,500 people aged 14 and over were asked about their use of and attitudes towards alcohol, illicit drugs, e-cigarettes and tobacco in the National Drug Strategy Household Survey, conducted every two to three years.

Almost one-third (31.9%) of people aged 18 to 24 used alcohol at a high-risk level in 2025.

The overall proportion of people drinking at levels that increase their risk of harm fell from 31% in 2022–2023 to 26%.

But women are increasingly experiencing harm from someone else’s alcohol use, with 3.2 million women reporting being verbally, physically or sexually assaulted or put in fear by someone who had used alcohol, an increase of 800,000 since the previous survey.

Foundation for Alcohol Research and Education CEO, Ayla Chorley,said:

Harm at this devastating scale should be a wake-up call for governments across Australia.

Almost 75% of respondents said they support limiting alcohol advertising online and on social media, and similar numbers support limiting alcohol advertising on TV until after 9:30pm.

Support also increased for banning alcohol sponsorship of sporting events, and for restricting where alcohol-branded zero-alcohol products can be displayed in stores.

The Cancer Council called on the federal government to adopt recommendations for a mandatory alcohol advertising framework.

Cannabis and cocaine remain the most commonly used illicit drugs by Australians, the report also found, followed by hallucinogens/psychedelics.

It looks like Labor will, after all, tick off everything on its Christmas list, with the Coalition announcing it won’t block the gas reservation scheme.

The Coalition will push for multiple amendments including greater support for the gas sector. Their biggest concern is that the designed oversupply of gas in the domestic market will deter new gas investment or send them out of business.

The shadow resources minister, Susan McDonald, told the Senate earlier:

We support a gas reservation, one that delivers cheaper energy for Australians, for manufacturing and more energy security. But we believe there are significant challenges with the legislation as it stands.

We will stand in the national interest, we will not oppose this legislation because we want affordable and reliable gas for Australians, more gas production and policies that encourage more investment.

In deciding not to oppose the bill, the Coalition has two options: they can either vote with the government or abstain. Labor has 30 seats; even though 39 are needed for a full majority, if the Coalition doesn’t vote at all, the government will still have more senators than the crossbench combined.

Blyth partly blames defection on Liberals supporting bill that One Nation also supported

During the press conference, new One Nation senator Leah Blyth explained that her defection from the Coalition was, in part, because the Liberals backed a controversial defence bill – but she quickly ended the press conference after it was pointed out that One Nation had also backed that bill.

Blyth, after numerous questions at the press conference about why she was shifting party allegiance, claimed she had been forced to “compromise” too many times on legislation.

But when pushed, she could only name a few examples – including the digital duty of care bill, which the Coalition has opposed from essentially day one.

Later in the press conference, Blyth pointed to the defence bases bill, which she noted would “hand sweeping powers to the defence minister … That was not up for debate, and I think that when we are handing government sweeping powers, there should be very robust debate on issues like that.”

But as we brought you earlier this week, One Nation also voted for that bill.

When we pointed that out during Blyth’s answer, the press conference was brought to a halt nearly immediately thereafter.

Later in her interview on News24, she admits she didn’t know that One Nation had supported the bill:

To be fair, I am not completely across all of the different policy positions that One Nation have … I didn’t pay attention to who else was in the chamber supporting that bill.

Blyth says she wasn’t promised a top spot on One Nation ticket

Blyth has gone straight from her press conference to the News24 studio, and says her defection is not about self-preservation.

Blyth had a major falling out with Liberal backbencher and SA state president, Alex Antic, and faced an unwinnable spot on the Senate ticket at the next election.

But she says she wasn’t promised the top spot on the One Nation ticket.

Blyth: There are no promises that have been made here. I am doing this because I believe it is what is best to get rid of the worst Labor government that Australia has ever had, and to represent South Australia without any fear or favour.

Laura Jayes: So you haven’t been promised a spot at the top of the ticket even?

Blyth: No.

Jayes: What did you negotiate? Anything?

Blyth: I’m not doing this for that reason, and for those that are out there saying this was all about self interest, this is all about self preservation. They are wrong, that is not what this is about.

Liberals privately believe One Nation will probably to win at least one Senate seat at the next election, which would keep Blyth in the upper house.

‘The Liberal party has lost its way, not me’: Blyth

A journalist asks Leah Blyth about her comments to them and her former colleagues labelling One Nation “a corrosive force” and that the Liberal party should not engage with them.

She’s asked if her decision today isn’t “blatant hypocrisy”.

She answer that she’s “never talked of a merger between One Nation and the Liberal party”.

The Liberal party had to be able to stand on its own two feet. The Liberal party needed to be able to abide by its own values. I don’t believe that the compromise that the Liberal party has to make with moderates as a conservative.

The compromise on those policy positions, constantly having to do that, like on things like net zero and Paris, like when we have the digital duty of care, that there were moderates in the party room of the Liberal Coalition that wanted to support that.

She then quickly adopts the lingo of her new leader, calling the Liberals and Labor a “uni-party”.

I got sick of having to make compromises on everything. The uni-party, that is what this is, the uni-party, Labor and Liberal.

I would say the Liberal party has lost its way, not me.

Blyth says she is the ‘proud daughter of migrants’

Leah Blyth says she no longer trusted the Liberal to make the “hard decisions”, arguing its position on climate is a contradiction.

And I no longer believed that the Liberal party was the vehicle that was going to bring about the change that our country so desperately needs. And part of that is the compromise in policy. So when the Coalition goes out and says, we’re going to scrap net zero, but we’re staying in Paris, there is a contradiction in that in itself. And I got sick of the compromise.

Blyth says she is the “very proud daughter of migrants that came to this country, from India, Burma, and England”.

I think I’m really careful that in this we are nuanced enough that we’re not anti-migration, immigration … We’ve got to be really careful in terms of how we do that in a way that’s sensitive.

My grandparents and parents came out here to build a life, to build a life that was full of opportunity. That was Australia. It was the lucky country where anyone could have a go and be successful. And my grandparents and my parents were that, and look at me, the daughter of migrants now stands in the Senate of the Australian parliament. I want every young Australian, I want everyone who comes to this country to have that.

But with the immigration numbers where they are under this Albanese Labor government, that is no longer possible.

Blyth defection ‘incredible endorsement for One Nation’: Joyce

Barnaby Joyce gets the next go at the mic, and says Liberals are forced to be silent in their own partyroom and don’t get the opportunity to speak out.

He says her decision to defect gives her the freedom to speak her mind.

The political environment’s becoming dynamic and it’s been reflected in the growth of One Nation, both in a parliamentary form and in a support on the ground.

What Leah has the capacity to do now is not be in that sort of invidious position of trying to make excuses for positions so often that you don’t have, and trying to be having to be silent on things that you might passionately disagree with.

Just before the Victorian election, what an incredible endorsement this is for One Nation.

He warns Blyth that her former colleagues will get “nasty”.

‘Spin doctors are working overtime’: Hanson celebrates Blyth’s defection

Pauline Hanson is speaking to the media with Leah Blyth and the rest of her party, after the South Australian senator’s defection.

The One Nation leader emphasises that she never approached Blyth, and accuses the “spin doctors” of working “overtime” to manipulate the narrative.

I never approached Leah at all, at no point, and I never have with any member of parliament here in this place. They approached me to come across [to] One Nation. There has been a lot of spin that’s been put on about her defection from the Coalition from the Liberal party. I’ll let her explain that herself. But the spin doctors are really working overtime.

Liberals say Blyth joined One Nation because she otherwise faced an unwinnable spot on the Liberal Senate ticket.

The Nationals have just held an all-in press conference, where leader Matt Canavan was unsurprisingly peppered with questions about defecting Liberal senator Leah Blyth.

While Blyth was in the Liberals, not the Nationals, Canavan – who would be deputy prime minister in a Coalition government – was asked numerous times about what this means for the unity of the opposition under Angus Taylor.

Look, I can’t control every person, of course, and people can make their own judgments about the defection this morning. This smacks very much of self-interest. Senator Blyth was proud and happy to stand with the Liberals last year. She got elected on their ticket, and I find it pretty disappointing when people desert the party that they were elected [in].

I take the criticism. I take the kickings we’ve got. We deserve to take some medicine. But we’re coming back stronger. We’re coming back more united.

Canavan went on to claim One Nation was “completely focused on defections, not a defeat of the Labor government”. While Pauline Hanson has spoken about wanting to cooperate with the Coalition, Canavan said he was frustrated with the minor party hoovering up disgruntled Coalition members.

What we are focused on is putting Labor members out of their job, so we can protect the jobs and incomes of the Australian people. That’s what’s going to deliver change … We’ve got to focus on changing Labor members of parliament, changing their seats. It doesn’t seem to be what One Nation is focusing on.
