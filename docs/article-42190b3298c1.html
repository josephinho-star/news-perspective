Pocock says interest rate rises should be replaced as companies ‘making bank’

David Pocock has urged the Reserve Bank not to raise interest rates again, calling for new tools to fight inflation.

The independent senator has told the ABC there were “a bunch” of alternatives to interest rates. He said:

We could start with something like a windfall profits tax. We’re seeing companies make absolute bank off the war in Iran, and most of those profits are flowing overseas. We could be funnelling some of that into genuine cost of living relief, anti inflationary measures when it comes to things like energy.

I think we should be looking at smarter ways, about potentially having a sliding scale for things like superannuation, where across the board, you can actually take some money out of the economy, and potentially lower it down afterwards …

We’ve got this blunt instrument [of interest rates] and it clearly just is not working at the moment.

I don’t know why we keep just smashing Aussie households, raising unemployment, where clearly there are so many other factors at play.

You look at some of the big companies that paid zero tax, or, you know, 1.5% tax, while banking huge, huge revenue and profits. That’s got to end. That is also adding to inflation, but we’re not addressing it.

Greens leader will meet Ukrainian ambassador after calling for cut to military aid

The Ukrainian ambassador to Australia says he will meet the Greens leader, David Shoebridge, after Shoebridge urged the government against sending Ukraine military aid.

Vasyl Myroshnychenko said he had met Shoebridge many times and would encourage him to reconsider his view. He told the ABC:

I kind of understand and agree with [what] the Greens said, that probably militarism is a curse on modern society. I wish we would have lived in a very peaceful time and country where our borders are intact, our sovereignty is protected by international law. Unfortunately, it’s not the case for us.

Myroshnychenko said he understood other Greens members like Sarah Hanson-Young disagreed with Shoebridge.

I’ve seen a lot of pushback from the members of the party. They were surprised by this position. So I think it should be revisited.

Australian defence force member ‘person of interest’ in hidden camera on navy ship

The defence department says a member of the Australian Defence Force is a “person of interest” in investigations after a video recording device was found in the bathroom of a navy ship off Cairns.

In a blistering statement, Vice Adm Matt Buckley – the chief of navy – called the incident an “act of sexual violence” and “the most despicable betrayal of trust”. Senator Jacqui Lambie had yesterday claimed a hidden camera had been found in the female shower facility onboard the HMAS Leeuwin, and Buckley later said it had been found in a bathroom.

Defence confirmed Lambie’s claims on Wednesday, and on Thursday said in an update that its investigation had progressed.

“An Australian Defence Force member has been identified as a person of interest in the investigation into a video recording device being located on Royal Australian Navy ship HMAS Leeuwin,” the department said.

This person is now assisting the Joint Military Police Unit with its enquiries. Navy’s focus and priority is providing support to those who are impacted by this incident.

Hume says Coalition probably opposed to private health cuts even if pensioners exempt

The deputy Liberal leader, Jane Hume, says Coalition members “don’t like” any effort to cut private health rebates for older Australians, as Labor looks for a deal.

Hume has told the ABC she “absolutely” supports the expansion of aged care beds that Labor has said the reforms would pay for.

But she indicated the Coalition would not be interested in negotiations for partial exemptions, such as for people on the full pension.

We don’t like the idea that older Australians are being targeted to have their health insurance, their private health insurance rebates removed from them …

Philosophically, we want more older Australians to have private health insurance, to take the pressure off the private health, the public sector, so that all Australians can benefit from our public health system. That is older Australians paying for themselves. Now there was an incentive to do that. It was a fair incentive, but the government wants to take that away.

Hume said the government could fund the aged care reforms from other areas, like winding back the National Reconstruction fund, the Housing Australia future fund and what she described as “climate bureaucracy”, including electric vehicle subsidies.

‘On what planet are you charging for a TV?’: Butler hits out at aged care providers

The health minister has skewered aged care providers for charging residents extra fees for basic services like TV.

Guardian Australia exposed the practice in July and recent ABC reporting has found new fee structure have made the problem worse.

Mark Butler has told the ABC he discussed the issue with providers earlier this week. He said:

I’ve been going to aged care facilities regularly since the early 1990s. TVs have been there for ever. Like, on what planet are you charging an extra service fee for a TV? It has been a basic entitlement for people in aged care facilities in their rooms as long as I can remember. …

I do not consider TVs extra services. I made that really clear to them … I’m not going to sort of do a rule-in rule-out, but TVs are pretty basic … I think that [wifi] is too.

He said he had requested advice from the department and the aged care minister had sought an investigation, adding:

I said to providers, look, the new arrangements provided a level of discretion … about what could be charged for and what couldn’t be, but if providers are going to basically abuse that responsibility, we’ll have to change our approach on this.

Butler won’t rule out pensioner exemptions for private health cuts

The health minister, Mark Butler, is defending Labor’s decision to put its private health insurance reforms back to a vote after the Senate punted the bills yesterday.

Butler has spoken to the ABC about the plan to reduce rebates for over-65s with private health insurance.

He refused to directly answer when asked if the government was considering carving pensioners out of the cuts and, when asked, didn’t close the door to exempting people who are on the full pension from the reforms. He said:

Well, I’m not going to have that debate. As much as I think this is a really useful conversation, I want to have that debate in the parliament in the Senate …

Of course, we recognise the reality: we don’t have a majority in the Senate. We need a constructive debate that determines whether or not we can find a path forward that has majority support from the Senate. …

I wish I didn’t have to bring this measure to the parliament. But at the end of the day, there’s not a money tree out there to fund a huge expansion that is needed in aged care in particular.

Butler said he had been caught by surprise yesterday when the Greens supported the Coalition’s move to effectively block the bill. He said he was in contact with the Greens’ health spokesperson but was also “unfortunately … having a proxy debate” with them through the media. He said:

I hope that yesterday was a rush of blood to the head, and that now over the next several weeks, we’re going to be able to have a reasoned debate.

Shoebridge states inquiry by WA Greens will be independent

Shoebridge defers several questions, saying there needs to be a “fair and independent” inquiry.

That inquiry will be done by the WA Greens.

I have confidence that there will be that degree of independence.I have confidence, that the independence is necessary for all parties to be involved. It is not my role and it would be inappropriate for me as the leader of the Australian Greens in the federal parliament to be imposing controls on that.

Asked if the party is in disarray, Shoebridge says the party has been united and effective in the Senate this sitting week.
