Tommy DeVito embracing latest chance to be NFL starting QB

2026-11-25 03:41

“Tommy Cutlets” has been in this spot before.

Former Giants quarterback Tommy DeVito, who became a household name in New York, is getting another chance to be in the pressure cooker as a starting signal caller.

This time his task is to replace the injured Drake Maye, who suffered a fractured right fibula in the Patriots’ blowout loss to the Lions in Germany on Nov. 15.

Maye is expected to be out at least six weeks, and though head coach Mike Vrabel hopes Maye can return at some point of the season, it’s no guarantee he will be ready to return for New England in 2026.

Either way, the chances of the Patriots (5-5), who are coming off a bye, to make the playoffs will hinge on how well DeVito plays, and that starts Sunday with a road test against the 1-9 Chargers, who suffered a loss to the Jets this past week.

“Everybody has embraced me from the first snap and first play I had last week (in the loss to Detroit),” DeVito told reporters, referring to an outing in which he struggled, completing just 12 of 24 for 81 yards with one interception and a fourth-quarter touchdown pass to Lan Larison. “I’ve put my best foot forward in trying to earn their trust.”

During the 2023 season with the Giants, DeVito, a northern New Jersey native, showed he can provide a spark when he was forced into the lineup after Daniel Jones and Tyrod Taylor suffered injuries.

In that six-game span, “Tommy Cutlets” led Big Blue to three straight victories in which he became the first undrafted free agent rookie since 1970 to have three consecutive games with a passer rating higher than 100. But he couldn’t sustain the strong play and was benched again by Brian Daboll for a once-again healthy Taylor during a loss to the Eagles later in the season.

DeVito, who is 3-5 as a starter, started two games for Big Blue in the 2024 season after the Giants benched and waived Jones, but an arm injury and concussion sent him to the sidelines as Drew Lock started the final six games.

He became the odd man out after the Giants signed veterans Russell Wilson and Jameis Winston and following the selection of Jaxson Dart in the first round of the 2025 draft.

The Giants cut DeVito that summer but hoped to re-sign him to the practice squad after he cleared waivers. Instead, he was claimed by the Patriots.

DeVito spent the 2025 season on the practice squad behind Maye and Josh Dobbs but then supplanted Dobbs as Maye’s backup when Dobbs was released. He signed a two-year extension worth up to $7.4 million with $2 million guaranteed.

Vrabel knows the intangible spark DeVito can bring to a team, but says the time he had working with the Patriots’ wide receivers during training camp will benefit him, as well as the starters’ reps he has received this week.

“A lot of energy. I think a lot of work in there with those guys,” Vrabel said. “I think he’s taken advantage of his opportunities. I think he’s also improved, just fundamentally. … Those things probably didn’t show up with the exposure from his last game and when he got into it. But I think being able to see some of these things, work on them, focus on them and understand what it is that he can do a little bit better, I think that that’ll obviously help him and in turn, help us.″

DeVito — who said offensive coordinator Josh McDaniels and quarterbacks coach Ashton Grant “want me to be me,” — is pumped up for his latest chance to lead a team as a starter.

“You feel more energized for sure. It’s time to go. Injuries are part of the game, unfortunately. For the players that are in that position, that’s how you get opportunities,” DeVito said.
