Raiders gave Ashton Jeanty what he’s needed all season in breakout game vs. Broncos

2026-11-24 19:39

If you want to know if Ashton Jeanty turned a corner Sunday in Denver, put yourself in Klint Kubiak’s shoes and take a look at the tape he broke down for his Raiders’ players Monday.

Cue up the run that went for 46 yards. The first time through, take your eyes off Jeanty.

Watch the right guard. Watch the right tackle. Then run it back and watch Jack Bech, the receiver lined up in the left slot. Bech is a big part of how an eight-yard gain became a 46-yarder.

So, did Jeanty turn the corner? If he keeps getting the kind of help he got Sunday, yes.

The Raiders ran for a season-high 143 yards on 24 carries in their 38-24 loss to the Broncos, their best rushing total since the season opener. You have to go all the way back to Week 3 for the last time they ran for 100 or more yards.

They went into Denver averaging 70.7 rushing yards a game, 31st in the NFL. Jeanty beat that number himself with 79 yards on 11 carries.

He added 90 through the air for 169 yards from scrimmage, the kind of afternoon the Raiders envisioned when they made him the sixth pick in last year’s draft. Put enough of those together and Offensive Player of the Year enters the conversation.

It also raises a fair question about how much of the blame for the first 10 games belonged on his doorstep. Go back to third-and-three at midfield. The Raiders trailed 17-0 in the second quarter. Before anybody could worry about springing Jeanty for 46, they had to get him three.

Start on the right side. Guard Trey Zuhn II adjusted to pick up blitzing linebacker Alex Singleton. Tackle DJ Glaze blocked down on Tyler Onyedim. Watch what that did for outside linebacker Que Robinson. For a beat, he had a clear path to the ball carrier.

Glaze could leave him there because he knew who was coming. Tight end Michael Mayer came in motion from left to right and buried Robinson. Inside, center Tyler Linderbaum turned D.J. Jones. Left guard Spencer Burford walled off Zach Allen, and left tackle Kolton Miller did the same to Dondrea Tillman.

There’s the hole.

Give Jeanty credit for seeing it and hitting it without wasting a step.

Now rewind and find Bech.

From the left slot, he immediately identified safety Talanoa Hufanga and took an angle that put him squarely between Hufanga and the runner. Freeze it as Hufanga comes into position to make the tackle. Bech gets to him at precisely that moment. Just inside that block, Zuhn is engaging Singleton.

Now watch Jeanty. He sees the second opening, cuts left off Bech and Zuhn and accelerates into open grass. According to NextGenStats, Jeanty hit a career-high top speed of 21.38 MPH.

By the time Patrick Surtain II pushes him out of bounds, the Raiders are at the Broncos’ 4-yard-line.

Those last 38 yards are why a wide receiver’s block belongs in Kubiak’s lesson plan. But it all started with the communication and execution at the line of scrimmage.

It’s been a work in progress all year.

New scheme. Some new players.

And the new voices of Kubiak, offensive coordinator Andrew Janocko and offensive line coach Rick Dennison.

But for one Sunday, it all came together to offer a glimpse of what is possible.

“I just think over the season, as it’s gone on, our guys are getting more familiar and comfortable with the scheme,” Kubiak said. “And the more time on task that you have, you anticipate improvement, and it was a step in the right direction.”

Later that quarter, Fernando Mendoza got his turn to help. Jeanty ran a wheel route, Mendoza dropped the throw to him in stride, and Jeanty took it the rest of the way for a 68-yard touchdown.

Twice before halftime, the Broncos had to chase the same running back to their goal line.

The first time a receiver helped get him there. The second time he was the receiver.

Activation Jeanty was in full force. And it took everyone doing their job to turn him loose.

“Our offensive line played fantastic, as well as our running backs,” Mendoza said. “I think that’s a real threat. To have those guys rolling helps the offense tremendously.”

Jeanty’s other 10 carries produced 33 yards. Turn a couple of those into five-yard gains and consider what that buys Mendoza in his third NFL start Sunday in Cleveland.

Kubiak can call another run on second down. Mendoza can sell a play fake. And when a linebacker takes two steps toward the line of scrimmage, there’s room to throw behind him.

That’s the assignment for the Raiders. Give Jeanty enough room to make the first read. Stay on a block long enough to give him a second.

Then roll the tape one last time and watch what happens when a back with his vision and burst gets to make both decisions at full speed.
