Jailed PKK leader moved to new section of İmralı prison: MPs

2026-11-02 09:27

Jailed Kurdistan Workers’ Party (PKK) founder Abdullah Öcalan has been moved to a new section of his island prison near İstanbul, lawmakers from the pro-Kurdish Peoples’ Equality and Democracy Party (DEM Party) said Sunday, calling for further steps to advance Turkey’s peace process.

DEM Party lawmaker Pervin Buldan said the move did not amount to a change in Öcalan’s status.

“This new facility is part of the prison; it is not somewhere outside the prison,” she said, stressing that “the question of status cannot be defined solely by Mr Öcalan having moved to this location.”

Öcalan has played a key role from his prison on İmralı Island in efforts to end decades of conflict between the PKK and the Turkish state. The PKK leadership has repeatedly demanded his release as part of the process.

Turkey’s parliament in August adopted a law allowing investigations, trials and prison sentences for certain PKK-related offenses to be suspended under specified conditions. The measure excludes offenses that would allow Öcalan, 77, who has been imprisoned since 1999, to benefit.

In September, senior presidential adviser Mehmet Uçum said Öcalan would join a panel overseeing the PKK’s laying down of arms, while stressing that this would not mean his release.

The DEM Party accuses President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s government of dragging its feet in the peace process.

“Mr Öcalan has said from the outset that he wants to meet with people from different sections of society… with women’s organizations, journalists and academics. But none of these meetings has taken place in the past two years,” Buldan said.

“This is not enough to move the process forward. Steps need to be taken on this urgently,” she said.

Fellow DEM Party lawmaker Mithat Sancar, a member of the delegation authorized to visit Öcalan, also called for “an acceleration of the process.”

“The process has been at a standstill for the past two months. Certain dates had been set, but they were not met,” he said.

Sancar said Öcalan needed to coordinate the process through a working group focused on peace and the transition to politics.

“This is a very important factor,” he said.
