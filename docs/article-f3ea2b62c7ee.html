Turkey, US sign grant deal on next generation nuclear reactors

2026-09-22 15:39

Turkey’s state-owned nuclear developer and a US agency signed a grant agreement on Tuesday to study whether small modular reactors and Generation IV nuclear technologies could be used in Turkey.

The agreement was signed at the Turkish House, Turkey’s diplomatic center in New York, by Türkiye Nuclear Energy Company (TÜNAŞ) General Manager Necati Yamaç and US Trade and Development Agency (USTDA) Deputy Director Thomas Hardy. Energy and Natural Resources Minister Alparslan Bayraktar attended the ceremony during a visit to the United States.

The study will examine small modular reactor designs offered by four US companies and five Generation IV reactor designs from US companies. It will cover their technical features, costs, licensing status, supply chains, financing models, safety requirements, possible sites and legal frameworks.

The project has a budget of about $2 million. USTDA will cover the cost of the technical work, which is expected to take at least one year.

Bayraktar said the agreement would allow Turkey to assess the technical, economic and regulatory aspects of next-generation nuclear technologies.

He linked the project to Turkey’s goals of reaching net zero emissions by 2053, diversifying its energy mix and reducing its reliance on imported energy.

Turkey is building its first nuclear power plant at Akkuyu in the southern province of Mersin. Russia’s state nuclear company Rosatom owns and is building the four-reactor facility.

The Turkish government aims to generate the first electricity from Unit 1 by the end of 2026. Rosatom expects to begin startup operations this year and start commercial operation in 2027.

Turkey is also pursuing large nuclear power plants in the northern province of Sinop and the Thrace region in the northwest. The government plans four reactors at each site but has not selected its partners or reactor technologies.

The new study will examine smaller alternatives to conventional reactors. The grant does not amount to a decision to buy or build any of the designs under review.

The agreement follows a memorandum on strategic civil nuclear cooperation that Turkey and the United States signed on September 25, 2025.

Bayraktar and US Secretary of State Marco Rubio signed the memorandum in the presence of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and US President Donald Trump at the White House. The memorandum was intended to support possible partnerships between Turkish and US companies.

USTDA announced a separate grant agreement with the Electricity Generating Authority of Thailand on September 14. That project will study US small modular reactor technologies as Thailand considers developing its first nuclear power plant.
