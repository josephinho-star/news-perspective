NJ mom fatally stabbed in Times Square during first week back at Bank of America after maternity leave

2026-09-02 14:47

A Bank of America executive who had recently returned to work after maternity leave was fatally stabbed Monday in New York City’s Times Square, where a knife-wielding woman also wounded another person before police fatally shot the suspect.

The victim was identified as Erin Piacenti, 32, of Chester, New Jersey. Piacenti was in her first week back at Bank of America after spending five months caring for her newborn daughter, The New York Times reported.

Four days before her death, Piacenti shared a photo on social media of herself with the baby and her husband, Frank Piacenti. She also posted about the child’s recent baptism and the family’s travels while she was on leave.

The NYPD said Piacenti was stabbed near West 42nd Street and Seventh Avenue before being taken to Bellevue Hospital, where she was pronounced dead.

A 68-year-old man was also stabbed in the abdomen and taken to Bellevue, where he was listed in stable condition.

Police said the attack began near West 41st Street when Pamela Cisneros, 49, of Queens, approached the man while armed with two large knives. He had just exited a subway station and was waiting to cross the street with his wife.

A witness told investigators that Cisneros pulled the knives from a red Target bag before attacking the man.

Cisneros then moved north on Seventh Avenue and stabbed Piacenti. NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said the two attacks occurred about 20 seconds apart.

Officers later confronted Cisneros while she was still holding both knives. Police recovered the weapons at the scene.

Cisneros refused repeated commands to drop the weapons and engaged officers in a standoff that lasted about four minutes, Tisch said. When she advanced toward officers while waving the knives, police deployed Tasers, but they failed to stop her. Two officers then fired their weapons.

"Our officers were confronted with a rapidly evolving threat, with countless innocent people around them," Tisch said.

Cisneros was taken to Bellevue, where she was pronounced dead.

Tisch described the attack as random and unprovoked. Cisneros had documented mental health-related encounters with the NYPD in 2018 and 2019 but no prior arrest record with the department. Police said there was no indication the attack was related to terrorism.

Bank of America confirmed Piacenti’s death in an emailed statement to Reuters.

"We are shocked and deeply saddened by the tragic loss of our colleague," the bank said. "She was a valued teammate who will be greatly missed."

Piacenti earned a law degree from Fordham University School of Law in 2021 alongside her husband, the school told The Associated Press. Fordham Law said it was heartbroken by the death of its former student.

The NYPD’s Force Investigation Division is reviewing the police shooting. Mayor Zohran Mamdani said body-worn camera footage from the encounter will be released.

Reuters and The Associated Press contributed to this report.
