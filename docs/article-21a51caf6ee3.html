Karl-Anthony Towns is letting his Knicks play speak loudest in face of uncertainty

2026-11-20 01:19

MINNEAPOLIS — In his own words, this should be an uncomfortable situation for Karl-Anthony Towns.

Regardless, it’s produced some of the best basketball of his career.

Before the season, when Towns still was publicly pushing for a contract extension from the Knicks, he said he wanted his long-term future settled “so I can feel as comfortable as possible.” Without a new deal, he said, “there’s no comfortability to be had.”

That new deal, of course, never materialized, as the Knicks shut down all extension negotiations for the season while navigating owner James Dolan’s mandate to stay under the second apron. It left Towns in that uncomfortable situation.

He’s responded by making a loud statement to Knicks brass with his play.

“I got a job to do, at the end of the day,” Towns said before the team’s game against the Timberwolves on Thursday. “I gotta come here and put that jersey on and do what’s best for my brothers and the guys they have in my locker room. So, I’m just here to do my job.”

Towns entered Thursday night’s game averaging 21.2 points, 3.9 assists and a league-high 14.1 rebounds while shooting 54.5 percent from the field and 40.0 percent from 3-point range.

He has been by far the most consistently strong performer through the Knicks’ rocky first few weeks of their championship defense.

He’s shown no signs of letting that discomfort affect him. If anything, it is fueling him.

“I gotta put that jersey on and put the best performance out there for my teammates,” Towns said. “So, that’s all I really think about.”

Towns’ standout performances came just as he returned to Minnesota, where he spent the first nine years of his career.

He didn’t want to opine Thursday about whether winning a championship with the Knicks validates the move for New York and means it won the blockbuster trade with the Timberwolves, which sent Julius Randle and Donte DiVincenzo back to Minnesota.

But at this point, it’s a clear Knicks win.

But it doesn’t make being back any less emotional for Towns, who still has a painting of him and his mother and father hugging that the Timberwolves gifted him. They were the last team he played for before his mother passed away during the COVID-19 pandemic.

“I still have so much love for this place,” Towns said. “It doesn’t go away. I think it’s actually grown as I’ve been away. I think that, even for me and my dad, we were just driving around the city and just going to places. We went to eat at a spot we love to go here. It always hits home. This is the last place that I got to play basketball with my mom. I got to see the kids be young before now. Jolani, my niece, who was in my rookie of the year photo actually in here, she was a baby, and now she’s in high school. It’s a different part of life, but it’s a part of life that I really enjoy.”

Thursday was the third time Towns returned as an opponent. But it was the first time as a champion.

“It’s always great to be home and to be back here,” Towns said. “Nine years of my life, some amazing years, amazing moments. Yeah, it’s special to be back. Obviously, it’s bittersweet to come back with a championship because I wanted to win it here so bad for the city, for Minneapolis, for Minnesota, for all the people who haven’t seen an NBA championship here. But it’s great to be able to come back and to be able to see friends, people I call family here.”
