Australia news live: Senior gallery worker accused of funnelling $8 million in mobile phone scheme; interest rate hike still likely after jobless rise

2026-09-24 05:54

Former NGV employee allegedly stole $8m of gallery money to buy phones

A former senior National Gallery of Victoria employee is alleged to have funnelled $8m from the gallery over six years, AAP reports.

He allegedly bought $8m worth of mobile phones using an NGV account, which he then sold for personal financial gain.

The state’s corruption watchdog has charged the former employee with eight offences alleged to have taken place between September 2019 and September 2025.

“IBAC will allege that mobile phones were purchased under an NGV account to the value of approximately $8 million and then sold for personal financial benefit,” the Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission said in a statement on Thursday.

The watchdog said it began investigating allegations of corrupt conduct in relation to theft and misuse of his position by the former senior employee earlier in September 2025.

The man has been charged with seven counts of theft and one count of misconduct in public office.

He is expected to appear before Melbourne magistrates’ court on 20 October.

Founded in 1861, the NGV is Australia’s oldest and most visited art museum.

RBA still tipped to lift interest rates to 14-year high despite jump in unemployment

Money markets are still predicting a 90% chance of the Reserve Bank raising interest rates next Tuesday.

The surprise rise in unemployment to 4.6% in August data today would normally reduce the prospect of a rate hike but today’s data also showed hiring actually increased, just by less than new jobseekers.

As a result, Commonwealth Bank, ANZ and NAB have all reaffirmed their predictions for a hike in the cash rate on Tuesday from 4.35% to 4.6%, as have analysts at AMP and UBS. That would take rates to their highest level in 14 years. (Westpac has not explicitly said whether it still agrees with that view.)

For someone with an average-sized new mortgage of $731,000, paying a typical rate of 6.2%, a rate increase would add about $119 to their $4,477 monthly repayments.

Violent, serious offences decrease as crime rate falls in Victoria

The crime rate in Victoria has fallen by almost 4% in the past year, with police praising controversial “adult crime, adult time” laws as being responsible for a major drop in youth offending.

AAP reports the state’s crime rate, which accounts for population growth, fell to 8,679.6 offences per 100,000 people, according to Victoria’s Crime Statistics Agency figures released on Thursday.

Overall, the state recorded 624,624 criminal offences in the 12 months to June, a reduction of 13,360 offences compared with the previous financial year.

Youth offending decreased by 8.1% in that time, but police maintain child offenders aged 10-17 continue to be over-represented in major violent crimes such as home invasions, aggravated burglaries and robberies.

Police attributed some of the reductions to increased visible and proactive policing. But other areas of offending continued to rise.

Drug offenders apprehended increased 11.6%, while the number of offenders processed for drug trafficking and dealing rose 18.5%.

Police also recorded a 2.7% increase in retail theft, with 43,124 offences recorded.

Last-minute decision to protect endangered birds from SA space project

Conservation groups have welcomed an 11th-hour decision to protect “precious and important habitat” on South Australia’s Eyre peninsula from plans for a rocket launching facility.

The Whalers Way “orbital launch complex”, which could see as many as 42 rockets a year take off from conservation land, had already been approved under federal and state planning and environment laws.

But a recent decision by the state’s independent Native Vegetation Council could scuttle the space project, located about 32km from Port Lincoln.

The 23-hectare site is protected under a longstanding heritage agreement, a type of covenant on private land designed to protect native wildlife and habitat in perpetuity.

The chief executive of BirdLife Australia, Kate Millar, welcomed the council’s decision to protect “one of the most precious and important habitats in the state”, which was a stronghold for one of Australia’s tiniest birds, the endangered Eyre Peninsula southern emu-wren, and home to 80% of the endangered mallee whipbird population.

An AI agent is software designed to perform digital tasks on behalf of humans with a high degree of independence. They’re built on different models, depending on their specific purpose or the task assigned by a human.

They’re often used for tasks such as writing code, conducting online research, generating reports based on that research or even booking a restaurant reservation.

They’re not the same as commonly used chatbots, such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT or Anthropic’s Claude, because they take actions and make decisions rather than just responding to prompts.

Developers can include guardrails, such as strict access controls, in the instructions to reduce privacy and security risks. However, as we’ve seen this morning, some agents can bypass those safeguards by finding loopholes in their instructions or the systems they interact with.
