New York State Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins dies at 76

2026-11-27 19:47

Video above features Stewart-Cousins taking about environmental legislation in February.

ALBANY, N.Y. (NEWS10) — New York State Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins died on Friday. She was 76. Senate Majority Communications Director Mike Murphy announced her passing.

“Serving the people of the 35th Senate District was one of the greatest honors of her life,” said Murphy. “Throughout her decades in public service, Stewart-Cousins made a profound impact on the lives of New Yorkers. She was a groundbreaking leader who shattered one of New York’s highest glass ceilings, becoming the first woman to lead the New York State Senate.”

Stewart-Cousins was the longest-serving Democratic Majority Leader in state senate history. She was also the first African American woman to have the role. She was elected to the state senate in 2006.

During her tenure, Stewart-Cousins focused on reproductive rights, women’s health rights, and education. She was born and raised in New York and was a resident of Yonkers. Before the senate, she served as a Westchester County Legislator and was the first African American Director of Community Affairs for Yonkers.

Stewart-Cousins is survived by her brother, Robert Stewart Jr., her three children, Kevin, Steven, and Candice, and her six grandchildren. She was preceded in death by her husband, Thomas Cousins.

“We ask that you keep Majority Leader Stewart-Cousins’ family, friends, and staff in your thoughts and prayers during this difficult time,” said Murphy. “Information regarding memorial services and public tributes will be shared in the coming days.”

New York State Governor Kathy Hochul said that Stewart-Cousins was a dear friend. Hochul said she’ll never forget introducing her as the Majority Leader for the first time in 2019.

“I knew how hard she had worked to get there and how many women had dreamed of seeing that day. Andrea made history that day, and every day that followed, she made that history mean something,” said Hochul. “Andrea possessed a rare combination of quiet grace and extraordinary strength. She never forgot where she came from, who she was fighting for or the doors she could open for those coming behind her.”

“During my time in Albany, I have always respected and appreciated her partnership, dignity and demeanor. While we didn’t always agree on policy, we shared a common belief in the value and importance of public service and the work we did on behalf of New Yorkers,” said Senate Republican Leader Rob Ortt. “As the first woman Senate Majority Leader, Andrea Stewart-Cousins leaves a lasting legacy in the halls of the State Capitol.”

New York State Sen-Elect Aber Kawas was recently sworn-in by Stewart-Cousins to represent Queens District 12. She told NEWS10’s Sarah Willson that she was shocked by the news of her passing.

“She was truly at a high, celebrating all of he achievements of having a huge caucus of 48, which was more than she imagined,” said Kawas. “It really is sad to have lost her in this moment.”

“The Leader had the energy of ten, and led with a powerful strength that began with her extraordinary, dignified presence, manner, and speech,” said State Senator Pat Fahy. “It is impossible to comprehend how she will be replaced. Not only was she the longest-serving Leader, she had the utmost respect from every member of the Senate on both sides of the aisle. Her leadership was unquestioned and her character beyond reproach.”

“As the first African American woman to lead a legislative conference in Albany, Sen. Stewart-Cousins left an indelible mark on New York State and set a historic standard for others to emulate,” said Assembly Minority Leader Ed Ra.”While our policy perspectives differed, I respected her professionalism, thoughtfulness and the historic achievements that defined her career.”
