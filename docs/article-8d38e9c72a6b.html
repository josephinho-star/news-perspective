Qld government to create 24/7 hotline for victims of crime

Queensland’s LNP government will create a 24/7 hotline for victims of crime - and reform its own domestic violence prevention process as “a top priority”.

Minister for victims Laura Gerber said she will table legislation today to create a “victim advocate service”.

Queensland already has an victims’ commissioner, to advocate on their behalf. The new organisation will be a “one stop shop”, with the ability the ability to record the a person’s individual details and then share them across departments rather than sharing the details of a crime multiple times, she said.

It’s the second bill of the week Gerber, who tabled legislation yesterday that would increase the bar for a convicted criminal to get bail, and expand the state’s ‘restricted prisoner’ laws to cover all prisoners sentenced to life behind bars. Restricted prisoners can be banned from applying for parole.

Meanwhile, police minister Dan Purdie announced that the state is working to fix its own police protection notice system, created last year.

It’s a top priority at the moment. It’s top priority for the police union. It’s a top priority frontline police, and I’ve visited well over 100 police stations now, and it’s priority for victims. So it’s something that we are working on at the moment.

Immigration debate is ‘overcooked’ and could damage Australia in long term, Bill Shorten

The former Labor leader and the University of Canberra vice-chancellor Bill Shorten says the immigration debate has been “overcooked” and that it’s an issue that people aren’t thinking about front and centre in their daily lives.

Speaking to News24, Shorten says Australia is also at risk of damaging foreign relations if the government cuts international student numbers too deeply

I think that just to translate the noise in Canberra to the world that I see with universities, or indeed the broader communities in which I move, I think some of this debate about immigration is overcooked.

While we chase the issue, while people are chasing the issue, the reality is it’s not the most important thing for people in their daily lives. And the problem is, as we chase the issue, even though it’s not crucial to a lot of Aussies at the end of the day, we could potentially be damaging Australia for the long term.

Shorten says his university doesn’t have as many international students as other institutions, so isn’t as exposed to cuts, but points out that they are a key source of revenue for universities underfunded by the government.

If we cut too tough, we’re going to damage Australia’s foreign relations, we’re going to damage the fact that every international student generates about $70,000 in surplus revenue for Australia.

All the universities, because universities are underfunded, use international students to cross-subsidise local research and local students. So I think this issue has got a more disproportionate importance in the political class’s head than it does out with the punters.

One in five Melbourne units sold in June quarter made a loss

One in five Melbourne apartments, and more than one in 10 Sydney units sold in the June quarter made a loss, according to Cotality’s latest “pain and gain” report.

Gerard Burg, Cotality’s head of research, said house prices in the capital cities had grown much more quickly than unit prices over the past decade.

The national median house price in June was 37% more expensive than the median unit price – five years ago houses were only 21% pricier.

“Unit losses are also highly concentrated,” Burg said.

Most apartment owners are still selling for a profit, but the risk of a loss is considerably higher in parts of Sydney and Melbourne where value growth has been weaker. In the Melbourne and Parramatta LGAs [local government areas], unit values are still below peaks recorded back in mid-2017.

Across the country and types of properties, Cotality’s analysis showed 95.4% of property sales in the June quarter were profitable. The typical gain was $371,000, and the median loss was $45,000.

The share of profitable sales was down from a 21-year peak of 96.1% in March, but still “exceptionally high”, Burg said.

Most sellers are still benefiting from the significant value growth accumulated over the past five years, which is providing considerable protection against the early stages of the downturn.

I think there are serious questions, not just for the prime minister, but for other ministers involved in this grants program, how did their projects get approved?

I made my decisions, and then there was criticism of those decisions. But ultimately, I resigned from my ministerial portfolio as a result of a breach of the ministerial code over a perceived conflict of interest, with one of the successful clubs, I was gifted a membership I didn’t know about.

So that’s why we’ve been pursuing this in question time. We’ve also been successful in getting a Senate inquiry up into this, to really unpack who knew what when.

Burke to appear at the National Press Club to announce migration policy

Tony Burke, is finally headed to the National Press Club tomorrow – a month after his eleventh hour cancellation.

Just in the past few minutes, we have confirmation that he will address the club in Canberra on Thursday.

The speech is titled: “The work of managing the migration program: Who arrives, who stays, who leaves”.

Labor’s efforts to regain the initiative on immigration comes as One Nation surges in the polls and as community sentiment on the number of overseas arrivals remains a key issue motivating voters.

Labor is expected to make it harder for some migrants to stay in the country, limiting visa hopping and ending some appeal rights for people looking to extend their time in the country.

Burke’s speech will be closely watched by all sides, especially after prime minister Anthony Albanese tried to secure Coalition support for legislative changes in talks with the opposition leader, Angus Taylor, last week.

NDIA says job cuts necessary, as it offers voluntary redundancies

A quick update on a story we brought you yesterday: voluntary redundancies at the National Disability Insurance Agency for the first time in its existence.

In a statement, the NDIA says these job cuts are necessary to meet the federal government’s budget requirements.

The budget papers show the agency’s average staffing level will need to drop from the 10,509 positions recorded in the 2025-26 financial year, to 9,840 by July 2027.

An NDIA spokesperson said the agency had “significantly strengthened its frontline workforce to reduce wait times” and improve quality in recent years.

Like all government agencies, the NDIA is required to operate within Government-approved average staffing levels.

These workforce measures are designed to help us meet these requirements while maintaining frontline services and having the right people, skills and capability in place to deliver the next phase of NDIS reforms.
