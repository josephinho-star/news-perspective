Don't major on minors: Why a great flat-rate card is the best way to optimize rewards

2026-11-12 21:14

Do you want to foolproof your rewards? Pass on headline-grabbing perks and opt for the tried-and-true flat-rate credit card with no annual fee.

Cards like the Wells Fargo Active Cash® Card and Citi Double Cash® Card offer double the standard 1% return on every purchase (for the Citi Double Cash, receive 1% cash back when you buy, plus an additional 1% as you pay for those purchases). What you may miss out on in bonus categories and benefits may not be as much as it seems. And there is real value in the simplicity these rewards credit cards offer.

Bonus spending categories can be valuable, allowing you to earn two or three times the rewards you would otherwise earn. But that's only if you remember to use the right card — a choice that isn't always obvious — and pay attention to category caps and other limitations.

Let's say you spend $6,000 a year at restaurants. With a top flat-rate credit card, you'd earn 12,000 points or $120 cash back. A card that earns 3% or 4% back on dining could net you an extra $60 to $120 in value from dining purchases in a year. That works out to only $5 or $10 per month more if you max out dining bonus rewards. So if you're not spending a lot on bonus purchases, it may not be worth the effort.

You have to pay attention to get the full value. And it's not just about remembering to use the right card when you're eating out. You could dine at a restaurant and still not earn bonus points for dining. Bonus rewards are earned based on the merchant category code, or MCC, of the purchase. If the restaurant is located in a shared space, such as a hotel, theme park or department store, it may not code as a dining purchase.

The rewards cards with bonus categories that earn 5X or 6X points typically have spending limits or other restrictions. For example, the Citi Strata Elite℠ Card earns 6X points at restaurants, but only on Friday and Saturday from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. ET; outside of that window, you earn 3X points.

Other cards have quarterly limits or rotating categories. The Discover it® Cash Back earns 5% cash back on up to $1,500 in combined eligible purchases per quarter in rotating categories (after activation), then you'll earn only 1% back on purchases above the limit or outside of that quarter's categories. You'll have to remember the categories for that quarter and how much you've spent.

It gets more complicated if you try optimizing multiple categories. When you juggle cards to earn bonus rewards in gas, groceries, travel, entertainment and streaming subscriptions, it adds to the mental load.

Even if you have a card with an excellent return where you spend the most, you could still benefit from a 2% cash-back card. Purchases that don't qualify for bonus rewards typically only earn 1X points or 1% cash back. Earning less on everyday purchases can cancel out what you earn from bonus categories.

Let's say you have a card that earns 3% cash back in two of your favorite categories and 1% cash back on everything else. In that scenario, more than 50% of your spending would need to earn 3% back to exceed what you would earn with a card that earns unlimited 2% back everywhere.

Annual fees can silently erode your points' value if you don't carefully consider them every year. Even a reasonable $100 annual fee requires $5,000 in purchases at 2% cash back to offset it.

Cards with higher annual fees usually offer perks outside of rewards that can help offset the fee. But this is another complication: You'll have to abide by the terms and pay attention to how your benefits work to receive the full value. The benefits may be too complicated or niche for you to fully utilize.

That's an excellent return on common expenses, and the annual caps are high enough to not be limiting for many household budgets. But the Amex Gold has a $325 annual fee (see rates and fees), which is a lot to pay every year.

That's $27 in monthly credits and $50 in semiannual credits. While the face value of these perks is worth more than the annual fee, it's nuanced. Aside from the effort required to earn back what you paid, these perks could incentivize you to make a purchase you wouldn't have otherwise. If you normally use Grubhub or Uber Eats, then the $10 monthly credit for each service is nice to have. But if you're ordering takeout just to use the credit, you're likely spending more than $10, and the food you buy may cost you more.

Even if you are diligent in using every card benefit, a no-annual-fee 2% cash-back card saves you the hassle of having to pay an upfront fee and then work to earn it back.
