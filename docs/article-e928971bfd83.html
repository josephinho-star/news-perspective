FAA orders nationwide ground stop

2026-11-13 21:23

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. flights, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed Friday.

“The @FAANews [FAA] issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers,” Duffy wrote on the social platform X. “FAA technicians are investigating the issue.”

The ground stop prevents all flights from taking off from U.S. airports, but flights currently in the air will be allowed to land.

The agency shared an advisory to the social platform X citing “extraordinary circumstances” and “reasons of safety” for the ground stop.

“Every flight has been accounted for and international flights will be handled on a case by case basis,” according to the FAA.

A nationwide ground stop is a rare move used only in extreme circumstances. The last time a ground stop was issued nationwide was in 2023 amid an outage caused by a contractor. The 2023 stoppage lasted approximately 90 minutes.

Prior to that, the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop on Sept. 11, 2001, in response to the terror attacks.
