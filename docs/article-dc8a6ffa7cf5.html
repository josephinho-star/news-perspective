California’s restaurant apocalypse is raging — while politicians cheer on their competition

2026-11-25 03:41

Another week, another restaurant closure in California. This time, it’s Georgia’s Greek Cuisine in San Diego, which has been in business for 50 years.

As The California Post reported, the owners, Georgia and Dennis Stathoulis, fell in love with the California coast when they visited on honeymoon.

A romantic visit turned into a life-changing decision, and a career in the restaurant industry. Together, Georgia and Dennis brought joy to many thousands of customers in San Diego.

But the business lost its lease, and is joining the many hundreds of other restaurants that have closed in Southern California already this year.

The restaurant apocalypse has swept the state. More than 300 spots have closed in LA alone.

A glance at a Fresno newspaper reveals a new restaurant closure every week.

The story is the same almost everywhere across the Golden State.

Yes, the food industry has always been tough, and restaurants have always operated on small margins.

The life of a restaurant entrepreneur only seems glamorous. In reality, there’s almost no harder way to make a living.

If your cook calls in sick, guess who has to work the grill?

If the sous chef decides to quit without warning, guess who has to wake up at 3 a.m. to butter 300 slices of bread?

It’s always the owner.

But California adds additional challenges.

As The California Post noted earlier this month, a beloved Malibu restaurant, Ollo Malibu, closed suddenly.

The owners had tried to keep their place open, but could not withstand the cumulative cost of fires, coastal floods, and construction along the Pacific Coast Highway.

Restaurants also face other obstacles. Taxes, regulations, and pressure from unions to use their preferred contractors all take a heavy toll.

Then there’s competition from street vendors, who have new advocates in Gov.-elect Xavier Becerra and LA Mayor-elect Nithya Raman.

Both of these politicians have championed vendors — who evade rules and costs that brick-and-mortar restaurants have to handle.

Becerra even claimed: “[T]here’s real street tacos and you don’t get them in some establishment that’s been around for a long time.”

With politicians like that in charge of the state, it’s no wonder that restaurants are in trouble.

It’s time for our elected leaders to stop taking restaurants for granted. They’re not just good for photo ops.

They are the backbone of our communities. And they need government to be an ally, not an enemy.
