Types of Sportsbook Promos Explained

In our table, we listed a few types of sportsbook promos and offer types. If you’re new to sports betting, you might not be familiar with what they mean, so let’s take a second to explain what the different offer types are.

- This is somewhat self-explanatory. You bet a set amount of money (often as low as $5), and you get bonus bets paid out in one day or over multiple days.

First-Bet Safety Net (First-Bet Insurance)

- If your bet loses, you receive bonus bets in the same value. Sometimes they will be issued as one bet, but in the case of theScore Bet, you receive five equal bets over five days. For example, if you lose a $1,000 bet, you’ll get five $200 bonus bets, one each day.

- A reward bonus is typically given on top of other promotions. So it may be as simple as depositing $100 and receiving an additional $20 bonus.

- The book matches a percentage of your deposit in bonus funds, often tied to a playthrough requirement.

- A second-chance credit if your qualifying bet loses. This is different from a welcome offer, because this can be provided by the sportsbook at any time. As long as your bet qualifies and loses, you’ll be given tokens or bonus bets. Keep in mind, those tokens or bonus bets tend to have expiration dates on them.

Odds Boosts and Profit Boosts

- Increase the payout on a selected bet or market. Common in ongoing promos rather than welcome offers.

How Bonus Bets Pay Out (Bonus Bets vs Cash)

- Bonus bets return winnings only, not the stake. For example, if you have a $100 bonus bet and it hits a +100 bet. You’re only collecting $100 total. If you bet that same bet in cash, you’d collect $200.

No-Deposit Sportsbook Bonuses

No-deposit sportsbook bonuses sound appealing because they give new users a promotional credit before they deposit or place a bet with their own money. The catch is that these offers are rare at regulated U.S. sportsbooks, which typically require a qualifying deposit and wager.

When no-deposit offers do appear, they are often tied to a new state launch or a limited sign-up promotion. The bonus is usually small and may come with minimum odds, playthrough and withdrawal requirements, so read the fine print before claiming it. For most bettors, a low-risk bet-and-get offer will provide more practical value.

Claiming a sportsbook promo is very easy. It is pretty straightforward and similar at almost every sportsbook app.

How to Get the Most From Your Bonus

Sportsbook Promo Glossary

- Short intro line: a quick reference for the terms used across sportsbook promos.

- Bonus bets: Credits you can wager but not withdraw. If a bonus bet wins, you keep the winnings, not the stake.

- Bet-and-get: Place a small qualifying bet and receive a set bonus, usually win or lose unless a win is required.

- First-bet safety net: Your first bet is refunded in bonus bets, up to a cap, if it loses.

- Bet reset: A second-chance credit if your qualifying bet loses.

- Deposit match: The book matches a percentage of your deposit in bonus funds.

- Profit boost: Increases the payout on a selected bet or market.

- FanCash: The Fanatics rewards currency, redeemable for bonus bets or merchandise.

- Minimum odds: The shortest price a bet can be and still qualify, often around -200 to -500.

- Playthrough (rollover): How many times a bonus must be wagered before winnings can be withdrawn. Most sportsbooks use 1x.

- Expiry: The window to use a bonus bet or reset before it is forfeited, often 7 days.

- Qualifying bet: The first real-money wager that unlocks the welcome offer.

Do I need a promo code to claim an offer?

Not always. Some sportsbook promos apply automatically through the correct sign-up link, while others require a promo code during registration. Check the offer’s terms before depositing or placing a qualifying bet.

What are bonus bets and how do they work?

Bonus bets are promotional credits awarded after you meet a sportsbook’s qualifying requirements. If a $100 bonus bet wins at +100 odds, you receive $100 in winnings, but not the original $100 stake.

What is the difference between a bet-and-get and a first-bet safety net?

A bet-and-get sportsbook promo awards bonus bets after you place a qualifying wager, often whether you win or lose. A first-bet safety net refunds a losing first wager in bonus bets, up to a stated limit, rather than returning the stake as cash.

Are there any no-deposit sportsbook bonuses?

No-deposit sportsbook bonuses are rare at regulated U.S. sportsbooks, which usually require a deposit and qualifying bet. Some offshore sportsbooks may advertise them, but read the terms carefully because they can include strict playthrough, minimum-odds and withdrawal requirements.

Can I claim promos at more than one sportsbook?

Yes! You can claim as many promos as you are eligible for. You just need to adhere to all of the rules, but this is a great way to get the most out of your money and the offers available.

Do bonus bets and bet resets expire?

Yes. Bonus bets and bet resets usually have expiration dates, so check the sportsbook’s terms and use them before the deadline or they may be forfeited.

Are sportsbook promos worth it?

Sportsbook promos are worth it as protection for your bet or as an added incentive for your continued use of their book. It does often require you to use your own money, but the promotions tend to give you better odds than the normal payouts, or give you free bets to make.

Are these promos available in my state?

Sportsbook promos and legal betting apps vary by state, so an offer available in one location may not be offered in another. Confirm that the sportsbook is legal where you live and check its current terms before signing up. Check each sportsbook for more information.
