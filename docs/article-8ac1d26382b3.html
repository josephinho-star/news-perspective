Former deputy prime minister blames Erdoğan for fund scandal

2026-11-25 16:27

Former Turkish deputy prime minister Ali Babacan said Wednesday that President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan bore political responsibility for Turkey’s investment fund scandal and urged him to release a list of people linked to the case.

Babacan, who now leads the opposition Democracy and Progress Party (DEVA), told lawmakers that Erdoğan had appointed many of the officials now under scrutiny. He argued that the alleged fraud could not have lasted as long as it did without help from senior bureaucrats and figures in the government.

“Why are you still hiding that list?” Babacan asked at a meeting of the New Path group, an alliance of three opposition parties in parliament. He said officials had told him they gave the list to Erdoğan and called for a parliamentary inquiry into the alleged political links.

A day earlier, Erdoğan’s Justice and Development Party (AKP) and its ally, the Nationalist Movement Party (MHP), blocked a motion to investigate lawmakers’ dealings with the funds. Erdoğan said last week that those responsible would be held to account regardless of their position.

Babacan also urged Turkey’s central bank to withdraw 50 and 200-lira banknotes bearing the signature of former officials who have faced criminal investigations.

The crisis began in September when some investment funds could no longer pay investors who asked to withdraw their money. Turkey’s Capital Markets Board (SPK) ordered 131 funds to sell off their assets, affecting nearly half a million investors. Prosecutors suspect the funds were used to inflate stock prices.
