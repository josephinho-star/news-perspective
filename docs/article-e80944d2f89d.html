Turkish rescue group founder convicted over coup post, released after 2 months

2026-09-16 14:33

A Turkish court on Wednesday convicted prominent mountaineer and search-and-rescue pioneer Nasuh Mahruki of inciting hatred in a social media post questioning the official account of a failed coup in 2016, handing him a suspended one-year sentence and ordering his release after two months in pretrial detention, the Anka news agency reported.

The Bakırköy 57th Criminal Court of First Instance in İstanbul convicted Mahruki at the first hearing of his trial. The court suspended the sentence and ordered his release, rejecting the prosecutor’s request that he remain in custody.

Mahruki had been jailed since July 17 on a charge of “inciting the public to hatred and hostility or degrading the public” under Article 216 of the Turkish Penal Code. The indictment had sought a prison sentence of up to four-and-a-half years.

The charge stemmed from a post Mahruki published on X on July 15, the 10th anniversary of the abortive coup.

He described the events as a “controlled coup attempt,” alleging that the plot had been detected in advance but was deliberately allowed to proceed because it offered an opportunity to change Turkey’s system of government.

Mahruki also accused the United States of being the main architect of the plot, using a chess metaphor to claim that Washington had sacrificed a bishop to capture a queen, “made [President Recep Tayyip] Erdoğan king and checkmated the Turkish people.”

The Bakırköy Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office described his claims as baseless and ordered his detention shortly after the post was published. Mahruki was taken into custody at his İstanbul home and jailed pending trial two days later.

At Wednesday’s hearing Mahruki rejected the charge and said the words “theater” and “game,” which had been attributed to him during the proceedings, did not appear in his post.

“I do not know what offense I am accused of,” Mahruki told the court, according to Turkish media reports. He asked whether his post had caused any public unrest and denied seeking to incite hatred.

Mahruki said he had founded the Search and Rescue Association (AKUT) at the age of 26 and had spent his life helping people.

“How could I incite the public to hatred and hostility?” he said. “There is no such thing in my character or nature.”

His lawyers claimed that the legal requirements for a conviction under Article 216 had not been met and said his remarks should be protected as political speech.

The prosecutor acknowledged that the words “theater” and “game” did not appear in Mahruki’s post but maintained that the offense had been committed and requested his continued detention.

The court instead convicted Mahruki, gave him a suspended sentence and ordered his release.

Turkey’s government blames the faith-based Gülen movement, inspired by the late US-based cleric Fethullah Gülen, for the July 15, 2016, coup attempt, in which 251 people were killed. The movement strongly denies involvement in the attempted coup or any terrorist activity.

Questions about what state officials knew before the coup attempt and their response to intelligence warnings are politically sensitive. Critics accuse Erdoğan of using the abortive putsch to purge opponents, weaken the military and consolidate his power.

In the aftermath of the coup attempt, the government declared a state of emergency and carried out a sweeping purge of the civil service, judiciary, military and police. Tens of thousands of people were arrested, while nearly 130,000 public servants were removed from their jobs under emergency decrees.

Mahruki is the founder and former president of AKUT, a volunteer organization that became nationally prominent for its rescue work following two devastating earthquakes in northwestern Turkey in 1999. He is also a well-known mountaineer and government critic.

Mahruki was previously arrested in November 2024 over social media posts accusing Turkey’s election authorities of preparing to undermine election security by introducing electronic voting.

He was released after spending 14 days in pretrial detention. An İstanbul court in February 2025 handed down a suspended sentence for “publicly disseminating misleading information.”
