California teacher watchdog let accused child molester keep working with kids for five years: report

2026-11-20 01:30

A California elementary school counselor accused of sexually abusing young girls was allowed to keep working with children for five more years — despite his school district reporting him to the state with detailed allegations, a damning investigation found.

David Braff, a former guidance counselor at McKevett Elementary School in Santa Paula, now faces trial on charges he sexually abused 15 students, 13 of whom attended Santa Paula Unified School District schools, according to an investigation by ProPublica and KQED.

Braff has pleaded not guilty and has remained behind bars since his November 2024 arrest.

Santa Paula Unified submitted a detailed report to the California Commission on Teacher Credentialing in summer 2019 after at least seven students accused Braff of inappropriate touching.

The 20-page report included summaries of interviews with the alleged victims and witnesses. Braff had resigned midway through the district’s investigation.

One fifth-grade girl alleged Braff locked his office door, grabbed her by the hips and forced her onto his lap. She said she cried before counseling sessions and eventually asked to transfer schools to get away from him, according to the report.

Two other girls, who did not know about each other’s allegations, separately accused him of touching them near their private parts.

One fourth-grader said Braff had been making her sit on his lap since second grade.

Yet the state commission never launched a full investigation, the outlets found.

Several girls who came forward said no one from the state ever contacted them, despite the district providing their contact information. Instead, the case was assigned to a staffer whose job did not include interviewing victims.

In December 2020, the commission’s Committee of Credentials — a volunteer panel — declined to recommend disciplinary action, meaning the full commission never reviewed the case.

He went on to work at three more Southern California schools before police arrested him in 2024.

A former student at the charter school later told police Braff touched his thigh near his groin during a meeting in his office, according to a court transcript.

Braff denied touching students near their private parts or making them sit on his lap when questioned by district administrators in 2019. He said he only gave side hugs, high-fives, fist bumps and taps on the knee.

His attorney declined to comment to ProPublica and KQED.

Following Braff’s arrest, one parent demanded answers from Santa Paula Unified Superintendent Jeffrey Weinstein, emailing him: “Who dropped the ball and allowed this monster to still be around kids?”

Weinstein pointed the finger at the state, saying the district had handed over all its evidence to the commission and police. He also said he strongly disagreed with the agency’s decision.

Braff was not the only educator whose alleged misconduct failed to trigger meaningful state action, the investigation found.

Romoland middle school teacher Brashawn Washington allegedly sent an 11-year-old student a video showing another boy’s genitals. The state was notified in 2020, but Washington was later hired at Pasadena High School and remained employed until after his 2021 arrest.

He is now serving more than eight years in prison.

In another case, Bay Area teacher Matthew Shelton had faced allegations of molesting students years before he was convicted last month of abusing four students in Benicia. He received only a confidential warning, which was later expunged.

The California Post has reached out to the Commission on Teacher Credentialing for comment.
