‘Delivery Protection Act’ only protects the Teamsters union

2026-11-17 23:40

Evidence keeps rolling in that the so-called Delivery Protection Act should really be named the Teamsters Protection Racket.

The bill before the City Council targets Amazon by outlawing private-sector use of last-mile-delivery subcontractors, but one of them (DashWave Delivery) points out that city agencies (Homeless Services, Aging, Transportation) depend on their own contractors to deliver meals, haul construction materials and similar work.

So it’s not that contracting for delivery is evil, it’s that the Teamsters Union wants its members to control the private market — and never mind that this would drive up costs and so harm affordability.

But the union makes progressive anti-Amazon noises, and that’s enough for the likes of Councilwoman Tiffany Cabán, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and so on.

Never mind that this would kill 5,000 good-paying delivery jobs, plus another 2,000 lost when Amazon closes its NYC warehouses rather than bend to the prog thugs and union goons.

The big, bad corporation will be fine; the losers will be those workers and their employers — largely minority mom-and-pop entrepreneurs who built their livelihoods from scratch.

Far from sweatshops, these local operations pay up to $24.75 an hour, complete with health benefits, 401(k) matches, paid vacation and real opportunities for growth.

The perversity of this “protection” push convinced Councilman Phil Wong (D-Queens) to switch from sponsoring the bill to opposing it; National Urban League President Marc Morial rightly warns that the measure would deal a devastating blow to “black and brown New Yorkers.”

Wary of calling out the perversity of this progressive push, Council Speaker Julie Menin is trying to amend the Delivery Protection Act into a less destructive form.

Better to just kill the bill before it destroys thousands of jobs and boosts costs for average New Yorkers in an act of outright economic injustice.
