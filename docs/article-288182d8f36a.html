Army detonates explosives in Iowa amid toxic lead risks

2026-11-25 15:17

The Army detonated a set of explosives in Iowa on Wednesday, a controversial move that is expected to spew toxic lead into the air.

The government has said the detonation was necessary to prevent an accidental explosion, though critics have questioned why the situation was allowed to get to the point where lead azide-containing weapons came to be at risk for exploding in the first place.

Officials have said they would implement mitigation measures, including a “water wall” aimed at absorbing the impacts and reducing the amount of lead to which the public will be exposed.

“Early reports from Iowa are that the Army’s mitigation efforts have far exceeded expectations,” President Trump said on social media.

Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Administrator Lee Zeldin told CNN he was receiving reports that there was “zero detection” of lead at this point on the agency’s air sensors and that the blast “caved inward perfectly.”

“It was the Army’s efforts, with the mitigation efforts to try to drive this into the ground, the detonation that is, rather than driving the detonation into the air,” Zeldin said.

Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said the detonation “exceeded all expectations.”

“The threat of an uncontrolled spontaneous detonation has been eliminated,” Telle said.

Locals and public health experts have raised concerns about the situation — warning that exposure to lead can be hazardous, especially to children.

Exposure to lead in children can cause brain damage, nervous system damage, learning problems, behavioral issues and a lower IQ. In rare cases, it can also cause seizures, coma and death, according to the EPA.

In adults, lead exposure can cause decreased kidney function, heart-related issues and fertility issues.

Lead exposure can also harm a developing fetus. In pregnant women, lead exposure can cause early birth or low birth weight, harm fetal development, and increase the risk of a miscarriage or stillbirth.

“The potential fallout could span multiple states and has forced Americans to evacuate their homes the day before Thanksgiving,” House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries (N.Y.) said in a statement. “Much remains unknown about how many people will be impacted by the explosion, the potential damage to crops and agriculture, the possible harms to clean air and clean water in the surrounding area and the long-term health impacts for the community.

“This administration has repeatedly proven that they cannot be trusted to guard the health, safety and well-being of everyday Americans,” Jeffries added.

Officials recommended evacuations for people in Middletown, Iowa, where the detonation took place, as well as in a 5-mile cone southwest.

Officials have also said the EPA would be on-site with air-monitoring systems during the detonation. The EPA is also expected to conduct lead sampling in the impacted area in the aftermath.
