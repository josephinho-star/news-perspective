Fernando Mendoza’s Raiders bromance is still going strong, as postgame phone call shows

2026-11-19 19:24

The Raiders traded Kirk Cousins to Minnesota. Nobody told Fernando Mendoza he had to lose his number.

So after Mendoza made his first NFL start Sunday against the Seahawks, he did what you might expect a young quarterback to do after spending the better part of eight months learning the job alongside an older one. He talked to Kirk.

Turns out the kid wanted to talk about how the old guy did, too.

“Yeah, we talked after both of the Raiders and the Vikings games on Sunday, and really proud of him and what he accomplished,” Mendoza said.

That last part is worth a second read. Mendoza had just experienced a day he’d imagined for most of his life. He scored a touchdown of his own on his opening drive. He got sacked six times. There was enough material in his afternoon alone to keep two quarterbacks on the phone until somebody in Minnesota had to get up and shovel the driveway.

And what did Mendoza want people in Las Vegas to know about the conversation? He was proud of Cousins.

For most of their time together, you could pretty well guess which way that sentiment traveled. Cousins was the veteran starter. Mendoza was the first overall pick who would eventually take his job. Every small victory for the rookie, even something as simple as recognizing a coverage in practice that had fooled him the day before, was the kind of thing an older brother could take some pride in.

Somewhere along the way, the younger brother started taking some pride in him, too.

“It’s great. He’s awesome. He’s been a great mentor of mine, and evidently he’s in a different building,” Mendoza said. “And I still lean on him, and still lean on all the great mentors that I have in this building with the coaching staff and other players and leaders on our team.”

There’s something to be said for what Cousins gave Mendoza while they shared a quarterback room in Las Vegas. The rookie could watch how he worked, ask him a question, then come back the next morning with three more.

There’s something even sweeter about what Cousins can give him now. His time is entirely his to offer, and Mendoza trusts him enough to keep asking for it.

The Raiders have a game to prepare for in Denver. The Vikings have their own business to attend to up in the frozen north. For the first time in months, these two can go an entire workweek without either one having much idea how the other guy is doing.

Which gives them one more reason to catch up on Sunday.
