Turkey jails 5 investment executives as 455,758 investors await fund payouts

2026-09-23 12:04

A Turkish court jailed five investment executives on Wednesday over alleged stock manipulation as 455,758 investors awaited payouts from the liquidation of 131 funds managing TL 891 billion ($18.3 billion) in assets.

The five include Serdar Turhan, chairman of the Pusula finance group, and Emre Tezmen, chairman of the Tera investment group.

Three other Tera executives were jailed in the same ruling.

The decision brings the number jailed pending trial in the case to nine.

Police also detained 14 more people on Tuesday as the investigation grew.

Prosecutors are examining alleged manipulation of shares in Katılımevim, a company linked to Pusula.

Katılımevim offers plans that let customers save and make payments toward a home or vehicle without a conventional bank loan.

Turkey’s Capital Markets Board (SPK), the stock market regulator, said Wednesday that 455,758 people have money in the funds marked for closure.

It counted each investor once, even if that person owned shares in more than one fund.

The official count is more than 100,000 above an earlier estimate of about 353,000.

Seven investment firms ran the 131 funds, which held more than $18 billion in assets before the shutdown, Reuters reported.

Some of the funds could not meet requests from investors who wanted to take out their money last week.

The funds had bought large amounts of shares that were hard to sell quickly.

To raise cash, some managers sold other stocks, adding to losses across Turkey’s stock market, analysts told Reuters.

The main stock index fell more than 8 percent last week.

The regulator halted trading in the 131 funds and ordered them shut down.

Two banks will sell the funds’ assets and use the proceeds to pay investors.

The regulator extended the deadline for that process from three months to six months, but the amount each investor will recover is not yet known.
