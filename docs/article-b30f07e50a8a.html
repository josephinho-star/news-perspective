Aaron Glenn makes stunning Jets change after ‘superpower’ boast backfires

2026-11-16 21:21

With his defense struggling and the losses piling up, Jets coach Aaron Glenn made a big change Sunday.

Glenn surrendered defensive play-calling duties to Brian Duker, the team’s defensive coordinator, in the loss to the Bills.

The move is stunning because Glenn took over play-calling in the offseason and called it his “superpower.” But the Jets are currently 22nd in points allowed and 30th in rushing yards against. They have only held an opponent under 20 points twice in 10 games this season.

tRY IT NOWGlenn said he did not remove himself completely from calling the defense, but he put Duker in charge.

“I gave Duker the lead when it comes to that,” Glenn said Monday. “Again, I’m still a huge part of what we call. It’s been a collaboration throughout the whole season, to be honest with you. I had the lead in the games up to this point. This game, I wanted to give him the lead. If there’s anything that I wanted to get called, I would definitely get on the headset and let him know it. If there were some calls I didn’t like, I would let him know it.”

The move worked pretty well … for a half. The Jets held the Bills to 10 points in the first half. Then, the Bills hit three big plays in the second half and scored three touchdowns. The Jets lost 31-12 and the defense gave up 30 or more points for the third straight week.

Glenn opened the door to a change last week, saying he would consider anything to help the Jets turn things around.

“Anything that we as a staff, anything that I can do as a head coach, can do to help us win games, I’m willing to do it and I’m not afraid to do that,” Glenn said. “I gave him the lead but I was very involved in what we ran.”

The Jets are 2-8 and have lost five straight games. It seems likely that Glenn and his staff will be fired at the end of the season unless the Jets pull off a miraculous turnaround.

The Jets travel to Los Angeles this week to face a Chargers team that is winless entering Monday night’s game with the Ravens. Glenn would not say whether Duker will call the defense again this week.

“That’s something that we’ll see moving forward,” Glenn said. “I don’t want to sit there and say that this is how it’s going to be from each week going out. But again, I’m going to do whatever it takes for us to go out there and try to win a game.”

Jets safety Dane Belton said the move to Duker did not affect the players.

“I don’t think there was a difference,” Belton said. “I think those guys work very closely together. For me, it was just going out and completing my job. Getting the play call and communicating to everyone and making sure we’re on the same page and just executing. As far as the play call itself, I think they’re very similar. I think they have very similar mindsets in calling the defense. I think there was not much difference from my perspective.”

Glenn was the Lions defensive coordinator from 2021-24 and his success in Detroit was one of the reasons the Jets wanted to hire him as their head coach in 2025.

Glenn took a CEO approach in his first season and defensive coordinator Steve Wilks called the defense. Glenn fired Wilks during the season last year. He decided to call the defense himself this year and hired Duker, who he worked with in Detroit, as his defensive coordinator.

Glenn praised Duker’s performance Sunday.

“I thought he was calm,” Glenn said. “I thought he got the calls out to [Demario Davis] in a good amount of time. I thought some of the calls he made were really good calls. As I’ve always said, calls and execution is something that goes hand in hand. That’s something that offense, defense and special teams, we’ve got to improve on.”
