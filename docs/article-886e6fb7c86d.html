53 newly elected House Democrats tell Harvard they will not cross a union picket line to attend orientation event

Fifty three newly elected members of Congress told Harvard university on Tuesday that they have decided to boycott an informal orientation program at Harvard’s Kennedy School next month in support of the school’s striking graduate students union.

In a letter to the university’s administrators, the incoming House Democrats expressed thanks for their invitations to the Bipartisan Program for Newly Elected Members of Congress, in which over 800 members have participated in since 1972.

“However, as much as we would like to attend, we have been informed that to attend your event we would be required to cross a union picket line,” the new lawmakers wrote. “From our understanding, thousands of UAW members who belong to the Harvard Graduate Students Union (HGSU-UAW Local 5118) are on strike, seeking basic provisions like a cost of living increase. In addition, thousands of members of the Harvard Academic Workers (HAW-UAW) have been bargaining for a first contract for over two years and recently went on a week-long strike.”

“While we appreciate the invitation to Harvard’s Institute of Politics, we will not cross a picket line. So long as these workers are on strike, any trip we take to Harvard would be to stand in solidarity with our striking union siblings,” they added.

Three of the new House members, Sam Forstag of Montana, Clare Valdez of New York and Bob Brooks of Pennsylvania, posted a social media video statement in support of the striking workers.

“For some of those workers at Harvard, it’s been more than two years since they organized that they have been fighting just to get a contract with basic protections against harassment at the workplace, basic discrimination protections and a cost of living raise to $50,000 a year,” Forstag said in the video. “Harvard has an endowment of $68 billion; they can afford to pay people a living wage. Our brothers and sisters there deserve a fair contract.”

“So to all the workers at Harvard, we’ve got your back,” he added. “And to the administration at Harvard. well, if me and so many other members of Congress are going to be making a trip out there in December, it’s going to be to join our brothers and sisters on the picket line.”

“This freshman class stands with the working class,” Valdez added in a social media post. “Solidarity to my @UAW @hgsuuaw siblings at Harvard who are holding the line for what every worker deserves: living wages, workplace protections, and dignity on the job.”

Incoming Iowa senator promises to investigate Iowa Army Ammunition plant failures

Senator-elect Josh Turek, an Iowa Democrat, promised on Tuesday to investigate the failures at the Iowa Army Ammunition plant (IAAAP) which made it necessary for the military to carry out a controlled detonation of unstable, degraded lead azide, scheduled for Wednesday.

“I just got off a briefing with U.S. Army leaders. The detonation of degraded lead azide is scheduled to go forward tomorrow morning around 9:00 am. Unfortunately, experts believe that due to the degraded state of the lead azide, a controlled detonation is the only viable solution,” Turek said in a statement.

“It’s clear: the materials at IAAAP should never have been allowed to degrade to this point, and the delay we demanded was important to give Iowans more time to prepare and learn how to best keep themselves safe. I’ve repeatedly reiterated that Iowans’ health and safety must be the number one priority in the execution of this operation, and that transparency and clear communication – both of which have been sorely lacking up to this point – are paramount,” he continued.

“I will continue asking questions about how we got here,” Turek added. “There is no excuse for such a failure of transparency and communication, especially when the health and safety of the public is involved. Iowans should have had a full and clear understanding of the health implications of this operation, especially as children, pregnant women, and those with chronic illness are being advised to stay away from the affected area. I will be using my position in the U.S. Senate to get answers as to why these failures took place, including investigations and hearings. Iowans, as well as the American people, deserve accountability and the peace of mind to know that this will never happen again.”

Van Hollen welcomes agreement to allow Kilmar Ábrego Garcia to leave US freely

Senator Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat who traveled to El Salvador last year to visit Kilmar Ábrego Garcia, the Maryland resident wrongfully deported to a high-security prison there, welcomed the federal government’s agreement to allow Ábrego Garcia to leave the US voluntarily on Tuesday.

“A year and a half ago, the Trump Administration wanted to leave Kilmar Ábrego Garcia to rot in a Salvadoran gulag. Today, Kilmar is traveling to a country of his choice, a free man – unharmed and with dignity,” Van Hollen said in a statement.

“While the Trump Administration had been pursuing their vindictive prosecution against Kilmar and desperately trying to ship him against his will to countries known for human rights abuses, the Administration was forced to come to this agreement that respects Kilmar’s will and the due process rights guaranteed to him and to all in our nation. I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free – including from this Administration’s constant persecution,” the senator added.

“We must never back down in standing up for what’s right, and I will continue fighting day in and day out for the rights of every person in our country,” Van Hollen, who was ridiculed by the administration for his advocacy on behalf of the undocumented father. “This is a victory for due process and our Constitution.”

After Van Hollen successfully pressured the Salvadoran government into allowing him to meet Ábrego García in April of last year, the senator accused the government of El Salvador of creating a hoax he called “Margarita-gate”, by placing a pair of cocktail glasses on the table between himself and Ábrego García as they met at a hotel, to make it look as though they were enjoying drinks.

Those photographs were then posted on X by El Salvador’s president, Nayib Bukele, along with a caption that downplayed the seriousness of the situation by falsely claiming that the senator and the wrongly deported man had been “sipping margaritas” as they met on Thursday evening.

But the senator said that the drinks were placed there during the meeting by someone from the Salvadoran government before the photographs were taken and that neither he nor Ábrego García had touched them. Van Hollen pointed out that there was visual evidence for this in the photographs: the rims of both glasses were covered in salt or sugar, but it was clear from the images that neither glass had been drunk from, since the rims were undisturbed.

Van Hollen then posted a photo of the meeting on X that was taken before the glasses were placed there, in which there were just cups of coffee and glasses of water on the table.

“This is a lesson into the lengths that president Bukele” will go to, Van Hollen said, “to deceive people about what’s going on”.

The next month, the false claim was repeated by Marco Rubio, the secretary of state, who insisted that, despite a lack of evidence, that Ábrego García was one of multiple “gang members” the administration had deported to El Salvador. “We deported gang members,” Rubio told a Senate hearing after Van Hollen said he regretted voting to confirm him. “Gang members, including the one you had a margarita with,” Rubio said.

Democrat Victoria Broderick has won Tennessee’s fourth congressional district, unseating eight-term Republican incumbent Scott DesJarlais in one of the biggest House upsets of the cycle. The victory expands Democrats’ historic House majority in the 120th Congress to 259 seats, the party’s largest advantage in three decades, and flips a seat that Donald Trump carried by more than 20 points in 2024.

Broderick, 34, a former technology and logistics professional who previously ran for Congress in 2024, told the Guardian in an interview before her race was called that she connected with constituents who had not voted in years and met Republicans concerned about affordability who appreciated the “integrity” she brought to the race.

“We’ve been neglected in this district,” she said earlier this month. “People want representatives who understand what they are going through, who understand the costs of going to the gas station or grocery store. I’m that representative.”

Tennessee’s congressional map was redrawn as part of the GOP’s mid-decade gerrymandering effort before this year’s midterm elections, and election forecasters had rated the district as “solidly Republican”. Yet Broderick prevailed by fewer than 400 votes, according to the Associated Press race call. DesJarlais, 62, who aligned himself closely with Trump during his time in office, conceded to Broderick last week after the razor-thin contest but the race was officially called on Tuesday.

'We don't know' if Jensen Huang is a national security risk, former US commerce secretary says

One day after the US commerce department added the Singapore-based company Megaspeed to a trade blacklist over concerns that it the firm might have purchased advanced AI chips on behalf of the Chinese military, a former commerce secretary, Gina Raimondo, said the question of whether Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, who had an affair with a Megaspeed executive suspected of links to Chinese intelligence, is a national security risk.

Raimondo, who oversaw export controls on advanced chips that were imposed on China during the Biden administration, told public radio in Boston that the export ban was important to US national security.

“In order to protect America and Americans from China, we need to stay ahead of them on artificial intelligence,” Raimondo told WBUR’s Here and Now on Tuesday. “And that means to the extent that we have AI semiconductor chips that are more sophisticated and more powerful than theirs, we need to deny them those chips because we know that China is trying to have access to our chips to put into their military, to use against us for AI-powered cyberattacks, to put into surveillance.”

The former commerce secretary said that imposing exports controls was a “very difficult” decisions because it does “involve denying US companies revenue,” but “I always felt that national security was the North Star. And frankly, we couldn’t put a price on it.”

Suspicions that Megaspeed might have been used to evade export controls, Raimondo added, is based on the fact that this is an “extremely common technique by the Chinese government and Chinese military: they will set up shell companies, front companies outside of China to get around our export controls.”

“They have a whole web, whole network of these front companies, all over the world, not just in Asia,” Raimondo added. “And so the US government, including the commerce department, is constantly trying to get intelligence on these companies. And when we have the enough evidence, we’ll put them on the entity list.”

“I do think Jensen is a genius, I stand by that,” Raimondo said. “I had a very professional and productive working relationship with him. And Nvidia is a company that matters a lot to America. That being said, this is America. I don’t care if you’re penniless or the founder and CEO of one of the most valuable companies on the planet, you need to be held to the same standards.”

“Right now, the FBI has an open investigation; the SEC is investigating; Congress is planning to investigate,” she added. “So let’s just let the process play out right. Like, let’s do the investigation. Let’s determine if, in fact, Jensen or anybody else did anything wrong. We don’t know until we fully investigate, and I would say we can’t allow those processes to be subverted because no one should be allowed to put America’s national security at risk.”

US intelligence agencies recently suffered cybersecurity breach

The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) recently discovered a cybersecurity breach that exposed some of their unclassified information, US officials and other people familiar with the matter have told the New York Times (paywall).

Per the NYT story: “The intrusions affected an open-source intelligence analysis system, a computer network that looks for intelligence insights in public, unclassified material, the people familiar with the issues said.

“It was not clear specifically what information was compromised, but classified material was not jeopardized, the people said. Information at other government agencies was also believed to have been exposed in the breach, according to two of the people.”

The Times’s sources did not say whether investigators knew who was responsible for the breach.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologizes to staff: ‘I made some terrible mistakes’

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to staff today for the tumult he’s caused at the company. He acknowledged he violated the code of conduct for having extramarital affairs, but pushed back on the reports that Donald Trump was instrumental in his reinstatement as chief executive.

“I made some terrible mistakes,” he said, according to a livestream reviewed by the New York Times. “I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and, of course, I hurt all of you.”

The apology came one day after Huang returned as CEO of Nvidia, and less than two weeks after he was ousted from the role. Following a whiplash series of events, the company’s board of directors reinstated Huang yesterday, despite his admission that he’d had an affair. News reports allege the CEO had romantic relationships with two company employees and a suspected Chinese spy.

During today’s apology, Huang told staffers he was in therapy and that it was “not acceptable” that he violated the company’s code of conduct. In an impromptu speech to employees yesterday, Huang said he was “terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this” and that he’s “going to be a better CEO the second time around”.

While Huang apologized for his affairs, he rejected narratives that he was a “tech bro” who was “above the law”, according to the New York Times. He added that it was “completely untrue” that Trump pressured Nvidia’s board to reinstate him. The board changed course, he said, because it “evaluated the entire situation” and “gathered facts”.

Nvidia did not immediately respond to request for comment.

Republican Dan Sullivan concedes Alaska election loss to Democrat Mary Peltola

GOP senator Dan Sullivan said in a post to X that he accepts the results of the Alaska Senate race, which was won by Mary Peltola, who became the first Democrat the state has elected to the Senate since 2008.

“Serving Alaska in the United States Senate for the past 12 years has been the honor of a lifetime, and I thank all Alaskans for that opportunity,” he wrote.

As I’ve always said I would, and out of respect for the people of Alaska and our democratic process, I accept the results certified by the Division of Elections.

Alaskans deserve confidence in the integrity of their elections, and I trust that the ongoing federal and state investigations will provide answers to the questions that remain.

Peltola faced significant controversy on the campaign trail, after the New York Times reported that her staffers were involved in a scheme to recruit another Republican challenger, also named Dan Sullivan, to appear on the ballot.

Peltola’s campaign had denied any involvement in encouraging the second Sullivan’s run, and after state election officials tried to remove him, Alaska’s supreme court in June ruled he be allowed to appear on the ballot.

Trump called for Peltola to be prosecuted over the apparent ruse, and Alaska’s top federal prosecutor announced that criminal and civil inquiries were under way, while the state’s governor separately announced plans to open a criminal investigation into the alleged scheme.

Meanwhile, in a legal filing on Thursday, the Trump administration asked a federal appeals court to dismiss the lawsuit it filed before the midterm elections seeking to block the certification of the US Senate race in Alaska should Peltola win.
