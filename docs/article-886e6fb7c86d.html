Trump’s DoJ ‘looked into’ concerns about Jensen Huang after alleged affair with Chinese spy but no security risk found – live

2026-11-24 19:29

Justice department and other agencies reached ‘collective consensus’ that Jensen Huang is not a national security risk, attorney general Todd Blanche says

Blanche: DoJ 'looked into' concerns about Jensen Huang's alleged affair with Chinese spy

Todd Blanche, the attorney general, defended his department’s activity related to Nvidia and Jensen Huang on Tuesday, telling reporters that they “looked into” concerns related to Huang’s alleged affair with a Chinese spy.

“What I said last week is that we were looking into it,” Blanche said during a press conference. “The FBI, law enforcement looks into things all the time. That doesn’t necessarily become a criminal investigation; it doesn’t necessarily become an indictment; it just literally is that. It’s looking into things.”

Blanche largely declined to give reporters detailed information about the Justice Department’s (DOJ) probe into Huang and Nvidia. But he insisted Nvidia is a “good corporate citizen” and that the DOJ had no information indicating Huang had committed a crime.

“To the extent that the Department of Justice had information that [Huang] was committing crimes or something like this… that’s something I would have certainly told the president had it been true,” Blanche said.

But the attorney general became defensive when reporters pressed him about the Justice Department’s process for ensuring Huang had not violated any laws.

“What does that mean, ‘Had Huang been investigated?’ For what?… I don’t understand the question,” Blanche said in response to a reporter’s question.

At another point, Blanche told a reporter: “I don’t know what folks are asking about specifically… I don’t, I mean, you’re mocking me, but when you say ‘get to the bottom of what is there,’ what are you asking me about?”

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologizes to staff: ‘I made some terrible mistakes’

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to staff today for the tumult he’s caused at the company. He acknowledged he violated the code of conduct for having extramarital affairs, but pushed back on the reports that Donald Trump was instrumental in his reinstatement as chief executive.

“I made some terrible mistakes,” he said, according to a livestream reviewed by the New York Times. “I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and, of course, I hurt all of you.”

The apology came one day after Huang returned as CEO of Nvidia, and less than two weeks after he was ousted from the role. Following a whiplash series of events, the company’s board of directors reinstated Huang yesterday, despite his admission that he’d had an affair. News reports allege the CEO had romantic relationships with two company employees and a suspected Chinese spy.

During today’s apology, Huang told staffers he was in therapy and that it was “not acceptable” that he violated the company’s code of conduct. In an impromptu speech to employees yesterday, Huang said he was “terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this” and that he’s “going to be a better CEO the second time around”.

While Huang apologized for his affairs, he rejected narratives that he was a “tech bro” who was “above the law”, according to the New York Times. He added that it was “completely untrue” that Trump pressured Nvidia’s board to reinstate him. The board changed course, he said, because it “evaluated the entire situation” and “gathered facts”.

Nvidia did not immediately respond to request for comment.

Republican Dan Sullivan concedes Alaska election loss to Democrat Mary Peltola

GOP senator Dan Sullivan said in a post to X that he accepts the results of the Alaska Senate race, which was won by Mary Peltola, who became the first Democrat the state has elected to the Senate since 2008.

“Serving Alaska in the United States Senate for the past 12 years has been the honor of a lifetime, and I thank all Alaskans for that opportunity,” he wrote.

As I’ve always said I would, and out of respect for the people of Alaska and our democratic process, I accept the results certified by the Division of Elections.

Alaskans deserve confidence in the integrity of their elections, and I trust that the ongoing federal and state investigations will provide answers to the questions that remain.

Peltola faced significant controversy on the campaign trail, after the New York Times reported that her staffers were involved in a scheme to recruit another Republican challenger, also named Dan Sullivan, to appear on the ballot.

Peltola’s campaign had denied any involvement in encouraging the second Sullivan’s run, and after state election officials tried to remove him, Alaska’s supreme court in June ruled he be allowed to appear on the ballot.

Trump called for Peltola to be prosecuted over the apparent ruse, and Alaska’s top federal prosecutor announced that criminal and civil inquiries were under way, while the state’s governor separately announced plans to open a criminal investigation into the alleged scheme.

The US army said it was working to keep toxic lead dust to a minimum when it detonates nearly 10,000 pounds of degraded lead azide in Iowa tomorrow, as it races to pre-empt a spontaneous explosion.

At a press conference earlier today, acting secretary of the US army Adam Telle told reporters that the detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, was projected to take place at 9am local timeonWednesday, when the weather forecast would be “ideal to protect public health and safety across the region”.

Federal officials from the army, the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives and other agencies, said extensive safety precautions and mitigation measures are being taken to protect the public. They also recommended that anyone in the immediate path of the expected plume evacuate.

Further to that, the Federal Emergency Management Agency (Fema) released an alert today saying residents within 5 miles of the blast may be evacuated.

Residents within 5 to 20 miles of the detonation were advised to stay indoors with windows closed to avoid lead dust exposure.

The army also released new maps showing forecasts of the possible dispersal of lead dust both on surfaces and in the air, extending southwest from Middletown into northern Missouri, Reuters reported.

Dust could fall onto surfaces as far as 40 miles from the center of the blast, the projections show. But airborne particles could travel significantly farther, reaching close to 100 miles away eight hours after the detonation, according to the latest maps.

The army said it would release updated projection maps based on forecasted weather conditions every hour starting six hours before the scheduled explosion, as well as a final estimate of the affected area afterward.

DHS secretary Markwayne Mullinsaid this morning that he had been briefed and had directed Fema “to provide all needed support to ensure the safety and security of the people of Iowa”.

Democrats flip Tennessee's fourth district, bringing number of House seats to 259

Victoria Broderick has officially won election in Tennessee’s fourth congressional district, after the Associated Press called the race this morning.

Her win delivers Democrats their 259th seat in the House and marks an extraordinary upset, ousting Republican incumbent Scott DesJarlais, who was seeking his ninth term.

“It’s very surreal,” she said a few days after election day. The prominent US election forecaster Cook Political Report rated the seat as a “solid Republican” district, and almost nobody in Washington thought DesJarlais could lose. “I think the first words to my team were: ‘Wait, what?’ I was just sitting there shocked,” she added.

According to the AP, Broderick won by a narrow 361 votes, in a district Trump carried by 24 points in 2024.

Analysis: the Nvidia case is just the latest episode of Trump disregarding intelligence

Donald Trump’s unwillingness to heed warnings of suspected Chinese penetration of the tech giant Nvidia follows a pattern of the US president ignoring sensitive intelligence that contradicts his world view.

Trump disregarded repeated information that the company’s co-founder, Jensen Huang, was having an extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy that threatened to undermine Washington’s geo-strategically vital goal of pipping China for global supremacy in the artificial intelligence race.

The woman, Alice Huang – no blood relation of Jensen – was also responsible for buying $4.8bn worth of Nvidia’s highly sensitive AI chips in her capacity as head of a Singapore-based technology company, Megaspeed. The chips were then diverted to China, despite US export restrictions forbidding such sales to a country Washington regards as a key strategic adversary.

The president was inactive even after Marco Rubio, the secretary of state and national security – and one of the administration’s renowned “China hawks” – repackaged the intelligence into a classified report last May warning of the geopolitical risks posed by the relationship.

“It’s clear that the White House was warned, had adequate opportunities to take action against what was an emerging and then eventually very clear chip diversion network that was emerging under Megaspeed and other Southeast Asian offshore data farms – and it just failed to act,” said Julia Curlee, a former Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) officer who specialized in the Middle East and China and also worked on the White House national security council during Trump’s first presidency. She added:

It’s the latest episode of the president ignoring or not believing the intelligence that’s being provided to him.

Despite the intelligence concerns raised by his conduct, Huang was reinstated as Nvidia’s chief executive on Monday following pressure from the White House and the company’s shareholders.

He has also sold some of his stake in the company since returning to the presidency, and gained between $3.5m and $10.2m, according to his government disclosures.

The mayor said Huang “had a massive impact”, with Nvidia’s share price dropping dramatically when he stepped down as the company’s CEO on 12 November after a reported extramarital relationship with a suspected Chinese spy.

House Dems call for Jensen Huang to testify at public hearing

Democrat Gregory Meeks, the ranking member on the House foreign affairs committee, has called on Jensen Huang to testify at a public committee hearing following Donald Trump’s intervention to get him reinstated as Nvidia’s CEO and amid national security concerns raised by Huang’s efforts to increase and accelerate the export of high-end chips to China.

Meeks said in a statement: “Americans deserve answers to the profound questions that are continuing to emerge about NVIDIA and our national security. President Trump’s intervention to reinstate Jensen Huang as NVIDIA’s CEO raises even more concerning questions about the administration’s own role, questions the American people deserve to hear answered in public.

“For the past year, committee members have raised concerns about NVIDIA’s effort to weaken U.S. export restrictions on advanced chips sales to China, as well as widespread allegations regarding the diversion of NVIDIA chips from third countries to China. We have many questions surrounding the administration’s role in approving exports that have put America’s national security up for sale.

‘The American people deserve to hear publicly from Mr. Huang about how potentially billions of dollars’ worth of our most advanced technology wound up in the hands of our greatest competitor, and what role this administration played. A transcribed interview is critical to our oversight efforts but it should not replace a public hearing. Chairman Mast should convene one.”
