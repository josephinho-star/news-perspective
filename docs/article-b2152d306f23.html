Woman says she was shackled to hospital bed, interrogated for hours after stillbirth: suit

2026-11-20 19:27

A woman suing the city of Los Angeles, alleging she was falsely accused of throwing her fetus out the window of the city shelter where she lived, leaving the newborn to die in 2024, can proceed as a Jane Doe, according to court papers obtained today.

The plaintiff alleges in her Los Angeles Superior Court lawsuit that she was called a “baby killer” by fellow residents when she returned from a hospital to a city shelter where she lived. On Thursday, Judge Andrew Esbenshade granted Doe’s request to remain anonymous in her future pleadings.

“The court finds the showing of plaintiff’s overriding interest in anonymity to be sufficient to outweigh the right of the public to access plaintiff’s name and the complaint,” the judge’s clerk wrote in a minute order.

Doe alleges that she was wrongfully shackled to her hospital bed, interrogated by two Los Angeles Police Department detectives, threatened with criminal prosecution for homicide and verbally abused for hours while she was suffering the aftermath of a stillbirth.

The investigation continued for a year, which the lawsuit contends was a clear violation of the legislature’s intent in amending the Reproductive Privacy Act in 2022 to prohibit the state from taking punitive action against people based on their actions or omissions regarding pregnancies, miscarriages and stillbirths.

“Defendants blatantly violated Ms. Doe’s rights, causing her extreme and ongoing harm and mental duress,” the suit alleges.

A representative for the City Attorney’s Office did not immediately reply to a request for comment on the suit filed Wednesday. A case management conference is scheduled for March 1, 2027.
