Stream It Or Skip It: ‘Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story’ On Netflix, Ryan Murphy’s Latest Lurid Retelling of an Iconic True Crime Story

2026-09-17 18:00

Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story on Netflix is the fourth entry in the Monster anthology series created by Ryan Murphy and Ian Brennan. The previous seasons had their share of lurid-for-lurid’s-sake scenes and weird timeline shifts. Will we see them in the story of the Fall River, Massachusetts woman accused of killing her parents?

MONSTER: THE LIZZIE BORDEN STORY: STREAM IT OR SKIP IT?

Opening Shot: Stage curtains open and a toy piano with the name “LIZZIE” on it starts to play.

The Gist: We see Lizzie Borden (Ella Beatty) do exactly as the famous schoolyard rhyme about her said she did: She takes an axe and kills her stepmother Abby (Rebecca Hall) with what seems like “40 whacks”, and then kills her father Andrew (Charlie Hunnam) with what seems like the 41 whacks in the rhyme.

Four months earlier, Lizzie is living under the dictatorial reign of Abby, who constantly berates her for being stupid and keeps trying to marry her off to men who tell Lizzie she has a horse face. Abby is especially upset at how heavy Lizzie’s menses are, especially when it ruins her fainting couch.

After they impetuously fire their maid, Bridget Sullivan (Vicky Krieps) wins the job by cleaning the blood stain on the fainting couch. Lizzie is taken with how bold and brash Bridget — whom Abby and Andrew call “Margaret,” the name of their previous maid — is. Bridget knows that Lizzie is a special person, and she tells Lizzie the tale of Elizabeth Báthory (Krieps), who bathed in the blood of murdered virgins back in the late 1500s.

What Shows Will It Remind You Of? This is the fourth installment of Ryan Murphy and Ian Brennan’s Monster anthology series, following seasons about Jeffrey Dahmer, the Menendez bothers and Ed Gein.

Our Take: Like with all of the entries in the Monster anthology, Murphy and Brennan can’t seem to avoid lurid moments that seem to be unnecessary, like some gruesome visuals we see as Bridget tells Lizzie about Elizabeth Báthory. But, for the most part, the beginning of Lizzie Borden’s story is relatively restrained, and bolstered by the chemistry between Beatty and Krieps.

The leadup to the murders, as well as the sensational trial that eventually acquitted Borden of those killings, will be examined, centering on Lizzie’s relationship with Bridget as well as Abby’s dissatisfaction with her life in general and Lizzie in particular. The show will also veer to a more modern timeframe, with Murphy muse Sarah Paulson playing serial killer Aileen Wuornos. A very 1980s soundtrack is likely what will pull both timelines together.

If Brennan and Murphy, along with directors Max Winkler and Sarah Adina Smith, keep the relatively restrained approach we saw in the first episode, we’re going to enjoy seeing how things led to the axe murders and the trial. If the group starts to do what they tend to do, which is get silly, bloody and indulgent, then that’s a whole other matter.

Performance Worth Watching: Ella Beatty makes Lizzie Borden less creepy and more, well, weird, which puts us in the strange frame of mind to actually root for the woman who was accused of hacking her parents to death.

Sex And Skin: The nudity in the first episode is mostly confined to the story of Elizabeth Báthory, though sex is implied elsewhere.

Parting Shot: Lizzie and Bridget take a “blood bath” (it’s actually cherry juice) together in Lizzie’s dove sanctuary.

Our Call: STREAM IT. Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story tells Borden’s story without most of the gimmickry we see in Brennan and Murphy’s productions. There’s still time for things to fly off the rails, but we’re enjoying the relative restraint so far.
