Indianapolis Colts cut NFL interception leader Daniel Jones and give starting QB job to Anthony Richardson

2026-11-20 19:20

Indianapolis Colts coach Shane Steichen told reporters after Thursday night's depressing loss to the Houston Texans that Daniel Jones would be evaluated just like all the other players, and that evaluation came back Friday with the team cutting their starting quarterback.

Steichen made the announcement during his day-after press availability.

The coach also announced that former first-round pick Anthony Richardson will be the team's starting quarterback going forward. Richardson's first start in this new era will come against the New York Giants on Nov. 29.

"I'm excited to go see him play and go compete," Steichen said of his new starting quarterback. "And he brings a unique skill set and can run around and make some plays and I'm looking forward to see that."

Yeah, good. Richardson’s ability to scramble will come in handy behind a makeshift offensive line, and he’ll have to compete like the dickens to complete passes to a wide receiver corps that’s been without its best player all season due to injury—and lost several others at the trade deadline.

There is no good news here, folks. There may be satisfying news for Colts fans who grew weary of Jones. But otherwise, no great news.

Jones, by the way, is subject to waivers. That means any dumb team wishing to claim him can do so but it would cost.

Any dumb team awarded Jones on waivers would be responsible for the remaining $2.1 million in salary for this season as well as a $10.5 million injury guarantee for 2027.

Steichen did not give an expansive reason for releasing Jones but the guarantee, which would have put the Colts on the hook for 2027 if Jones got hurt, was likely involved in the logic.

And that was too high a financial risk for a quarterback that has been a massive disappointment in Indianapolis after he was a massive disappointment with the New York Giants.

Jones makes more sense on a team desperate for middling quarterback help after he clears waivers. The New England Patriots, who are managing a Drake Maye leg injury, make sense.

The Colts pretty much waved the white flag on the 2026 season at the NFL trade deadline nearly two weeks ago. They sent multiple players off in return for modest 2027 draft choice compensation.

This move is not that. This move feels more like the club simply ridding itself of a potential injury cost for next season while also moving on from a player who frustrated people within the organization this season.

That frustration came because Jones played well for Indy early in the 2025 season before getting hurt. The Colts believed that was the real Jones and rewarded him last offseason with a two-year contract worth $88 million.

Then Jones didn't live up to the salary. He leads the NFL with 13 interceptions. He's thrown an interception in six consecutive games.

And he always has the look of a victim on his face.

Jones is not a victim. He pocketed $50 million from the Colts this season to start 11 games. And the Colts might've known this was possible because Jones got paid by the New York Giants in 2023 when they gave him a four-year deal worth $160 million...

...And then his play fell off the table once he got paid and had his contract terminated in 2024.

So now the Colts turn to Richardson, their once and future starter.

He was drafted with the No. 4 overall pick in the 2023 draft. The team picked him knowing he needed to develop after playing only one full season at the University of Florida.

Except he never developed because he was injured multiple times, taking him out of the lineup. And then he struggled, which also got him benched.

So the guy needing experience didn't get experience. That led Richardson to request a trade earlier this season. Now he instead gets the final six games of the season to prove whatever he can prove.

"It's going to be great," Steichen said. "He's going to get a full week of practice. From his rookie year to now obviously he's matured a lot with his preparation and how he goes about it.

"And so to get him a full week of practice going into the game, I think is benefit him well."
