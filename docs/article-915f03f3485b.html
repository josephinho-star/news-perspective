Van Hollen says he will challenge Schumer for majority leader

2026-11-13 02:43

Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-Md.) said he would challenge Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) for the role of majority leader on Thursday, setting up a showdown in the upper chamber as Democrats prepare to retake the majority.

In a post to the social platform X, Van Hollen called for “new leadership” in the Senate.

“In this election, Americans called on us to unrig an economy rigged against working people & fueled by political corruption,” he wrote. “To deliver, we need new leadership. This isn’t about any one person – it’s about answering that call.”

Schumer, who served as majority leader from 2021 to 2025, previously announced he had the votes to return to the post, even as Democrats from both progressive and moderate sides of the party say they will not support him.

Sens. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) and Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.) appeared together at a news conference on Tuesday and said they would not back Schumer. Some incoming senators said the same, including Sens.-elect Adam Hamilton (D-Kan.), Annie Andrews (D-S.C.), Abdul El-Sayed (D-Mich.) and Troy Jackson (D-Maine).

Sanders and El-Sayed both voiced their support for Van Hollen on Thursday night following his announcement.

“The American people voted for change. They want a Democratic Party that will stand up to powerful special interests, take on the billionaire class and fight for working families,” Sanders wrote on social media. “That is why I am supporting Senator Chris Van Hollen to lead Senate Democrats.”

Schumer appears to remain confident he has the votes but said in a post to X on Thursday that he welcomes the challenge.

“I welcome any discussion about the future of our caucus and the important work ahead. I’m proud of what we’ve accomplished together—55 seats and counting is an amazing result—and I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic Leader,” Schumer wrote.

The vote for Senate leader requires a majority of the caucus and is a closed-door process scheduled for Jan. 4, after the new Congress is sworn in.

Calls for Schumer to step down are due in part to his age, as he will be 76 years old by the time of the vote.

In an appearance on MS NOW on Thursday evening, the Maryland Democrat outlined the differences between him and his opponent, pointing to his urgency to end the war in Iran rather than waiting until their party has the majority.

“I think that we heard loud and clear from the American people that they want to end the war in Iran and bring down prices,” Van Hollen told MS NOW’s Jen Psaki. “We should do it now. We shouldn’t say we’re going to wait till January.”
