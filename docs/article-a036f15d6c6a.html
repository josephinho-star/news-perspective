Dennis Haskins, beloved ‘Saved by the Bell’ star who played Mr. Belding, dead at 75

2026-09-29 05:05

Dennis Haskins, best known for playing the beloved principal Mr. Belding in “Saved by the Bell,” has died. He was 75.

Haskins’ death was confirmed by a longtime friend and caretaker, WTVC reported from his hometown of Chattanooga, Tennessee.

“It’s a tragic loss. Everyone loved Mr. Belding and those of us who got to know Dennis, loved him even more. He was a great person, and he loved, I mean absolutely LOVED his fans,” Haskins’ agent, Jay Schachter, told the Hollywood Reporter.

“He always had time for them, no matter what. He was my client and dear friend for over 20 years, and I will miss him terribly. I’m heartbroken.”

His cause of death has not been disclosed.

Dimples Karaoke, a Burbank, Calif., bar where Haskins was a “staple” and which many patrons called his “second home,” said in an Instagram post Monday that the actor had spent the last eight years battling Parkinson’s disease.

“He had been silent about his fight with Parkinson’s, among other health concerns, because he did not want the attention. He did not want to have to tell people. He would say, ‘What do I even say?’ So I respected his wishes and stayed quiet, and I helped him through his journey into the next chapter of his life,” the post read.

“What a cool and amazing life he had. Rest in peace, Dennis. You were very much loved and will never be forgotten.”

Page Six has reached out to Haskins’ agent for further comment.

Haskins spent more than a decade roaming the halls of Bayside High as Principal Richard Belding on the hit sitcom “Saved by the Bell” (1989-93) and its spinoffs, including “Saved by the Bell: The New Class” (1993-2000) — earning the nickname “America’s Favorite Principal.”

His character’s exasperated “Hey, hey, hey, what is going on here?” became one of the show’s most famous lines as Belding struggled to keep Zack Morris and the other students in line.

Before his television career, Haskins attended the University of Tennessee at Chattanooga, where he was deeply involved in campus life, according to the school’s website.

He helped found the Harlequins theater group, wrote and staged original plays and as student government entertainment chairman, booked Ike and Tina Turner for a 1973 campus concert.

He also wrote for the student paper and organized a charity basketball game pitting students against city officials to raise scholarship money for the children of local police officers.

Before pursuing his acting career, Haskins worked as a music manager, agent and concert promoter, counting singer Tom Jones and rock star Gregg Allman among his clients, according to his IMDb page.

Haskins made his network television debut on the 1979 pilot episode of “The Dukes of Hazzard,” playing a Boar’s Nest bar patron who takes a stiletto to the foot from Daisy Duke, according to the Daily Actor.

His big break on “Saved by the Bell” precursor “Good Morning, Miss Bliss, “Saved by the Bell” came a decade later.

Haskins racked up more than 100 acting credits over his career, including guest spots on hit shows like “The West Wing,” “Mad Men,” “It’s Always Sunny in Philadelphia,” and “7th Heaven,” according to IMDb.
