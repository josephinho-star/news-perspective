Muslims poised to outnumber Christians globally by 2035 amid demographic shifts

2026-11-19 00:28

People practicing the Islamic faith are poised to outnumber Christians globally by 2035, becoming the world's largest religion, according to a new study released by the Pew Research Center.

The detailed report attributes the shift primarily to higher fertility rates and younger populations among Muslims, who are growing at a faster rate than other major faiths.

"Around the world, we find that Muslims tend to have more children than non-Muslims, [and] they tend to be younger as a result of having more children in the past," Pew senior demographer Conrad Hackett told Fox News Digital.

Muslims have the highest fertility rate at 3.1 children per woman, followed by Christians at 2.5, according to the study. Between 2020 and 2025, Muslim women gave birth to 252 million babies, more than women of any other religion and compared to 208 million born to Christian mothers.

If trends continue, the United States will also undergo a major religious shift. While Christians currently account for roughly 62% to 64% of U. S. adults, that share is expected to fall below 50% by 2065.

By 2070, Christians are projected to make up less than half of the U. S. population, while religiously unaffiliated individuals, known as "nones," will rise to about 41%.

"The Muslim population in the U. S. is growing faster than the Christian population, but Christians vastly outnumber Muslims in the U. S., and that's not going to change," Hackett said.

By 2070, the Muslim population in the U. S. is expected to increase to nearly 4%, making Muslims the third-largest group in the country behind Christians and the religiously unaffiliated, surpassing the U.S. Jewish population.

Globally, the demographic center of major faiths is also moving. By 2070, Pakistan is projected to have the largest Muslim population, followed by India, Nigeria and Indonesia, which currently has the largest Muslim population of any country.

Meanwhile, the presence of Christianity is shifting southward. By 2070, more than half of the world's Christians will live in sub-Saharan Africa, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo is projected to surpass the U.S. as the country with the single greatest number of Christians.

A century ago, most of the world's Christians lived in Europe, Hackett noted. However, Europe's population is now growing slowly and expected to shrink in the coming decades, while Africa's population expands rapidly.

"Europe's population has been growing slowly, and Africa's population has been growing very quickly," Hackett said. "In the coming decades, Europe's population is expected to shrink, [while] Africa's population is going to expand a lot."

Other religious demographics are shifting as well. The global Jewish population is expected to become increasingly concentrated, with 62% living in Israel by 2070, up from 46% in 2020.

In addition, the global Muslim population is projected to exceed 3.6 billion by 2070, compared to just under 3 billion Christians, according to Pew.

Overall, Muslims and Christians are the only major religious groups projected to keep pace with the world's overall population growth, which is expected to increase by 29% between 2020 and 2070. Christians are projected to grow by 30% and Muslims by 80%.

Beyond organized religions, many countries continue to see a surge in "nones," those with no religious affiliation, atheists or agnostics.

While China holds a massive proportion of the world's religiously unaffiliated, its population decline will offset growth elsewhere, Hackett said. Excluding China, the global population of "nones" is projected to grow by more than 180 million by 2070.

The religiously unaffiliated already account for a majority of the population in 10 countries, including the Czech Republic, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Uruguay and Vietnam.

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
