‘Strange sound’ leads homeowner to unearth massive Viking silver hoard while digging for new patio

2026-09-20 17:52

A homeowner preparing the yard for a new patio recently made an incredible find — and it’s been declared the largest known Viking Age silver hoard ever discovered in Denmark.

Officials from North Jutland Museums in northern Denmark announced the discovery on Aug. 31.

The hoard was discovered by a private citizen who was preparing the ground for a new tiled terrace, according to a statement from the museum.

The homeowner, who wished to remain anonymous, called the discovery “the biggest surprise of my life” after stumbling upon the hoard while doing yardwork.

“It should have been just a few hours of hard gardening to remove stubborn grass, stones and gravel in relation to a new tiled terrace,” the person said.

About half an hour into the work, the homeowner struck “something that made a strange sound.”

The person added, “At first I thought it was old iron or fence wire as well as some potsherds, but when I started digging with my hands, I could suddenly fish one metal object after another out of the ground.”

The individual said that “after a short time of careful work, it dawned on me that it was jewelry, coins and silver bars.”

The hoard, which was buried in a clay vessel, is believed to date to the 10th century. It contains around 700 objects and fragments, including silver ingots, bracelets, coins, a small Thor’s hammer and various pieces of cut silver.

It’s Denmark’s largest known Viking Age silver hoard, according to North Jutland Museums.

The find eclipses the previous record holder — a roughly 14-pound Terslev hoard — and weighs nearly three times as much.

Some 320 objects with a total weight of 14 pounds were still in the original clay vessel, while the rest were found in the ground around the site.

The homeowner will not keep the treasure. Under Danish law, the hoard belongs to the state — though the finder is eligible to receive a reward.

The discovery came after archaeologists recovered six Viking Age gold arm rings from the same area earlier in 2026, Torben Sarauw, head of cultural heritage at North Jutland Museums in northern Denmark, told Fox News Digital.

“Almost everything about this discovery surprised me,” Sarauw said.

“We did not expect that the same area would also produce one of the largest Viking Age silver hoards ever found in Denmark.”

He said the hoard “is remarkable because of both its size and its contents.

“It contains an unusually large number of silver ingots,” Sarauw went on. “One ingot bears runic inscriptions that may have been intended to protect the treasure, while others are marked with Thor’s hammers and Christian crosses.”

He added, “The combination of pagan and Christian symbols, together with the runic inscription and the sheer quantity of silver, makes the find unique.”

The owner of the hoard must have been “extremely wealthy and of very high social status,” Sarauw noted, though the individual’s identity is lost to time.

“The size of the hoard suggests access to extensive networks of trade, exchange and perhaps political power,” the official said.

“The discovery also indicates that Himmerland was home to a significant center of wealth during the Viking Age, something we are only beginning to understand.”

Sarauw said hoards were typically buried to safeguard wealth during uncertain times.

This treasure may be connected to another silver hoard found nearby several years ago.

“Someone who buried more than 18 kilograms [40 pounds] of silver was clearly concerned about losing it,” he said.

“One possible explanation is that the owner died before he could recover his treasure, perhaps while traveling or taking part in an overseas expedition. Of course, this remains a hypothesis, and we cannot know for certain.”

There is additional significance, Sarauw said.

“This discovery is helping to change our understanding of northern Jutland during the Viking Age,” he said.

“Together with the nearby gold arm rings and other important finds from the area, it suggests that Himmerland may have played a much more prominent role in the political and economic landscape of Viking Age Denmark than previously believed.”
