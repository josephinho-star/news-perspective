Turkey proposes Black Sea deal to protect civilian shipping

2026-09-17 13:32

Turkey has submitted a proposal to Russia and Ukraine aimed at ending attacks on civilian ships in the Black Sea, a Turkish diplomat told Agence France-Presse on Thursday.

The initiative follows a sharp increase in attacks on commercial shipping in the region, many involving drones. At least 25 Turkish-owned vessels, four of them Turkish flagged, have been hit since late June, according to industry figures.

“We have shared the text of a memorandum, a draft text, with the Russians and the Ukrainians to come up with a solution to those strikes taking place in the Black Sea,” the diplomat said.

Turkey has been pressing Russia and Ukraine to halt attacks on civilian vessels in the Black Sea. Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan called for a “moratorium” on such attacks on August 8, but no agreement followed.

Turkey, a NATO member, has maintained relations with both Kyiv and Moscow since Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022 and has declined to join Western sanctions against Russia. Turkish-owned and operated vessels are active in the Black Sea, although many sail under other countries’ flags.

In August the Turkish Foreign Ministry summoned Ukraine’s ambassador following an attack on the Turkish-owned container ship Lider Bordo Mavi off Russia’s Black Sea coast and a subsequent attack on the Lider Kocatepe, which had been sent to tow the damaged vessel.

The Lider Bordo Mavi, later anchored in the northeastern Turkish port of Trabzon, appeared to have sustained extensive fire damage to its bridge and the two decks below, according to AFP correspondents.

Turkey has repeatedly raised the possibility of reviving the Black Sea grain corridor initiative to protect civilian shipping. President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has said he plans to discuss the issue with Russian President Vladimir Putin in November.

The Turkish diplomat said the new proposal was inspired by the Black Sea Grain Initiative brokered by Turkey and the United Nations in 2022. The agreement allowed Ukrainian grain to be exported through the Black Sea until Russia withdrew from it a year later.

Ukraine subsequently established an alternative shipping route close to the Romanian and Bulgarian coasts.

“In that deal, there is maybe a four or five-mile gap where the Ukrainian ship has to leave the national territorial waters and travel in international waters, which is the risky part,” the diplomat said.

“So our offer is also inspired by this.”

In addition to a route along the Romanian and Bulgarian coasts, the diplomat suggested another along the eastern and southern shores of the Black Sea through the territorial waters of Georgia and Turkey.

“We want to use the territorial waters of the adjacent states, including Turkey — and Georgia, if they are willing to take part,” the diplomat said.
