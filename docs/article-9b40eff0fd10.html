Viral body-positivity lardy leopard ditches weight-loss program, deemed too old to diet

2026-11-27 01:42

An obese 19-year-old leopard in China who became the internet’s unlikely body-positivity movement hero and was previously on a weight-management program has now been deemed too old to diet.

For years, the chunky cat — nicknamed Pan Baobao — has drawn crowds to Panzhihua Zoo in south-western China to see the full-figured feline on display, The US Sun reported.

The leopard’s pudgy figure made him a viral internet sensation, reportedly becoming an icon in the country’s body-positivity movement as well as for his striking resemblance to Officer Clawhauser, the plump cheetah in the Disney film franchise, “Zootopia.”

But in 2024, the heavyset leopard was put on a strict weight-loss program to manage his rounder-than-usual belly.

After consulting with experts sometime later, the zoo took Pan Baobao off his calorie-deficient diet and gave him a larger enclosure to stretch out, according to CNN.

Despite his famously round figure, zoo officials have now deemed the elderly chunky cat too old to be losing weight.

“It’s not that we’re not controlling its diet, but it’s getting old and shouldn’t be losing weight,” zoo spokesperson Zhang Songwen recently told the publication.

“Animals are actually like people. As they get older, their skin becomes loose,” Songwen said, adding that, “At this stage, the most important thing is to maintain their condition and ensure their health.”

Songwen explained that “not only must they eat enough, but they must also get proper nutrition” — which is evident from the porky leopard’s current diet of over one pound of beef a day, in addition to chicken, to provide balanced nutrition.

Although visitors to the Sichuan Province animal park have pointed out that Pan Baobao’s waistline continues to expand, the zoo insists the 19-year-old cat’s weight has not changed all that much and a vet recently determined he is in good health.

“As long as its weight remains stable and it’s in good spirits, that’s the best condition for it,” the spokesperson added.

The zoo previously came under fire when critics accused the park of purposely fattening up their animals to create their own sensation like the internationally beloved hippo Moo Deng.

But for now, Pan Baobao’s keepers say their priority is no longer achieving a slimmer figure for the leopard, but rather ensuring the 19-year-old feline remains comfortable, active and healthy.
