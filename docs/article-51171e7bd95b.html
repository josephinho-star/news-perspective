Turkey prepares indictment in fund scandal involving 638 suspects

2026-11-26 19:40

Prosecutors in Turkey have begun drafting an indictment in an investment fund scandal involving 638 suspects, Justice Minister Akın Gürlek said Thursday at a briefing in Ankara.

The scandal erupted in September when funds could no longer meet withdrawal requests, affecting nearly 456,000 investors.

Investigators suspect the funds were used to inflate share prices, allowing people with advance knowledge to profit before other investors were left unable to recover their savings.

Gürlek appeared alongside Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz and Treasury and Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek to explain the investigation, repayments and proposed changes to financial regulation.

Of the 638 suspects, 138 are in pretrial detention, Gürlek told reporters.

The investigation’s main findings are established, but inquiries will continue as suspects cooperate with prosecutors and identify others involved, he added.

Gürlek cautioned that returning money would not end criminal proceedings against a suspect.

About 13 billion lira ($257 million) transferred abroad has been frozen, with efforts under way to bring it back, while 95 people have returned 45.36 billion lira ($895 million) through the recovery process.

The crisis prompted the Capital Markets Board (SPK), Turkey’s securities regulator, to order 131 funds managed by seven companies to close and return available money to investors.

Payments to investors are expected to reach 199.25 billion lira ($3.93 billion) by Friday, Yılmaz announced.

About 98 percent of affected investors have received at least one payment, though that does not mean they have recovered their full account balances.

The payments have come from fund assets and recovered money, without Treasury funding, he explained.

Another 10,680 investors received no payment because they had already withdrawn amounts equal to their original deposits.

Interim repayments have excluded an inflation adjustment, but final repayments could include one if sufficient money becomes available, Şimşek told reporters.

The scandal has also reached politicians and financial regulators, with Şimşek on Thursday authorizing an investigation into former banking regulator chief Şahap Kavcıoğlu.

Gürlek described pretrial detention as an “exceptional” measure requiring evidence and legal grounds, days after former family minister Fatma Betül Sayan Kaya was released to house arrest in the case.

Asked about demands to disclose those involved, Yılmaz pointed to published regulatory complaints and promised that names would become public through indictments.

The government is preparing legislation to increase penalties for market manipulation, strengthen the SPK’s powers to intervene in troubled funds and establish a system to recover investors’ losses from those responsible.

Şimşek also announced plans for an artificial intelligence system to detect manipulation and a 35 billion lira ($691 million) loan guarantee package for companies affected by the crisis through the Credit Guarantee Fund (KGF).

He argued that the crisis posed no threat to the banking system as a whole and that global market losses had contributed to Turkey’s stock market decline.

On elections, Yılmaz said voting would take place in 2028 but could be moved forward by one or two months, linking that option to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s bid to run again.
