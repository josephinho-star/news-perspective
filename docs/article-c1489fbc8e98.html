‘Below Deck’ alum Joao, Karlos Franco’s mom ‘distraught’ over sons’ imprisonment: ‘Nobody can communicate with them’

2026-11-19 01:28

The loved ones of former “Below Deck” star João Franco and his brother, Karlos, are in shambles as the two sit in a Turkish prison.

“I spoke to the brothers’ mother yesterday,” “Below Deck” alum Chef Ben Robinson told Page Six. “She was crying and in pieces. As the case continues to unfold, there may be some additional footage that has yet to be seen. Their mom is very distraught.”

“Nobody can communicate with them,” he added. “It’s awful. Cheryl, their mother, appreciated the chat and wants the American public to know the circumstances and cherishes everyone’s support for her boys amidst this tragic situation. Cheryl and I will continue chatting as this unfolds.”

Page Six exclusively obtained CCTV footage of the Nov. 7 incident showing Karlos appearing to throw a punch toward a group of store employees as João seemingly tried to defuse the situation.

“They have legal representation in Turkey,” Robinson added. “That’s how the mother knows where they are and what’s going on is through the lawyers. João wrote a handwritten note and one of the lawyers took a picture and sent it to the mother via cell phone.”

Robinson and João worked on yachts together during “Below Deck Mediterranean” in 2019 and “Below Deck Down Under” earlier this year.

As the brothers await their day in court, New York federal criminal defense attorney Todd Spodek shared a few possibilities for what could happen next.

“The single most important question in this case is how Turkish prosecutors charge it because that decides almost everything about the sentence,” Spodek said.

“If they treat it as an intentional killing, the brothers are facing a potential life sentence,” he continued. “If it’s charged the way these bar-fight cases often are, as an assault that unintentionally resulted in death, the exposure drops to roughly a decade or more. If a court finds it was reckless rather than intentional, it drops further still.”

“There’s also a middle option called ‘eventual intent,’ which means the person didn’t mean to kill but knew death could happen. It’s less than life [in prison] but still a long sentence.”

The investigation into the altercation remains ongoing.
