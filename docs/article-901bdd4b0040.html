Top Senate Democratic Super Pac pours millions more in Michigan and Maine races

The top Super Pac for Senate Democrats will pour more than $7m into two marquee races this cycle: Michigan and Maine.

The Senate Majority Pac has reserved $3.7m for its campaign in Michigan, to support the Democratic nominee , Dr Abdul El-Sayed, against the Trump-backed Republican, Mike Rogers. The group is also investing $3m in Maine, to boost Democrat Troy Jackson’s chances against Susan Collins – the five-term moderate GOP incumbent, who Democrats see as increasingly vulnerable in a cycle where anti-Trump sentiment has colored much of the election.

This comes after reports that the Senate Majority Pac has also paused ad spending in North Carolina and Georgia. Semafor was first to report on the updated spend.

Donald Trump claimed that he has secured $21.2tn in foreign investments in his first 15 months in office.

The White House website itself disputes that number, claiming instead that “the US has seen a surge of private and foreign investment” totalling $11.3tn.

That figure includes not only foreign funding, but also domestic deals that have not yet been finalized, such as a $608bn investment from Nvidia, a $600bn investment from Meta and a $500bn deal with Stargate partners Softbank, OpenAI and Oracle. According to its own website, about half of the White House’s $11.3tn figure comes from domestic projects.

Some of the largest deals touted on the White House website, including a $1.2tn “investment” from Qatar have previously been described by the White House itself as an “economic exchange”, and others are still being negotiated, such as an alleged $1tn deal with Japan that is still in the framework stage.

Trump has boasted of false investment numbers during his campaign tour ahead of the midterm elections. Speaking in Alaska yesterday, Trump repeated his claim that South Korea has agreed to spend $50bn to build a liquefied natural gas pipeline in Alaska, just days after Alaska Public Media reported that Korean officials told state lawmakers in Anchorage that the country had not agreed to invest in the pipeline and would consider investing in the project if it’s commercially reasonable.

At his campaign rally in Arizona, Donald Trump said “incomes for hard-working Americans have soared” and said inflation was record high during Joe Biden’s administration. The president’s statement came as inflation accelerated to 3.7% last month amid the Iran war. Today, US stocks dropped to their worst day since July.

Speaking in Arizona, Donald Trump just repeated his false claim that he had to start his war on Iran because Iran was “two weeks away from having a nuclear weapon”.

In fact, two months into Trump’s second term, US intelligence agencies reported to Congress in 2025 that Iran had suspended its pursuit of nuclear weapons in 2003.

Despite that assessment, in the summer of 2025, Trump ordered the US bombing of Iran’s nuclear enrichment facilities which he later claimed had “obliterated” the country’s nuclear program.

Eight months later, in February 2026, Trump announced that a full-scale war on Iran was necessary to stop Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons.
