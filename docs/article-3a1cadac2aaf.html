Nearly 1M ACA beneficiaries due to get $500 in the mail — here’s what to do if you haven’t seen yours

2026-11-17 15:36

Back in September, President Trump announced that nearly one million Americans would score $500 rebate checks across 30 states. The cash was coming from alleged overcharges from the Affordable Care Act, aka Obamacare.

President Trump claims the Biden administration overcharged Americans through “user fees” passed on to consumers in the form of higher premiums. Checks should have been sent to eligible Americans beginning in October 2026.

The excess fees were in the following 30 states: Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Delaware, Florida, Hawaii, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, West Virginia, Wisconsin, and Wyoming.

This means you must be a resident of one of those states to be eligible. Additionally, you must have used the federal Healthcare.gov exchange to pay the full cost of your insurance premiums — with no subsidies.

Most folks with incomes above 400% of the federal poverty level will be receiving the checks. However, those earning between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level who did not receive subsidies may also qualify.

The federal poverty level is $15,960 a year for an individual across most states.

Since one million across multiple states are set to receive the checks, it may take some time. Be sure to check your mailbox daily or any addresses related to your Health.gov account. These accounts help manage your health insurance through the official federal or state portals.

An administration official told ABC News that Americans in the 30 states “do not operate their own ACA insurance plan exchanges and instead rely on federal exchanges administered by [the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services].”

Residents in the 20 remaining states will not receive the ACA refunds “because their states run their exchanges, and thus the federal government did not oversee collecting user fees on them,” the official told ABC News.

The news outlet also said folks living in Texas and Florida will be receiving the largest amount of refund checks followed by Wisconsin, Arizona and Oregon, respectively.

The Trump administration originally said checks would be sent in October along with a letter stating, “For years, the Biden Administration overcharged you to fund the operation of HealthCare.gov,” ABC said. “That money belongs to hard-working Americans, not the Government, and now, I am returning it to you.”

If you have not received yours, it’s likely heading to your home soon. Again, make sure to check your mailbox and your Health.gov account regularly.
