RFK vows to ‘work to regain’ US measles elimination status after devastating health setback

2026-11-18 19:14

Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. vowed to “protect American families” and “regain” measles elimination status after the US lost its disease-fighting designation, which the country held for 26 years.

The vaccine skeptic — who has presided over a dramatic plunge in immunization rates — responded to the devastating health setback with some of the strongest pro-vaccine comments he’s made during his time in office.

“We will stop measles outbreaks, protect American families, and work to regain US measles elimination status,” Kennedy wrote in a lengthy post on X Tuesday afternoon.

“I continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles,” referring to the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine.

“We are working to close vaccination gaps and rebuild public trust. We will answer families’ questions honestly about vaccine benefits and risks and work with trusted health care providers and community leaders to prevent measles, limit its spread, and support timely treatment,” Kennedy added.

He went on to blame COVID for the surge in the highly contagious disease.

“Worldwide COVID-era disruptions interrupted routine childhood vaccination, leaving gaps in protection here and abroad. The multi-year global outbreak has caused many nations to lose their elimination status.”

There were more than 5,400 confirmed measles cases and 10 deaths in the US in 2026—more than double 2025’s case count and the highest amount since 1991, according to the CDC.

The US previously averaged 180 cases a year — mostly from patients bringing in cases from abroad — but there’s been a recent surge in homegrown outbreaks, including inTexas, South Carolina and Pennsylvania.

“CDC has worked alongside state teams in Texas, South Carolina, Virginia, Kansas, New Mexico, and Wisconsin to help bring their outbreaks rapidly and successfully under control. CDC is also supporting Pennsylvania and New York with epidemiologists and other experts,” RFK added in his tweet Tuesday.

In January, Kennedy directed the CDC to alter the routine childhood immunization schedule— cutting universal recommendations from 17 diseases to 11 — prompting legal challenges.

He also ousted all 17 members of the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices in June 2025 and appointed new members, some with histories of vaccine skepticism.

A federal judge in Boston later blocked Kennedy’s revised childhood vaccine schedule and ruled that some changes to the committee were “arbitrary and capricious.”

The legal battle is still pending in federal court.
