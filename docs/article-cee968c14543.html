U.S. to co-host Women's World Cup in 2031; U.K. wins bid for 2035

2026-11-23 23:08

The United States has been confirmed as co-hosts of the 2031 Women's World Cup, before the United Kingdom will host the tournament four years later.

The FIFA congress on Monday approved a joint bid from Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica to host the 2031 World Cup.

The combined bid from England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland was the sole bidder for the 2035 competition. To date, 19 venues have been put forward as potential options to host the tournament, with 14 grounds across England, two in Scotland, two in Wales and one in Northern Ireland.

The winning candidates and FIFA president Gianni Infantino promised commercially successful tournaments -- the first editions with 48 teams and 104 games -- and to reinvest all that money in women's soccer.

"This will be the largest women's sporting event in history," the U.S. Soccer Federation president, Cindy Parlow Cone, told FIFA members in a pre-vote presentation at the governing body's headquarters in Zurich. "Which means we have to deliver."

The U.S.-led bid previously predicted tournament revenues of $4 billion, and Parlow Cone said after the FIFA decision that "if we do it right we probably will surpass that."

FIFA wanted these heavyweights of women's soccer to face no opposition without rival bids and host the first expanded tournaments. That will bring parity with the men's World Cup which expanded to 48 teams this year in the U.S., Canada and Mexico.

The 2027 Women's World Cup will have 32 teams in Brazil.

The U.S.-led 2031 project across the Concacaf regional soccer body bid offers FIFA a choice of stadiums in 20 cities: 14 in the U.S., four in Mexico plus San José in Costa Rica and Kingston, Jamaica.

Women's soccer in Jamaica already has a good reputation, with standout forward Khadija 'Bunny' Shaw starring for Manchester City and the national team finishing unbeaten above Brazil in their group at the 2023 World Cup.

Parlow Cone said "we have a responsibility to raise the boats of every country" in women's soccer.

"I know first hand what hosting a Women's World Cup can do, and I wanted the same for throughout our region. It's going to be transformative for us, but imagine what it will do for Jamaica for the 'Reggae Girlz' to play in their home stadium in a World Cup match," Parlow Cone said.

The pair of four-nation co-hosting bids had a clear run in the past 20 months and were approved Monday by acclamation in an online meeting of FIFA's 211 member federations.

The U.S. previously hosted the Women's World Cup alone in 1999 and 2003. The national team's tournament win in 1999 was the second of its four titles.

It will be the first time since 1966 the U.K. has hosted a World Cup. The U.K. will also host the men's European Championships in 2028, but had previously failed in bids to secure the 2006 and 2018 men's World Cups.

The English stadiums included in the bid are: Brighton's Amex, Bristol City's Ashton Gate, Nottingham Forest's City Ground, Leeds United's Elland Road, Arsenal's Emirates Stadium, Manchester City's Etihad Stadium, Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium, Manchester United's Old Trafford, Newcastle United's St James' Park, Crystal Palace's Selhurst Park, Sunderland's Stadium of Light, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, Villa Park and Wembley.

Chelsea's potential new stadium, Wrexham's redeveloped ground and Birmingham's new 62,000-capacity Powerhouse Stadium could also be added.

In Wales, the Principality Stadium and Cardiff City Stadium are on the list. A redeveloped Windsor Park in Northern Ireland is also on the list as are Edinburgh's Easter Road and Glasgow's Hampden Park in Scotland.

FIFA's evaluation of the bid gave it a rating of 4.3 out of five, with the contractual framework of the bid rated "medium risk."

FIFA's evaluation said: "The bidder has submitted an extensive set of government support documents and contractual hosting agreements, demonstrating the commitment of the relevant authorities and stakeholders.

"However, certain government guarantees, particularly those relating to safety and security and tax, contain certain qualifications which have been duly noted in the legal evaluation.

"While certain contractual matters remain subject to further discussion, the bidder's proactive engagement with FIFA and the substantial lead-in time provide a sound basis for resolving these outstanding issues."

English FA chair Debbie Hewitt addressed the congress after the bid's success had been confirmed.

"Thank you for your trust and confidence in awarding us the FIFA Women's World Cup 2035," Hewitt said.

"We're deeply honoured by your support and grateful for the opportunity to host the pinnacle of women's football on behalf of the global football family.

"As we said throughout our bid, we want this to be your FIFA Women's World Cup, a World Cup for everyone.

"Together, we will celebrate the power of women's football, inspire the next generation, and unite fans from every corner of the world. We look forward to welcoming you to the United Kingdom in 2035 and just as importantly, to working closely with you all in the years ahead to deliver an exceptional FIFA Women's World Cup that the entire football community can be proud of."

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: "The world is coming to the United Kingdom. In 2035 the biggest tournament in women's sport will be played in cities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, bringing visitors, investment and jobs to every part of the country.

"Our players have inspired a generation of girls. In 2035 that generation will watch the best in the world play on their doorstep -- and some of them will be on the pitch. But the impact of this World Cup will go much further. It will reach into communities across the country, bring more people into the game and leave a legacy that lasts long after the final whistle."

England forward Michelle Agyemang said it was "incredibly special" and added: "I've been lucky enough to know what it feels like to play for my country in a major tournament, and those are moments you dream about as a young girl playing football.

"The thought of another generation growing up knowing that the biggest stage in the game is coming to their home country is amazing. To have the chance to play in a home World Cup would be a dream, but even more exciting is thinking about all the girls watching who might see 2035 and believe they can be part of it one day."

Scotland captain Caroline Weir added: "The women's game in Scotland has so much potential and this tournament is a real opportunity to drive momentum and help take it to another level both nationally and internationally.

"We're so proud to play our part in welcoming the world in 2035 and I know I speak on behalf of all of the players and staff when I say that we are ready to rise to the challenge and help deliver a truly memorable, world-class tournament."

For the second time in less than two years, FIFA is confirming a double award of World Cup hosts without rival bids that gives clarity for the decade ahead.

In December 2024, FIFA member federations were asked to rubber-stamp the unprecedented six-nation, three-continent bid for the men's World Cup in 2030 from Spain, Portugal, Morocco, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay.

That cleared the way for FIFA to award also the 2034 World Cup to Saudi Arabia in a fast-track process that seemed designed to exclude other candidates.

Information from The Associated Press and PA contributed to this report.
