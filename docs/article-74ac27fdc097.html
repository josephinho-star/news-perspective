Stream It Or Skip It: ‘Youth’ On HBO, Where Sharon Horgan Is A Fiftysomething Woman Trying To Date While Dealing With Ailing Parents And A Nest That’s Not So Empty

2026-09-20 18:00

Sharon Horgan continues to make very personal comedies that connect with their audiences, mainly because the characters she writes for herself are so very vulnerable while being hilarious. That’s the case in her new HBO comedy Youth.

YOUTH: STREAM IT OR SKIP IT?

Opening Shot: “Every single move you make in your sixth decade is significant. Monumental,” a voice says as we see scenes of women 50 and over. Then, we see a woman lying on the couch, looking at TikToks. When a text comes in, she’s so startled by it she drops the phone on her face.

The Gist: Alex (Sharon Horgan) is 50 and she feels like she’s quickly approaching a time where she can no longer be sexy or date anyone but creaky old men. But she’s also dealing with a lot: She works for her friend Sam (Sharlene Whyte) as a literary agent, dealing with clients like actor Damien Lewis wanting to write about dogmeat. Her jaw is locking because of arthritis. Her ex-husband Logan (Owen McDonnell) wants to move to Canada for an acting job. Her parents are both ailing in different ways. And her son, Rauri (Aran Murphy) is leaving for college, which she’s alternately thrilled and depressed about.

Sam sets her up on a blind date, and Alex is appalled that he’s in his late 60s — but she goes back to his place, anyway. She has a great casual time with Patrick (Rupert Friend), who is in her phone as “Ex Pat,” but he’s a few years younger and she’s not sure he wants a relationship.

What Shows Will It Remind You Of? Youth, created and written by Horgan, feels like a spiritual sequel to two of Horgan’s previous shows, Catastrophe and Bad Sisters.

Our Take: Horgan has always been good at making the characters she writes for herself funny, sexy, and self-deprecating all at once, and a scene in the first episode of Youth brings that home: Alex is on the bus after her emotional blind date, wearing her bright yellow platform heels, and when the burger she bought falls on the floor, she considers picking it up and eating it. It really encapsulates what Alex is struggling against: She’s at the age where she shouldn’t have to give a flying fig about what people think of her, but she also really wants a relationship with someone she doesn’t want to be a caretaker to in a few years.

Because she is so good at playing all of those aspects of Alex, it makes the rest of Youth funny, even if some of the other aspects of the story feel superfluous right now, like Sam being constantly suspicious of her husband Frank (Robbie Gee), or Alex dealing with a veteran author (Anne Reid) writing a book that’s full of erotica and racism. They’re goofy side stories that just fill in the cracks between Alex dealing with family life, her immature ex, and wondering if things with Patrick are ever going to move into relationship territory — and if that’s what she actually wants.

Performance Worth Watching: Horgan and Friend have amazing chemistry with each other, especially as they tiptoe from mutual booty calls to something resembling a relationship.

Sex And Skin: There is definitely sex, but much of it is clumsy and sometimes ends in tears — just like real life.

Parting Shot: As Alex lays in bed, looking forlornly out her window, Ruari gets into bed with her, looking for comfort from his mom just like he did when he was a kid. But he won’t let her touch him.

Our Call: STREAM IT. Youth should be relatable to anyone in their “sixth decade” who really hope that they too are still desirable and interesting to others. But it’s also just a funny show in general, thanks mainly to Horgan’s writing and lead performance.
