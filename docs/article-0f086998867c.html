Nets’ Mikel Brown Jr. may be set to play first back-to-back of career

2026-11-28 09:04

TORONTO — Could the training wheels finally be coming off Mikel Brown Jr.?

The Nets rookie had a career-high 21 points on 7-of-10 shooting and was 4 of 6 from deep in Friday’s loss at Toronto. And after logging just under 25 minutes, there is at least a chance of him playing the first back-to-back of his career Saturday in Charlotte.

“We’re going to evaluate him right now. So I haven’t talked to anybody; I haven’t had the chance. I cannot answer [if he’ll play],” coach Jordi Fernández said. “I know he played 24, did some good things out there. And we’ll talk [Friday] night and see where he’s at. Once again, our priority is his health. Keep him healthy.

“Keep him ready to play as hard as he can and just keep building from here.”

Brown had a soft tissue back injury as a Louisville freshman last season, and has been treated with kid gloves by the Nets, not yet allowed to play both ends of any of their three back-to-backs.

“We will evaluate after this game so we will see how it feels,” Fernández said. “Obviously you know we have a travel day and we’re going to get there late and all those things, so we’ll make the decision at some point.”

Brooklyn called up point guard Ben Saraf from the G-League as protection in case Brown can’t play Saturday. But it has more to do with the lottery pick’s availability against the Hornets than starter Nolan Traore being out 8–10 weeks.

“We have to make a decision on Mikel, and that’s pretty much the reason why,” Fernández said. “There’s an opportunity for (Saraf) to play. We want all these guys to develop at the same time that we compete. There’s nothing that can be given.”

“The standards of playing in a certain way, and then your opportunity could be there. So if I throw him in, then I expect him to play well. He’s been very good with Long Island. I watched his games. And not just so much about the numbers, but his intentions, and he’s gotten better every game.”

Saraf had four points, two assists and two rebounds in 8:32 Friday.

Fernández went to his bench fairly early, playing nine players in the first quarter.

Grant Nelson played 21:23, while little-used Josh Minott got 8:32.

Moritz Wagner was still out due to knee soreness with no timetable.

“Nothing new. Just managing his soreness and helping with the process,” Fernández said.

“It’s not just, it goes away right away. So, just helping him and supporting him and working on that knee to be stronger and for him to be confident.”

The Nets complete a comparatively soft three-game portion of the schedule Saturday in Charlotte. After that, they’ll run a gauntlet of Oklahoma City twice in a home-and-home, then Boston and Detroit.
