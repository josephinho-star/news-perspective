City inspectors rush to 160 locations tied to contractors working at fatal Brooklyn collapse site

2026-11-18 19:48

City officials have halted construction at multiple sites tied to engineers involved in last week’s deadly Brooklyn building collapse, as officials widen their investigation into the disaster that killed eight people.

The crackdown is part of a sweeping inspection effort reviewing roughly 160 construction projects involving six firms connected to the doomed development, according to the City Reporter. The DOB has not released its full inspection findings.

A review of city building records identified at least 24 sites across four boroughs that inspectors have visited since the tragedy. At least five received orders halting some or all construction.

The projects are linked to two engineers involved in the Flatbush excavation: Jieming Wang of LMW Engineering Group, who submitted structural plans, and MD Ariful Islam of RI Consulting Services, who oversaw excavation monitoring.

The affected properties include a Greenwich Village parking garage being converted into luxury condominiums, a six-story apartment development in Queens and a former Brooklyn church being transformed into a 10-unit residential building.

At 599 Willoughby Ave., the former Hebron Baptist Church was cited for safety violations, while demolition sites at 285 and 291 Eastern Parkway have accumulated multiple violations and stop-work orders.

In Queens, inspectors issued a partial stop-work order at 56-12 Roosevelt Ave. in Woodside after spotting a worker on scaffolding without a safety harness. Another construction site at 131-28 40th Rd. in Flushing was hit with a partial stop-work order over scaffolding violations.

Elsewhere, inspectors flagged loose safety netting above a neighboring school playground and missing guardrails at a Douglaston construction site, while unsafe scaffolding prompted a halt to exterior work at 332 W. 11th St. in Manhattan.

In The Bronx, inspectors issued a full stop-work order at 58 W. 184th St. Tuesday after a contractor allegedly refused to allow an inspection and threatened to lock an inspector inside the site.

At another Bronx excavation site, 2167 Arthur Ave., a contractor had previously been cited for damaging a gas main. Also at that site, Elite Concrete, which was performing excavation at the time of last week’s collapse, was performing excavation there. DOB records, as cited by the outlet, show in March a neighbor called 311 to report their excavation had caused cracks in the foundation of his home. (Inspectors arrived two days later and did not issue violations for the foundation issue.)

Wang’s approved plans did not call for steel bracing or underpinning at the collapse site, though investigators have not determined whether that contributed to the disaster.

The Flatbush site had previously been fined $10,000 after demolition work continued despite a March stop-work order, which was lifted in May.

Among those killed was 26-year-old construction worker Jose Luis Caguana, whose wife was heavily pregnant. First responders also rescued a pregnant woman trapped beneath the rubble.

The cause remains under investigation, with Brooklyn prosecutors examining whether criminal conduct played a role.
