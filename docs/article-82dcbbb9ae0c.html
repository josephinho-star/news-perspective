JD Vance says Trump’s White House media ban is ‘totally appropriate’ as networks halt TV pool coverage in response – live

2026-09-21 17:41

NBC, ABC, CBS and Fox News, the fourth other networks that make up the White House TV pool, decided they would cease coverage in solidarity with banned CNN

'I think it's totally appropriate,' says JD Vance on Trump's White House media bans

JD Vance defended Trump’s ban of three news outlets from the White House while speaking to reporters en route to a campaign event in North Carolina.

“He’s not banning media outlets,” Vance said on the tarmac at Joint Base Andrews. (Trump, in his social media post announcing the move, referred to his actions as a ban.)

Vance continued: “What he’s doing is saying we’re not going to give you special access to the White House if you engage in what is effectively propaganda.”

He criticized the outlets for coverage that he said was overly negative of the Trump administration.

“That’s not representative of the American people. It’s not representative of public opinion. So if you’re going to engage in what is effectively far left propaganda, why would a Republican White House give you special access?”

But, he said, Trump’s actions didn’t violate the outlets’ rights, because “they can still report on the Trump White House, they can still get the public reports” and “they can still, of course, talk to people in the administration and outside the administration”.

“It’s about basic fairness. They can still report. They still have access to free speech. They’re just not going to get an office in the White House anymore. And I think it’s totally appropriate, unless, of course, they start to behave like real news organizations.”

Iowa GOP Senate candidate calls for "immediate end" to Iran war

Ashley Hinson, a Republican member of the House of Representatives campaigning for a valuable Senate seat in Iowa, publicly called for an end to the war with Iran in a social media post on Monday.

“Iowans are being squeezed and shouldn’t have to foot the bill at the pump or the checkout line for the war in Iran,” Hinson wrote. “We need to use every option at our disposal to provide some relief from high prices.”

After listing some potential policy measures to mitigate the costs of diesel fuel, she added: “These actions to lower costs are needed ASAP, and the war needs to be brought to a successful and immediate end.”

While Hinson didn’t name Trump outright, her comments diverge from the oft-repeated stance of a president who has said the high prices are a worthwhile setback to bring Iran’s leadership to heel.

Hinson, once a steady ally of Trump, has drifted further from the president in recent weeks. When JD Vance, a key surrogate for Trump on the 2026 campaign trail, headlined a rally in her state last week, Hinson was notably absent. Her comment also comes as Republicans worry that the unpopular conflict will hamstring the party in the midterm elections and help secure control of one or both chambers of Congress for the Democrats.

Key ally urges Trump to back US farmers amid anger over rising prices

Donald Trump is facing growing calls, including from within his own party, to boost support for US farmers as his administration faces mounting anger over its treatment of the agricultural sector.

Republican senator Chuck Grassley of Iowa called on the US president to place an embargo on diesel exports this weekend and help farmers grapple with high prices.

Grassley, who is largely aligned with Trump, suggested the embargo in multiple social media posts, calling on the president to issue a 1970s-style embargo on diesel and agricultural products to help farmers.

“If our [government] can embargo chips to China it can embargo diesel to help American farmers & truckers,” Grassley wrote on Sunday. “We need our family farmers who feed&fuel the world [to be] on the strongest footing possible no matter what’s happening across the globe.”

For months, farmers – traditionally a reliable Republican voting base – have been angry with the Trump administration because they believe some of its policies adversely affect them.

That includes tariffs, which have driven up the cost of fertilizer, machine parts, seed and more; increasing beef imports to lower prices for Americans, which undermines domestic producers; and the Iran war, which has made it more expensive for farmers to operate machinery and transport products.

The national average cost of diesel was $6.51 as of Sunday, according to AAA. In Iowa, it was slightly lower at $6.31 – still nearly $3 more than the cost one year ago.

Gas prices are not the only rising cost Americans are facing as a result of the Iran war. Groceries have also become more expensive, and the Trump administration has sought to lower some of those prices, particularly beef, by allowing 300,000 metric tons of overseas beef trimmings to enter the US at a reduced tariff rate.

But domestic farmers in the US, who are already struggling with smaller herds, say the decision will increase competition and make it more difficult to sell their own.

“It takes something big to change long-established voting patterns, but I think there’s a possibility that the Trump administration has done things that are big enough and bad enough from the standpoint of rural America to make some of the voters who shifted a decade ago reconsider their choice,” William Galston, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution, told the Washington Sun.

US strikes on alleged drug boats may constitute ‘crimes against humanity’, UN finds

South America correspondent

A special rapporteur for the UN human rights council has concluded that there are “reasonable grounds to believe” that the United States attacks on boats allegedly carrying drugs in the Pacific and Caribbean “constituted crimes against humanity”.

The boat strikes, carried out since September 2025 on the stated grounds of stopping drugs being trafficked by sea to the US, have killed more than 230 people. The latest, on Saturday, killed four people in the Caribbean.

The UN itself, along with several human rights organisations, lawyers and international law experts, had already described the bombings as “extrajudicial killings”, but special rapporteur Ben Saul went further.

In a briefing note posted by Saul on social media on Sunday night and first reported by the BBC, Saul wrote that, in addition to “serial extrajudicial killings”, the attacks were “in violation of the right to life, being unjustified in national or personal self-defence or under international humanitarian law, the international law of the sea, narcotics suppression conventions, or international counter-terrorism law”.

He added: “There are reasonable grounds to believe that the reported 68 attacks that killed 223 people constituted crimes against humanity of murder under customary international law.”

The report cites 68 US attacks that killed 223 people. It is unclear how recently the figures were updated – tallies by organisations such as the Washington Office on Latin America put the death toll at 234 people in 66 attacks.

US Southern Command, which has coordinated the attacks, posted on Saturday, alongside a grainy video, that it had “executed a lethal, kinetic strike on a go-fast vessel operating along established narco-trafficking routes in the Caribbean. Confirmed intelligence revealed the vessel’s active involvement in narco-trafficking. The operation killed four narco-terrorists”.

As has become standard in announcements of such attacks, the US did not identify the victims or provide any details about the alleged intelligence that supposedly confirmed their involvement in drug trafficking.

Saul’s report rejects the claim made by Donald Trump before Congress that the strikes amounted to “self-defence” against “designated terrorist organizations”.

The special rapporteur wrote that the crews were not “militarily armed and possessed the intent and capacity to launch military attacks” against the US.

“Even assuming that the vessels and crew attacked were members of the criminal groups listed as terrorist, such organizations have not mounted any ‘armed attack’ on the United States so as to trigger a right of self-defence,” he added.

The US Department of State has not yet responded to a request for comment.

Lauren Boebert slams ethics complaint alleging sexual relationships with three staffers as 'politically motivated'

Lauren Boebert, the firebrand Colorado Republican congresswoman, is the subject of a House ethics complaint that she had sex with three staff members, and paid one to keep quiet. She said the allegations are untrue, and part of a “politically motivated” campaign against her.

Boebert’s denials came through a formal statement from her office, which said “the congresswoman rejects the claims as utterly false”, and a verbose video rant on social media in which she accused the person who filed the document as a “sloppy, desperate, obsessive hack”, and journalists who reported the story as falling victim to a “scam”.

According to the complaint, filed last week by David Wheeler, an independent journalist, activist, and founder of the American Muckrakers political action committee, Boebert committed at least seven violations of House ethics rules.

Among the most prominent allegations are that Boebert conducted a four-year “romantic and sexual relationship” with a senior female staffer, named as Clarice Navarro Ratzlaff, then paid her $200,000 when Ratzlaff broke off the affair and threatened to file an ethics complaint of her own.

Additionally, Wheeler alleges, Boebert had a sexual relationships around the same time with Raven Finegan, a congressional employee and the congresswoman’s district chief of staff, and Jeffrey Small, who was her chief of staff from 2021 to 2025.

The code of official conduct prohibits House members from engaging in sexual relationships with employees under their supervision, or the committees on which they serve, unless a couple is married. Consent, the rules state, is not a defense.

Boebert frequently promotes her own Christian values and has previously been publicly critical of same-sex relationships.

CNN, MS Now and Politico file lawsuit against Trump over White House ban

The three news outlets President Trump banned from the White House last Friday have filed their anticipated lawsuit against his administration to restore their access.

In the new complaint filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on Monday, CNN, MS Now and Politico alleged that Trump had violated the First Amendment and due process by stripping the outlets of their White House access in a blatant act of retaliation for coverage he disliked.

“This ban could not be a more direct assault on the First Amendment nor a more blatant violation of our most fundamental constitutional principles,” CNN, MS Now and Politico wrote in the complaint.

In his Friday social media post announcing the ban, Trump attributed his decision to no specific issue with the outlets’ coverage, but rather to their pattern of producing “fake news” – his usual label for news coverage that is not favorable to him or his policies. He threatened bans against other outlets could follow.

The move, the outlets alleged in their lawsuit, was a deliberate attempt to unlawfully pressure them and other outlets to avoid critical reporting on his administration.

“The Constitution does not allow a president or any other government official to deprive the press of their First Amendment rights and liberty and property interests with no notice or process based solely on his dislike of the content of their reporting,” the outlets’ complaint continued. “The ban is unconstitutional and should be immediately restrained.”

Networks halt TV pool coverage of Trump following CNN ban

After President Trump’s snap decision to ban CNN from the White House, the four other networks that comprise the White House’s TV pool decided they would cease their pooled coverage of the president, Politico and other outlets are reporting.

Alongside CNN, those four networks – NBC, ABC, CBS, and Fox News – have provided rotating video coverage of the president both in the White House and around the country, broadly sharing the footage they gather. They are a near-permanent fixture of the presidency.

But they won’t be for the time being, it seems.

In an email to TV pool subscribers obtained by several outlets, the Washington bureau chief for Fox News said that the decision to ax pool coverage “follows the White House’s position preventing CNN from fulfilling its assigned pool duties.”

The five networks that comprise the standard pool rotation published a statement Monday afternoon: “The public has a vital interest in receiving accurate, independent information about its government. No administration should restrict a news organization because it objects to its reporting.”

A cryptocurrency industry super PAC is planning a sizable ad blitz to try to tip the scales against the Democratic candidate in a key midterm Senate race, according to a new report by The New York Times.

The group, Fairshake, told the Times that it’s launching $30 million in advertising money at the Ohio Senate race in a bid to help sink Sherrod Brown, the Democratic nominee for the seat. Brown, a critic of crypto, was once the ranking Democrat of the Senate Banking Committee, which handles crypto legislation, before losing his seat in 2024 – when the industry previously wielded its money against him.

(Brown has, since then, taken a somewhat softer stance on crypto, the Times reported.)

The spending comes after Senate Democrats took down the Clarity Act, a piece of legislation that the crypto industry was desperate to see passed. If successful, it would’ve carved into law many aspects of the Trump administration’s crypto-friendly regulatory environment. (The Biden administration, by contrast, had cracked down on the industry.)

Democrats’ objections to that legislation stemmed, in large part, from Trump himself. They wanted to see stricter provisions barring lawmakers from leveraging their offices to get rich off crypto – like Trump has done in his second term to the tune of $1.4 billion.

Trump set to begin busy week of diplomacy – with a Mamdani meeting along the way

President Donald Trump is set to depart for New York later today ahead of the United Nations General Assembly, kicking off a busy week of international diplomacy.

Trump is expected to meet with several key figures on the sidelines of the annual gathering of world leaders. Among them will be Delcy Rodriguez, the interim president of Venezuela, who took charge after the US raided her country and arrested her predecessor, Nicolás Maduro, in January. That’s all on Tuesday. On Wednesday, Trump is planning to host a visit from Chinese leader Xi Jinping, during which the two are expected to discuss trade, AI, and the war in Iran.

Trump will also meet today with New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, the democratic socialist whose insurgent campaign to run Trump’s home city rattled his MAGA allies, but with whom he has maintained a strangely cordial relationship. Afterwards, he’s scheduled to talk with French President Emmanuel Macron.

Trump responds to CNN, Politico, MS Now lawsuit over stripped White House access

Hours after the three news outlets he summarily barred from the White House announced their intentions to file a lawsuit, Trump took to social media to defend himself.

“The White House is not instituting an assault on the Free Press, something which I cherish,” Trump wrote on Truth Social, in response to the pending challenge from CNN, MS NOW, and Politico, the three outlets he banned.

Trump continued: “It is instituting an assault on the FAKE NEWS, something that has grown like Cancer in our beloved United States of America. It is corrupt, purposeful, pervasive, fully coordinated, and totally out of control. It is a threat to our National Security, and must be stopped, NOW!”

Trump banned the three news organizations from the White House on Friday via a surprise social media post. He cited no specific grievance against the outlets’ coverage when announcing the decision, saying only that they were “fake news” and that similar sanctions may follow for other outlets down the line.

Since then, reporters and camera crews for the three organizations have been barred from entering the building. A CNN crew slated to accompany Trump to New York today was reportedly nixed.

Earlier this morning, the three organizations (as widely predicted) announced their intentions to sue the administration to restore their access.

The lawsuit, the three outlets said in a joint statement, will aim “to protect our First Amendment rights and defend the principle that the government does not decide what the press reports or publishes.”

ICE agent shoots and wounds Venezuelan man at traffic stop in Austin, Texas

A US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot and wounded a Venezuelan man on Sunday afternoon in Texas’s capital city, Austin, prompting demonstrations at the scene against the Trump administration’s hardline anti-immigration campaign.

The man, identified by relatives and his lawyer as 28-year-old Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, was taken to a hospital in Austin in a serious but stable condition, officials said, before being taken into custody.

He was shot once in the torso on the city’s north side, Austin’s police chief, Lisa Davis, said at a news conference with the mayor and other city officials.

The Department of Homeland Security, which oversees ICE, said it was leading an investigation into the officer involved in the shooting, with the help of the FBI.

Austin police officers were not involved in the operation or the shooting, which occurred after a traffic stop, Davis told reporters.

The city’s police arrived on the scene after the shooting and could be seen on bystander video asking the federal officers what had happened and then rushing back to their squad car to get surgical gloves and a medical kit and administering first aid to the man.

The federal officers could be heard saying they had called an ambulance, but they did not appear to attempt to give first aid.

CNN, MS NOW and Politico to sue over White House ban

MS NOW, CNN and Politico said they were suing the Trump administration to protect their First Amendment rights after president Donald Trump’s announcement that he would ban them from the White House’s building grounds.

The three news outlets said they had notified the government that they were filing their lawsuit in the US District Court for the District of Columbia and could see emergency hearings in the case as soon as this week, according to CNN.

All three news outlets had hinted at legal action, saying they would vigorously defend their First Amendment rights.

“Without notice or process, the White House revoked our journalists’ credentials because it objected to our reporting,” the outlets said in a joint statement posted on X. “Left unchallenged, this threatens press freedom and the public’s right to independent journalism free from government interference.”

Representatives for the White House did not immediately respond to a request from Reuters for comment outside normal business hours.

Donald Trump was accused of “manufacturing a national security justification” to push forward his plans for a triumphal arch.

The president said yesterday that the planned structure near Washington would double as a “military complex” housing drones and snipers, making it the second major construction project he has sought to justify on national security grounds.

“The president’s belated efforts to manufacture a national security justification for this project do nothing to cure its fundamental illegality,” Nicolas Sansone, one of the attorneys representing litigants suing to stop the plan, said on Sunday. “Congress has not authorized the arch and unless and until Congress does so, the project should not be permitted to move forward.”

The arch and plans for the White House ballroom are part of Trump’s broader effort to remake the nation’s capital, including the renovation of the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, the redevelopment of a golf course in East Potomac Park and a push to add his name to the John F Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts.

The initiatives have drawn criticism from preservationists and others who argue they would alter some of Washington’s most prominent historic landmarks.

US representative Don Beyer, a Democrat whose Virginia district includes Arlington National Cemetery, posted on Sunday that operating drones so close to the airport would be dangerous and impractical, and suggested the move was aimed at bolstering the administration’s legal position.

“Trump clearly expects to lose a lawsuit and therefore wants to set up a pretext to argue that the arch is tied to national security,” Beyer, who has introduced legislation to block the arch’s construction, wrote in a social media post.

In a social media post, Trump said that at the “strong request” of the military he had agreed to turn the planned 250 ft (76m) arch “into a top grade Military Complex/Triumphal Arch, to house, store, and have the rapid ability to use large numbers of drones, plus Snipers, on both the roof and plaza areas, and additionally have and hold large quantities of sniper ammunition in storage.”

Trump refused to say whether he would attempt to demolish the John F Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, the iconic facility on Washington’s Potomac River. Speaking from the Oval Office, Trump repeated his belief that the center should append his name to the building in exchange for his work to raise money to renovate the facility.
