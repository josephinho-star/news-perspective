EBRD cuts Turkey’s 2026 growth forecast to 3 percent

2026-09-24 12:51

The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD) has cut its growth forecast for Turkey this year to 3 percent from 3.5 percent, citing weak domestic demand, high inflation, expensive credit and the economic impact of the conflict in the Middle East.

The bank expects growth to pick up to 4 percent in 2027, according to its latest Regional Economic Prospects report released Thursday.

The EBRD said households and businesses were still being squeezed by high borrowing costs and inflation, while Turkish exporters were also facing pressure from weaker cost competitiveness.

It warned that a further escalation in the Middle East could push energy prices higher, hurt tourism, trigger capital outflows and disrupt trade. Turkish companies with growing foreign-currency liabilities would also be more vulnerable if the lira weakened further, the bank said.

The regional economic picture worsened after Israel and the United States launched strikes on Iran in February, setting off a wider war that disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and drove up oil and gas prices.

The EBRD said in June that the conflict had become the main drag on growth across the economies where it operates, as higher energy costs, shipping disruptions and tighter financial conditions fed inflation and weakened growth.

For Turkey, which relies heavily on imported energy, higher oil and gas prices can feed directly into inflation and the current account deficit while also putting pressure on the lira. The World Bank has likewise said the conflict is weighing on Turkey through higher energy prices, higher risk premiums and higher interest rates.

The EBRD said a lasting easing of regional tensions, stronger demand from abroad and continued efforts to keep economic policy on track could improve the outlook.

Turkey has been trying to bring inflation down through high interest rates after years of steep price increases. The central bank kept its benchmark rate at 37 percent for a fifth consecutive meeting earlier this month, citing continued weakness in domestic demand and warning that high energy prices linked to the war on Iran posed a growing risk to inflation.

Annual inflation stood at 31.51 percent in August, while the central bank raised its year-end inflation forecast to 28 percent.

The EBRD’s forecast is in line with estimates from other international institutions. The OECD expects Turkey to grow by 3.1 percent this year and 3.8 percent in 2027, while warning that the Middle East conflict could slow the decline in inflation.

The IMF projects growth of 2.9 percent in 2026, while the World Bank expects 2.8 percent.

Across the countries where it operates, the EBRD now expects growth to average 2.5 percent in 2026 before rising to 4 percent in 2027. It cut its regional forecast by 0.6 percentage points from June.

The EBRD is one of Turkey’s largest institutional investors and has invested more than €25 billion in the country, mostly in the private sector.
