Trump says he's OK with Jensen Huang returning as Nvidia CEO, sees no national security risk

2026-11-16 18:55

President Donald Trump said he would be open to former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang returning to the company he resigned from last week amid the fallout from an extramarital relationship he had with an alleged Chinese spy.

"I think he learned a big lesson," Trump said of Huang when asked by reporters on Monday whether he wants to see the company co-founder reinstated as CEO.

Trump, speaking to reporters outside the White House before departing for a trip to Asia, downplayed national security concerns stemming from the affair with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based technology company that U.S. officials suspect of having funneled Nvidia's advanced chips to China.

"It was not put at risk, and our country is now stronger than ever before," Trump told reporters when asked about U.S. national security concerns. Trump is due to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum.

The president told reporters he is not planning to replace Howard Lutnick as commerce secretary after questions arose about the Cabinet member's handling of export controls involving Nvidia's advanced chips.

Republican and Democratic members of Congress have raised concerns about the national security implications of the episode after it was revealed that Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in the 1990s, had an extramarital affair with Chinese businesswoman and alleged spy Alice Huang. Nvidia is the leading global supplier of advanced artificial intelligence chips.

Critics argue the affair could jeopardize the U.S.' global dominance in AI and that it raises questions about influence exerted by Nvidia's Huang on regulators and lawmakers as he opposed export controls that limited the sale of the company's chips to Chinese firms.

The New York Times reported Tuesday that the U.S. believed Alice Huang was a Chinese spy and that Secretary of State Marco Rubio alerted Trump to the situation aboard Air Force One in May before Jensen Huang joined the president on a flight to China.

The Washington Post reported Friday that Rubio had issued a memo to Trump warning the president about Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy.

But Trump on Monday said "I haven't heard that" when asked about Rubio's warning.

"I don't know that she is a spy. Usually, what happens with that is that any woman coming to the country that's a beautiful woman that comes from China, they say she's a spy," Trump said, of Alice Huang. "I don't know that she was a spy at all."

Trump also attempted to defuse tension bubbling within his Cabinet between Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Lutnick.

Axios reported last week that Lutnick has "repeatedly stood in the way" of cracking down on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia's advanced AI chips to China, prompting a Situation Room confrontation between the men. Both are traveling with Trump on Monday to Asia, as is Rubio.

And Bessent, in an appearance on CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Friday, seemed to express frustration at Lutnick for not putting Megaspeed, Alice Huang's former company, on a Commerce Department list of companies that pose national security concerns.

The departments of Treasury, Defense and State and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative all "were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the 'entity list' for months," Bessent said.

Trump acknowledged on Monday that Lutnick might have delayed putting Megaspeed on the list but defended his rationale as essential for diplomacy.

"He was doing that because you could have — if you didn't delay it, If you didn't do something — you could have a blow-up with China," Trump said. "We're having a very good relationship with China right now, and I understand both sides of it, and I'll make a decision. But I understand very well both sides."

Trump, who previously had said he did not plan to raise the Jensen Huang issue with Xi when they meet this week, said he may do so. He also said he may meet with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un in China.

As Trump downplays concerns about fallout from the affair and advanced chips finding their way to China, Capitol Hill leaders are taking a different approach.

A spokesperson for House Foreign Affairs Committee Chair Brian Mast, R-Fla., told CNBC "negotiations are still going" about Jensen Huang testifying before a trio of House committees.

Mast, along with House Select China Committee Chair John Moolenaar, R-Mich., and House Intelligence Committee Chair Rick Crawford, R-Ark., had given Huang a deadline of 8 p.m. ET Friday to respond to their request to appear before the committees. Mast has said he would subpoena the former CEO if he doesn't accept the invitation voluntarily.
