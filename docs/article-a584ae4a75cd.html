Mike Waltz warns Kremlin faces 'unsustainable' losses approaching WWII scale as Trump seeks deal

2026-11-21 17:57

U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz warned Friday that Russia is suffering "unsustainable" battlefield losses, with monthly deaths outpacing recruitment numbers.

Speaking on "The Brian Kilmeade Show" podcast, Waltz echoed President Donald Trump's assessment that Russian President Vladimir Putin is "worn down," arguing the war in Ukraine cannot be won militarily and must end at the negotiating table.

"Right now, the numbers that we have, the Russians are recruiting 20,000 to 25,000 a month of new fresh recruits," Waltz said. "They're losing 30,000 to 35,000 a month, dead every single month."

"That is exponentially more than they lost in Afghanistan and approaching what they lost in World War II, a thousand a day," he added. "It's just unsustainable."

The interview followed Trump's return from Shenzhen, China, where he met with Putin before joining the Russian leader and Chinese President Xi Jinping for the first-ever three-way summit between the leaders of the United States, Russia and China.

The summit produced a joint declaration pledging dialogue on reducing nuclear risks, maintaining stable energy supplies and reforming the United Nations, but the leaders stopped short of announcing breakthroughs on Ukraine or Iran.

At a White House news conference Friday, Trump addressed questions about Putin's intent regarding the war.

"He'd love to end the war. It's a very hard thing to do," Trump said Friday. "To be honest, you have two guys that don't know how to end it. We're trying to help them."

"You have two people that hate each other, Zelenskyy and Putin. They don't know how to end it. They want to end it. They're both stuck in it," he added. "And it's a horrible war, actually. And I thought it would have been maybe one of the easier ones to get ended. But the personalities play."

Waltz argued that both Ukraine and Russia have "worn each other down," saying it's unlikely the two countries will be able to sustain the current level of fighting.

"It's a horrific war, and the president remains determined to find some construct of a deal that will end it because neither side can sustain this war of attrition," Waltz said. "There is no winning it militarily. It's got to end diplomatically."

Fox News Digital’s Morgan Phillips contributed to this report.
