Family ‘90 percent sure’ they held funeral for wrong grandmother after mortuary allegedly mixed up bodies

2026-11-26 01:03

A grieving Louisiana family reportedly claimed they held an entire funeral for a stranger after a mortuary allegedly mixed up the bodies of two elderly women.

Relatives of Gloria English, 83, questioned whether the woman lying in her casket at Heavenly Gates Funeral Home in Shreveport was actually their grandmother during an open-casket service earlier this month, KSLA reported.

“They basically assured us that that was her, even though we told them then that we didn’t feel like that was her,” granddaughter Treasha English told the outlet.

Around the same time, the body of another woman — 78-year-old Jean Mims — went missing from the funeral home.

After comparing photos of the deceased, both families believe the woman in English’s casket was Mims.

“I’ve seen a picture of this lady in the casket, and it looks a whole lot like my sister,” Mims’ sister Marie Cooper-Johnson told The Post.

“Her cheek bones, her nose, her hands. … I’m about ninety percent sure.”

Cooper-Johnson said Mims died at the hospital after a battle with cancer, and her daughters watched as staff loaded her into the funeral home van.

But they spotted another wrapped body already in the van — and suspect the funeral home switched up the corpses when they unloaded them.

Gloria English’s family also believes it was Mims in their grandmother’s casket, relatives told KSLA.

But there is no way to know for sure since the body was cremated after the funeral.

If the families’ worst fears are true, it would mean Mims was actually the one cremated — a fate she was adamantly against, according to Cooper-Johnson.

Heavenly Gates has no record of Mims and initially told the family she may have been mistakenly cremated.

The funeral home issued an apology Wednesday and announced it was conducting an internal probe, according to WAFB, but wouldn’t confirm the bodies were swapped.

The Louisiana State Board of Embalmers and Funeral Directors received two complaints on Nov. 19, and is investigating the mortuary.

Cooper-Johnson described her sister as a beloved great-grandmother who cherished her family and tried to spread peace wherever she went.

“She was all about love. I’m the fighter. She would tell me, ‘Don’t fight.’ But in this situation, she would tell me, fight. ‘Fight!'” Cooper-Johnson said.

“I want [Heavenly Gates] shut down. I want those people who were negligent prosecuted. I’m gonna fight this until we get to the bitter end. … I want the corner brick on that building as a memorial to my sister.”

Heavenly Gates did not immediately respond to The Post’s request for comment.
