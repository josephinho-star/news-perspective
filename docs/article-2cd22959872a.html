Coaching commiseration: How Chargers' Harbaugh, Ravens' Minter aim to revive their seasons

2026-11-16 20:45

In January 2022, Jesse Minter was hot on the recruiting trail as the Vanderbilt defensive coordinator when his phone rang with an unfamiliar number.

It was Jim Harbaugh, who was the head coach at Michigan.

"Hey, I'm pretty sure my brother's hiring Mike [Macdonald]," Harbaugh told Minter. "You want to come to Michigan and be the DC?"

Minter was stunned because he had never really spoken with Harbaugh. Their first real conversation started with a job offer.

There was an opening for the defensive coordinator job at Michigan because Macdonald was leaving to run the defense for John Harbaugh's Baltimore Ravens. Both Macdonald and John, Jim's older brother, knew Minter when he was a defensive assistant in Baltimore from 2017 to 2020. They strongly vouched for Minter, insisting that Jim should hire him.

"And Jim listened to them," Minter said.

Minter was sure he had spoken with Jim because it sounded like him. But he wasn't certain that he landed the biggest break of his career. He knows there are some coaches who will call with an offer and you still need to go through the interview process.

Minter quickly called Macdonald to ask if Jim had really just hired him as the next defensive coordinator at Michigan.

"I'm telling you, this is real," Macdonald said.

Jim Harbaugh and Jesse Minter formed one of the NFL's most successful coaching tandems over the previous four seasons. With Harbaugh as the head coach and Minter as his defensive coordinator, they won 50 of 63 games and captured a national championship at Michigan in 2023. And in 2024, Harbaugh and Minter were hired to be head coach and DC over the Los Angeles Chargers, where they earned playoff berths in each of their two seasons together, including last season.

Minter feels Harbaugh set his path to becoming an NFL head coach by not only hiring him but empowering him. Harbaugh let Minter have full control of the defense while building him up with "ridiculous positivity," as Minter described it. "Dude, I believe in you," Harbaugh once told Minter. "You're going to be the best coach I've ever hired."

In January, Minter was hired as the fourth head coach in Ravens history, replacing Jim's older brother. Jim Harbaugh then hired Western Michigan defensive coordinator Chris O'Leary to become the Chargers defensive coordinator.

In their first season apart, both Harbaugh and Minter are dealing with once promising seasons that have gone terribly wrong by the NFL's midway point. Monday's game between the Chargers and Ravens (8:15 p.m. ET, ESPN/ABC) has become a commiseration reunion for Harbaugh and Minter, both of whom are looking to live up to their reputations as problem solvers who instill confidence during tough times.

In Harbaugh's third season in Los Angeles, the Chargers (0-8) are the league's only winless team. In Minter's first season in Baltimore and his first as a head coach, the Ravens (4-5) have lost four straight to plummet into last place in the AFC North.

"He's a leader through adversity," Minter said of Harbaugh last week. "When you go through [a bad] stretch, I was always just really impressed with his ability to keep the team intact, keep them motivated, find improvement, find little things to get better at every day and hold players accountable. I mean, that's what Jim's doing."

The struggles for Harbaugh and Minter have reached historic proportions.

With ankle and wrist injuries to quarterback Lamar Jackson, the Ravens have lost three consecutive games by more than 20 points for the first time in their 31-year history. The Chargers, meanwhile, are tied for the fourth-longest losing streak by a playoff team from the previous season.

After starting the season with the third-best odds to win the Super Bowl, the Ravens currently have a 3.5% chance to make the playoffs, according to ESPN Analytics. The Chargers have gone from entering the season with a 68% chance to make the playoffs to now having a 74% chance of landing a top-5 pick in the 2027 NFL draft.

Each week, Harbaugh is asked about offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel's job security and the expectations laid upon QB Justin Herbert. And, each week, Minter is asked about the defensive breakdowns in the second half, the kicking struggles and Jackson's timeline to return.

On Monday night, the paths for Minter and Harbaugh converge again at a time when the questions outnumber their collective wins.

"He just figures it out," Harbaugh said of Minter. "He has got a great ability to make things right."

EIGHT DAYS AFTER the Chargers grabbed headlines for naming Harbaugh their new head coach, he touched down in Los Angeles on an atypical dark and rainy Southern California day.

Chargers owner Dean Spanos warned Harbaugh about the untimely storm he'd be arriving to.

Harbaugh's response? "Dean, we are the storm."

Two and a half seasons later, the Chargers have transformed from a franchise once infused with confidence to the worst team in the NFL following the offseason firing of offensive coordinator Greg Roman and Minter's departure.

After boasting the fifth-ranked defense in the NFL last season with Minter, Los Angeles has dropped to No. 21 at the midway point this season. The Chargers are coming off a loss to the Houston Texans in which they allowed a season-worst 44 points and 485 total yards.

It's been a swift change from the previous two seasons, when Los Angeles made the playoffs with 11-6 records in 2024 and 2025. In losing their first eight games in 2026, the Chargers are in the midst of the longest losing streak in 15 years by a team that had made the playoffs the previous season.

"There's fighting or there's slunking away," Harbaugh said this week. "That will never be a part of our culture, our organization. Any narrative that we're not hellbent on winning these games is completely wrong."

This is uncharted territory for Harbaugh. Entering this season, he ranked ninth among NFL coaches with the ninth-best all-time winning percentage (66-31-1, .678).

Before this disastrous start, Harbaugh had never had a losing season in six seasons as an NFL head coach. He had only lost eight games in a season once -- an 8-8 season in 2014 with the 49ers -- much less lose eight in a row.

"He's literally been the same," safety Tony Jefferson said about Harbaugh through their winless start. "He's been -- sometimes a little snappy, which I like, but he hasn't changed as far as there's no -- as a leader, he's doing exactly what we would want, is to see a motivated, highly spirited coach every day, regardless of the result, just trying to get it fixed."

While Harbaugh and Minter's on-field philosophies quickly produced results, Harbaugh and McDaniel have not experienced a similar dynamic.

Harbaugh, 62, is the old-school coach known for his buttoned-up polo shirts. McDaniel, 43, is the eclectic playcaller with a joggers-only wardrobe. Their identities have clashed as much on the field as they do in a photo lineup, with the offense, ranking last in points per game (15.1) and touchdowns scored (11). In last week's loss to the Texans, a third-quarter touchdown ended a 37-drive touchdown drought.

Herbert, a two-time Pro Bowl quarterback, ranks last in the league with a 29.8 Total QBR. He has thrown nearly twice as many interceptions (11) as touchdown passes and has been sacked 31 times, the second most in the NFL.

"Just got to keep fighting," Herbert said. "As tough as it is, there's nothing else that we can do other than go out there and give our best effort throughout the week, understanding that it's only us in that locker room that can do anything about it. Obviously not where we want to be."

And, if there's any hope for salvaging the season, it seems to reside inside the locker room, where on a recent Friday afternoon, Mariah Carey blasted from linebacker Denzel Perryman's DJ setup, as players milled jovially about and filled the adjacent barbershop in preparation for a game.

"Can I be honest?" Jefferson asked rhetorically when the surprisingly upbeat locker room was brought to his attention. "I think it's like this because we know we're a good team... there's just been games where it's like it's been a play here or a play there because pretty much every game we've been in, we've lost them, but had a play or two gone a different way, we probably would be winners of these games and our record would probably be a lot different than what it is."

But for now, it's a winless team, and the storm rages on.

IT WAS 10 months ago when Baltimore felt like it had won the offseason. After all the angst that led to the firing of John Harbaugh, the Ravens celebrated the hiring of Minter, who was considered the top candidate in this head coaching cycle.

"Jesse's smart. He's a leader. He has great humility," Ravens general manager Eric DeCosta said on Jan. 29 before adding, "He's a problem solver."

Minter's resolving of issues has been pushed to the limit halfway through his first season as a head coach. The Ravens are already working with their fourth starting quarterback, third starting center and third kicker.

Jackson, a two-time NFL Most Valuable Player, has played 14 full quarters this season due to ankle and hand injuries. Tyler Huntley, the team's top backup quarterback, is out for the season with turf toe.

With Jacoby Brissett set to start Monday night, the Ravens will become the third team since 1950 (excluding the 1987 strike season) to start four different quarterbacks in four straight weeks. And, with all the injuries up front, Baltimore has had a league-high 25 different offensive line combinations.

The Ravens have quickly gone from a team eyeing a championship to one staring down crisis after crisis.

"[Minter has] come in and done a great job, especially for the cards he's been dealt with," offensive tackle Roger Rosengarten said. "He's one of the most levelheaded coaches I know."

The players have praised Minter being levelheaded despite the season not going as planned.

Minter's mantra this summer was "Be at your best when your best is needed." But Baltimore's second-half collapses continued this season, when the Ravens failed to hold double-digit leads in their first three losses of the season.

Minter arrived in Baltimore with the reputation as a defensive mastermind. But the Ravens continue to give up too many big plays in the passing game and rank in the bottom half of the NFL in pass defense (23rd), points allowed (25th) and red zone efficiency (21st).

Asked if he's considered giving up the defensive playcalling duties, Minter sternly replied, "No."

In nearly two decades under Harbaugh, the Ravens were used to confrontation. Harbaugh called out players and even kicked them out of practice.

It has been the other extreme with Minter. When he reviews game film in team meetings, he points out the mistakes without singling out the players who made them.

Those inside the team facility collectively describe Minter's voice as "steady."

"It's one of those jobs where, if you're insecure or if you have an ego, people know," defensive coordinator Anthony Weaver said. "It usually affects the team the wrong way. [Minter's] who he is, and he's comfortable in his skin. I think that's the sign of a good leader. Just his emotional intelligence, his ability to stay calm in the chaos and make rational and logical decisions as opposed to emotional ones."

After Baltimore allowed 35 unanswered points in a 35-13 loss to the Cincinnati Bengals on Oct. 25, Minter challenged his leaders to speak up more. After a 33-10 defeat to the Jacksonville Jaguars on Nov. 5, Minter spoke about a "reset" to reestablish the team's identity and didn't rule out lineup changes for Monday night.

The Ravens are looking to avoid their first five-game losing streak in five years.

"He's been poised throughout the whole process of this whole season, through the highs and through the lows," defensive tackle Nnamdi Madubuike said of Minter. "It definitely gives us reassurance as a team just to keep going out in practice, keep working on the little things and just tighten up those loose bolts."

The toughest of times have defined the careers of the Ravens' previous two coaches.

In 2000, Brian Billick kept the team together during a five-game touchdown drought and led the Ravens to their first Super Bowl. In 2012, Harbaugh unified the players after a contentious team meeting and rallied the Ravens to their second championship.

Now, it's Minter's time to place his stamp on a team where the injuries and losses continue to mount and their playoff chances continue to fade.

"You know there's going to be good times and bad times, and I think the easiest thing to do is to say, 'Well, we didn't have this,' or 'This happened,' [or] 'This happened.' I've never really tried to live that way," Minter said. "So, it's my responsibility, solely my responsibility and our staff and obviously our players, for us to be playing the type of football that we're capable of playing regardless of whatever."
