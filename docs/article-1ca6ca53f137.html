Russell Brand’s wife vows to abide by prison rules after video controversy

2026-11-22 17:49

Laura Gallacher, Russell Brand’s wife, has promised to “absolutely honour” rules set by the prison where her convicted sex offender husband is being held, in another YouTube video published on Sunday.

This week Andy Burnham called on Google to take down a video uploaded to Brand’s YouTube channel last Sunday that contained a statement from the disgraced comedian in prison, where he is serving a 10-and-a-half-year sentence for rape and sexual assaults.

Gallacher began the latest episode of Sunday Service – described as a Christian series, which the couple started together six months ago – by telling viewers: “Last week’s episode drew considerable amount of attention and now that I understand the parameters set by the brilliant and professional staff at HMP Littlehey, I will absolutely honour that.”

She went on to address scrutiny regarding her continuing to run her husband’s YouTube channel, amid a Ministry of Justice inquiry focusing on whether Brand has broken rules that are supposed to prevent “serious distress” to the victims of crime.

“I am choosing to continue Sunday Service because I really enjoy it,” Gallacher said. “It is also a place where I will continue to be proud to be a wife, a mother and a woman defending her family at a time when the media’s image of what kind of a woman I am is under so much scrutiny.”

In the previous episode, the first published since Brand was jailed – after being convicted of one count of rape and three sexual assaults at Southwark crown court on 5 November – Gallacher read out a message from him protesting that he was not guilty.

The video, titled “The Good Work Continues”, has remained on YouTube despite ministers repeatedly asking for it to be removed.

That video has now accumulated more than 1m views, becoming the best-performing video on Brand’s channel since 5 December last year, when he published a video conversation he had with the American conservative influencer Candace Owens. Many of his videos in the last year have drawn only between 50,000 and 100,000 views.

On Thursday night, Burnham said he “wouldn’t leap” to changing the law to force social media companies to take down such content. But he added: “I would call on those social media companies to take these interviews down. You know, why are we having to hear from him? We shouldn’t be, and certainly [the victims] shouldn’t be hearing from him.”
