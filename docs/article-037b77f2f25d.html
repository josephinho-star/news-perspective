MICHAEL RUIZ: Lindsay Clancy scores 'little win,' but appeal could 'unravel' future trials, retired judge says

2026-11-19 19:10

Lindsay Clancy, the Massachusetts mom accused of murdering her three young children while their father was out running errands in 2023, scored a small legal victory when her appeal was referred to the state's Supreme Judicial Court Thursday, experts say.

Earlier this week, Clancy's attorneys filed an appeal of Judge William Sullivan's denial of their motion to dismiss on double jeopardy grounds with Justice Elizabeth Dewar.

Dewar referred the case to the full court Thursday, setting briefing deadlines next month and oral arguments for January. Outside the appeal, jury selection in Clancy's retrial is scheduled to begin March 29.

"This is a small win for the defense," said Jack Lu, a retired Massachusetts judge and legal scholar who has been following the case. He said the appeal could "unravel" future trials.

"This is still difficult and unforgiving terrain," he said. "Look at it from a common sense perspective, the defendant’s position would interfere with jury deliberations in future cases and presents an unworkable framework for handling deliberating juror complaints."

The first trial ended with a hung jury Sept. 4 after a week of deliberations. With a single holdout juror voting to find her guilty, her defense argues that he should have been dismissed and replaced with an alternate after the foreperson accused him of failing to follow the law on "reasonable doubt."

Sullivan, in his earlier ruling, wrote that he was skeptical of the foreperson's accusation, and the holdout, Michael Desronvil, has insisted in post-trial interviews that he believes the evidence shows Clancy is guilty of murder in the deaths of Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months.

The 36-year-old Clancy's defense at trial argued she suffered from postpartum psychosis and should not be found guilty due to mental disease or defect.

Prosecutors alleged she planned the slayings, sent her husband out on errands and killed the kids before he got home and jumping from a second-story window in a failed suicide attempt that left her paralyzed.

At trial, Clancy did not dispute that she strangled the children with exercise bands on Jan. 24, 2023.

Desronvil, in an interview with Fox News after Clancy's trial, said the prosecution's case convinced him that Clancy had methodically and logically planned the "execution" of her children, especially when Patrick Clancy's 911 call was played in court.

The 36-year-old father returned home from running errands to find his then-wife seriously wounded in the yard and their three children in the basement.

"That was really big for me, you know, from watching the video and also listening in the courtroom," Desronvil said. "The thought process that goes behind … to fulfill the commitment of doing that."

At issue in the appeal is whether there was "manifest necessity" to declare a mistrial with jurors unable to reach a unanimous verdict. Clancy's attorney, Kevin Reddington, had asked for the holdout to be dismissed and replaced with an alternate after a note from the foreperson accused him of ignoring reasonable doubt about the prosecution's case.

Sullivan, in a 17-page order published earlier this month, found that "manifest necessity" did exist and wrote that he was skeptical about the foreperson's allegation against Desronvil.

Carol Erskine, another retired judge and child welfare advocate, said it's not easy to get similar appeals in front of the full SJC. She filed three during her career as an attorney, with only one making it, she said.

"The issue has to be one of exceptional circumstances, constitutional rights at stake, urgent, and the case cannot wait for the ordinary appellate process," she told Fox News Digital.
