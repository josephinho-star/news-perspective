Fire at confectionery factory in Indonesia leaves at least three dead and dozens missing

2026-11-22 07:11

A fire at a confectionery factory west of Jakarta has killed three people with 36 remaining missing, an Indonesian rescue official says.

Drone footage showed large parts of the factory destroyed, with the roof collapsed in places and smoke still rising from the complex. Photos and videos shared by rescuers show officers entering the charred building in search of the missing victims on Sunday, with small fires still burning in some spots.

Relatives of the missing victims flocked near the factory’s entrance, seeking updates about their loved ones, footage aired by local broadcasters showed.

The Fonusa Agung Mulia factory, located in Cikande in Indonesia’s Banten province about 50km from the capital, caught fire on Saturday afternoon, search and rescue official Rizky Dwianto said on Sunday.

“All elements of the joint SAR [search-and-rescue] team are making maximum effort to locate the victims while prioritising the safety of personnel in the field,” he added.

Rizky said four people were injured and taken to a nearby hospital and health clinic.

Firefighters were alerted about 4.30pm on Saturday and found the building already engulfed in flames when they arrived, fire official Tubagus Machfudz told local broadcaster Kompas TV.

“When we arrived … a large area was already on fire. So we could not determine whether the company’s protection system was functioning properly or not,” he said.

The Banten province governor Andra Soni told reporters overnight that an estimated 60 people were inside the factory when it caught fire.

The cause of the incident is still under investigation, the local disaster agency said in an Instagram post on Sunday.

Deadly fires are not uncommon in Indonesia.

In December last year, a blaze that was likely caused by a drone battery explosion tore through a seven-storey building in Jakarta and killed at least 22 people, police said.

In 2023, at least 13 people were killed in the country’s east after an explosion at a nickel-processing plant.
