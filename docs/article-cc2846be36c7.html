Jerry Jones opens up on Aaron Rodgers’ accusations about Mike McCarthy’s time in Dallas

2026-11-27 15:17

Jerry Jones may have dismissed Mike McCarthy, but he’s not siding against him in his very public breakup with Aaron Rodgers.

After Rodgers indicated that McCarthy’s issues in Pittsburgh, which he alleged included missing meetings and dozing off during those he attended, also happened during his tenure in Dallas, Jones said Thursday “he never saw anything.”

“I offered him to stay at least another year when he left,” Jones said Thursday after the epic choke in the 39-38 home loss to the Eagles. “And so I thought he was outstanding as the head coach and had a great command of, frankly, quarterback, offense. Can’t say enough good things about him.

“He’s, frankly, one of the finest people around football that I’ve been around. And I can’t say enough about him. I never had the type of thing that is being said about him. I never had that in consideration in any of my time with him, extending his contract. Any of that, that was never on the table. And we never had an issue within the organization.”

While the Rodgers-McCarthy beef is centered on their 13 years together in Green Bay and one season in Pittsburgh, the Cowboys entered the mix when the quarterback brought up McCarthy’s time in Dallas from 2020-24 that ended when the sides could not agree on a new contract.

During Rodgers’ airing of grievances on the “Pat McAfee Show” on Wednesday, he alleged that McCarthy skipped meetings with the Steelers, snored often during those he actually attended and broke down the team saying “1-2-3 Packers” during halftime of last Sunday’s loss to the Eagles.

“At the same time I looked into what was going on in Dallas and what happened there,” Rodgers said Wednesday afternoon, “and it’s the same thing that went on in Green Bay for a decade because the coaches and the support there know exactly what happened.”

He added: “They know what’s going on. Like I said, they all know. Everybody knows. Just like the guys in Green Bay knew. It’s like the people in Dallas knew. Like, they know what the deal is.”

While Jones may not have seen those purported behaviors, he ultimately did not go out of his way to keep McCarthy in Dallas.

McCarthy’s Cowboys tenure featured the team going 49-35 in the regular season and reaching the playoffs three times, but Dallas went 1-3 in the postseason.

The lowlight came in 2024 when the second-seeded Cowboys got throttled 48-32 by the seventh-seeded Packers.

Dallas then went 7-10 the next season and Jones ultimately moved on for current coach Brian Schottenheimer, who is now on the hot seat amid a 5-7 start in his second season.
