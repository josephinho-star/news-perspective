Macron condemns racism after one killed in ‘terrorist’ shooting near Paris

2026-10-23 21:44

Emmanuel Macron has condemned racism in French society after a gunman opened fire from a car west of Paris on Friday, killing one person, wounding a 12-year-old girl and injuring four others in what the president called a “racist terrorist attack”.

Macron arrived at the scene of the drive-by shooting in Trappes on Friday night, saying he was deeply saddened by what he called the “premeditated” attack. The 12-year-old girl, who was shot eight times, is being treated in hospital.

“What happened today marks a turning point for our country; what happened today was a terrorist and racist attack,” Macron said.

He said the gunman had targeted people because of the colour of their skin. The president said racism had often become part of the “ambient discourse in France” and created “an atmosphere”.

He said: “Don’t let this terrorist attack divide us.” He called for “respect and unity”, saying many French people would be scared and thinking “this could happen to me”.

Ali Rabeh, the leftwing mayor of Trappes, said the gunman had set out by car on Friday morning “firing at random, wanting to cause a bloodbath” as he drove through the town and neighbouring La Verrière. The mayor called it “objectively a racist attack, probably Islamophobic”.

Rabeh, whose family has Moroccan roots, said: “The people targeted today could just as easily have been my mother, my brothers or my sisters-in-law.

“A 12-year-old girl is between life and death after being targeted by a volley of automatic gunfire in our country. Her crime was probably being of colour,” he added. “It’s probably one of the first attempts at a mass crime of Islamophobia and racism.”

Rabeh said everyone in France had a responsibility to bring “calm and harmony”. He called for a collective condemnation of rhetoric that “ideologically arms those capable of carrying out such acts”.

Anti-terrorism prosecutors are investigating whether the gunman targeted the “real or supposed race, ethnicity, nationality or religion” of the victims.

The 39-year-old suspect, who was stopped by police in his car, was named by France’s anti-terrorism prosecutor as Kevin T. He had driven through the towns of Trappes and La Verrière in Yvelines at about 10.30am on Friday. Over the course of 15 minutes, he repeatedly fired an automatic weapon, first at a petrol station, then a medical centre and a children’s playground.

The French public broadcaster reported that he shot dead a young black man, then targeted a woman wearing a headscarf, before shooting the 12-year-old girl, who was being treated for her eight gunshot wounds at a children’s hospital on Friday night.

The prosecutor said in a statement on Friday night that the medical status of the five wounded victims was not stable.

The man killed was named as Pengo Bossio Wangi in an Instagram post by the mixed martial arts academy of which he was a member. The Atch Academy paid tribute to a man who “set an example” and said that memories of “his smile, his energy, and his generosity will remain etched within our walls and in our hearts”.

Nicolas Dainville, the mayor of La Verrière, told Agence France-Presse that two of the wounded people in his town were cleaners of African origin attending to a school Friday on morning when the shooter got out of his car to open fire.

The prosecutor said that when the suspect was stopped by police in his car he was found with a sten gun – a British submachine gun used in the second world war – as well as teargas grenades.

The man had also booby-trapped his home with gas canisters and electric wires connected to a timer, authorities said, adding that a member of his family was arrested and taken into custody for questioning.

“The investigations are ongoing, but it is clearly a terrorist crime. It is an extremely serious act,” the interior minister, Laurent Nunez, told reporters from Yvelines.

The shooting comes at a moment of particular tension in France. Last Saturday saw one of the most violent days of unrest in years, which left public buildings burned, hundreds of shops destroyed and looted, and at least 2,400 people arrested in cities across the country.

Renewed protests, over teacher shortages and crumbling buildings in high schools as well as the high cost of living, are expected on Saturday.

Marine Le Pen, the leader of the far-right anti-immigration the National Rally party, is polling highly ahead of next spring’s presidential election when Macron steps down.

After the Trappes attack, Fabien Roussel, a Communist candidate, wrote on social media that the far right was responsible for “racist hatred” in France.

Le Pen condemned what she called the “abject motive of racism” of the “barbaric act”.

Additional reporting by Yassin El-Moudden
