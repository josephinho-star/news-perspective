Federal judge blocks FCC from reviewing ABC station licenses early as part of agency's spat with network

2026-11-26 04:05

A federal judge ordered Wednesday that the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) halt its early review of broadcasting licenses of eight ABC stations as part of the agency's intense feud with the Disney-owned network.

U.S. District Judge Loren L. AliKhan ruled in favor of ABC, which sued the FCC on First Amendment grounds after the agency announced it was moving up the license renewals of eight stations owned by the network years ahead of schedule.

"This is not a close case," AliKhan wrote in the ruling.

"Today’s decision is an important victory for the First Amendment. We thank the court for its careful consideration," an ABC News spokesperson told Fox News Digital.

"While we appreciate the district court denying in part Disney’s request for preliminary injunctive relief, the portion of the decision granting Disney’s motion is wrong on the facts and wrong on the law—as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear," an FCC spokesperson said in a statement. "The FCC looks forward to prevailing in full on this matter. We will continue to use our lawful authority to hold broadcasters accountable to their public interest obligations."

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr had called for early renewal of eight ABC-owned stations' licenses in April. The licenses were originally scheduled to be renewed between 2028 and 2031.

KFSN-TV in Fresno, KABC-TV in Los Angeles, KGO-TV in San Francisco, WLS-TV in Chicago, WABC-TV in New York, WTVD in North Carolina, WPVI-TV in Philadelphia and KTRK-TV in Houston were the stations named by the FCC.

While the FCC maintained the license crackdown was focused on the legality of Disney’s diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) practices, Carr's announcement came as tensions reignited between President Donald Trump and ABC late-night host Jimmy Kimmel.

Kimmel had quipped that first lady Melania Trump had the glow of an "expectant widow" on his show, a joke made days before a gunman targeted members of the Trump administration at the White House Correspondents' Association Dinner but caught national attention after the assassination attempt. Both the first lady and President Trump called for ABC to take action against Kimmel at the time.

Carr had previously leveled a thinly veiled threat against Disney last year over controversial remarks Kimmel made about the assassination of Turning Point USA founder Charlie Kirk.

The FCC separately launched a probe into ABC's daytime talk show "The View" in an effort to upend its status as a bona fide news program, which exempts the network from abiding by long-standing rules requiring equal time for political candidates.

In previous court filings, ABC claimed that the actions taken by the FCC had a chilling effect on the network's free speech, pointing to how "The View" has refrained from inviting political candidates onto the show as a result of the crackdown.
