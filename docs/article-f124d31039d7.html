Supreme court’s decision seems to spell end for Republican-drawn congressional maps in Missouri – US politics live

2026-09-10 17:46

Supreme Court sides against GOP-redrawn Missouri congressional maps, again

The Supreme Court has blocked a federal judge’s ruling that would’ve restored Republican-drawn congressional districts in Missouri.

The decision, issued by justice Brett Kavanaugh, appeared to sound a death knell for efforts by the Missouri GOP to extract an additional Republican-leaning congressional seat for their party ahead of the midterm elections.

The latest decision is the second time the country’s highest court has weighed in on the matter in the last several days. It follows a winding legal saga that has seen the exact boundaries of Missouri’s congressional districts for the 2026 election kept in protracted limbo, with election day less than two months out.

The legal back-and-forth began last September, when Missouri Republicans redrew their state’s electoral maps to carve up a Democrat-controlled district around Kansas City, likely securing them one more of the state’s eight House seats. The decision came as Donald Trump urged Republican state leaders to gerrymander their congressional districts to help their party retain control of Congress, a move that prompted a retaliatory redistricting blitz by Democrats.

Last week, the Missouri supreme court ruled that the new maps weren’t valid until they were put to a public referendum. That meant the state’s congressional districts would revert to their 2022 borders ahead of the midterms. Missouri leaders appealed that decision to the supreme court, which left the lower court’s ruling in place.

A federal judge in St Louis, presiding over a different case, issued a conflicting ruling soon after, ordering the state to return to the Republican-favored maps. It was that ruling Kavanaugh blocked today, in a brief decision with no noted dissents.

Republican leaders in Missouri have argued that requiring the state to shift back to its earlier district boundaries so close to November could throw the state’s elections into chaos. The state held its primaries using the new, GOP-leaning maps, meaning that candidates have won party nominations to represent districts that may no longer exist.

Appeals court upholds block on Trump’s executive order limiting mail voting

A US appeals court has rejected Donald Trump’s bid to impose sweeping restrictions on mail-in voting, dealing a fresh blow to the president’s attempted crackdown.

The Boston-based first US circuit court of appeals three-judge panel declined to put on hold an injunction issued by a lower court judge last week, preventing the US Postal Service from tightening rules around postal voting.

The decision comes as the Supreme Court considers the same case and states have already started sending out ballots for the high-stakes midterm elections.

James Talarico accuses Paxton of dismissing Epstein survivors

James Talarico, the Democratic candidate for Senate in Texas, has accused his rival Ken Paxton of “ignoring” survivors of Jeffrey Epstein’s sexual abuse, as Democrats seek to increase pressure on Republicans ahead of the midterm elections.

Speaking in Dallas, where Republicans are holding a midterm convention this week, Talarico appeared alongside one of Epstein’s victims at an exhibit which featured about three million pages of Department of Justice files related to Epstein.

“When did pedophilia become a partisan issue? When did holding sexual abusers accountable become controversial?” Talarico said. He said survivors of Epstein’s abuse had requested to meet with Paxton, the Texas attorney general, but he had refused.

“Our attorney general here in Texas has the power to get justice for these survivors, and he’s refusing to do so,” Talarico said.

He added: “Ken Paxton has two choices: he can stand with these survivors and get justice, or he can continue to cover up Jeffrey Epstein’s abuse. The choice is entirely his.”

On Wednesday Gina Hinojosa, the Democrat running against Texas Governor Greg Abbott, visited the same exhibit and met with Epstein’s victims, in a sign Democrats plan to highlight Trump’s relationship with the convicted sex offender.

“It’s really incredible that we live in a country where the justice system is so broken at every level that there has not been accountability of what we know was extensive sexual abuse, exploitation that happened for decades,” Hinojosa said, according to Associated press. “And that powerful people who run this country are still living their lives without having been held to account.”

Beto O’Rourke calls Trump's promise of $5,000 payments "a bribe"

Beto O’Rourke, a former US congressman and presidential candidate, has dismissed Donald Trump’s offer of $5,000 to US citizens if Republicans win Congress as “the stink of desperation.”

“I think people will take it for what it is: it’s a gimmick, it’s a bribe,” O’Rourke said on Thursday at a roundtable hosted by Bloomberg News in Dallas. “It’s another promise that the president will be unable to fulfill. He’s promised these kinds of dividends before. I think we were supposed to get one from all of the money we were making off of tariffs. It’s never materialised.”

O’Rourke, founder of Powered by People, a Texas-based progressive organisation focused on turning out voters, continued: “It adds to the stink of desperation. That is emanating from that convention centre and every action that we’ve seen over the last 12 months: gerrymandering five congressional districts, screwing with vote by mail, shutting down 92 polling places, more than a million Texans on the suspense list.

“This is a party that cannot compete on the power of its ideas or on its accomplishments while in office and so they’re trying to stop people literally from voting or bribe those who they think can be persuaded. I just don’t think that’s going to be effective.”

O’Rourke, who lost a Texas Senate race to Ted Cruz in 2018, struck an optimistic note about Democrat James Talarico’s chances against Republican Ken Paxton. He suggested that Trump is even more unpopular now than he was then and that Talarico will be boosted by Democrats competing in every down ballot election this time.

“If you had to dream up the perfect opponent in the Senate race, if you’re a Democrat, you could not do better than Ken Paxton. Zero charisma, no connection, no money. The president is openly mocking him at every single turn. Those are all totally different from what we saw in 2018.”

Trump administration allows 170,000 migrants from El Salvador to keep deportation protections, for now

After temporary protections were set to expire Wednesday for hundreds of thousands of Salvadoran migrants in the United States, the Department of Homeland Security said it would, at least for now, allow them to keep their protected status.

The department said in a statement that the temporary protected status that has permitted roughly 200,000 Salvadoran migrants to live in the US without fear of deportation would remain for the time being.

“An announcement on El Salvador’s TPS will be made at the appropriate time,” the Department of Homeland Security said in a statement. “Until such announcement is made, Salvadoran individuals present in the U.S. under TPS retain protection.”

Top Trump advisors warn Iran war could outlast his second term, report says

Both before and during Republicans’ midterm convention yesterday, President Trump predicted that the war in Iran that he helped launch would end immediately after the midterm elections (assuming that his party held onto its majority in Congress).

But a new report from the Wall Street Journal, published Wednesday evening, indicates that, behind closed doors, Trump’s top advisors have given him less optimistic forecasts. The paper reports that top officials – including JD Vance, Trump’s vice president, and Marco Rubio, the secretary of state – have warned that Iran could very well hold out against US pressure through the remainder of Trump’s second term.

The unpopular and seemingly frozen conflict, as well as the surging energy prices it touched off, have become a sore spot for Republicans ahead of the midterms. Fighting has flared up again after negotiations between the US and Iran fell through, with both sides trading missile attacks on oil tankers.

The Trump administration’s latest step, a new sanctions regime aimed at strangling what remains of Iran’s economy, would punish nations that continue to trade with the regime.

Trump has cast his party’s victory in the midterms as the difference between US success and failure in Iran. He’s repeatedly dismissed economic pain downstream of the conflict as a temporary but worthwhile sacrifice to subdue Iran and prevent it from obtaining a nuclear weapon.

Democrat John Fetterman makes surprise appearance at Republican convention

The video message will do little to dampen speculation that the renegade senator, who is now more popular among Republican voters in Pennsylvania than the Democrats who helped elect him, is preparing to switch political parties.
