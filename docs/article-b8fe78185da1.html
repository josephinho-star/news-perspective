Russian warship sinks off Libya as suspected Ukrainian strikes take war beyond Black Sea

2026-11-23 01:30

A Russian warship capsized and sank Sunday after suspected Ukrainian sea drones struck a naval base in eastern Libya, triggering an explosion and fire, according to Libyan officials.

Hours later, a second drone attack also hit a commercial ship off the coast of Benghazi, Reuters reported.

The first ship, the Russian navy corvette Merkury, was docked at Benghazi Naval Base when two explosive-laden sea drones struck the vessel, according to Libyan military officials who spoke to The Associated Press.

At least four other drones were intercepted and destroyed, the officials said.

Satellite images showed most of the warship capsized and submerged, with only its bow remaining above water. Smoke could be seen rising from the ship's superstructure, and debris was scattered along the pier.

Ten people, including Russian and Libyan nationals, suffered minor injuries in the attack and were receiving treatment in Benghazi, medical sources told Reuters.

The warship had stopped at the port to refuel and resupply while returning to Russia under a maritime cooperation agreement with Libya's eastern-based government, officials said in a statement.

Libyan military officials said an initial investigation indicated that Ukrainian sea drones had been launched from a base in western Libya, according to The AP.

Hours after the warship was struck, a Maltese-flagged commercial ship was also hit by a sea drone off Benghazi, two Libyan military sources told Reuters.

Libyan naval forces rescued the commercial ship's crew, while health officials reported that some crew members sustained minor injuries and were in stable condition.

Libyan naval and air forces also intercepted more than 20 drone boats off Benghazi on Sunday evening, the military sources told Reuters.

The attacks prompted Libya's eastern-based government to seek an international investigation.

Prime Minister Osama Hamad said his government had submitted a formal request to the United Nations General Assembly and Security Council to investigate the attacks, including suspicions that the drones were launched from western Libya.

Hamad also called for an investigation into the possible presence of Ukrainian military experts in Libya and requested an explanation from Kyiv about their activities.

The eastern government insisted that Libya was not a party to the war between Russia and Ukraine and condemned attempts to use its territory to settle foreign conflicts.

"The Libyan government will not allow Libyan ports, territory or territorial waters to be turned into an arena for settling scores or exporting international conflicts," the government said.

Benghazi is controlled by forces loyal to Gen. Khalifa Haftar, whose self-styled Libyan National Army dominates eastern and southern Libya and maintains close ties with Russia.

Libya has been divided since the 2011 NATO-backed uprising that toppled longtime dictator Moammar Gadhafi.

A U. N. recognized government operates from Tripoli, while a rival administration controls much of the east.

The suspected attacks also come as Ukraine has expanded its campaign against Russian naval assets beyond the Black Sea. In December 2025, Ukraine said it struck a Russian-linked oil tanker with aerial drones in the Mediterranean Sea.

In March 2026, Russia accused Ukraine of attacking a Russian liquefied natural gas tanker with naval drones launched from the Libyan coast.

Libyan officials previously told the AP that Ukrainian drone specialists had been operating from military facilities in western Libya, including an air base in Misrata and sites in Tripoli and Zawiya.

Since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Ukrainian forces have used explosive-laden sea drones and missiles to strike Russian warships and naval installations, primarily in the Black Sea.
