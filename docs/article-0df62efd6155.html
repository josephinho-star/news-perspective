‘Heated Rivalry’ star’s girlfriend calls out fans for crossing ‘huge’ boundary: ‘Hugely uncomfortable for everyone’

2026-09-23 02:11

Katelyn Larson, the girlfriend of “Heated Rivalry” heartthrob Hudson Williams, asked fans who come to her tattoo studio solely because of her connection to the Netflix star to “please stop” testing boundaries, in a heated social media post.

“Obviously a lot of you have found the studio because of a certain show and that’s more than okay,” the tattoo artist — who works in Canada — wrote via Instagram Stories on Tuesday.

“This is where I draw a very hard line: booking an appointment with me or anyone else purely because of the connection to the show crosses a huge territory.”

She continued, “Showing up to your appointment and asking my coworkers any sort of questions about myself or about Hudson is hugely uncomfortable for everyone.”

Larson emphasized that it’s fine to book an appointment “if you’re following me or any of the other artists because you genuinely like their art and want to get some cool tattoos.”

She told fans to “please go right ahead,” and urged them not to “ever feel weird about that.”

She concluded the lengthy statement “with peace and love” before asking fans to “please stop” the previously mentioned uncomfortable behavior.

Williams and Larson began dating years before the 25-year-old TV star’s sudden rise to fame on the steamy hockey-themed drama opposite on-screen love interest Connor Storrie, 26.

The longtime couple made their red carpet debut at the Vanity Fair Oscar party in March, posing affectionately on the red carpet together.

But Williams has been candid about his girlfriend’s desire to remain out of the public eye amid his high-profile career.

“She’s not trying to be out there or be perceived,” the actor explained to ETalk Canada back in March. “She’s always like, ‘I don’t want to be perceived!'”

Last month, the TV star took to Instagram Stories to gush over their romance in rare comments on her birthday.

“My darling you were eternalized when you ran to that sunset up that red dirt road,” he wrote alongside a photo of the artist, per E! News.

“I’ve loved people deeply for a day, for a week and some for years but I’ll love her until I die,” he continued.

“Not because I’m supposed to, or because that’s how I was raised, but because every time she smiles I see the little girl whose joy is so unpolluted.”

He concluded the tribute by labeling Larson his “person.”
