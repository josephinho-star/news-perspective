Hakeem Jeffries formally nominated as first Black US House speaker

2026-11-18 15:37

Washington, DC, United States – Hakeem Jeffries has become the first Black lawmaker officially nominated for speaker of the US House of Representatives, marking a milestone in the country’s political history.

In a closed-door vote on Wednesday, the 56-year-old New York congressman secured his party’s nomination for the post just weeks after Democrats scored sweeping victories in the midterm elections, reclaiming control of both chambers of the US Congress.

After voting concluded, Jeffries took to social media to thank his fellow Democrats for their support.

“Together, we will deliver bold change. And make a big difference in the lives of the American people!” he wrote in a post on X.

Amid growing dissent among progressives over his leadership, Jeffries confirmed plans to force members of his party to take a recorded vote on Wednesday.

Some of those members, most of whom were elected this month, declined to say whether they would support Jeffries. Several said they wanted a leader who would support bold, populist policies they had campaigned on.

One of the most vocal holdouts, Congressman-elect Don Leonard of Ohio, refused to commit to Jeffries unless he received guarantees on some progressive priorities, including halting US military aid to Israel and reforming campaign finance standards set by the Supreme Court case Citizens United.

“Unless Speaker Jeffries is willing to commit to enforcing our nation’s laws like the Leahy Law against Israeli genocide in Gaza, unless the speaker is willing to get to the heart of this crisis, which is Citizens United, he will not have my vote,” the Ohio Democrat said in a video posted to social media.

Jeffries has received pushback within some Democratic circles over his record on Israel and ties to pro-Israel lobbying groups, like AIPAC, the American Israel Public Affairs Committee. During an interview with The Atlantic magazine on Tuesday, Jeffries addressed some of that criticism, calling for “dramatic change” in the Middle East and a two-state solution.

Despite some opposition in Wednesday’s vote, Jeffries received overwhelming support from the House Democratic Caucus with 251 members voting to back his candidacy. Only 11 members opposed it.

Seven of those who voted against Jeffries included newly-elected Democrats Darializa Avila Chevalier of New York, Adam Hamawy of New Jersey, Melat Kiros of Colorado, Donavan McKinney of Michigan, Chris Rabb of Pennsylvania and Claire Valdez of New York.

Those members released a statement, which was shared with Al Jazeera, detailing their opposition they said was over failure to receive commitments from Jeffries on policies like “Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics or ending the genocide in Gaza”.

“While we have seen meaningful progress, there is still work to be done to ensure that the direction our party takes this coming Congress reflects what our constituents are demanding of leadership”, the soon-to-be lawmakers wrote. “Those conversations are ongoing. As such, our vote in caucus today was no”.

Another future member, Frederick Haynes of Texas, was included in the joint-statement against Jeffries’ nomination, but clarified later, in his own statement, he “narrowly” missed Wednesday’s vote due to illness.

Most Democrats, however, praised Jeffries’ nomination for speaker, which will put him on track to become one of Donald Trump’s top political opponents during the final two years of his presidency.

Outgoing Congresswoman Nancy Pelosi, who served for eight years as the first female speaker during two non-consecutive terms, marked the historic moment with a social media post, which included a photo of her holding Jeffries hand.

“Congratulations, Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries!” she wrote.

Just before the vote, Jeffries took to social media to post a Bible verse from the book of Corinthians reading,” For we walk by faith, not by sight.”

In 2023, Jeffries was put forward by Democrats as their pick to succeed former US House Speaker Kevin McCarthy, who was ousted by his fellow Republicans in favour of the chamber’s current speaker, Mike Johnson. Wednesday’s vote, however, means Jeffries has now been formally nominated by members of his party.

Jeffries is poised to replace Johnson as speaker when the new Congress is sworn in on January 3 with a Democratic majority and a vote on the new speaker is held in the full chamber.
