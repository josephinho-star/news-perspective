Texas executes Jamaal Howard despite same lethal injection drug used in failed Christa Pike execution

2026-10-08 05:50

Michael Sinkewicz, Fox News

Published Oct. 8, 2026, 1:50 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

A Texas man convicted of killing a convenience store clerk during a robbery was executed Wednesday evening after the Supreme Court declined to halt the execution, despite his attorneys raising concerns about the lethal injection drug used in a failed execution attempt in Tennessee last week.

Jamaal Howard, 46, was pronounced dead at 8:03 p.m. He was sentenced to death for the 2000 killing of 42-year-old Vickie Swartout in Silsbee, Texas.

“Texas should not be eager to follow in Tennessee’s footsteps; a stay would keep that from happening,” Howard’s attorneys argued in their filing.

The Supreme Court rejected Howard’s emergency appeal without explanation. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only justice to publicly dissent, Fox News reported.

The Texas Court of Criminal Appeals had previously rejected two requests to stop the execution based on concerns about the drug’s use.

According to The Associated Press, as the pentobarbital began taking effect Wednesday evening, Howard took a couple of breaths and gasped.

Howard then began making snoring sounds, coughed and resumed the snoring sounds, which grew progressively quieter.

His movements reportedly stopped entirely after around 30 seconds, and he was pronounced dead 19 minutes later.

Howard’s attorneys also said in court filings that they had requested information about the dose of pentobarbital the Texas Department of Criminal Justice planned to use to carry out the execution.

The concerns stemmed in part from Pike’s execution attempt, during which she reportedly survived two doses of pentobarbital.

Howard’s execution took place the same day a Tennessee judge granted a request from Pike’s attorneys to order the Tennessee Department of Correction to preserve evidence connected to the failed execution attempt.
