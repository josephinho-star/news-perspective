Tom Brady on speed dial? Welcome to Fernando Mendoza’s world

2026-11-26 00:47

Imagine making the first two starts of your NFL career, getting sacked 11 times in the process, and finding the same invitation waiting on your phone after each game.

“Hit me up when you can.”

Now imagine the guy on the other end is Tom Brady, the greatest quarterback of all time. And he’s got time if you want to talk about what happened on third down.

Fernando Mendoza can skip the imagining. This is what the first couple of weeks on the job have looked like for the Raiders’ rookie quarterback.

Play Seattle. Hear from Brady.

Go to Denver, throw the first three touchdown passes of your career and one you’d love to have back. Hear from Brady again.

“He usually reaches out to us after every game,” Mendoza said.

Brady is a minority owner of the Raiders, their de facto chief of football operations, and one of the most influential voices in the decision to make Mendoza the first overall pick in the draft. He had plenty of say in putting the kid out there. What’s interesting is how much say he’s giving Mendoza in what happens next.

Hit me up when you can. Think about how differently that lands, coming from someone who owns a piece of the team, than a message telling a rookie quarterback to call him the minute he gets on the bus.

Mendoza called it “an open line of communication” and gets to decide where the conversation goes. Maybe the most interesting throw in Denver was one of the touchdowns. Maybe it was a ball that got batted back at him. Imagine being able to ask Brady what he used to look for in a defensive lineman who’d stopped rushing and started watching his eyes.

Whatever the question, Mendoza gets to ask a guy who spent 23 seasons working out the answer.

“I’ve really tried to exercise that resource, as he’s the GOAT, and nobody better to take advice from.”

Smart kid.
