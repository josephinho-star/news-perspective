Duffy says he ‘can’t cancel’ contract with firm behind FAA outage

2026-11-15 17:27

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says he “can’t cancel” control with L3Harris, the firm blamed for the Federal Aviation Administration’s (FAA) outage on Friday that resulted in a nationwide ground stop.

“The reason is that L3Harris decades ago got the contract to run and own the network that operates communication for the FAA. They own the network,” Duffy said during an appearance on NBC News’s “Meet the Press.”

“Which is why I’m asking for billions of dollars from Congress to build a brand-new architecture of a network, by the best companies in the world including AT&T, Verizon and Lumens.”

The transportation secretary continued that he had the architecture “ready” and the companies were ready “to deploy tomorrow,” but the last piece he needed was money from Congress.

“I want to get away from L3Harris and get to a new one but they own the network. I can’t cancel the network that they own,” he concluded.

On Saturday, L3Harris released a statement, saying it had “been modernizing the communications infrastructure supporting the National Airspace System in close coordination with the FAA.”

“We are supporting the FAA in its investigation to understand the conditions that led to the Nov. 13 ground stop of our nation’s air travel,” the company continued. “L3Harris takes the nationwide ground stop and its impact on travelers, airlines and air traffic operations seriously.”

Duffy has credited the outage to a lack of Congressional funding that would have enabled the FAA to transition from antiquated old copper wiring to modern fiber, though Democrats were quick to question why he had spent only a fraction of the money already allocated by Congress.

Rep. Robert Garcia (D-Calif.), ranking member of the House Oversight Committee, has pledged to investigate Friday’s incident.

In another Sunday interview with CNN, Duffy deflected responsibility for the outage, saying he takes “responsibility for what was given to me by prior administrations.”

The outage resulted in fifteen percent of all flights in Atlanta, the world’s busiest airport, being canceled, with another nearly 20 percent delayed on Friday, according to flight tracking website FlightAware.
