Deadly SoCal Six Flags rollercoaster claims third victim as mother tells heartbreaking story

2026-11-18 19:13

The infamous Six Flags rollercoaster linked to two deaths and numerous injuries over the past 24 years has allegedly claimed a third victim.

A 17-year-old autistic boy reportedly died in June this year after riding X2 at Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California.

Christopher Gibson and his mother, Leanndra Fascaldo, drove from Colorado to California to celebrate the teen’s high school graduation. The mother-son duo rode several famous roller coasters, including the X2 over four days.

Gibson, who was diagnosed with autism when he was five or six, rode his last roller coaster ride at the amusement park on Jun. 3 and complained of a bad headache soon after.

According to ProPublica, he texted his mother from a park restroom, saying, “I don’t think I can do any more today. My head is hurting me.”

They decided to return to the hotel, and Gibson took refuge in the room’s closet. His mother said it was a normal thing for him to do as he turned to dark spaces to decompress.

Fascaldo gave him Advil for the headache and listened to his snoring while he rested. But sometime in the afternoon on Jun. 4, his snoring stopped.

She looked into the closet and saw his face was covered with “white and pink foam,” according to ProPublica. She also noticed that his lips had turned blue.

Paramedics responded to the hotel room after she called 911, and Gibson was pronounced dead at 3:16 p.m. on Jun. 4.

The teen’s mother told a sheriff’s detective that Gibson was “high-functioning and verbal” and had recently graduated high school. He was headed to Colorado Mesa University for college.

“[He] had so many dreams, and had his whole life ahead of him,” Fascaldo said in a statement released through her lawyer, reported ProPublica.

The County of Los Angeles Medical Examiner determined “blunt force head trauma” as the cause of Gibson’s death.

The California Post reached out to Six Flags Magic Mountain, and a spokesperson for the park said that safety is “at the heart of everything we do.”

According to the spokesperson, Six Flags maintains a multilayered safety program with documented daily pre-opening inspections, ongoing preventive maintenance, and internal and external oversight.

“That oversight includes independent third-party ride inspection firms, insurance and state inspectors where applicable, and Six Flags corporate engineering and safety professionals,” the spokesperson said in an email statement.

It isn’t the first time the controversial coaster has been in the headlines.

His family sued Six Flags in 2023, and the case was set for trial in September before reportedly being settled for an undisclosed amount.

Back in 2010, Hilda Farias, 28, also died after riding X2, although medical experts at the time put her death down to multiple factors, including an existing brain abnormality that may have ruptured due to the ride’s intensity.

After two incidents that left riders with serious injuries, Magic Mountain President Brian Oerding announced that retiring X2 was “the right thing to do” to protect guest confidence.
