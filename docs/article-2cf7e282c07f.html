Aaron Rodgers and Mike McCarthy disagreements at heart of quarterback's benching by the Steelers

2026-11-25 16:36

The relationship between Pittsburgh Steelers coach Mike McCarthy and Aaron Rodgers has been strained at times in recent weeks and apparently hit a new low on Tuesday, which led to the quarterback being deactivated for the next game and possibly being done playing for the team.

Rodgers and McCarthy have disagreed about multiple "football issues" with the team's offense in recent weeks, a source told OutKick. The quarterback wanted some changes that McCarthy did not immediately agree with.

The source said those disagreements were part of McCarthy deciding to deactivate Rodgers for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos and name Mason Rudolph as his starter, but that there was a bigger difference between the men that sealed the decision.

The source said he could not offer the specifics of the situation that apparently reached a climax on Tuesday.

But the timing of the matter is easy to pinpoint.

The whole episode seemed to come in like a flood.

McCarthy told reporters on Monday that "Aaron Rodgers is our quarterback."

On Tuesday, the Steelers held a walk-thru practice and Rodgers was reportedly at his spot with the starters. He also held a press conference on Tuesday, as the team's starting quarterback is required to do.

But later the same day, something happened and things changed.

Late Tuesday night, the Steelers announced their quarterback change.

Whatever happened, Rodgers was excused from reporting to work on Wednesday. He did not attend a team meeting and was not expected to be at practice.

And here's the greater issue: If Rodgers and McCarthy cannot somehow reach agreement in their relationship at some point in the coming days or after the Denver game, the possibility Rodgers may be done playing for the Steelers grows significantly.

The NFL Network reported on Wednesday the Steelers are not inclined to release Rodgers at this point. But the situation is fluid and it might reach a point where the Steelers feel the need to cut ties rather than let the issue fester not only between the Rodgers and coach, but also in the locker room.

The Steelers locker room is a dynamic that McCarthy was managing on Wednesday. Players were actually not informed of the quarterback change by the coach until Wednesday, according to a source.

Some players found out about the quarterback change on social media or via reports Tuesday night.

The reaction to all this even as the Steelers are locked in a race for the AFC North title or perhaps a wild card berth threatens to rock the team.

One player texted OutKick on Wednesday that whatever was decided at quarterback would have to be fine with him. "Got no choice," he wrote.

But there are reports of dissatisfaction among some players with McCarthy and the benching of a four-time MVP is probably not going to soothe that feeling.

What happens next is up in the air.

If new starter Mason Rudolph plays well against the Broncos it increases the chances the Steelers would release Rodgers if he requests it because they do not want an unhappy Rodgers around the team the final six weeks of the season.

But what if Rudolph lays an egg? It would make McCarthy's decision to bench Rodgers seem impulsive and hasty. And it might require the coach to try to mend proverbial fences with Rodgers.

A league source told OutKick that McCarthy and Rodgers reconciling and showing a public united front is "not on the table right now."

None of this is good for the Steelers and the only thing that can salvage the situation is Rudolph rewarding McCarthy's promotion with a great performance.

Otherwise the Steelers soap opera could continue in the direction of a Shakespearean tragedy.
