Of course the malicious media is already attacking 2028 Republicans

2026-11-18 00:08

Death, taxes and tenuous hit pieces on any and every prospective Republican presidential candidate: Why is it that all of life’s certainties are so vexing?

The midterms are in the rearview mirror, so the 2028 election cycle is now, like it or not, upon us — and the media is already in midseason form.

With Vice President JD Vance waiting to decide whether to seek a promotion and picking fights with his own coalition in the meantime, others are emerging as viable alternatives to the veep.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis and former Virginia Gov. Glenn Youngkin, in particular, have carved out potential lanes for themselves.

Naturally, the press is trying to torpedo them before any of them delivers his first stump speech.

Rubio, the runner-up to Vance according to most polls, has set himself apart in his current roles.

His juggling of countless complex tasks, clear communication style and close relationship with President Trump has many speculating that he might be the man best suited to unify the party and compete in a general election two years from now.

It’s also made him a target.

Hence the recent Washington Post report about his use of government aircraft to attend his son Anthony’s football games at the University of Florida.

“Rubio says his trips are about something familiar to any parent: showing up. But they also come at a cost to taxpayers,” reported the Post, which castigated Rubio for making it to 10 games since he was sworn in last January.

Only later is it revealed that “Like the defense secretary and the directors of the FBI and CIA, Rubio is barred from flying commercially for security reasons and is required, when traveling for personal reasons, to reimburse the government the equivalent of an economy-fare ticket.”

That didn’t stop Sally Jenkins — the sportswriter whose pivot to politics over at The Atlantic is failing miserably — from piling on in true mean-girl fashion.

“Rubio’s son Anthony may be a very nice young man. What he isn’t, arguably, is a college football player worth flying in to watch — because he barely ever plays,” Jenkins jeered.

“He has made it onto the field for exactly three rushing attempts this season. Yet Rubio, a Florida alum, has flown to at least 10 Gator games,” she marveled.

Earth to Sally: you can support your child and the team he’s a part of even if he’s not its star.

Turns out she’s emotionally as well as intellectually vacant.

The Post followed up its “Father Supports Son, Follows the Law” bombshell with another misfire.

“Despite months of former governor Glenn Youngkin (R) touting his decision to enroll Virginia in a new federal school voucher program, a recent letter from the IRS revealed that his administration did not complete the process, according to documents reviewed by The Washington Post, and his successor will reverse Youngkin’s decision,” blared the lede of a recent “Exclusive.”

You have to read deeper to find out why, exactly, Youngkin “did not complete the process.”

As it turns out, “Virginia did not complete a mandatory second step to enroll in the program because a portal to submit a list of scholarship-granting organizations was not available at the time Youngkin’s administration started the process.”

The paper went on to nevertheless parrot a nameless flack for incumbent Democrat Abigail Spanberger, who offered a dig about how her predecessor had failed to “actually enter Virginia into the program.”

And that, my friends, is how you turn a real story about a teachers-union pet betraying her young constituents into a fake one alleging carelessness on the part of the students’ champion.

Not to be outdone, ABC’s “Good Morning America” recently turned back the clock to the early 2020s and churned out a story about how a DeSantis-backed law had compelled Delray Beach, Fla. to cancel its St. Patrick’s Day festivities.

The governor himself submitted that “What they are doing is not mandated by Florida law,” and was “just an attempt to make a political point — a point that legacy media will no doubt try to amplify.”

“We’ve seen these stunts throughout the years,” he mused. “Rinse and repeat.”

His counterargument is evidenced by the fact that this story is about a single municipality, rather than every one in the state.

If no one else thinks that the big, bad, mean governor has outlawed Guinness and shepherd’s pie, maybe, just maybe, Delray Beach is wrong to insist he has.

Everyone who runs is about to be put under the micro-kaleidoscope through which the Fourth Estate views all nationally relevant Republicans.

Voters ought to steel themselves against the coming smears, because the press’ most vicious partisans are already flooding the airwaves with fake news.
