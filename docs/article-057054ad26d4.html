Tennessee’s likely next gov calls Christa Pike’s botched execution reason to ‘bring back the electric chair’

2026-10-06 12:21

Sen. Marsha Blackburn has vowed to “bring back the electric chair” if she becomes Tennessee’s next governor – saying Christa Pike’s failed execution “should not be grounds to halt” the death penalty.

“While there should be a thorough review of what happened and why the process failed, the answer is simple: bring back the electric chair and deliver justice for these victims swiftly,” said Blackburn, who is expected to win November’s election.

“As your governor I will do just that.”

Blackburn said Monday that Pike’s unsuccessful execution “should not be grounds to halt carrying out the death penalty,” but a reason to reevaluate the best method.

“If it’s lethal injection, if it is electric chair, what we do know is we have to find a way to do this — and do it successfully,” she said, citing other states using more successful methods.

You look at Florida; they’ve had 19 executions this year. Not one of them has been botched.”

Inmates who committed crimes before Jan. 1 1999 can choose electrocution over lethal injection by signing a waiver, according to state law. Electrocution can also be carried out if the doses of lethal injection are unavailable.

Blackburn’s position is a sharp contrast to that of her Democrat opponent Jerri Green, who called for a moratorium on all future state executions.
