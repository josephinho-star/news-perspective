Unforgiving rim denies Arkansas a miracle comeback against Michigan State on Thanksgiving

2026-11-27 01:08

With 1:18 left in a game played on Thanksgiving, Arkansas trailed No. 11 Michigan State by 10 and the Spartans were at the free-throw line. If you’d offered John Calipari one shot to send the game to overtime, I’m guessing he’d have taken it before you finished the question.

Well, he got his shot. Jordan Smith Jr. pulled up from 29 feet, and the ball found just about every part of the rim at Little Caesars Arena before it finally bounced out. Michigan State escaped Detroit with a 70-67 win to move to 6-1, and Arkansas fell to 4-3.

Arkansas took a 31-27 lead into halftime, pushed it to eight early in the second half and still led 47-40 with 13:31 left. If you’re a Michigan State fan who watched the Spartans lose at Tennessee nine days earlier, I can’t blame you for thinking, "Uh-oh." They’d made all of three baskets in the second half in Knoxville and shot 18% for the game.

Down seven in Detroit, Jeremy Fears Jr. hit a three. Then he found Jesse McCulloch for a hook shot and Coen Carr for the dunk that tied it.

Michigan State scored 15 straight, and by the time Kur Teng buried his third three in less than two minutes, the Spartans led 64-51 with 4:55 left. That’s a 24-4 run. The same team that went 3-for-26 after halftime at Tennessee went 17-for-21 (81%) in the second half against Arkansas.

Michigan State still led 68-58 when Fears stepped to the line with 1:18 left. He missed the front end of a one-and-one, but Arkansas still needed 10 points just to pull even.

So how did the Razorbacks make it interesting in the final seconds? For starters, Michigan State just couldn’t grab a defensive rebound. The Razorbacks missed two threes on the same possession before Billy Richmond III tipped the ball in with 1:03 to go.

Then Smith picked off a Fears pass. On the ensuing possession, Arkansas rebounded a missed free throw AND a missed three before Smith found Richmond for the three that cut it to 68-64.

Arkansas gets a very generous and-one

Jeremy Fears Jr. made both free throws with 16 seconds left to put Michigan State back up six. Then came the call I’d still be asking about if I were Tom Izzo.

Smith drove to the basket and spun past Fears for a layup. The officials called Fears for a foul, counted the basket and gave Smith a chance to cut it to three with 10 seconds left.

Did Fears foul Smith? Absolutely. Smith still had his back to the basket and was about 13 feet from the hoop when the contact came. Smith finished his spin, took another full step and then laid the ball in. How much continuation are we giving this guy? Even the CBS crew thought the foul should have been ruled on the floor.

Smith made the free throw with 10 seconds left, Fears missed the front end of a one-and-one with eight seconds left, and guess who came down with the rebound? Smith had six seconds and the length of the floor to find a three. From 29 feet, he came about as close as you can get.

A Thanksgiving bad beat for Michigan State bettors

If you laid 5.5 with Michigan State, how good did you feel with 1:18 left? Your team was up 10, and you had a career 81% free-throw shooter at the line. You could give up a couple of garbage-time buckets and still cash.

Fears missed the front end, Arkansas grabbed four offensive rebounds in two possessions, and Michigan State threw the ball away in between. And after all that? You were still covering when Fears hit both free throws to make it 70-64 with 16 seconds left.

It took that generous continuation call to put Arkansas back inside the number. Then your 81% free-throw shooter went back to the line with eight seconds left and missed the front end of another one-and-one.

That left Michigan State bettors rooting for the same guy who’d just cost them the cover. If Smith’s three drops, you get five more minutes to win by six. If you had Arkansas +5.5, on the other hand, you had every reason to cheer when that ball finally bounced out.

Which probably raised a fair question from across the Thanksgiving table: "Wait, didn’t you bet on Arkansas?"
