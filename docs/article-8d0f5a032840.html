Here's some tips on how to organize photos with keywords on your phone

2026-10-17 17:15

Your phone probably holds thousands of pictures. Some are important family memories, while others are screenshots you saved because you thought you might need them later. Then comes the frustrating part. You remember taking a particular photo, but finding it feels like searching for a needle in a digital haystack.

Apple's iOS 27 offers a way to make that search easier by letting you add keywords to individual photos. Instead of scrolling through months of pictures, you can search for a word you've assigned to a photo and pull it up again.

Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel users have similar ways to organize their pictures, although the tools work differently. Here's how to take advantage of them and spend less time hunting for photos.

How to add keywords to photos on iPhone

Apple's Photos app can already recognize people, pets, places and objects. However, automatic searches don't always understand why a particular photo is important to you. That's where manually assigned keywords can help.

For example, you might photograph a parking spot at an airport or save a picture of a particular appliance model number. Adding a keyword you will remember later can make the photo much easier to find.

Add keywords to photos in iOS 27

These instructions are based on an iPhone 15 Pro running iOS 27.

- Open the Photos app on your iPhone.

- Find and open the photo you want to organize.

- Tap the Info (ⓘ) icon or swipe up on the photo.

- Tap Add Keywords.

- Enter the keyword or keywords you want to associate with the picture.

- Tap the checkmark icon to save your changes.

- If you want to edit or add more keywords later, you can tap Edit .

Choose words that make sense to you. For example, a picture of your car's license plate might get a keyword such as "license plate." You could also tag pictures from a family vacation with a memorable trip name.

How to find photos using keywords on iPhone

Once you've added keywords, searching for those pictures takes only a few seconds.

- Open the Photos app.

- Tap the Search icon, which looks like a magnifying glass.

- Type one of the keywords you previously added.

- Look for a suggested search result with the Aa symbol.

- Tap that result to see photos and videos associated with the keyword.

You can also manage your keywords by opening Photos > Collections > your profile icon > Manage Keywords. From there, you can rename keywords, delete them or add frequently used keywords to your favorites.

Pro tip: Start with photos you actually struggle to find. Important documents, appliance labels and travel information are useful candidates. There's no need to spend an entire weekend tagging your photo library.

How to organize photos on Samsung Galaxy

Samsung Galaxy phones also let you add tags to pictures using the built-in Gallery app. That means you can assign your own keywords and search for them later, much like you can on an iPhone. Depending on your Galaxy model and Gallery version, the exact location of the tagging controls may vary. However, Samsung supports manual photo tagging on compatible devices.

Add tags to photos on Samsung Galaxy

Here's how to tag a picture using Samsung Gallery.

- Open the Gallery app.

- Select the photo you want to organize.

- Swipe up on the photo to open its details, or tap the Details icon (ⓘ) if it appears at the bottom of the screen.

- Select Add tag.

- Enter a keyword or phrase that describes the picture.

- Tap Save.

On some Galaxy versions, you may need to tap Edit in the photo's details to add a tag. If you don't see the option, make sure your Gallery app is updated.

How to find tagged photos on Samsung Galaxy

Once you've tagged a photo, you can use the Gallery's search feature to find it again.

- Open the Gallery app.

- Tap the Search icon.

- Enter the tag you assigned to the photo.

- Select the matching picture from the search results.

For example, you could tag pictures of your home appliances with their model names. Later, when you need a serial number or replacement part, you can search for the tag instead of scrolling through your entire camera roll.

Create an album on Samsung Galaxy

Albums offer another way to keep related pictures together, especially when you want to organize several photos from the same event or project.

- Open Gallery.

- Tap Albums.

- Tap the plus (+) icon or the option to create a new album.

- Choose Create album or Album if prompted.

- Give your album a descriptive name.

- Tap Create.

- Select the photos you want to add.

- Tap Done.

- If prompted with "Copy or move items?", choose Move to move the photos into the new album or Copy to create duplicates in the new album.

For example, you could create an album for home repairs and keep pictures of paint colors, appliance labels and receipts in one place.

Pro tip: If you regularly photograph product labels or serial numbers, keep them together in an album called "Home information." You'll appreciate having those pictures handy when something breaks.

How to organize photos on Google Pixel

Google Pixel phones take a different approach. Instead of relying on manually assigned keywords, Google Photos uses automatic recognition to help you find pictures based on what's in them. For example, you can search for "dog," "beach" or "receipt" and Google Photos may find relevant pictures without requiring you to tag them first. You can also create albums to organize pictures you want to keep together.

How to find photos using Google Photos

Google Photos can recognize many objects, places and other details in your pictures. Here's how to use its built-in search feature.

- Open the Google Photos app.

- Tap Search at the bottom of the screen.

- Enter a word describing what you're looking for , such as receipt , dog or beach .

- Browse the matching pictures.

Google Photos can often identify objects and locations without requiring you to manually label every picture. However, results depend on what the app can recognize, so you may need to try a different search term if your first attempt doesn't find the right photo.

How to create albums on Google Pixel

If you prefer organizing pictures yourself, albums offer a straightforward way to group related photos.

- Open Google Photos.

- Touch and hold a photo, then select any additional pictures you want to organize.

- Tap Add to album.

- Create a new album.

- Enter a descriptive album name.

- Tap Done.

To find your albums later, open Google Photos > Collections > Albums.

You can create albums for vacations, family events or important household information. That way, pictures you frequently need are grouped together instead of scattered throughout your photo library.

Find receipts and documents in Google Photos

Google Photos also offers a useful feature for organizing certain documents automatically. On supported versions, backed-up pictures of receipts and other paperwork may appear in a dedicated Documents collection.

To check, open Google Photos > Collections > Documents. If Google Photos recognizes documents in your backed-up pictures, you may find them organized into categories.

This can be particularly helpful when you're trying to locate a receipt or another document you photographed months ago.

Pro tip: Try searching Google Photos before creating dozens of albums. You may discover that it already recognizes much of what you're looking for.

Smart ways to organize photos without wasting hours

You don't need a complicated system to keep your pictures under control. In fact, a few consistent habits can make a bigger difference than spending hours organizing every image you've ever taken.

Start with photos you know you'll need again. Travel confirmations and warranty information are good candidates. Then choose simple names that you'll remember when searching.

Avoid overly specific labels that might be difficult to recall months later. For example, "kitchen repair" will probably be easier to remember than the exact date you photographed a broken dishwasher.

Also, remember that photo organization doesn't replace a backup. Important pictures should be protected through a trusted cloud backup service or another reliable backup method. And if you're photographing sensitive information, think carefully about whether you want those pictures stored in a cloud account.

I've lost count of how many times I've taken a photo specifically so I wouldn't forget something, only to spend several minutes trying to find it later. A simple keyword or well-named album can solve a surprisingly annoying problem. What I like about Apple's approach is the ability to assign a word that means something to you. Your phone might recognize a picture of a car, but it doesn't necessarily know that you took it because you needed to remember where you parked. Samsung Galaxy users can take advantage of manual tags, too, while Google Pixel owners have powerful automatic search tools built into Google Photos. My advice is to start small. Organize the pictures you'll actually need again, and let your phone's built-in search handle the rest.
