Trump orders federal contractors to alert White House on congressional record requests

2026-11-21 13:52

President Donald Trump signed an executive order late Friday instructing federal contractors to notify the White House of congressional information requests and withhold material the administration deems privileged, a move that comes as Democrats prepare to launch oversight investigations.

The order applies to all federal contractors and subcontractors. Companies that fail to comply risk losing their existing contracts or being suspended from future work, according to the order.

Trump framed the directive as a pushback against lawmakers whom the order accuses of "harassing private companies" to get confidential executive branch information.

The order could invite legal challenges, particularly if contractors are told to withhold records sought by subpoena. Although executive privilege lets presidents protect certain internal communications, courts can intervene in clashes over congressional oversight powers.

The order builds on a directive issued earlier this week by White House Counsel Will Scharf, who instructed federal agencies to clear employee congressional testimony with the administration first. Scharf added that some Democratic investigations could cross into criminal conduct, drawing pushback over what critics call a broader effort to shield the executive branch from oversight.

Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections and plan to investigate the administration as well as the Trump family's business dealings and ties to private firms.

Other investigative priorities include how the administration conducted immigration enforcement and its handling of the Jeffrey Epstein probe following the release of the government's case files.

Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif., who will likely lead the House Oversight Committee next year, said the probes "will lay the groundwork for legislation which helps all Americans."

This week, Garcia sent document preservation letters to 12 current and former administration officials, including to Vice President JD Vance, Donald Trump Jr., Eric Trump, Jared Kushner, former U.S. Ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle and White House chief of staff Stephen Miller.

"Oversight Democrats have been investigating the potential corruption, self-dealing, and abuses of power that have funneled billions of dollars to the Trump family as Americans struggle with rising costs," letters to the president's two eldest sons read.

"While the Trump Administration’s failed policies have made life increasingly unaffordable for the public, you have engaged in business pursuits that appear to have allowed you to personally profit from your father’s presidency on a massive scale and at the expense of the American people."

The letter to Miller read in part, "Oversight Democrats have heard directly from the victims of the lawless conduct of President Trump’s mass deportation campaign and from whistleblowers appalled by agency behavior. There must be accountability for those responsible for orchestrating, carrying out, and covering up these abuses, at all levels of the agency."
