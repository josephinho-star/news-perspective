Jets left with uncertainty at QB after Smith hurts ankle, rookie Klubnik debuts

2026-11-16 00:24

EAST RUTHERFORD, N.J. -- The New York Jets, mired in a historic offensive funk, have a new concern on their long list: an uncertain quarterback situation.

Geno Smith injured his left ankle in the third quarter of the Jets' 31-12 loss to the Buffalo Bills and left MetLife Stadium on a golf cart Sunday evening after a brief stop in the X-ray room. There were no crutches and no visible walking boot.

Coach Aaron Glenn didn't provide an update after the game.

The Jets turned to rookie Cade Klubnik, who made his NFL debut after riding the bench for nine weeks. The former Clemson, facing a 17-12 deficit when he entered the game, threw an interception and was sacked three times as the spiraling Jets (2-8) lost their fifth straight.

They haven't scored a touchdown in nine quarters, tied for the second-longest drought in the NFL this season and their longest since 2023.

"Bad ball. Bad football. Not a recipe to win," wide receiver Garrett Wilson said.

Things were so bad for the Jets that they became only the second team this century to lose back-to-back games by at least 17 points despite having four scoring drives in each game. It's like there's an invisible wall at the goal line; they haven't reached the end zone despite 64 plays in opponents' territory over the last two games.

"Definitely frustrated," said Glenn, who dropped to 5-22 as the head coach, including 0-9 in the AFC East.

Glenn was calm after the game, almost too calm.

"It doesn't do me any good to sit here and scream and yell at guys [the media]," he added. "That's not going to win us games."

Ordinarily, the Jets would've heard loud boos from their home fans, but it was a pro-Bills crowd. Wilson said he was "confused." When he heard cheers, he thought something positive had occurred.

"It was like a road game," he said in a moment of gallows humor. "I like road games."

Now the Jets face the prospect of playing without Smith, who had been playing reasonably well until the last two weeks. The Jets play the Los Angeles Chargers (0-8), whose 12-quarter touchdown drought -- the league's longest this season -- was snapped last week.

Smith (13-for-21, 148 yards) was injured with 4:26 left in the third quarter, when safety Cole Bishop came free on a third-down blitz. Smith unloaded an incomplete pass and fell backward, his left ankle twisting underneath him.

Earlier, Smith missed the first series of the third quarter as he underwent a concussion evaluation during halftime. With 25 seconds left in the first half, he was sandwiched by linebackers Terrel Bernard and TJ Parker, with his helmet hitting the turf. The Jets argued in vain for a roughing-the-passer penalty.

"He was kind of scrambling around and stepped up," Bernard said. "I tried to get my pad level low to stay off of that strike zone. I tried to go shoulder to him. I hope he's all right. He came back in the game. But yeah. I hope he's all right."

He was -- until the ankle injury.

In came Klubnik, a fourth-round pick who hadn't played since the preseason. Klubnik, who led a field-goal drive during Smith's concussion evaluation, didn't fare nearly as well in his return stint. All told, he completed only 11 of 20 passes for 137 yards, throwing an interception on a play in which he was drilled by a blitzer.

"This was as tough as it gets for any young quarterback, going against this front that really does a good job of rushing the passer," Glenn said. "But just from the naked eye, I thought he did a pretty good job."

Klubnik was the No. 2 quarterback in every game this season but never saw action until Sunday. His teammates and coaches love his moxie.

"I've got a lot of belief in myself," he said. "There's been a lot of times throughout my life that nobody else believed in me and I've had to believe in myself. I've always had that mindset."

Klubnik wanted a different outcome but said it was "a lot of fun" to get in a game. He received a souvenir from the day: Josh Allen's jersey.

"I've looked up to him for a really long time," Klubnik said. "I appreciate him and love watching him play. Wish I could've gotten the win, though."
