Cowboys great Michael Irvin outlines franchise's timeline to retain 'America’s Team' status

2026-11-20 00:34

More than three decades have passed since the Dallas Cowboys last advanced to a Super Bowl. In the years since, the franchise has experienced limited postseason success and has not returned to the NFC Championship Game.

Even so, the Cowboys remain one of the NFL’s most popular teams, frequently appearing in nationally televised windows and routinely drawing prime time interest. Their history, including five Super Bowl titles, which rank second among NFL teams, has helped the franchise retain its widely recognized "America’s Team" moniker.

However, Cowboys Ring of Honor member Michael Irvin says the popular nickname should be retired unless Dallas starts winning.

Irvin was a key part of the Cowboys’ three Super Bowl-winning teams in the 1990s, a run that built on the "America's Team" status. The retired wide receiver is giving his former team a decade-long runway to prove it should keep the label.

"A whole generation goes by and never experiences the glory of a championship story from the Dallas Cowboys," Irvin told CNBC on Thursday. "A generation is considered 40 years in the bible. So the Cowboys got about 10 years [to win a Super Bowl and keep the 'America’s Team' title]."

Irvin wore the famed "star" on the side of his helmet for his entire NFL career and was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2007. He finished his career with more than 11,000 regular season receiving yards.

The Cowboys have missed the postseason in each of the past two seasons and could face another early offseason this year. Dallas is competing in an uneven NFC East, with the Philadelphia Eagles dealing with notable injuries and the Washington Commanders struggling.

After the Cowboys' defense faltered again in a 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers last Sunday, defensive coordinator Christian Parker was relieved of his play-calling duties. The Cowboys enter Sunday’s game against the 1-8 Tennessee Titans at 4-6, needing a win to keep their playoff hopes alive.

Parker, 34, spent the previous two seasons as the Eagles passing game coordinator & defensive backs coach.

In the the 10 games he's handled defensive playcalling for Dallas, the team ranks 32 in the NFL in total yards allowed and rushing yards given up. The 49ers put up 584 yards against Dallas in Week 10, the most the Cowboys’ defense has allowed in a home game in franchise history.
