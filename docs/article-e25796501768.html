Nine Syrian sailors missing after drone strike sinks Turkish-operated ship

2026-10-08 09:44

Nine Syrian sailors remain missing after a drone strike sank a commercial ship managed by a Turkey-based company off Bulgaria’s Black Sea coast, a Syrian official told Agence France-Presse on Thursday, after rescuers ended their search.

The Togolese-flagged Alfa Watan was one of two commercial vessels hit by drones early Tuesday. Both were managed by companies based in Turkey, according to the Equasis merchant shipping database.

The other vessel caught fire, and 18 Turkish and Indian crew members were rescued. Bulgarian officials said no survivors had been found from the Alfa Watan.

Mazen Alloush, an official at Syria’s customs authority, told AFP that “the ship’s crew was composed of 10 sailors, nine of them Syrians and one Egyptian,” adding that “their fate is still unknown.”

The specialist shipping website Maritime.bg also reported that 10 people were aboard the sunken vessel.

Alloush said Syria had sent urgent messages to the International Maritime Organization and Bulgaria’s maritime administration “calling for an independent and transparent investigation” into the incident.

Syria also demanded “the reasons that led to the suspension of search and rescue operations,” he said.

The strikes are the latest maritime incidents in the Black Sea linked to the war between Russia and Ukraine.

Rumen Nikolov, head of Bulgaria’s Maritime Search and Rescue Coordination Centre, said this week that the search had ended late Tuesday and would not resume.

“What was found from the sunken ship was one overturned boat, three life rafts and debris from the ship’s equipment that was floating on the surface of the sea,” he said.

The Syrian sailors’ union said contact with the crew had been lost and no survivors had been found before search operations were halted.

Relatives have shared photographs and death notices on social media, although the crew’s fate remains unconfirmed.

Walaa Melhem mourned her brother Tarek, whose obituary described him as a “martyr of the sea.” Yasmine Merei shared photographs of her nephew Raed, a 26-year-old cook aboard the Alfa Watan.
