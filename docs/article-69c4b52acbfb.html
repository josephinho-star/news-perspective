Multiple protesters interrupt Trump as he speaks at Paxton rally in Texas – US politics live

Trump rally speech in San Antonio interrupted by multiple protesters as he repeats lie about Talarico

Donald Trump’s midterm rally speech in San Antonio, Texas has been disrupted by protesters at least six times so far.

Television coverage of the rally showed that one of the protesters was violently assaulted by an older Trump supporter as he attempted to leave the arena.

“You know, many of these protesters are paid,” Trump said, repeating a baseless claim he has been making for a decade. “These are paid protesters. They’re paid by enemies of our nation. Now, I’m telling you, they pay them for this.”

The interruptions seemed to momentarily derail the president as he repeated a lie about James Talarico, the Texas Democrat he claims is a vegan who only pretends to eat meat and secretly vomits every time he does. There is no evidence for this imaginary tale the president has repeated multiple times in recent weeks as Talarico has pulled ahead of the Trump-backed Republican candidate for the US Senate, Ken Paxton.

Trump began his riff with the rare admission that he has been mispronouncing the word vegan, as “vaygan”.

“He is a fake,” Trump said of the Democrat. “He’s a vegan. You know, my wife told me, ‘Sir, do me a favor. You have to do me just a little bit of a favor. It’s not vaygan; it’s vegan’”.

As the president told this story about being asked to pronounce the word correctly, he appeared to get confused about who, exactly, had corrected him. Trump does not usually refer to his wife calling him “Sir”. Moments later he said that the person who made this request of him was a man.

“I don’t know about that stuff. I’m not into it. And he said ‘It’s vegan! Please pronounce that word properly. I called it, vaygan, vaygan. I thought it- how the hell would I know? I like steak.”

Trump repeats false claims of booming US economy and invented investment figure, ignoring inflation and spiking fuel prices

In his speech to supporters in San Antonio, Texas, which is still in progress, Donald Trump has been repeating the same false claims about the US economy he has made again and again in recent weeks.

Ignoring soaring inflation and fuel prices exacerbated by the war he started with Iran and has been unable to stop, Trump repeated his entirely imaginary claim that he has already secured $21tn in foreign direct investment in the US since returning to office lat year.

In fact, as CNN reported last month, federal data shows that new foreign direct investment in the US was about $232bn in 2025, and Trump appears to be “counting trillions in vague investment pledges — pledges that were about ‘bilateral trade’ or ‘economic exchange’ rather than investment in the US — and vague statements that didn’t even rise to the level of pledges.”

Trump also repeated his false claim that tariff revenues have swelled federal coffers so much under his administration that he was able to pay a $1,776 bonus to military service members and would also cover the $5,000 checks to all US adults he is promising voters if they keep Republicans in the majority in the House and Senate.

The $1,776 payments to troops that Trump first announced in December, which he said would be paid for by tariff revenues, were not funded by the import taxes Trump imposed. As the Associated Press reported at the time, the money was actually drawn from a congressionally-approved housing supplement — money service members were already set to receive.

Federal lawsuit from Democrats says eight more pro-Trump ads are ready to air, including attacks on Biden and Obama

The federal lawsuit Democrats filed on Wednesday to stop the Trump administration from using millions of taxpayer dollars to air a series of pro-Trump ads includes details of another eight ads that have already been prepared for broadcast – all of which celebrate Donald Trump, and appear to violate a federal ban on the use of public money for “self-aggrandizement” or “purely partisan” messaging from the government.

The Democratic National Committee says in the suit that “there are at least 13 campaign-style political ads in a Dropbox account controlled by LMD Agency Inc, the government contractor bringing the ads to air”. Each ad in the Dropbox account includes on-screen text saying that the ad celebrating Trump in the run-up to midterm elections his party is in danger of losing was “Paid for by the U.S. Government”.

In addition to the five pro-Trump already broadcast since last month, the suit describes another four in detail:

An ad titled “UFC 250”, which celebrates the fights staged at the White House on Trump’s birthday in June.

An ad titled “King of the Jungle”, which shows a lion hunting in the wild and disparages Trump’s political enemies, including former president Barack Obama and other Democrats. In February, Trump shared a racist video on social media that portrayed him as a lion and Obama and his wife, Michelle, as apes.

An ad titled “Resilience”, which includes images of Trump announcing his candidacy for the presidency in 2015; his mugshot from Fulton county, Georgia, in 2023, when he was charged with racketeering and a conspiracy to overturn the results of the 2020 election in the state; video of the assassination attempt on him during the 2024 election campaign.

An ad titled “God Made Trump”, a fan-created hymn of praise to Trump that was shown during the Republican midterm convention.

According to CNN, which gained access to the Dropbox last week, another ad in the series features attacks on Joe Biden, including the mocking image of the former president as an autopen installed by Trump on an exterior wall of the White House.

Democrats sue Trump administration, asking judge to declare Trump ads paid for with taxpayer dollars illegal propaganda

The Democratic National Committee sued the Trump administration in federal court on Wednesday, asking a judge in Washington to stop the president from using taxpayer funds to air a series of ads promoting himself, arguing that the ads are illegal government-sponsored propaganda.

The ads promoting Donald Trump, including one recycled from his 2024 presidential campaign, have cost taxpayers more than $12m so far, according to the media tracking firm AdImpact.

After a bipartisan backlash to the use of public money to promote the president during the final month of the midterm campaign season, the president claimed on Monday that he would direct the Maga Inc Super PAC he controls to pay for the ads instead. The next day, however, when the government unveiled a fifth ad in the series, celebrating the military intervention in Venezuela ordered by Trump, that new ad was also paid for by the public.

The DNC complaint, filed in the US district court for the District of Columbia, alleges that the ads violate federal law, including a ban on congressionally appropriated money being used for “publicity or propaganda”.

The complaint gets right to the point in its opening lines:

The Trump administration is using taxpayer dollars to air political advertisements, a blatant violation of the statutory ban on the use of appropriations for ‘publicity or propaganda purposes’. President Trump personally directed this illegal activity. The White House office crafted the partisan campaign. The office of management and budget shifted $20m to pay for it. And US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) has carried it out. These unlawful actions are ultra vires and violate the Administrative Procedure Act (APA).

The suit also notes, as the Guardian reported last month, that the first ad in the series features an image of Fifa’s president, Gianni Infantino, awarding Trump the so-called ‘Fifa peace prize,’ to support its case that the ads all violate a prohibition on the use of taxpayer funds for “self-aggrandizement”.

The text of the complaint also references new polling from Reuters/Ipsos released on Tuesday which found that an overwhelming majority of Americans, 86%, called it inappropriate to use taxpayer funds for TV ads featuring the president around an election. The survey reported that 80% of Republicans called the ads inappropriate, with only 17% of the president’s party approving of them.

According to the suit, the ads have been broadcast on ABC, NBC, MS Now, CBS, CNN, Fox News and Newsmax and appeared during a variety of programs, including popular NFL and college football games, and news and entertainment shows on which the president has been criticized, including Meet the Press, Jimmy Kimmel Live! and Saturday Night Live.

Ken Martin, the DNC chair, said in a statement that Trump is misusing taxpayer dollars in “a last-ditch attempt to save Republicans in November”.

“Americans deserve better than to have their hard-earned tax dollars used for Trump’s illegal schemes,” he added.

This will mark the first time in more than 20 years that the group, the world’s largest regional security organization, would not be present to monitor the elections in the US.

“The Trump Administration’s failure to continue this practice is yet another step that risks undermining public trust in America’s safe and secure election system,” Shaheen said.

A reminder that monitors do not interfere with the voting process itself, but observe it.

“I was an official election monitor in the country of Georgia in 2012 and most recently in Bosnia and Herzegovina in 2022, so I know first-hand the importance of showing up for allies and partners to improve the public’s confidence in election integrity,” said Shaheen, an outgoing senator from New Hampshire who is also the top Democrat on the foreign relations committe.

Hegseth welcomes USS Abraham Lincoln crew back to US after record-breaking deployment

Pete Hegseth, the defense secretary, welcomed back the crew of the USS Abraham Lincoln back to the US in San Diego today.

A reminder, the aircraft carrier has been at sea for more than 300 days, after its deployment was extended in May, amid the ongoing Iran war.

“You responded from minute one, constant combat operations, which Iran never expected-the kind of fire and fury that you were able to bring off this flight deck, along with other incredible Americans,” Hegseth said. “The only reaction the American people and the American media should have is pride.”

However, time on the ship has been marked by controversy. According to Hung Cao, the acting US navy secretary, eight US navy personnel assigned to the Lincoln carrier strike group attempted suicide since the start of their lengthy assignment – the longest continuous period at sea without a port call.

On Wednesday, Hegseth repeated his frequent critique of the media, and accused some members of the press of turning the record-breaking deployment into “some sort of symbol of dysfunction, or a symbol of low morale or a symbol of a failed mission”.

Last week, Hegseth appeared to reference the mental health crisis on board while speaking to lower-ranking troops at the Marine Corps Base, insisting there are “disingenuous headlines about deployment lengths, patently false claims of low troop morale, or flat-out propaganda, as if it’s written by our adversaries”.

MS Now also reported in August on alarming allegations of mold in the ship’s showers and dwindling supplies of food and soap, among other basic necessities. At the time, Hegseth dismissed reports about deteriorating conditions on board as “completely misrepresented”.
