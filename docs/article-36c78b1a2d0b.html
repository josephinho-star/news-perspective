Iran regime's execution spree continues as twins face death penalty, decades in prison after alleged torture

2026-11-19 19:14

Two Iranian twin sisters imprisoned for participating in anti-government protests came face-to-face with the very official they accused of torturing and sexually assaulting them when they appeared in court to seek justice.

They discovered he was the judge assigned to hear their complaint, their U.S.-based cousin told Fox News Digital in an exclusive interview.

Masoud Nourmohammadi, the cousin of Taraneh and Romina Rahimi, alleged that the official had been involved in their interrogation and subsequently presided over the complaint they filed against the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).

"As soon as they walk in, they see the exact person who tortured them and sexually assaulted them is the judge," Nourmohammadi said.

He claimed the complaint was immediately dismissed by that same official.

"The judge was their prosecutor during the building (of) the case, and he was the one that did the sexual assault on the kids and tortured them as a prosecutor and for the lawsuit he was assigned as a judge for that lawsuit," Nourmohammadi said.

Fox News Digital has not independently verified the allegations or reviewed the court documents.

The twins, now 21, were arrested after January anti-government demonstrations in Isfahan. According to the Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA), Taraneh was sentenced to death and an additional 11 years in prison, while Romina received a 36-year prison sentence. The sentences were issued in the so-called Shohada Square case involving 16 defendants.

Nourmohammadi said the sisters' legal situation has become increasingly precarious. One of their attorneys has been arrested, another withdrew from the case and the family has since secured a new lawyer to pursue the appeal of the criminal verdict.

He also described alarming new reports about their health.

"We just heard from another inmate who was with them in the same prison that the girls have been throwing up, vomiting blood in the night," Nourmohammadi said.

He claimed Taraneh had previously been unable to walk after allegedly being tortured and that both sisters were being denied necessary medical treatment.

Their case comes amid growing international concern over Iran's use of the death penalty against people arrested during nationwide anti-government demonstrations.

A Nov. 16 report by Norway-based Iran Human Rights (IHRNGO) documented at least 121 protest-related death sentence cases after January's demonstrations, including six women and at least five people who were minors at the time of their alleged offenses. The organization cautioned that some sentences had subsequently been overturned or referred for retrial.

According to IHRNGO, 41 protesters have been executed since March 18, including 39 connected to January's protests and two linked to the earlier Woman, Life, Freedom movement.

"Despite the execution of more than one protester every week on average since March, the international response has failed to raise the political cost of these executions sufficiently to deter the Islamic Republic," IHRNGO Director Mahmood Amiry-Moghaddam said.

The warning coincided with the U.N. General Assembly's Third Committee adopting a resolution Wednesday condemning Tehran's human rights record, with 76 countries voting in favor, 27 against and 64 abstaining.

Explaining the U.S. vote, Ambassador Dan Negrea said Washington believed the text did not go far enough in holding the Iranian government accountable and contained language that distracted from its central purpose.

"Let no one mistake our abstention for any softening toward Tehran," Negrea said. "For 47 years, the regime has ruled through torture and terror. It crushes dissent with public executions and beatings. It remains the world’s leading state sponsor of terrorism, and its repression does not stop at its own borders. It pursues those who dare to speak up against it and hunts them down around the world.

"Today, it unjustly executes more people than any other country. The United Nations has documented these human rights violations and abuses at length, and responsibility for them belongs to the regime alone."

Iran's U.N. mission rejected the resolution, accusing its sponsors of politicizing human rights and ignoring the consequences of sanctions and military conflict.

Nourmohammadi, who lives in Utah, said he has contacted congressional representatives seeking help but has not received a response.

He said Tehran uses executions to intimidate Iranians considering further demonstrations.

"That's one of the reasons that the Islamic Republic is executing people is just to create that fear," he said.

The cousin said the family remains focused on fighting the sisters' sentences and securing medical treatment for them.
