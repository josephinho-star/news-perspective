Australian politics live: Hastie ‘lived a lot’ of SAS report findings; Paterson accuses PM of ‘no standards’ over golf club

2026-10-29 03:31

PM questioned again over Marrickville golf club grant

In case you were wondering, the MP for Cook, Simon Kennedy, asked about the Marrickville golf club grant.

Yesterday, the prime minister claimed the sewerage works were originally part of the $6 million invite-only taxpayer funded grant to Marrickville golf club.

Can the prime minister explain, with a straight face, why there’s no mention of the sewerage works in the prime minister’s own announcement on March 2025?

The prime minister answers: “on the 27th of April, 2025, we made an election commitment, building and securing the Marrickville Golf Club precinct that went to a range of activities … and ensure this inner west institution can be enjoyed for decades to come.”

In one fell swoop he has punted the shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, the member for Herbert, Phillip Thompson, and the former Nationals leader David Littleproud.

Tribute to Queensland police officers who lost their lives at Wieambilla

The prime minister and the home affairs minister, Tony Burke, have paid tribute to a number of police officers, including Matthew Arnold and Rachel McCrow, who died while conducting a check at the property in Wieambilla.

The opposition leader, Angus Taylor, agrees:

I’d like to associate the Coalition with the fine words of the prime minister and the minister of home affairs, and pay tribute alongside them to those brave police officers who put themselves in harm’s way, to all our police officers, preventing, detecting, responding to or investigating crime.

Asked by shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, whether Jim Chalmers will “take responsibility and admit his out of control spending is forcing Australian families to pay more for everything”, the treasurer hits back.

“Their argument about spending and inflation, like the Liberal party itself, is falling down all around them.”

As he continues his answer, listing government promises, he mentions “Medicare”, and the opposition begins jeering. Tim Wilson is seen laughing and pointing while those around him make faces.

MP for Forrest, Ben Small, has been pulled up for interjecting throughout treasurer Jim Chalmers’ answer to a dixer on the impact of tax reform during times of economic uncertainty.

Chris Bowen ‘triggers’ the Coalition after question from Monique Ryan

Independent for Kooyong, Monique Ryan, asks: “Will the government legislate to ensure that new datacentres are not permitted to use expensive, polluting backup diesel generators?”

Energy minister, Chris Bowen, says the government is ensuring datacentres bring their own new energy, and “that energy should be the cheapest form of energy”.

While giving his answer, he says there will be a focus on renewable energy, attracting a barrage of discontent from across the aisle.

EU free-trade deal ‘in Australia’s national interest’, Albanese says

First dixer of the day, from the MP for Bullwinkle, asks how the proposed free trade agreement with the EU benefits Australia’s economic and security interests.

Through a rabble of Coalition complaints, the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, points to improved diversification, quotes support from the Business Council and other bodies, and finishes with:

This is in Australia’s national interest. It should be agreed, and everyone in this parliament should back it.

Bowen proposes ‘last resort’ penalties on datacentres flouting power usage rules

Energy minister, Chris Bowen, said new rules to potentially cut off the power supply to AI datacentres would only be a “last resort” if operators consistently flouted rules.

As part of the Labor government’s new rules on power-hungry datacentres, Bowen has floated that the Australian Energy Market Commission be able to turn off supply if certain facilities disregarded national rules requiring the use of renewable energy.

Power supply to datacentres has been a key policy debate, with the federal government initially insisting on the AI factories being powered entirely by renewables, but later relenting with dispensations for gas-rich Queensland and Northern Territory.

Bowen’s office has asked the AEMC to assess a number of options, including financial penalties, but there are concerns that fines could not be enough to force such change. The government is still keen to hear feedback from developers, industry and the community, and remains open to what penalties could apply.

‘Makes no sense’ for Australia to miss out on trade opportunities, Steggall says

Crossbencher Zali Steggall has backed Australia’s free trade agreement with the EU, saying it creates opportunities for Australian businesses, strengthens ties and diversifies markets.

Climate denialism must not stand in the way of Australia’s trade future.

Europe is a $30 trillion market and it makes absolutely no sense for Australia to miss out on the opportunities a free trade deal would bring … Claims that the free trade deal would take away Australia’s right to set its own climate policies are simply wrong.

Steggall said the agreement requires parties to uphold their commitments under the Paris agreement, which itself does not dictate Australia’s emissions targets. The targets are determined through Australia’s laws and processes.

But Canavan claims it is “no good in its current form”, telling reporters at a press conference:

From day one, we said this is a bad deal … The Coalition wants to see a successful EU [free trade agreement] concluded, but it has to be in the national interest.

We cannot have a situation where our farmers, our producers, our business people end up being penalised because Australia misses the target set by the Albanese government with respect to climate.
