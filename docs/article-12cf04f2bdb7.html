Australian politics live: Hastie dubs inflation a ‘silent tax eating away at wealth’; Greens plan to make the Yarra River swimmable

2026-10-28 21:44

PM insists government is addressing the cost of living

Following yesterday’s latest inflation figures, the prime minister has maintained his government is bringing down the cost of living.

Anthony Albanese points to his government’s budget surpluses and other measures that “ensure that any cost-of-living measures are ones that not just provide help for people today, but build a stronger tomorrow”.

Opposition to the EU free trade deal is “an excuse for going down the road of protectionist and nativist politics”, Anthony Albanese says.

Nativist politics is not in Australia’s national interest. We are a trading nation. We are an island continent in which economic growth and productivity is boosted by engaging with the world.

PM says Coalition's fair trade credentials will be 'shredded' if EU deal support withheld

The Coalition’s support for free and fair trade will be undermined unless it supports the government’s deal with the EU, the prime minister has said.

For people who say they belong to a political party that supports markets and supports free and fair trade – that will be completely undermined and shredded unless they support this legislation.

PM spruiks social media reforms in press conference

The prime minister is urging the Senate to pass his government’s latest social media reforms, telling reporters it is about returning control to Australian people:

Rather than international base companies, determining through algorithms what Australians receive, it empowers people themselves to determine what you receive on your devices, so that you should have that control.

This isn’t about government having a greater role. It’s about people having a greater role, having more control themselves, and it’s deserving of support of this parliament.

Australia must navigate global factors in battle against inflation, assistant treasurer says

Higher-than-expected inflation figures and an increasingly likely rate rise are continuing to dominate the news cycle.

Asked why the government is unable to bring inflation down, the assistant treasurer, Daniel Mulino, pointed to the impact of international affairs on Australia’s economy.

He told ABC Radio National:

What we saw in the figures released earlier this week is that the conflict in Iran is lasting longer and is more severe than had been forecast, and that means that global energy prices are higher than had been expected a few months ago.

We’re seeing higher inflation than expected right across the advanced economy world.

Pushed on why other nations were having more success in bringing inflation down, Mulino said other countries “have not been anywhere near as successful as Australia in terms of their labour market outcomes”.

US increasingly seen as a ‘threat’ by Australians, survey shows

Australia’s immediate neighbours are trusted more than historic allies, according to a new survey AAP reports on, which shows the United States is increasingly considered a “threat”.

Only half of the Australians polled in a YouGov survey, commissioned by the Australia Institute, considered longtime ally the US a “friend”. Meanwhile 42% perceived America as a “threat” (defined as a nation viewed with caution or suspicion).

Only 4% of young voters labelled the US a “close friend” while over half of young Australians considered it a threat.

Two-thirds of One Nation voters perceive the US as a friend, with the Coalition close behind. Labor voters are largely split and only one-quarter of Greens voters view the global superpower positively.

Only 21% of respondents had confidence in DonaldTrump to “do the right thing” in world affairs, ahead of only the Chinese president, XiJinping, the Russian president, VladimirPutin,and the North Korean leader, Kim Jong-un.

Indonesia, a close geographic neighbour and emerging partner, has been rated a more trusted partner than the United States, leading by 14% in the friend category.

Social media a ‘business model for hate’: Chalmers

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, says overheated discourse on social media is in large part responsible for the toxicity in politics, including the threats and abuse being sprayed at politicians.

Speaking at the Political Book of the Year awards in Canberra last night, Chalmers reflected on the alleged attack against Indi MP HelenHaines last week. He said hate had always been present in Australian society but it now has “a business model, a formula, an assembly line for mass-production.”

He described a new kind of digital misogyny that follows women in public life wherever they go, making the current climate harshest for women.

That climate is no accident. Hate is monetised and weaponised by algorithmic rage-bait. Extremism is incentivised; nuance is penalised. The most available public figures are the most vulnerable.

So tonight is a little reminder of why reason and research matters, why nuance matters, what’s at risk and what’s at stake and why that matters too.

Books inform our views without hardening them. Social media hardens our views without informing them.

Haines is recovering in hospital. Police have charged a 43-year-old man over the alleged attack outside her Wangaratta electorate office.
