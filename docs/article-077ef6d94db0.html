'One Job Away' campaign is helping SAG-AFTRA members meet work thresholds to keep health insurance

2026-11-26 20:00

A grassroots social media campaign led by actor and singer Cheyenne Jackson has helped fellow SAG-AFTRA members struggling during declining film and TV production earn enough to keep their union health insurance.

Jackson, known for his roles in "American Horror Story," "30 Rock" and "Glee," launched the "One Job Away" movement on Instagram last month to spotlight fellow performers struggling to earn enough to maintain medical coverage through the Screen Actors Guild-American Federation of Television and Radio Artists.

SAG-AFTRA requires members to earn at least $28,000 annually to qualify for health insurance, with four quarterly deadlines for meeting that threshold, depending on when they joined the union.

For actors working job to job, meeting that requirement has become increasingly difficult as U.S. film and TV production contracts and healthcare costs rise.

"So the success of the project so far at last count is that eight actors have been hired directly and at least 12 to 15 have been pinned or called back or gotten really close," Jackson told Fox News Digital. He described an overwhelming response, including hundreds of direct messages from actors hoping to be featured, many of whom are just a few hundred dollars short of qualifying.

Among them is William Ngo, who was born and raised in Southern California but relocated to Philadelphia after work dried up following the pandemic and the 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike. Ngo, whose credits include The CW’s "The Vampire Diaries," HBO Max’s "The Staircase" and Apple’s "Bad Monkey," is just $500 short of qualifying for SAG-AFTRA health insurance.

Actress and stunt performer Marie Gibeault, whose credits include Netflix’s "The Four Seasons" and multiple commercials, is only $800 short of qualifying. Navy veteran, actor and voice-over artist Jonathan Medina needs one guest role to qualify by the end of the year.

Actor Alejandro Cardenas reached out to thank Jackson after the campaign helped him book a small role that put him over the earnings threshold.

"After Cheyenne posted my picture and info, an executive from Lionsgate and friend of mine immediately called me and set me up into this job so I could make the money needed to reach the minimum to qualify for my health insurance again," Cardenas told Fox News Digital from the set he was working on in New Jersey.

The actors’ profiles share a common thread: dwindling opportunities in Hollywood during fewer scripted television orders, increased cost-cutting and the continued shift of production elsewhere.

Jackson previously described the industry’s struggles as "systemic" during an interview with The Hollywood Reporter.

"People that have been leads of television shows have worked for years and years and years, and you know their faces, and you know their work. This year, they’re like, ‘What am I going to do?’" he said.

Meanwhile, beginning Jan. 1, quarterly premiums and earnings thresholds are expected to rise by 1%, with additional annual increases of 1% to follow, according to terms negotiated in SAG-AFTRA’s latest contract with streamers and studios.

A petition calling for an audit of the union’s health plan has also gained traction among members.

SAG-AFTRA, which represents approximately 170,000 media professionals worldwide, has faced criticism over the nearly $1.1 million annual salary of its national executive director and chief negotiator, Duncan Crabtree-Ireland, as members struggle financially.

Fox News Digital reached out to SAG-AFTRA’s chief communications and marketing officer, Pamela Greenwalt, for comment. In response, a union representative provided a healthcare fact sheet and a membership termination sheet.

Jackson, who also performs onstage, has expressed a desire to discuss how the system works, noting that actors can have a strong earnings year followed by a lean one that leaves them ineligible for health benefits.

He told Fox News Digital that launching "One Job Away" was a way to pass along kindness he had received.

Last year, for the first time in his 20-year career, Jackson found himself $6,000 short of meeting the health insurance eligibility requirements. Actress Kathy Bates, who had worked with him on "American Horror Story," arranged for him to have four speaking lines on her CBS series "Matlock." The work was enough to qualify him and his family for coverage.

"I really did nothing," Jackson said of launching "One Job Away." He added that several peers, including actresses Jamie Lee Curtis and Sarah Paulson, have reached out to ask how they can help.

"Just this helping one person makes this worth it," Jackson added, expressing disbelief at the outpouring of love he has received for trying to help.
