Six Florida family members killed in plane crash on way home from fishing trip identified

2026-11-26 00:46

Six family members from Florida were killed Tuesday when the small plane they were aboard crashed off the Mississippi coast on their way home from a fishing trip, according to reports.

Those killed were identified by Jackson County Coroner Bruce Lynd Jr. as Brent Stokes, 50; Hunter Stokes, 16; Michael Knox, 43; Arlyn Knox, 43; Charles Holden Knox, 12; and Truett Knox, 10.

The family, from Fort Myers, had been on a fishing trip in Louisiana, Lynd said. Brent Stokes was piloting the Cessna 210 aircraft, which was descending at 10,000 feet per minute when it crashed, the Federal Aviation Administration said.

Brent Stokes was traveling with his son, Hunter Stokes, and his sister, Arlyn Knox, NOLA.com reported. Arlyn and Michael Knox were the parents of Charles Holden Knox and Truett Knox.

Fox News Digital has reached out to Lynd's office for comment.

The plane lost contact with air traffic control at about 3:45 p.m., according to authorities.

The downed aircraft was found 2 miles off Horn Island, Mississippi. Divers from the Mississippi Department of Marine Resources located the remains of all six people inside the fuselage after an hours-long search, the U.S. Coast Guard said.

"This is a heartbreaking outcome, and our deepest sympathies are with the families and loved ones who are facing an unimaginable loss," said Cmdr. Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard Sector Mobile search and rescue mission coordinator.

"While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency coordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families."

Paradise Outfitters, which offers sport fishing charters in Venice, Louisiana, said it had gotten to know the Stokes and Knox families over the years.

"Even after Brent built a boat like ours, he still wanted our captains out there with him, we continued to show him and his family the waters we all love. Those days together meant so much to our crew," the company said Wednesday in a Facebook post.

"Please pray for the Stokes and Knox families and all who love them as they face the days ahead. Please keep our captains and our Venice fishing community in your prayers, too, as we grieve the loss of our dear friends."

The National Transportation Safety Board is investigating the crash.
