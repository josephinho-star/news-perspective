FLASHBACK: Two presidents pulled off secret Thanksgiving surprises for US troops — see the moments

2026-11-27 02:00

Under a cloak of secrecy, two American presidents made surprise Thanksgiving visits to war zones, bringing a taste of home to U.S. troops serving thousands of miles from their families.

Sixteen years apart, President George W. Bush celebrated with American troops in Iraq in 2003, while President Donald Trump served Thanksgiving dinner to service members in Afghanistan in 2019. Both visits were carried out under tight secrecy, catching troops by surprise as they spent the holiday far from home.

Trump secretly traveled to Bagram Airfield in Afghanistan on Nov. 28, 2019, where he served turkey, posed for photos and thanked American troops for their sacrifices amid the ongoing war.

"There's nowhere I'd rather celebrate this Thanksgiving than right here with the toughest, strongest, best, and bravest warriors on the face of the Earth," Trump told the troops at the time, later adding, "And you don't even know how much the people of our country love and respect you."

The visit came as roughly 12,000 American troops remained in Afghanistan more than 18 years after the U.S. invasion.

During the visit, Trump met with Afghan President Ashraf Ghani to discuss efforts to end the war and told American troops that the Taliban wanted to reach a deal with the United States. He said his administration was reducing America's military presence in Afghanistan while pursuing a political solution to the more than 18-year conflict.

Less than two years later, President Joe Biden oversaw the chaotic U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan in August 2021.

The withdrawal unfolded as the Taliban rapidly seized control of Afghanistan, culminating in the fall of Kabul and a frantic evacuation at the city's airport.

Sixteen years earlier, Bush pulled off a similar Thanksgiving surprise in Iraq , secretly flying into Baghdad International Airport on Nov. 27, 2003, to celebrate with American troops just eight months after launching the U.S.-led invasion that toppled Saddam Hussein's regime.

Bush stunned hundreds of service members when he appeared at their Thanksgiving dinner, emerging to cheers and applause before helping serve a traditional holiday meal and posing for photos with troops stationed far from home.

"I can't think of a finer group of folks to have Thanksgiving dinner with than you all," Bush told the troops at the time.

"We're proud of you," he added. "Today, Americans are gathering with their loved ones to give thanks for the many blessings in our lives."

News of his trip was not even released until Air Force One had already left Iraq.

Bush's visit came as American forces battled a growing insurgency following the fall of Saddam Hussein's government earlier that year. Despite Bush's declaration in May that major combat operations had ended, U.S. troops continued to face deadly attacks as the military worked to stabilize the country.

Just weeks later, U.S. forces captured Saddam Hussein in December 2003.
