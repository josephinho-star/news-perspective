Man convicted of starting Palisades fire in Los Angeles

2026-11-21 04:34

A jury in Los Angeles has found a 30-year-old man guilty of starting one of the most destructive wildfires in California's history.

Jonathan Rinderknecht was convicted of arson after starting the fires that swept through Pacific Palisades and parts of Malibu in January last year, killing 12 people and destroying more than 6,800 buildings in the US state.

The conviction was handed down nearly five months after deadlocked jurors led to the judge declaring a mistrial.

Rinderknecht, who had pleaded not guilty, was convicted of two federal charges. He is due to be sentenced on 17 March when he faces between five and 20 years in prison on each charge.

Rinderknecht was found guilty of lighting a fire on 1 January last year which burned undetected deep in root systems before flaring back up six days later.

One of the charges was for starting the original brush fire, while the other centred on the damage caused when it reignited into a catastrophic blaze.

"We were determined to hold him accountable, and today's guilty verdict delivers a measure of justice for the families who lost loved ones and the thousands whose lives were upended," said Bill Essayli, who leads the US Attorney's Office for the Central District of California.

Rinderknecht is a Los Angeles resident and former ride-share delivery driver.

Defence attorney Steve Haney had argued at trial that prosecutors lacked sufficient physical evidence or records to prove their case.

Lawyers for the defendant said they were "disappointed" by the verdict but respected the jury's decision.

Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass, who was widely criticised for her handling of the fires, said in a statement that "no verdict can bring back the lives lost or erase the pain families have endured".

"So many are still struggling to rebuild," she said. "For those still displaced, the devastation remains a daily reality."

Prosecutors had argued that Rinderknecht was driven by revenge, anger and loneliness because he had nowhere to go for the holiday.

They cited geolocation data from his iPhone, security camera footage that showed his car's location and witness testimony from passengers.

They alleged that Rinderknecht had a "deeply entrenched belief that the wealthy were destroying the world" and in turn purposely started the blaze in the affluent neighbourhood.

The fire was one of several that ignited back-to-back across the LA region in January, which in total left 31 people dead and destroyed 16,000 structures.
