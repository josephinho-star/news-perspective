Burnham: 'Winter of great flooding' could be on its way

As south west England deals with the aftermath of Storm Austen, Andy Burnham said the United Kingdom could face “a winter of greater flooding”.

The prime minister was asked if the Government was playing catch-up with civil preparedness as he attended a summit organised to learn from the summer’s record wildfires.

A second storm, Storm Boelo will bring disruption across the UK on Saturday.

Burnham told reporters on Friday: “No, we are having the conversation with people about being better prepared because we’re living in a world where unexpected events seem to be becoming more and more common.

“Obviously weather events – and we’re in a situation today where the UK is facing storms, and obviously we feel for the people without power in Cornwall right now – it is just having those supplies in the house to get you through those moments.

“So we are having that conversation about resilience. That’s why we’re holding this summit today, it’s why we’re putting the guidance out to the public.

“We potentially are facing a winter of greater flooding, so it’s about recognising all of those risks and taking action.”

As the government continues to talk about the importance of building up supplies at home in case of an emergency, Andy Burnham has told BBC Radio 5 Live what would be in his essentials.

According to the prime minister it’s baked beans, soup and twiglets making up his crisis store. In fact Burnham said he “can’t get by for too long” the marmite-flavour rod-shaped crisps.

Asked on BBC Radio 5 whether, like Angela Rayner, he would make sure his emergency supplies included a bottle of sauvignon blanc, Burnham said: “I’m not sure a bottle of wine is your best first port of call if you’re in a slightly crisis situation, but it probably helps when you’ve done the hard work.

“I’ll stick to the basics, I think. I do like my beans, so I have plenty of those in the cupboard and soup. I think those are the kind of things that I would recommend people to have as well as well as Twiglets. I’m not sure I can get by for too long without those.”

He and Healey rated their favourite crisps from a selection provided, with the PM deciding McCoy’s Salt and Vinegar beat the pub staples pork scratchings and scampi fries to take his top spot. Twiglets were not an option.

In a comment piece, Polly Toynbee has said London Assembly member Andrew Boff, who resigned from the Conservative party this week, is a sign of where the party’s future should be.

The chair of the London Assembly, Andrew Boff, resigned this week from the Conservative party after 53 years of membership. He quit after being blocked from entering the race to be Tory candidate for London mayor because he supports the European convention on human rights (ECHR), putting him at odds with Kemi Badenoch’s hardline position.

But the rest sent messages privately, not daring to reveal themselves under a leadership that doesn’t tolerate dissent. In an interview last year, Badenoch decreed that rebels “should not and cannot stand as a Conservative candidate” if they do not agree with leaving the ECHR. She also bars anyone supporting net zero.

When Boff applied, for the seventh time, to be considered as Conservative candidate for London mayor, he didn’t really expect to be selected – but nor did he expect to be summoned to be told he was barred from entering the race. In a statement he pointed to the fact that Winston Churchill helped found the ECHR after the second world war, arguing “it was never a leftwing project” but a “safeguard against arbitrary government”. What’s more, he points out to me,the “convention doesn’t even mention asylum seekers”, Badenoch’s main justification for leaving the treaty.

Asked about the possibility of courts overturning a Rosebank approval, Burnham said on Friday: “Well, that’s a decision that will be taken in due course.

“What we have done this week is taken great care over the decision about Jackdaw because it wasn’t done properly in the past, and that led to the decision being struck down.

“What people can be certain about is things will be done properly going forward.

“And in relation to Jackdaw, people will question: Was that the right decision? It is going to be a bridge to a renewable future?

“The funds that it will release will be able to go into building out that clean energy system that the country needs, which is a further part of our national resilience going forward.”

Andy Burnham is addressing the wildfire summit in north west England after a summer which saw homes destroyed by fires during the record-breaking heat.

He said Britain needs to be on the “front foot” and that the country had been playing “catch up” for too long.

The prime minister said he expected similar summers going forward.

He told the summit: “I think too often in the past we’ve been playing catch up, and today is trying about trying to get on the front foot and recognise that the summer we’ve just had is likely to be a more regular situation going forward. So make ourselves that bit stronger when it comes to the summer of 2027, and then indeed summers beyond.”

He earlier told journalists: “We lived through a summer that we’ve never seen before, with so many wildfires across the country. Fire and rescue services were stretched, firefighters working extremely difficult conditions, and it was right in that moment to commit to this summit, and today we’re making good on that, but also making some initial steps to improve our response because we will have to plan for summers like this going forward.

“So firstly: better equipment for our firefighters, particularly the PPE, more appropriate to working in very hot summer conditions because it’s very punishing work what they did this summer.

“Secondly, aerial support: so we may see on holiday other countries where there are planes putting water from above on the fires. We believe we need that capability. The Ministry of Defence will be equipping aircraft with that capability, so we’ll be better prepared for summers to come.

“Then thirdly: just better management of the land, so that fire doesn’t get the ability to spread. Those are the early commitments, but the summit will give us more as we go through it today, and people can be sure that we will be even better prepared next summer.”
