Former Ankara mayor, family members questioned in overseas funds probe

2026-09-07 15:04

Former Ankara mayor Melih Gökçek gave a statement to prosecutors on Friday, and his son and daughter-in-law appeared for questioning Monday in an investigation into allegations that undeclared funds were transferred to companies abroad, the T24 news website reported.

The Ankara Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office opened the investigation on August 29 following journalist Murat Ağırel’s reports questioning the financing of multimillion-euro property investments in Germany and Luxembourg linked to the Gökçek family. All three were summoned as suspects.

Gökçek, a longtime politician from President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP), governed Turkey’s capital for 23 years before resigning in 2017 at Erdoğan’s request.

After more than two hours of questioning Friday, Gökçek declined to disclose his answers to prosecutors but defended his record and challenged criticism from Ankara’s current opposition mayor, Mansur Yavaş of the opposition Republican People’s Party (CHP), who was first elected in 2019.

“Don’t expect me to say I said this or that,” he told reporters, citing the confidentiality of his statement.

Gökçek said 21 investigations involving him had been concluded during Yavaş’s time in office and that challenges to those decisions had also been resolved in his favor.

He disputed suggestions that he had avoided questioning, saying he had given statements in the proceedings.

He also accused Yavaş of applying a double standard by questioning decisions not to prosecute Gökçek while accepting similar outcomes in cases concerning himself.

Gökçek said he would discuss the overseas funds investigation on television after prosecutors reached a decision.

He also claimed that 500 investigations and reviews during his first nine years as mayor had all ended without prosecution.

His son Ahmet Gökçek and daughter-in-law Hülya Gökçek arrived at the Ankara courthouse Monday after being summoned in the same inquiry and testified to prosecutors.

Ağırel, who has testified as a witness, said he provided prosecutors with documents concerning the family’s overseas business dealings. His reporting questioned how a German company established with €30,000 in capital acquired property worth approximately €3.8 million.

He also reported on an unsuccessful property purchase in Germany and negotiations to buy a shopping center in Luxembourg.

Gökçek has denied wrongdoing while acknowledging that some transactions and negotiations took place. In an earlier account relayed by journalist Sinan Burhan on TV100, Gökçek said his son financed the German investments by selling property and a vehicle in Turkey, transferring the proceeds through banks and obtaining loans. He said the Luxembourg purchase did not move forward.

Separately, the opposition-run Ankara Metropolitan Municipality filed a criminal complaint on August 7 against Gökçek, his family members, municipal bureaucrats, businesspeople and executives of Beyaz TV and the Osmanlıspor football club.

That complaint alleges they operated a criminal network that used municipal money, property and other assets for personal gain, including through irregularities in public tenders.

Since Yavaş took office in 2019, the municipality has filed nearly 100 criminal complaints concerning Gökçek-era spending and projects.

Many ended without prosecution, while others were blocked after government officials declined to authorize investigations. The number of complaints does not necessarily correspond to the number of investigations opened.
