Turkey raises year-end inflation forecast to 28.4 percent from 16 percent

2026-09-07 08:35

Turkey has raised its year-end inflation forecast to 28.4 percent from 16 percent, blaming the war involving the United States, Israel and Iran for higher energy and commodity prices.

Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz announced the revised projection on Sunday while unveiling the government’s medium-term economic program for 2027–2029.

“We expect inflation to begin declining again in the fourth quarter of 2026 and to stand at 28.4 percent at the end of the year,” Yılmaz said in a televised address.

The new projection is 12.4 percentage points higher than the 16 percent forecast included in last year’s medium-term program covering 2026–2028.

Under the new program, the government expects annual inflation to fall to 21 percent in 2027, 13.5 percent in 2028 and 9 percent in 2029.

Official data released last week showed that Turkey’s annual inflation rate eased slightly to 31.51 percent in August from 31.75 percent in July. The independent Inflation Research Group (ENAG), however, put annual inflation at 49.03 percent in August.

Yılmaz attributed much of the upward revision to the economic effects of the war involving the United States, Israel and Iran, which began in February and drove up energy and commodity prices.

“According to our central bank, the cost of the war in terms of its impact on inflation is estimated at close to seven percentage points,” he said.

The government also lowered its economic growth forecast for 2026 to 3.3 percent from 3.8 percent. It projects growth of 4.2 percent in 2027, 4.6 percent in 2028 and 5 percent in 2029.

The program raised Turkey’s projected energy import bill for 2026 to $71 billion from $63 billion and increased the projected foreign trade deficit to $105 billion from $96 billion. The government also lowered its tourism revenue forecast to $65 billion from $68 billion.

Annual inflation peaked at 75.45 percent in May 2024 before beginning a gradual decline as the government pursued tighter monetary and fiscal policies.

Yılmaz said the government had made “significant progress in combating inflation,” describing price stability as the top priority of its economic program.

“Inflation, which had risen to 75.5 percent in May 2024, began to show a clear downward trend as a result of the policies we implemented,” he said.
