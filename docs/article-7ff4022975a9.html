‘Disgusting’ Karen Bass, Gavin Newsom warned about dragging heels on wildfire docs: ‘We’re going to get them’

2026-09-08 23:31

Sen. Rick Scott (R-Fla) tore into Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass and California Gov. Gavin Newsom Tuesday over what he says has been a nearly yearlong delay in turning over documents tied to the devastating Palisades Fire.

“Mayor Bass and Governor Newsom seem more interested in advancing their political ambitions than serving the Americans that elected them,” the Florida senator told the California Post. “When government fails and harms the American people, the public deserves answers. We’re going to get them.”

Scott’s comments come nearly one year after he and Sen. Ron Johnson (R-Wis.) launched a congressional investigation into the January 2025 Palisades Fire, which destroyed thousands of homes, caused billions of dollars in damage and killed 12 people.

Scott and Johnson subsequently sent requests to the Los Angeles Fire Department, the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power and Los Angeles city officials for documents covering everything from emergency preparations and staffing to water supplies, evacuation plans and after-action reports.

One request to the LAFD sought records concerning the department’s preparation for the fire, the deployment of firefighters and equipment ahead of the wind event, as well as communications with Bass and Newsom’s office and the preparation of the department’s after-action report.

“Senator Johnson and I have been requesting documents for nearly a year, and despite the delays, we are not going to take our foot off the gas,” Scott said.

The Florida senator’s office said Bass and Newsom have “stonewalled” the investigation, but did not provide details on where the inquiry currently stands.

The Post has contacted both the mayor and governor’s offices for comment.

A July 2025 preliminary report from LADWP flagged problems with critical water systems during the fires, including fire hydrants that lost pressure. Scott and Johnson later sought records from city officials about the water-system failures and the city’s response to the blazes.

Reports and subsequent legal filings have also alleged that the mayor’s office pressured officials to soften the Palisades Fire after-action report in an effort to limit the city’s liability over pre-deployment shortcomings — allegations Bass has strongly denied.

The senators held a field hearing in Pacific Palisades in November 2025 titled “Forgotten After the Flames: Stories from the Palisades Fire,” where survivors and local leaders detailed their experiences and ongoing recovery challenges.

The investigation has continued into 2026, with Scott’s office specifically accusing Bass of stalling the release of requested documents.

“The lack of accountability to victims of the Palisades wildfire is disgusting, and it demands action to force answers from Bass, Newsom and everyone else that allowed this tragedy to occur,” Scott told the Post.

Scott’s broadside comes as congressional Republicans have increasingly put California officials under the microscope, with House Republicans separately targeting Bass over the handling of Los Angeles’ homelessness crisis.

The House Oversight Committee’s Delivering on Government Efficiency subcommittee is moving ahead with a Sept. 15 hearing examining federal homelessness funding in LA, despite Bass’ decision not to appear.

Meanwhile, the wildfire investigation remains a political headache for California’s Democratic leadership.

Bass is fighting to retain her seat in the November mayoral election against LA city councilmember Nithya Raman, while Newsom is approaching the end of his second and final term as governor and has continued to fuel speculation about a potential 2028 presidential bid.

The Palisades Fire killed 12 people and officially started on Jan. 1, 2025, before explosively spreading amid severe Santa Ana winds on Jan. 7, 2025. The fire burned over 23,000 acres and destroyed more than 6,800 structures. Its lethal combination with the neighboring Eaton Fire, which ravaged Altadena, resulted in a total of 38,000 acres burned, 16,000 structures razed and 31 dead.
