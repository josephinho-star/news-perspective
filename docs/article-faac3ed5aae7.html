Clive Davis’ final will reveals telling glimpse involving his longtime partner — and his vast fortune

2026-11-16 21:26

Clive Davis refused to forgive a loan involving his longtime partner of 21 years, Greg Schriefer, despite wiping out debts owed to him by other loved ones following his death, Page Six can exclusively reveal.

According to his final will, signed April 16 and obtained by Page Six, the legendary music mogul made a specific exception for Schriefer when outlining which debts should be forgiven.

“If, at my death, GREG shall be indebted to me,” Davis stated in the document, referring to a promissory note dated September 18, 2025. The will went on to specify that any outstanding debt under the agreement “shall not be extinguished” and “shall not be cancelled.”

It was unclear how much money Schriefer may have borrowed or whether he still owed anything when Davis died on June 22 at age 94.

The surprising financial exception came despite Davis directing that debts owed to him by his children, former wife Janet Davis and several close friends and relatives be forgiven.

The instructions appeared in a section of the will titled “Forgiveness of Indebtedness.”

Schriefer was also included among those whose debts were generally to be forgiven, making the separate exception for his September 2025 loan particularly notable.

Reps for Davis’ estate and Schriefer did not immediately respond to Page Six’s requests for comment.

Meanwhile, a probate petition filed in New York revealed that the music industry titan left behind an estate estimated at more than $50 million.

The filing listed more than $25 million in personal property and more than $25 million in New York real estate.

The figures were preliminary estimates and did not establish the full value of Davis’ fortune, including assets that may have been held separately in trusts.

Davis also directed that his residences, personal belongings and remaining estate assets be transferred to the trustees of his revocable trust, which was originally established in March 2018.

The will named two of his sons, Frederic and Douglas Davis, along with longtime friend Donald Zakarin, as executors.

Meanwhile, Schriefer was identified in the probate petition as a beneficiary of the will’s debt forgiveness provision, although the document did not disclose whether he was entitled to additional money or property through the trust.

Davis came out as bisexual in 2013, and wrote about Schriefer in his autobiography, “The Soundtrack of My Life.” The pair had been together for more than two decades before the music executive’s death.

Schriefer previously spoke about being with Davis in his final moments, describing the legendary producer as the love of his life.

Davis took his final breath at 10:58 a.m. on June 22, with Schreifer by his side. “I was holding his hand as he took his final breath, surrounded by family and dear friends who loved him deeply,” Schriefer told us.

The details of Davis’ estate emerged as his lavish Manhattan apartment was put on the market for $11 million.

As Page Six previously reported, the late music mogul’s Upper East Side duplex at the Ritz Tower sparked criticism from some of his friends, who questioned the asking price and the way the residence had been staged for prospective buyers.

However, a spokesperson for the Davis family pushed back, insisting that the music executive had left specific instructions about how he wanted the apartment presented and that his wishes were being followed. The family representative also said the property had already attracted multiple serious inquiries and viewings.

Davis, who died inside the apartment, had lived in the building for decades.

Davis’ final will also included a provision warning that beneficiaries who challenged the will or his revocable trust could lose their inheritance, subject to applicable law.

There was no indication in the probate documents reviewed by Page Six that Schriefer had challenged the will.

The will was admitted to probate in New York on October 30.
