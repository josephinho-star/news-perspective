Turkey jails 17 more, seizes assets amid $18 billion fund turmoil

2026-09-24 11:49

Turkish courts jailed 17 more people pending trial as authorities froze or seized nearly 750 million lira ($15 million) in an alleged stock manipulation case involving investment funds with more than $18 billion in assets and 455,758 investors.

The rulings late Wednesday and Thursday brought the number jailed in the investigation to 26.

Fourteen people were jailed in the first set of rulings, according to the state-run Anadolu news agency.

The court then jailed Tera Investment board members Abdulkadir Özkan and Bülent Uygun and Doğa Insurance Chairman Nihat Kırmızı.

Three others from the group of 20 were released under court supervision.

Police sent another 21 suspects to prosecutors in İstanbul on Thursday, Anadolu reported, with no court decisions on that group announced at the time.

In a separate financial move, investigators froze 387 million lira in cash held in fund accounts, according to Demirören news agency.

They also seized 105 million lira in assets belonging to jailed Pusula fund manager Muhammed Yarız and blocked a proposed 257 million lira transfer through Tera Portfolio Management, the agency reported.

Those three amounts total about 750 million lira; the figure does not include the value of property seized under other court orders.

A court separately ordered the seizure of assets belonging to 11 suspects, including Yarız and Tera Investment Chairman Emre Tezmen.

Investigators say five luxury cars owned by Yarız, worth an estimated 200 million to 220 million lira ($4 million to $4.5 million), were transferred to four people for a total declared sale price of just 26,280 lira ($540) on September 7 and 8.

The cars are under a seizure order, four have been recovered and police are searching for the fifth.

Prosecutors are investigating suspected manipulation of shares in Katılımevim, a company that helps customers save toward home and vehicle purchases, as well as other stock market transactions.

They have asked Turkey’s financial crimes investigators to examine executives’ transfers abroad and cryptocurrency transactions since 2024.

The inquiry has widened amid a selloff that left some funds unable to meet investors’ withdrawal requests.

Turkey’s Capital Markets Board has ordered the liquidation of 131 funds run by seven investment firms and says they have 455,758 distinct investors.

Those funds held more than $18 billion in assets before liquidation, Reuters reported.

The regulator has allowed up to six months to sell the funds’ holdings, though it says the process could end sooner.

Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek is due to meet officials Thursday to discuss the sales and payments to investors.
