Relive golden age of American road trips at these 10 classic Route 66 stops

2026-11-22 17:23

Route 66 has spent a century connecting travelers with the people, places and roadside attractions that help define the American road trip.

Stretching about 2,400 miles through eight states from Illinois to California, the historic highway marked its 100th anniversary in 2026, bringing renewed attention to communities along the route.

Known as the Mother Road, the highway carried generations of families west during the Dust Bowl and the travel boom following World War II. Today, it gives drivers a chance to revisit a classic era of American life, complete with neon signs, vintage diners and small-town main streets.

Route 66 author Jim Hinckley previously told Fox News that the road's lasting appeal has always come down to the communities along the way.

"From its inception, what it's about is the people. It has always been about the people," Hinckley said. "That's what gives it that infectious magic and enthusiasm."

For anyone planning a drive along Route 66, here are 10 stops worth adding to the journey, blending classic landmarks from AARP's online newsletter "The Girlfriend" with small-town gems along the way.

The cross-country drive starts in downtown Chicago at the "Begin Historic Route 66" sign across from the Art Institute of Chicago, according to AARP's "The Girlfriend."

Before hitting the open road, travelers can grab breakfast at Lou Mitchell's, an old-style diner long known as a traditional first stop for motorists, AARP noted.

Visitors can also walk along Jackson Boulevard, which formed part of the original route, or take in views of the city from Skydeck Chicago, according to the outlet.

Heading southwest into Missouri brings travelers to Springfield, widely known as the "Birthplace of Route 66."

Officials met in the city in 1926 and proposed the name "U.S. Route 66" for the highway linking Chicago to Los Angeles, AARP noted.

Today, visitors can browse shops along historic Commercial Street, explore the Wonders of Wildlife National Museum & Aquarium or catch a movie at the restored Gillioz Theatre, a 1926 theater that hosted live shows during the route's early years, according to the guide.

3. Oklahoma City, Oklahoma

Farther down the road, Oklahoma's capital sits near the middle of the route and showcases Western history and art at the National Cowboy & Western Heritage Museum, according to AARP's "The Girlfriend."

Travelers can also stroll through the 15-acre Myriad Botanical Gardens or explore the shops and restaurants in the Bricktown Historic District, AARP noted.

Those staying overnight can book a room at the Skirvin Hotel, a landmark property that has welcomed guests since 1911, according to the outlet.

Continuing west into the Texas Panhandle, smaller towns show what Route 66 looked like before major interstates took over.

In Vega, which was bypassed when Interstate 40 was built, travelers can visit Dot's Mini Museum one block off the old highway, Fox News Digital previously reported.

The free museum stays unlocked so visitors can browse 20th-century antiques, and Dot's granddaughter, Keila Bain, told Fox News her late grandmother inspired a character in the Disney-Pixar movie "Cars."

"Our town was bypassed by I-40, but you know, there are a lot of people who travel, who love to go into the town, meet the people, see the places," Bain said.

Just 15 minutes west of Vega sits Adrian, recognized as the geographical midpoint of Route 66, according to Gary Daggett, president of the Old Route 66 Association of Texas.

"It's the nostalgia, ya know. There's so much of American history here at Route 66," Daggett told Fox News Digital.

While in town, drivers can stop at the Midpoint Café, which owner Brenda Hammit Bradley said is known for its homemade pies and inspired Flo's V8 Café in "Cars."

6. Albuquerque, New Mexico

Crossing into New Mexico, Albuquerque has the longest surviving city stretch of original Route 66, according to AARP's "The Girlfriend."

Eighteen miles of neon signs, retro motels and vintage storefronts line Central Avenue, leading visitors from historic Old Town to the art galleries and music venues of Nob Hill, AARP noted.

Travelers can also ride the Sandia Peak Tramway or spend the night at Hotel Parq Central, a restored building that once served as a railroad hospital, according to the outlet.

7. Painted Desert, Arizona

Heading into eastern Arizona, Route 66 passes near the Painted Desert, where old-school roadside attractions still draw passing drivers, Fox News Digital reported.

One popular photo stop along that stretch is a large dinosaur model near the highway that dates back to midcentury family road trips.

Farther west in Arizona sits Winslow, a desert town immortalized in The Eagles' hit song "Take It Easy," Fox News Digital noted.

That musical tie has turned Winslow into a lasting stop for music fans and road-trippers looking to visit a piece of American pop culture.

Flagstaff is one of the best-preserved Route 66 towns and long served as a mountain stop for travelers heading west, according to AARP.

Visitors can stop by the Flagstaff Visitor Center and Historic Train Depot to learn how Americans shifted from train travel to road trips in 1926, the outlet said.

Travelers can also shop for Native American jewelry or visit the Museum Club, a former log cabin that now hosts country music acts, according to the guide.

10. Santa Monica, California

After more than 2,400 miles on the road, travelers reach the Pacific Ocean at the Santa Monica Pier, celebrated as the "End of the Trail" for Route 66.

Visitors can take a photo next to the historic "End of the Trail" sign, ride the pier's solar-powered Ferris wheel or walk along Ocean Avenue to mark the end of the trip, AARP noted.

Fox News Digital's Gary Gastelu and Chelsea Torres contributed reporting.
