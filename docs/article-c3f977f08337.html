Jensen Huang breaks silence on resignation, says he’d be ‘honored’ to return as Nvidia’s CEO

2026-11-17 16:56

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang broke his silence on Tuesday following his resignation from the company, stating he would be “honored” to return to the role should the company’s Board of Directors support the move.

In a statement obtained by The Hill, Huang said he saw an “outpouring of calls” from some Nvidia staff, investors, business partners and customers for him to return as CEO, just days after he resigned from the role amid a national security scandal.

“Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company,” Huang wrote. “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”

A source familiar told The Hill on Monday Nvidia’s Board of Directors is not weighing Huang’s return.

Huang resigned days after admitting to an extramarital affair, and reports suggest it was with executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. officials suspected was a spy for China. The allegations sparked a flood of scrutiny of the longtime CEO, including an independent investigation by Nvidia’s Board of Directors.

Alice Huang is a former executive of the Singaporean data center and cloud computing firm Megaspeed, which is under investigation over allegations it smuggled Nvidia’s AI chips into China despite chip export controls.

Jensen Huang said on Tuesday he “did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life.”

“I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation,” he wrote.

Shortly after his resignation, petitions circulated calling for his return, while President Trump said on Monday he “certainly wouldn’t mind” if Huang returned as CEO.

“I think he learned a big lesson,” Trump said. “I guess maybe one lesson more important than another, but I would certainly not mind seeing that.”

The Financial Times reported over the weekend he is discussed a comeback with allies in Silicon Valley. Several tech executives signed an open letter calling for his reinstatement, including SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan.

The executives argued Huang should be brought back while an independent investigation is completed.

The open letter, which has more than 170,000 signatories, asked the board to “provide compelling evidence that [Huang’s] removal is in Nvidia’s best interests,” and stated the former CEO “deserves due process, not mob justice.”
