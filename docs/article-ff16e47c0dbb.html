Poundland collapses into administration, putting 12,000 jobs at risk

2026-11-25 16:28

Poundland has called in administrators, putting 12,000 jobs at risk in the run-up to Christmas after its owners rejected at least two last-ditch bids for the budget retailer.

Administrators at AlixPartners have been appointed to the retailer, which has 600 stores in the UK and Ireland, by Poundland’s directors. The chain is now expected to be broken up with significant job losses and store closures.

AlixPartners said a sales process “did not identify a viable solvent solution for the business as a whole”.

Poundland would continue to trade with all stores and distribution centres remaining open while administrators pursued “all options including sales of the business and its assets, either in whole or part, from administration”, AlixPartners said

The retailer’s US owner, Gordon Brothers, which bought Poundland for a nominal £1 from the European retail group Pepco last year, has been under pressure to accept an offer that would save jobs and protect thousands of the retailer’s suppliers.

The US finance firm said on Wednesday afternoon it had “explored every avenue to secure a sale of the whole company” but that Poundland was “still vulnerable to inflationary pressures or any downturn in sales and margin”.

“Poundland has faced several years of instability. In July 2025, Gordon Brothers supported the company’s restructuring plan – stepping in when no one else was willing to finance the business,” it said.

High inflation and heavy competition from budget stores, online specialists and supermarkets have left the 36-year-old retail chain – the UK’s first and biggest nationwide single-price retailer – struggling.

Poundland’s board filed a notice of intention to appoint administrations last month – which was later extended – protecting the retailer from the demands of creditors until 26 November while a solution to its problems was sought.

Just two bidders interested in the whole company remained in talks right up until this week’s deadline.

Martin Property Group, a Northern Ireland family-owned company, which has been working with the existing management, led by Barry Williams, for about two months, remained interested.

It was joined by a last-ditch bid from Germany’s Zachert Private Equity, a fund founded by a former Bloomberg journalist that specialises in acquiring mid-sized companies such as the German photo studio group Studioline, and the US food and drink company Brands Within Reach.

Other bidders for the whole group included the little-known finance group Xyra Group Holdings, and the Irish investment company Innovest, which both fell out of the bidding race over the past week.

AlixPartners said: “We appreciate this is a deeply concerning time for all involved – customers, suppliers and, in particular, Poundland colleagues.

“We would encourage anybody interested in acquiring the business in part or as a whole, as well as UK retail operators with open employment opportunities, to contact the administrators.”

Poundland’s board was forced to appoint administrators before Thursday’s deadline as shelves began to empty because the company was running short of funds to restock during its peak Christmas trading period.

Gordon Brothers’ UK director, Mark Newton-Jones, held the latest of several meetings with the business secretary, Jonathan Reynolds, on Monday, more than a week after giving evidence to the business select committee. MPs called on the government to protect Poundland and other high street stores from “predatory capitalism” and asset-stripping last week.

Liam Byrne, the Labour chair of the business select committee, said he had recalled Newton-Jones to give public evidence. He said the committee was seeking assurances that Gordon Brothers or any related party would “not act as administrator, liquidator or in any other insolvency capacity in relation to Poundland”.

Poundland, which was founded with one store in Burton upon Trent in 1990, has endured a rocky history under a string of different owners as it has faced rising costs and heavy competition from chains such as Savers, Home Bargains and B&M, as well as from the big supermarkets.

It is now expected to be broken up with retailers such as Poundstretcher, B&M and The Range taking parcels of stores.

Gordon Brothers rejected an initial management-backed rescue bid financed by Martin Property Group for the discount chain.

The US-based finance company said it could raise more from a breakup of Poundland - selling off stock and parcels of stores to rival retailers - than through the solvent bids. Those bids promised only a small upfront payment for the £30m in shareholder debts that Gordon Brothers took on with its fellow shareholder – Poundland’s former owner Pepco – at the time of buying the chain.

The deals were rejected by Gordon Brothers despite Pepco, which holds £7.5m of the debt, saying it was prepared to wait for repayment in the hope of saving the business.
