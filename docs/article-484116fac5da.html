‘Bratty men’ demand ‘prince treatment’ in dating trend, sparking debate over who should pay for meals

2026-11-22 08:01

Published Nov. 22, 2026, 3:01 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Some men are demanding “prince treatment” in the dating world, according to a dating trend making headlines, with men expecting women to pay for meals and spoil them.

Cosmopolitan magazine relationships writer Syeda Khaula Saad dubbed men with these expectations “bratty men” in a piece published in early November.

VICE, meanwhile, reported that the debate over “who’s supposed to spoil whom” has gotten out of hand.

According to the publications, “bratty men” are turning the concept of “princess treatment” on its head.

Men who want “prince treatment” expect pampering, extra attention and gifts. The trend is a reversal of “princess treatment,” the notion that women should be spoiled with flowers, love notes and other romantic gestures.

“I’ve seen countless women sharing their experiences with what I’m now calling ‘bratty men.’ They’re demanding to have their meals paid for, that women drive them around and whatever this is,” Saad wrote Nov. 9.

“They’re announcing that they’re ‘the prize’ and that a woman, or a date, should be spoiling them.”

Toronto publication Now Toronto asked residents to weigh in on the “prince treatment” trend, highlighting a video by Toronto-based TikTok creator Shaurya Manocha arguing that men deserve to be spoiled.

“I would want my daughter to have somebody treat her like the princess that she is … I take care of her, I cherish her — same thing with my wife,” a father and husband told Now Toronto.

Barstool Sports founder Dave Portnoy was asked about the “bratty men” trend during an interview with Fox Business host Stuart Varney on Wednesday.

Portnoy said he wasn’t sure how much of the trend he believed, asking, “What type of woman would ever want this, or go out with this?”

“This seems a made-up thing,” he added. “I haven’t seen anywhere in my travels that women are willingly or wanting to pick up the check and pick up after and be treated like garbage. So, I hope this trend isn’t real.”

Susan Trombetti, matchmaker and CEO of Exclusive Matchmaking, told Fox News Digital in a statement, “The bratty men and bratty women are out there. It’s a competition of the brats and a response to the women who expect the guy’s first-class upgrade.

“All the social media influencers and podcasters are trying to up the ante to get subscribers and promote this idea that men should be the ones on the pedestal. Your normal, nice guy that can’t get a date or find someone is vulnerable to this sort of nonsense. They figure, ‘Why not give it a try?’

“Women are demanding Ubers and gifts on dates, so why can’t they sit back and demand, too? The problem is that neither the men nor the women who do this are high-value partners.”

“I think it’s more of a social media narrative where influencers try to get the most views and go viral,” she said.

Relationship expert April Davis, founder of Luma Luxury Matchmaking, told Fox News Digital, “A man wanting to be taken out to dinner once in a while isn’t the problem. Why wouldn’t he want that? The question is whether he does thoughtful things for you too or if you’re the one always taking care of him.

“If you want a man who plans dates and picks up the check, you can look for that. If he wants someone who takes turns, that’s fine, too. You may just not be right for each other. There’s no need to turn it into a fight about what all men or all women should do.”

A debate over “princess treatment” spread across social media in July 2025 after a video went viral of a woman explaining what her husband does when the two of them go to a restaurant together.

“If I am at a restaurant with my husband, I do not talk to the hostess, I do not open any doors and I do not order my own food,” the woman said in the video, according to the New York Times.

Some women reacting to the video called it “regressive,” according to the Times. A 26-year-old grad student told the outlet at the time that “princess treatment” can look “however you want it to look.”
