Turkey removes park director after mother bear and cub are shot dead

2026-11-27 15:43

Turkey’s Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry on Friday announced it had removed the director of Soğuksu National Park in Ankara’s Kızılcahamam district after a mother brown bear and one of her cubs were shot dead in the park.

The mother bear, named Kar, had three cubs. The other two survived the shooting.

Security camera footage showed the bear family wandering around a restaurant in the area before the shooting, Demirören news agency reported.

The ministry has yet to identify who shot the bears or say whether anyone authorized the shooting.

Brown bears are protected under the Bern Convention, a treaty on wildlife and habitat conservation to which Turkey is a party. Turkey’s Land Hunting Law bars the hunting of protected wildlife.

The ministry announced on November 22 that its Inspection Board had opened an administrative investigation and that it would take action if it found negligence or misconduct.

It removed the director after a preliminary review and said the investigation was continuing.

The Directorate General of Nature Conservation and National Parks (DKMP) assigned a 25-member team to monitor the two surviving cubs. The team installed 45 motion-activated cameras, 23 of which can transmit live footage, and used three drones with thermal cameras and night-vision equipment to search the area.

The cubs appeared to be in good health, the ministry said. Experts will assess whether the cubs have eaten enough to hibernate on their own or need extra food or care in a shelter.

Perihan Koca, a lawmaker from the pro-Kurdish Peoples’ Equality and Democracy Party (DEM Party), submitted a written question to Agriculture and Forestry Minister İbrahim Yumaklı asking who carried out the shooting, who ordered it and what the ministry had done to protect the surviving cubs.

Koca also asked whether officials had acted on complaints from businesses operating in the park and questioned the legal basis for killing wildlife there.

The Nature Association (DD), a Turkish conservation group, estimated in a November 20 statement that the country had between 3,400 and 4,000 brown bears.

The group linked more encounters between bears and people to construction, mining and logging, which break up habitats and reduce the food available to bears. It called for tougher penalties for crimes against wildlife and for constitutional protection for wild animals.

The Ankara Bar Association’s Animal Rights Center called for a full investigation. It argued that an administrative order alone could not make the killing of protected wildlife lawful.

The brown bear is Turkey’s only bear species. It lives in forests and mountains, particularly in the Black Sea region, eastern Anatolia and parts of the Mediterranean region.

A study published in April 2025 estimated that about 17 percent of Turkey was suitable habitat for brown bears. Under its projections, climate change and human activity could shrink that area by roughly 40 percent or more by 2050 and by up to two-thirds by 2070.
