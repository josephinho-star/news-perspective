Israel detains dozens of Americans, including a congressional candidate, in West Bank

2026-10-21 13:28

NAHALIN, West Bank — U.S. Democratic congressional candidate Brad Lander was detained along with about 80 American and Israeli rabbis and activists detained by Israeli security forces as they helped Palestinians harvest olives from their grove close to a settlement in the occupied West Bank.

Lander later told NPR he has been released from Israeli military custody but several dozen others are still detained.

This is the largest mass detention of activists in the occupied West Bank in at least a decade, two Israeli lawyers who work on related cases told NPR. Rabbi Arik Ascherman, a longtime Israeli human rights proponent, called the detentions "unprecedented." This comes amid a spike in violence by Israeli settlers against Palestinian farmers in the West Bank.

The Israeli military said in a statement that harvesting in the area requires the farmers to submit requests to the authorities before the olive harvest season in order to coordinate "security of the area and maintain public order." The military said Palestinians in this area did not submit a request, so soldiers and police arrived and detained those present.

"Regarding reports of the detention of a congressional candidate, we note that he was released once the matter was brought to our attention," the Israeli military told NPR.

Lander, an ally of New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and a former city comptroller, denounced the Israeli authorities' actions. He told his followers on social media, "Occupation is a crime under international law," but harvesting olives is not.

"We were on private Palestinian land. We were with the farmers whose land it is. There's no obligation to coordinate with the Israeli military," Lander told NPR and WBUR's Here & Now. "This was the IDF making it impossible for these poor Palestinian farmers to harvest their olive trees," he added, using the initials for the Israeli military.

American rabbis from the U.S. organization T'ruah, the Israeli group Rabbis for Human Rights and other activists had joined Palestinian families in picking olives at their grove in Nahalin, close to the Israeli settlement of Beitar Illit. They said they were participating in what they called Protect the Harvest, bringing an international coalition of volunteers together to accompany Palestinian harvesters at a time when they and their farms and other property have come under attack from Israeli settlers in the area.

Olive harvesting is an important time for Palestinians both culturally and economically. But it has become increasingly hard for Palestinians in the occupied West Bank to access their fields. Several Palestinians there told NPR they have been unable to harvest their olive trees there since the Hamas-led attack on Israel on Oct. 7, 2023, saying it had become too dangerous in the face of rising settler attacks in the occupied West Bank.

The detentions come less than a week after American photojournalist Heidi Levine, working for The Washington Post was assaulted by Israeli settlers in the West Bank, the latest journalist to come under attack during the Palestinian olive harvest season. Democratic congressman Ro Khanna of California was also briefly detained by Israeli forces while visiting the West Bank in July.

On Wednesday, the group of Americans and Israelis joined the Palestinian farmers hoping their presence would help them harvest in peace. T'ruah said the action was called Protect the Harvest.

After roughly two hours, drones began circling overhead and Israeli security forces arrived and declared the area a closed military zone. They detained the Israeli and American activists and took them to the police station for questioning. The rabbinical human rights group T'ruah said a Palestinian family was also detained.

"Let us be clear about what happened here: Rather than protecting Palestinian farmers and ensuring their right to work their own land, the Israeli military forcibly removed those who came to provide a protective presence," T'ruah CEO Rabbi Jill Jacobs said in a statement. "This is an outrageous abuse of power, and part of a systematic, state-backed campaign of ethnic cleansing aimed at driving Palestinians from their land," she said.

Lander, who won the Democratic primary for a New York House district in June, has been an outspoken critic of the Israeli government.

"What's going on here cries out for attention. I feel that profoundly as an American Jew, and as someone who's headed into the next Congress. My constituents don't want their tax dollars paying for this," Lander told NPR's team in the West Bank, before he was detained.

"When we say state-sanctioned ethnic cleansing, it's not just settler violence. It's the IDF's effort to prevent Palestinians from living their lives," Lander told Here & Now after his release.

Israeli authorities have rejected accusations of ethnic cleansing. Israel's Foreign Ministry accused Lander of a political "stunt," saying he was looking for a confrontation.

Amy Spitalnick, CEO of the Jewish Council for Public Affairs in New York, said 33 of those detained are U.S. citizens and she was in contact with some of them. She wrote on social media that some have been charged with "disturbing the peace."

The State Department told NPR it is aware of reports of Americans detained in the West Bank and is tracking the situation, but said it would not comment further due to privacy and other considerations.

Daniel Estrin reported from Tel Aviv, Israel, where Itay Stern also contributed reporting. Alex Leff, Michele Kelemen and Luke Garrett contributed to this story from Washington, D.C.
