Turkey’s central bank keeps policy rate at 37 percent for 5th straight meeting

2026-09-10 12:13

Turkey’s central bank left its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 37 percent for a fifth consecutive meeting on Thursday as energy prices fueled by the US war on Iran threatened to slow the decline in inflation, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

The Monetary Policy Committee, led by Governor Fatih Karahan, kept the one-week repo auction rate at 37 percent. It also held the overnight lending rate at 40 percent and the overnight borrowing rate at 35.5 percent.

The decision was widely expected, with 16 of 17 economists surveyed by Reuters forecasting no change.

The central bank said recent data pointed to a decline in underlying inflation and confirmed continued weakness in domestic demand.

It warned, however, that high energy prices resulting from geopolitical developments posed an increased risk to the inflation outlook.

The bank said it would tighten monetary policy if it detected a “significant and persistent deterioration” in inflation and would continue to make decisions on a meeting-by-meeting basis.

Oil prices climbed above $100 a barrel this week as escalating fighting involving the United States war on Iran raised fears of further disruption to Middle Eastern supplies. Turkey relies heavily on imported oil and natural gas, leaving consumer prices vulnerable to increases in global energy costs and depreciation of the lira.

Official data showed that consumer prices rose by 1.84 percent in August, while annual inflation eased slightly to 31.51 percent from 31.75 percent in July.

Energy prices increased by 5.46 percent during the month, led by an 11.23 percent rise in fuel prices, according to the central bank’s assessment. Annual energy inflation reached 39.23 percent.

The independent Inflation Research Group (ENAG), whose estimates regularly exceed the official figures, put August inflation at 2.24 percent month-on-month and 49.03 percent annually.

The central bank raised its year-end inflation forecast from 26 percent to 28 percent in August, citing higher prices for diesel, natural gas, food and other commodities.

The government separately revised its year-end projection to 28.4 percent in its medium-term economic program announced this month. The bank’s medium-term target remains 5 percent.

Thursday’s decision was the fifth consecutive hold since the bank lowered the policy rate by 100 basis points, from 38 percent to 37 percent, in January.

Turkey returned to more conventional economic policies following President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s re-election in 2023. The central bank subsequently raised borrowing costs sharply after years in which Erdoğan pushed for low interest rates despite soaring inflation and a steep decline in the value of the lira.

The Monetary Policy Committee’s next meeting is scheduled for October 22.
