NY woman Deirdre Keane runs 7 marathons while pregnant, finishing NYC race at 34 weeks

2026-11-20 09:57

A New York mom-to-be has completed seven world marathons in a single year — running every race while pregnant.

Deirdre Keane, 38, crossed the finish line of the New York City Marathon earlier this month in 4:06:55 at 34 weeks pregnant, completing her seventh marathon during pregnancy.

The nurse practitioner and management consultant had already completed six of the seven World Marathon Majors, competing in Tokyo, Boston, London, Sydney, Berlin and Chicago.

“I knew how bizarre it was to be running a marathon at 34 weeks, but I also knew how fit I was and self-aware of my body at this point,” Keane told the Associated Press. “And while it is very unusual, I wasn’t the first woman to have ever done this.”

After marrying her husband, Brian, last year, Keane expected it might take time to start a family, so she signed up for 11 races in 2026, including all seven majors, two ultramarathons, an Ironman and a trail marathon in Ireland.

“In my mind, 2026 was going to be my year of bucket list items,” she said. “I was like, this is going to be my last hurrah before settling down and becoming pregnant, becoming a mother.”

But after running the Tokyo Marathon in March, Keane noticed something was off during the Boston and London races in April. A pregnancy test confirmed she was expecting.

Rather than abandon her plans, she decided to continue running, while dropping the Ironman and one ultramarathon.

“Brian was understandably skeptical and worried at first. Neither of us fully understood the physical limitations of pregnancy and why. The research we found was surprisingly limited and frustrating,” Keane said.

“So, we decided to take it one marathon at a time, with me not pushing the pace and keeping walking in my back pocket,” she added.

Her OB-GYN cleared her to compete, advising her to prioritize hydration and nutrition and stop exercising at the first sign of muscle cramps, according to the World Running Network.

Keane ran the Sydney Marathon at 25 weeks pregnant, Berlin at 29 weeks and Chicago at 31 weeks before finishing in New York at 34 weeks.

The hometown race was her biggest concern as her pregnancy became increasingly noticeable. Friends cheered her along the course, while Brian made several stops to bring her water.

The course also passed near Weill Cornell Medical Center on the Upper East Side, where she plans to deliver her baby.

“The marathon passed a block away from Weill Cornell at mile 16, where I’m delivering, so there were plenty of jokes about just pulling off there to have the baby or testing the medical volunteers’ training on the course with an unusual medical intervention of a delivery,” she told the Associated Press.

Keane has now completed 74 marathons and ultramarathons, including the World Marathon Challenge in 2023, running seven marathons across seven continents in seven days. She was the fastest woman to complete the challenge that year.
