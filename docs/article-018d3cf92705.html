This AI infrastructure stock has fallen 25%. Why I'm buying the dip

2026-11-17 15:24

Quanta Services Inc. has fallen roughly 25% from its highs. The blue wave has raised concerns about tougher restrictions on AI data centers, weighing on technology stocks and the companies building the infrastructure to support them. But I think the market is overreacting. Even if the data center buildout slows, the need for power infrastructure isn't going away. That's where Quanta comes in. The Houston-based company helps build, upgrade and maintain the nation's power grid. Think transmission lines, substations and the connections that bring renewable energy projects onto the grid. Key points The AI data center boom may slow, but trillions in infrastructure spending are still expected. America's aging electrical grid needs major upgrades. Strong earnings and a record $63 billion backlog. I get why investors are nervous. As more data centers are proposed across the country, concerns about electricity costs and local infrastructure are prompting calls for tighter restrictions. Some guardrails are necessary. But I don't believe this will mean the death of data centers. In fact, a slower pace could be a good thing. Rather than experiencing a massive boom followed by a bust, the investment cycle could stretch over a longer period. PWR YTD mountain Quanta, YTD There's also a bigger strategic issue at play. The U.S. has an advantage over China in AI development. There's no way we'll want to give that up. Why I'm buying The data center buildout isn't going away Even if certain states make it harder to build new facilities, companies still need the computing power to compete in AI. That means they'll likely look for other locations where development can move forward. And those facilities need electricity. Quanta is a business that stands to benefit from the broader investment needed to support rising power demand across the country. America's aging grid needs an overhaul Roughly 70% of the grid is more than 25 years old, and much of it was originally built in the 1950s and 1960s. With many components designed to last 50 to 80 years, we're reaching a point where upgrades are becoming increasingly necessary. At the same time, electricity demand is accelerating, driven in part by AI and broader electrification. So now we have an aging grid that needs modernization and growing demand that requires additional capacity. Quanta is positioned to benefit from both. In April, the company increased its estimate of its total addressable market through the end of the decade from $970 billion to $2.4 trillion . Strong earnings and a record backlog Third-quarter revenue jumped 57% from a year earlier, while organic growth reached 37%. Adjusted EBITDA climbed 69%, and earnings increased 82%. I'm not saying that kind of growth can continue indefinitely, but those are extraordinary numbers. What also stands out is that Quanta's backlog has reached approximately $63 billion, up 62.7% from a year earlier, with both its electric and underground infrastructure businesses reaching record levels. This is key because it gives us a clearer picture of the demand Quanta is seeing and provides visibility into potential future revenue. Cash generation is another bright spot. The company reported $1.2 billion in free cash flow and raised its full-year free cash flow guidance to between $2.5 billion and $3 billion. Why now? Quanta shares have fallen roughly 25% from their highs as investors worry that political opposition and tighter regulations could slow the data center buildout. To me that reaction has created a nice entry point. Rather than viewing a longer investment cycle as a negative, I see it as an opportunity for Quanta to benefit from infrastructure spending over many years. Bottom line Tighter regulations could delay data center projects, and a slowdown in infrastructure spending could weigh on Quanta's growth. But the market is putting too much emphasis on the possibility of a near-term slowdown and not enough on the long-term demand for electrical infrastructure. With a record backlog, strong earnings and a massive market opportunity ahead, I think Quanta is a wonderful company that's gone on sale.
