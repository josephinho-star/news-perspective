Georgia ‘twisted twin’ who killed mom with sister released from prison after serving half her sentence

2026-09-11 08:44

A Georgia woman who admitted to killing her mother alongside her twin sister when they were teenagers – later earning the chilling nickname “Twisted Twins” – was released from prison, having only served a portion of her sentence.

Tasmiyah Whitehead was freed on parole from Pulaski State Prison on August 5 after she was ordered to spend 30 years behind bars for murder, according to jail records viewed by The Post.

Whitehead – along with her sister Jasmiyah – stabbed their mother Jarmecca “Nikki” Whitehead between 50 and 80 times during a fight inside her Rockdale County home on January 13, 2010.

Whitehead, who is now 33 years old and goes by the nickname Tas, was released as part of the plea deal that reduced a murder charge to voluntary manslaughter, according to WSB-TV.

Tasmiyah had been sentenced to 20 years in prison for the manslaughter charge, plus five years for possession of a knife during a crime and another five for false statements to the government.

She had served 16 years in prison between pre-trial detention and the 12 years after her conviction.

The sentencing the girls received angered their grandmother, who claimed the court didn’t care about Nikki Whitehead.

“You offer someone 30 years with parole?” Lynda Whitehead said during her granddaughter’s trials in 2014, according to WSB-TV. “It is like she is not important, she is the victim, it is like no one is concerned with what happened to her.”

Tasmiyah had been eligible for parole since 2017, but served an additional nine years before the parole board granted her eligibility, the outlet reported.

Jasmiyah isn’t eligible for parole until May 2028, despite taking the same plea deal as her sister.

The demented sisters had only been back living with their mother for a week because they had become violent and too hard to handle for her, in January 2010.

The three got into a fight after the twins woke up late for school and were told by their mother, “You’re late for school, you’re not going to do what you want to do, you have to live by my rules,” according to 11Alive.

The twins claimed their mother threatened them with a cooking pot before they tackled her, igniting a full-out brawl.

Tasmiyah admitted to investigators that she hit her mother in the head with a pot during the fight, before grabbing a knife from the knife block.

She then stabbed her mother after her sister was bit in the chest.

“She bit me in the chest and like I said, I’m not that that big, so when she bit me she latched on to me and I tried to get her off because it hurts so I’m trying to punch her, I guess that then Tas stabs her. She stabbed her,” Jasmiyah told police.

The twins continued stabbing and hitting their mother before dragging her bloody body into the bathroom and dumping her in the bathtub, submerging her in water.

Police allege Nikki Whitehead was still alive when her daughters moved her into the bathtub.

The twins headed to school after the murder but called police when they got back home.

Officers found the mother’s body in the bathtub and initially treated the twins as victims who had just returned home from school.

Investigators described the scene as “the bloodiest scene I think I’ve ever been to.”

The twins had played innocent, distracting detectives from obvious signs of evidence on them including hiding bite marks and scratches they sustained in the fight by wearing gloves indoors or claiming the marks were self-inflicted stress relief because their mother had just died.

The lies grew until they were arrested four months later.

Investigators partially uncovered the ruse when they placed both sisters inside a car with a microphone and caught them talking about the “lack of evidence” they had on them because they couldn’t find the murder weapon.

The girls initially pleaded not guilty to the murder charge before accepting the plea deals in 2014.

“Just really wish it didn’t happen like that and I wish I could have solved something. I do I wish…sorry…It was just confusion and turmoil and it never stopped,” Tasmiyah cried, according to the outlet.
