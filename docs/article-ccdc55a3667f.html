Mary Peltola flips Republican Senate seat in Alaska

2026-11-18 03:21

Former Rep. Mary Peltola, a Democrat, is projected to win the race for Senate in Alaska, defeating GOP incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan and flipping a seat for the Democratic Party, according to Decision Desk HQ.

With 94 percent reporting according to DDHQ, Peltola has 50.12 percent of the vote to Sullivan’s 45.79 percent, which puts her above the 50 percent threshold to avoid ranked-choice voting coming into play.

Her win is an upset in a state that generally leans Republican, signaling that support for President Trump dragged down incumbent Republicans this year.

The race had been very competitive all summer, in part due to Peltola’s unique profile as a candidate, Sullivan’s ties to the unpopular war in Iran and President Trump and the independent nature of Alaska politics.

Peltola also tried to hit Sullivan over his ties to large trawling companies headquartered in Washington and Oregon and link the practice of large-scale trawling to the diminishing fish stocks in Alaska.

Peltola, 53, is a two-term former congresswoman who has also worked on fishing boats and campaigns and has served in local government and as a tribal judge. She was raised in the Kuskokwim River region in southwest Alaska and now lives in Anchorage.

Peltola will become the first Alaska Native senator. While a congresswoman, she stood out from many in her caucus for supporting the oil and gas industry and gun ownership. She introduced bipartisan legislation to limit trawling, a type of commercial fishing, and pressed the need for institutional reform in Congress like term limits and stock trading bans.

In the House, she was a leader of the Blue Dog Caucus, a group of moderate Democrats that also included Reps. Jared Golden (D-Maine) and Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-Wash.).

During the Senate campaign, the Sullivan campaign and national Republican groups ran attacks targeting Peltola’s treatment of her staff based on a New York Times report in which former aides alleged Peltola demeaned and berated staff. Republicans also hit Peltola over votes she took in Congress, including against a version of an annual defense bill.

The race could put Alaska in the crosshairs of federal intervention. The Department of Justice sued in October to block the results if Sullivan lost, arguing that an independent candidate by the same name, Dan Sullivan, would lead to voter confusion.
