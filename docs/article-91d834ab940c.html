Home Depot vs. Lowe's: Which stock has the edge and our support after earnings

2026-11-20 19:28

Earnings from Home Depot and Lowe's this week have made one thing clear: Neither company is going to get any help from the stuck housing market and elevated interest rates anytime soon. That keeps this two-way race firmly centered on how each company can control what it can control — our money continues to be on Home Depot. The reason why comes down to Home Depot's greater focus on the professional customer, which provides a more durable revenue stream and puts the company in a better position when housing and rates turn. Home Depot's edge over Lowe's was evident in key metrics in the fiscal third quarter, from same-store sales, or comps, to revenue scale to earnings quality. Home Depot scored another quarter of better comps growth of 2.2% versus a gain of 1.8% for Lowe's; revenue of $43.4 billion versus $22.8 billion; growth in earnings-per-share (EPS) of 3.2% versus 1.3%; and a stronger operating margin of 12.6% versus 11.3%. Following a nice quarter for Home Depot on Tuesday but a tough stock reaction, Jim Cramer advocated buying the dip. "The negative [stock] response just seems to be based on a myopic view of what the company was implying about the current quarter with its updated guidance," Jim said on "Mad Money" that evening. "Home Depot is traditionally conservative with its guidance and with the new CEO at the helm starting next week, that's all the more reason to put forward a forecast that can be beaten." Alongside earnings, Home Depot announced that CFO Richard McPhail will succeed Ted Decker as CEO next week. Decker, who took a temporary medical leave in August, will remain chairman of the board. Jordan Broggi will move into the role of executive vice president and CFO. Ann-Marie Campbell, who had been helping lead the company in Decker's absence, will take over as president and COO. On the post-earnings conference call, McPhail said investments throughout the company's various businesses are resonating with customers, especially the pros. Campbell said on the call: "All of our pro cohorts positively comped in the quarter, including pros shopping in the store and larger pros working on complex projects and using more of our ecosystem capabilities." While pro outperformed do-it-yourself customers, both posted positive comps in the quarter. On Wednesday, Lowe's also reported a solid quarter . Unlike Home Depot, Lowe's raised its guidance, citing pro strength, sending the stock higher. "LOW had a solid quarter in a tough environment. Similar to HD, comps accelerated as it appears that the worst is behind us in home improvement even as we are still aways from recovery," analysts at D.A. Davidson wrote to clients Wednesday. Analysts noted that Lowe's saw sequential improvement in comp sales for both DIY and pro customers, though only pro was positive versus the prior year. Lowe's said it was gaining share in the cohort of small-to-medium professionals, touting deeper relationships, thanks to improved fulfillment and loyalty options. It's not surprising that both companies are claiming the professional customer as their own. However, it was Home Depot that was quicker to expand its distribution operation, buying three speciality building products companies in three years — SRS in 2024, GMS in 2025, and Mingledorff's in 2026. Lowe's played catch up in the space with acquisitions of Artisan Design Group and Foundation Building Materials last year. HD LOW YTD mountain Home Depot vs. Lowe's YTD To be sure, the macro environment is terrible for both companies. Both stocks bounced off their 2026 lows last month. Their rallies coincided with the peak in the 10-year Treasury yield , which tends to impact the direction of mortgage rates. Remember, it's elevated mortgage rates that are keeping a lid on housing activity, which is the lifeblood of both renovations and new construction. While both are coming off the boil in recent weeks, Home Depot shares are down less than Lowe's year to date. Jim admits that being an investor in Home Depot in the past few years has been tough, with several head-fakes on rates looking like they're going out way, only for them to reverse higher. We may be in another of those tricky times as sticky inflation is raising the prospects of additional Federal Reserve rate hikes in the coming months. But this too shall pass, and Jim remains convinced Home Depot is the best play for when rates do come down, and housing recovers. "Whenever the broader housing market does return, Home Depot's positioned to be one of the companies that stand to benefit the most," Jim said. "I'll tell you what's going to happen with Home Depot because I've seen it happen again and again and again. This $339 stock will go right to $500. It just will."
