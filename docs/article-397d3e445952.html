Midterm elections 2026 live updates: Economy, inflation and other voter concerns

2026-11-03 20:39

Voters are at the polls on Tuesday, Nov. 3 to decide control of Congress, in an election that could define President Donald Trump's final years in office.

All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are at stake, as Republicans try to defend narrow majorities in both chambers despite strong headwinds. Voter frustration with Trump's war in Iran, inflation, perceived government corruption and immigration policy could weigh down Republicans this cycle. But Republicans have the bulk of billionaire campaign spending, and Democrats have faced drama in the Alaska and Michigan Senate races in the waning days of campaigning.

FiftyPlusOne projects Democrats have a significant edge in the House and are favored in the Senate, with key races tightening in recent weeks.

In the Senate, races in deep-red states such as Kansas, South Carolina and Nebraska could be in play, according to recent projections. Republicans could win in traditional Democratic strongholds such as Minnesota and New Hampshire. And toss-up races in states including Alaska, Iowa, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas will determine who wins the gavels next Congress.

In the House, Republicans will find out if partisan redistricting in states including Texas and Florida that they'd hoped would net them nine seats actually pans out. And Democrats, who are hoping for a blue wave year, will try to knock off GOP candidates in districts that in past cycles were seen as out of reach.

A Democratic win in either or both chambers would end unified Republican rule in Washington, disrupt the president's legislative agenda and begin a period of Democratic oversight and investigations aimed at the Trump administration, his associates and companies that have cozied up over the past two years.

The Trump administration's monthslong campaign to challenge how states conduct elections and push back against those efforts come to a head as well. The Justice Department said Tuesday it's sending about 1,000 election observers and monitors to all 50 states, a broader effort than the agency has ever deployed before.

The Associated Press provides vote data and race projections for CNBC. Polls will begin to close at 6 p.m. ET, with Alaska being the last to close at 1 a.m. ET. Check back for Election Day updates and race calls throughout the night as results come in.
