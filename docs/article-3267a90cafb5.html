Registered sex offender was allowed to participate at South Carolina church for decades

2026-09-12 11:00

A registered sex offender who was sentenced to prison in the 1980s after he told authorities that he had molested several boys whom he met through his religious work with Christian youth in Georgia was later allowed to participate for decades in ministry at a South Carolina church.

The Brookwood church, a southern Baptist institution in the South Carolina city of Simpsonville, only just disqualified Jerry Sidell from serving there in any capacity, after he joined it in 1999 and fulfilled various ministerial roles.

The church’s senior pastor, Bryan Jones, told congregants at a Thursday night meeting that Sidell had been “forthright” with Brookwood leaders back then about his previously pleading guilty in Georgia to the crimes of enticing a minor for indecent purposes and child molestation.

Without mentioning Sidell’s name, Jones said that church leaders implemented boundaries addressing his participation at Brookwood, which operates a preschool. Jones said those rules included a prohibition from working in or being around children or student ministries.

Sidell, 69, also could not set foot in the school or those particular ministries’ spaces, Jones said. And Sidell had to disclose his past to those in charge of any non-children or non-student ministries in which he wanted to take part, Jones told congregants, according to an audio recording of the meeting provided to the Guardian.

Yet Jones – who was named Brookwood’s senior pastor in late 2022 – also suggested that he personally had only recently learned of Sidell’s status as a registered sex offender.

Jones didn’t elaborate. But he said he reacted to that discovery by meeting Sidell and informing him that he could no longer perform with the church’s adult musical band or be a member of the small prayer group – also made up of adults – to which he belonged.

Jones also said Brookwood had enacted a new policy requiring “any” registered sex offender attending church services to enter through a specific door, check in with the institution’s security team and then sit with a designated guard. That designated guard would then escort the registered sex offender out of Brookwood after the completion of services, Jones said.

As Jones put it, he and his fellow Brookwood leaders believe in “grace and forgiveness” – but, he added, “we felt … there are consequences for actions”.

An email to the Guardian from an individual familiar with Brookwood and Sidell contended that the church specifically put those heightened rules in place in either July or August. That was after authorities in Pawleys Island, South Carolina, reported arresting a registered sex offender named Warren Spence Perry for allegedly violating his probation by working at a school and children’s daycare run by a local southern Baptist church.

Five other people connected to the Pawleys Island community church and its school, about four hours away from Simpsonville by car, were later also arrested on suspicion of illegally concealing Perry’s involvement with children there.

Perry, 70, pleaded guilty in 2023 to sexually exploiting a minor three years earlier, and a proposed federal class-action lawsuit has been filed at the church and school where he had been working, as WCIV and other South Carolina news outlets have reported.

Furthermore, the individual who emailed the Guardian about Sidell accurately noted his having founded Rusty Bucket Music, a venue which – according to social media video and images – has hosted youth performers and patrons since its opening in 2019.

Videos available online suggest Brookwood leaders have had a presence at Rusty Bucket Music in the South Carolina town of Gray Court, which is adjacent to Simpsonville. Two videos show Jones and his fellow Brookwood pastor JC Thompson delivering prepared, recorded discussions of biblical scripture there.

Rusty Bucket Music’s website and its Facebook page had been taken down as of Friday.

A professional networking page under Sidell’s name described him as a Brookwood church musician since at least 2013. He reportedly partook in worship ministry, played the keyboard and participated in small men’s groups at Brookwood, according to the Christian church watchdog website MinistryWatch, which first reported on Sidell in late August.

The individual who corresponded with the Guardian about Sidell said the Brookwood church has about 5,000 congregants. According to the individual, “Sidell’s sex offender status was not disclosed to many of the people” who served alongside him or encountered him at the ministries in which he was involved.

As the individual put it: “Sidell kept this information extremely private.”

Sidell did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Neither did Brookwood.

At Thursday’s meeting with congregants, Thompson and Jones made it a point to say that their own children were Brookwood congregants, too.

“We care about protecting our people,” said Thompson, who also said that Brookwood had security cameras throughout the entirety of its campus, among other safety measures.

Another pastor, Matt Plunkett, said all church volunteers must also take an abuse-prevention class.

Jones at one point asked the congregation to pray for victims. But at another juncture, in a clear allusion to Sidell and the media’s interest in him, he asserted: “Satan would love to disrupt what’s happening in this church. He would love to disrupt what God is doing in this church.”

The cases centering on Sidell and Perry came amid a broader, years-long scandal involving the handling of matters related to sexual abuse by the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC), the US’s largest Protestant denomination. It is also the second-largest Christian denomination in the US behind the Roman Catholic church.

The SBC, a politically influential group of loosely affiliated churches with about 12 million congregants, in June 2022 issued a 205-page document listing hundreds of Baptist leaders and congregants accused or found guilty of sexually abusing children.

Neither Sidell nor Perry were named in that specific document.

Other religious denominations in the US and across the world besides the SBC and the Roman Catholic church – Christian or otherwise – have also spent years grappling with sexual abuse within their ranks.

With respect to his case, Sidell pleaded guilty in 1983 to five charges of child molestation and one of enticing a minor to commit indecent acts, a 1986 Atlanta Journal-Constitution article reported.

The newspaper reported that Sidell at one point told detectives that he had molested nine boys whom he knew from serving at the nondenominational First Christian church of Jonesboro, Georgia, as a youth minister. He received six concurrent 10-year prison sentences, the Journal-Constitution reported.

The parents of two of Sidell’s victims – a pair of brothers – sued Sidell for $2m. The Journal-Constitution reported that the younger son secured a $500,000 jury verdict, but the award was eliminated entirely after Sidell was granted a new trial on what the outlet termed a legal technicality. The plaintiffs then dropped the litigation, and told the Journal-Constitution that members of their church ostracized them for going public against Sidell.

In addition to South Carolina, Sidell is registered as a sex offender in Florida, a US justice department tool shows. Both registrations list a home address in Simpsonville about a mile and a half away from the Brookwood church.
