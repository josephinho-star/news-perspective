Thanksgiving storm strands thousands of holiday travelers — but good news is coming

2026-11-26 20:22

A Thanksgiving storm slamming the East Coast spread holiday travel chaos across the country — with hundreds of flights canceled, thousands delayed and roads and bridges washed out from record-setting flooding on the eastern seaboard.

Some 400 flights had been canceled by noon Thursday, just hours before most families sat down to carve their holiday turkeys together.

The good news is that people who managed to get to their destinations were in for clearer conditions for the return journey. Forecasts showed clear skies and milder weather for Friday and Saturday in New York and most of the northeast.

And more than 1,300 flights had also been delayed — with nearly a quarter of all planes departing New York City running behind schedule, figures from FlightAware showed.

Other East Coast travel hubs faced similar grinds as the storm blasted through the area with 75 mph winds and torrential rains — with nearly half of all flights out of Miami and Washington, DC delayed, while Atlanta and Charlotte also saw delays nearing a quarter of all flights.

That’s all while air travel faced record numbers — with nearly half a million flights forecast to fly across Thanksgiving week, a 16-year high and a 7.5% increase in holiday air travel from 2019.

But Thursday’s mayhem wasn’t reserved for the skies — roadways up and down the eastern seaboard were swamped by the storm, leading to road and bridge closures.

Upwards of an inch of rain per hour was forecast to fall in some places, with the high winds building a dangerous storm surge that left coastal areas in New Jersey, Virginia and Delaware flooded by water levels higher than 2012’s devastating Hurricane Sandy.

All that water and wind left Virginia’s 17.6-mile Chesapeake Bay Bridge closed, while the vital James River Bay Bridge was also shut down.

North Carolina’s Outer Banks was also cut off from the mainland, with the stretches of its lifeline Highway 12 closed by flooding, CNN reported.

And the problems weren’t just reserved to the coast — Missouri found itself buried in storm-driven snow, with 9 inches falling in some eastern parts of the state and prompting the state’s transportation department to urge all travelers to delay their plans.

Those who have made it home for the holidays are in for their own helping of trouble, however, with more than 630,000 customers losing power.

Most of those outages were concentrated in Missouri, Virginia and Maryland, though Pennsylvania, North Carolina and Illinois also saw swaths of power loss, PowerOutage.us showed.
