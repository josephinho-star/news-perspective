Dan Campbell’s huge Lions gamble pays off in Bears safety disaster

2026-11-26 20:36

Dan Campbell opted to go for an extra helping of points to end the half on Thanksgiving and it paid off for the Lions.

Detroit made what was already a disastrous sequence for the Bears worse, turning a tie game into a 19-10 advantage at the break in less than two minutes at Ford Field.

With the Bears backed up near their own end zone, Lions defensive end Derrick Moore forced a fumble by Caleb Williams that was ultimately recovered by the Lions.

With the ball rolling into the end zone, Bears tackle Darnell Wright was flagged for illegally batting the ball out with 1:13 left in the half.

It left Campbell with the decision of taking a safety and getting the ball back with all three of his timeouts or getting the ball at the Bears’ 14-yard line and being in prime position for a go-ahead touchdown.

Campbell, always a bit of a risk-taker, opted for the safety and faith in his offense to get more.

Jared Goff then marched Detroit down to the Bears’ 11-yard line with 10 seconds remaining. With the risk of it being the final play of the half, Campbell, of course, opted to go it.

Goff repaid his coach’s faith when he connected with tight end Sam LaPorta for an 11-yard touchdown with six seconds to go to help Detroit jump ahead in a battle of 8-2 NFC North teams.

“They had two timeouts left with a minute,” Campbell said on the CBS broadcast before the start of the second half. “I just felt good about our offense being able to get the ball. We had three timeouts to go down and score to make it a nine-point difference.

“They did a hell of a job. It came down to the wire. It was a hell of a job by the defense and offense to make those plays. LaPorta big-time catch. Goff big-time throw.”

The Bears, who saw injured star tight end Colston Loveland carted off in the first half, quickly answered with a Kyle Monangai rushing touchdown to get the Lions’ lead to 19-17, so the extra points certainly proved critical.
