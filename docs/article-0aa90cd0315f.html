S&P 500 futures rebound sharply to rise 2%, kick-starting shortened week after tech rout: Live updates

2026-11-23 01:08

U.S. equity futures bounced on Sunday, as a shortened week on Wall Street got underway after all three major averages ended Friday in correction territory.

Futures tied to the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 673 points, or 1.4%. S&P 500 futures added 2.03%, and Nasdaq-100 futures rose 2.76%.

In Asia, South Korea's Kospi fell 2.34% in early trading, with heavyweights Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix down about 3% and 2%, respectively. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was 0.11% down.

Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index futures were last at 22,238 compared to the index's last close of 22,410.8. Japan's markets were closed for a public holiday.

Last week, major indexes tumbled as worries about the artificial intelligence trade continued to spook investors, despite Anthropic's record debut on Thursday.

By the end of the rout last week — which was the worst weekly decline for the Nasdaq Composite and Dow in more than six years, and the largest for the S&P 500 since April 2025 — all three of the major averages were in correction territory, and the Nasdaq turned negative for the year.

The Nasdaq-100 index ended Friday in bear market territory on an intraday basis, but was just shy of that mark based on the decline from its closing high on Oct. 6.

Investors this week will get a breather, no matter what price action looks like. Markets are closed on Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday, and Friday's session will be shortened with the closing bell set to ring at 1:00 p.m.

All eyes are set to be on the shaky tech trade, as investors examine if last week's sell-off represented the market finding a durable bottom, or if equities have more room to fall.

Drew Pettit, chief investment strategist at Roundhill, said in an appearance on CNBC's "Closing Bell: Overtime" on Friday that the indiscriminate selling at the end of the week — which extended beyond just tech — is a sign the sell-off may be concluding. But, he said the burden of proof is on the bulls to undermine the bearish sentiment dominating the market.

"Fundamentally, I think the floor is a lot higher," he said about how low equities could go. "Technically… you need a string of catalysts for the bulls to prove the long-term earnings story isn't broken."
