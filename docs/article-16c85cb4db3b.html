Oil soars above $105 in wake of Saudi airport strike, Ukraine energy attack blamed on Russia

2026-10-12 14:05

Oil soared above $105 a barrel Monday morning in the wake geopolitical turmoil, with an Iran-backed group launching a deadly strike on a Saudi airport and Ukraine accusing Russia of attacking energy facilities in spite of a ceasefire declared by President Trump.

Brent crude oil futures jumped 1.9% to $106.73 a barrel by about 9:40 a.m. ET, while West Texas Intermediate rose 2.1% to $93.73.

The benchmarks initially fell after Trump announced over the weekend that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to stop targeting each other’s energy facilities.

He also said Russia’s President Vladimir Putin vowed to supply more than 4 million tons of diesel to the global market, potentially helping lower prices.

On the ceasefire, Zelensky said, “We support it, but I have not heard approval of this proposal from the Russian side.”

National average gasoline prices have remained stubbornly above $4 a gallon, and diesel – which often impacts the costs of food, apparel and other consumer goods – has soared above $6.

The commander-in-chief has been eager to tamp down prices ahead of the November midterms, as frustrated Americans report affordability concerns.

But fighting in the Middle East conflict has ramped up, and experts have pointed out challenges to Trump’s efforts to lower gasoline prices.

A Saudi-led coalition said Monday it’s facing another round of drone and missile strikes from Yemen’s Houthi rebels.

The Iran-backed terror group repeatedly launched attacks on an international airport in Riyadh over the weekend, killing at least 13 people.

The escalation threatened Saudi Arabia’s efforts to maintain energy production during the global oil crunch.

The kingdom vowed to stand firm in the face of the attacks, going forward with a major energy conference scheduled for Thursday. However, heads of oil majors including BP, ExxonMobil and Shell have cancelled plans to attend, a sign of ongoing safety fears in the region.

Meanwhile, Hurricane Isaias has forced oil producers off the US Gulf Coast to shut down production, further restricting global supply after months of conflict in the Middle East.
