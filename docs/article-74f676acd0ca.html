‘Hexed’ review: A watered-down ripoff of better Disney movies

2026-11-24 19:33

Running time: 103 minutes. Rated PG (action and some rude humor). In theaters.

Let me set the scene: A little girl’s magic powers are so dangerous that her mother is forced to put something on her hand to muffle them, and the child forgets she has any abilities at all.

Years later, the girl’s spells and sparks calamitously return, and she runs away to ultimately learn that being a witch is what makes her special.

If you were at bar trivia night, you’d write down “Frozen,” correct? Now, you want to belt out “Let It Go.”

Yes and no. I’m actually talking about Disney’s latest, lesser movie, “Hexed.” And as you can tell, it was definitely molded in “Frozen”’s old ice cube tray. The studio has also released the film in its usual Thanksgiving week sweet spot reserved for animated hits. It’s easy to mix the two up.

Except for one important difference: “Frozen” is good and “Hexed” is bad.

And, holy Elsa, is it generic. The characters are forgettable, the boring adventure is follow-the-map video gamey and the typical takeaway is “be yourself.” The movie’s use of witchcraft as a shortcut to “empowerment!” is so obvious, I suspect many 5-year-olds will feel patronized.

“Sometimes we’ve gotta keep our magic inside,” mom Alice (Rashida Jones) tells baby Billie at the start. Shudder. Meanwhile, we sit in the theater thinking: “If only we’d kept our brains and bodies outside!”

Billie grows up to be a rebellious teen (voiced by Hailee Steinfeld) in Ohio, who resents her protective mother so much that she inputs her into her phone as “The Warden.”

This is where the movie’s rather simplistic soundtrack begins: “I Want To Break Free” by Queen. Thanks. Otherwise, we’d never have known!

One day when Billie’s enchanted bracelet falls off — simple as that! — her supernatural skills resurface and cause a portal to open to another world called Hexe.

And down the rabbit hole she goes.

For Millennials, Hexe is Halloweentown — a hidden city of witches and wizards who fled there during the witch trials.

That dark and edgy backstory whooshes away fast and bears little on the plot, besides allowing for the false equivalency of a hormonal high school outcast and people who were drowned and burned at the stake.

Their realm is basic and lusterless, designed like an animatronic Ren faire via Main Street Disney. This studio has, for the most part, given up on beauty and sided with bland and cute. Characters spout lines like “that’s my resting witch face.”

When Billie arrives, the townsfolk are stunned to observe she can fly.

Cue the next track on the soundtrack of “Duh!”: “Levitating” by Dua Lipa.

Long ago, a prophecy foretold that during “the rare moon’s return” an aerially inclined witch would, well, I’m not sure what. Free them in some way, shape or form, I guess?

Standing in her way is her villainous grandmother Celeste (Jodie Foster), who happens to be the queen of Hexe. Was she elected, or empowered by divine right? I’m not sure. I don’t think the writers know either. But, like Billie, she is similarly airborne and ruthlessly craves being the prophesied one. She’ll mow down anybody in her way without a second thought.

“Hexe” is the rare family movie that is ardently anti-grandma.

It’s also resolutely anti-detail. For instance, there are other half-bird, half-human creatures that can soar. They’re Celeste’s equivalent of flying monkeys. (Actually, there’s a lot of “Wicked” in this as well.)

If flying is such a spectacular wonder, why can so many things do it?

With the help of a three-eyed cat named Beef (Walton Goggins) who lives in a swamp and is weirdly reminiscent of Lafayette from “True Blood,” Billie practices flight to save Hexe… from something.

Mom arrives and during one of her pilot lessons gives her daughter the most Disney advice of all time: “Just be you.”

Sheesh. Suddenly “Let It Go” seems like the height of sophistication.
