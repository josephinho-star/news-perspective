Texas execution of Reinaldo Dennes takes over an hour as prison staff struggle to find suitable veins

2026-11-20 07:48

Michael Sinkewicz, Fox News

Published Nov. 20, 2026, 2:48 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

A Texas inmate’s execution lasted more than an hour Thursday as prison staff struggled to find suitable veins for a lethal injection, with the condemned man repeatedly complaining of severe pain before being pronounced dead.

Reinaldo Dennes, who was convicted of capital murder in the 1996 killing of Houston diamond dealer Janos Szucs, repeatedly cried out during the procedure at the state penitentiary in Huntsville, according to The Associated Press.

Dennes was pronounced dead at 7:08 p.m., one minute after the procedure was completed.

The procedure lasted 63 minutes from the time Dennes was strapped to the gurney until its completion, as prison staff struggled to find suitable veins for the intravenous needles used to administer pentobarbital.

Dennes repeatedly complained of pain after Thursday’s procedure began, groaning and calling out to God, according to witnesses cited by The AP.

“The pain! Go! Go!” Dennes exclaimed.

“My right leg is killing me!” he later shouted, describing the sensation as a cramp.

“I don’t think this is normal. Take me, father. Don’t let me suffer like this. C’mon,” he added.

Texas Department of Criminal Justice spokesperson Amanda Hernandez said all procedures were followed appropriately but acknowledged that staff made multiple unsuccessful attempts to establish IV access before placing needles in Dennes’ left foot and the left side of his neck.

Hernandez said she did not know how many attempts were unsuccessful but maintained that the locations ultimately used complied with the state’s execution procedures.

She also suggested that Dennes may have exaggerated his discomfort, noting that he complained of pain before the drugs began flowing and in areas where no IV had been placed.

According to The AP, when asked whether Dennes was faking his pain, she replied, “I think, yeah, there were allegations. … There was nothing abnormal on our part.”

“This isn’t the first time we had to insert IVs into other locations. It’s also not the first time we’ve had an inmate make allegations of pain immediately,” Hernandez said.

Dennes was the second person executed in the United States on Thursday, following Florida’s execution of Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, who was convicted of killing a pregnant woman and her husband during a home invasion.

Overton’s execution marked Florida’s 20th of the year, a state record, according to the AP.

Fox News Digital has reached out to the Texas Department of Criminal Justice for additional comment.
