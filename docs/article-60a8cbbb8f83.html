Taylor Swift and Travis Kelce’s extravagant wedding décor was burned to the ground immediately after $30M ceremony: report

2026-10-27 16:52

Taylor Swift and Travis Kelce’s lavish $30 million wedding reportedly ended with their custom-made décor being burned to the ground.

A source told the Daily Mail on Tuesday that Swift’s team instructed crew members to “dispose” of the extravagant decorations to ensure there weren’t “any traces” left behind.

“They didn’t want people to steal it and then auction it or sell it, so they just burned it all,” the insider claimed to the outlet.

The custom-built furnishings, which included monogrammed items featuring two interlocking Ts and garden-like structures, were reportedly transported from New York to Pennsylvania — where they were originally built — to be burned.

“They had told us it was going to be incinerated, but I don’t know where they actually physically incinerated it,” the source added.

The source also said the sets were transported in 11 semi-trucks that were reportedly “super packed.”

“The fact that no one has relics of it and it all had to get burned and properly disposed of is kind of crazy,” the insider said. “Eleven semi-trucks to transport cross-country and then just burn it all down was pretty wild, just because you don’t want people [to have access to it].”

Swift herself did not make the request, though she had a hand in planning and designing the ceremony because she “didn’t want to just be handed a wedding,” according to the Daily Mail.

Page Six reached out to reps for Swift and MSG for comments but did not immediately hear back.

Swift, 36, and Kelce, 37, tied the knot at Madison Square Garden on July 3 in front of more than 1,000 guests, including A-listers Selena Gomez, Brad Pitt, Dakota Johnson and Ed Sheeran.

The Daily Mail reported on Monday that the pop superstar and the Kansas City Chiefs tight end scrapped plans for a two-story carousel and a man-made pond at their nuptials.

The carousel was reportedly axed over its $7 million price tag and replaced with a large tent for carnival games, while the pond, which was planned beneath the altar, was abandoned over safety concerns and because it would “require too much engineering.”

“The edges weren’t tall enough, and it would be really easy to fall over,” an insider claimed to the outlet.

The festivities, which Kelce would later deem “the best night of my life,” were also filmed by multiple camera crews, with a source telling Page Six, “It felt like you were at a movie set.”
