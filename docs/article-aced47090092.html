UN court rules that Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya

2026-11-19 19:09

The United Nations’s International Court of Justice (ICJ) on Thursday found in a new ruling that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim population, following thousands of deaths amid the county’s “clearance operations” in 2017.

The Hague-based world court said Myanmar violated its obligation to prevent the destruction of evidence in connection with allegations of genocide, and that it violated obligations under the 1948 Genocide Convention to punish those who committed acts of genocide, among other items.

“However, the Court found that Myanmar did not directly and publicly incite the commission of genocide by private persons against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine State,” the ruling states, referring to where the Rohingya lived in Myanmar.

The ICJ ordered the country to immediately cease all ongoing violations, take steps to investigate all persons suspected of committing acts of genocide, ensure that acts of genocide do not continue and adopt measures to bring restitution to the Rohingya. It has given the country two years to report back to the court on these restitution efforts.

The world court stated that Myanmar’s military, police and border guards killed around 10,000 Rohingya civilians in incidents involving mass shootings, sexual violence and arson. These attacks drove more than 810,000 Rohingya out of Rakhine and into Bangladesh as refugees.

ICJ President Yuji Iwasawa said nine judges voted in favor of the ruling and six objected, The Wall Street Journal reported.

The decision comes seven years after Gambia, a largely Muslim country, filed the case. It’s the fourth time that the world court has ruled that a country met the legal threshold to prove genocide since the Nazi Holocaust and the first time that a state has been held responsible, according to Reuters.

Myanmar, which is in the midst of a civil war, has previously denied accusations of genocide.

International law has faced criticism in recent years after South Africa filed its case against Israel, accusing it of committing genocide on Palestinians in Gaza during the nation’s retribution campaign — sparked by a Hamas-led attack on Oct. 7, 2023.

The ICJ ordered Israel in a 13-2 ruling to cease its military operation in May 2024, but Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu vowed to continue until there was “complete victory” over Hamas. Israeli officials have denied South Africa’s accusation of genocide, with the late Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-S.C.) at the time telling the world court to “go to hell” after the ruling.

The world court is limited in how it carries out its orders. Russia ignored a 2022 ruling to stop its invasion of Ukraine, and the U.S. refused the ICJ’s 1986 decision to pay Nicaragua war reparations after the ICJ ruled that the U.S. violated international law.
