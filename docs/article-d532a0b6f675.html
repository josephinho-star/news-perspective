Turkey condemns Israel over renewed military work in southern Syria

2026-10-05 12:35

Turkey on Monday condemned Israel for resuming work on a military fortification project in southern Syria’s Quneitra province, accusing it of undermining a decades-old disengagement agreement and trying to create new facts on the ground.

The Foreign Ministry said Israel had restarted work on what Ankara called the “so-called Sufa 53 line” and said the activity was eroding the 1974 Disengagement of Forces Agreement between Israel and Syria.

“We strongly condemn Israel’s resumption of work on the so-called ‘Sufa 53 line’ in Syria’s Quneitra province, thereby undermining the 1974 Disengagement of Forces Agreement, as well as its activities aimed at creating new faits accomplis in Syria,” the ministry said in a statement on Monday.

Ankara also called on the international community to take “effective and determined steps” to push Israel to end what it described as expansionist policies and withdraw from the Syrian territory it occupies.

Turkey said it would continue to support Syria’s sovereignty, political unity and territorial integrity.

The statement followed reports that Israeli forces had resumed excavation work Sunday west of al-Asha village in Quneitra, near the separation line with the Israeli-occupied Golan Heights. Syria’s state-run Alikhbaria television said military vehicles and bulldozers were working as part of the Sufa 53 project.

The project, which Israel began in 2022, runs through rural parts of Quneitra and includes a military road, trenches and earth berms. Local residents have reported losing access to agricultural and grazing land, while some of the work has altered natural water flows, according to the Anadolu news agency.

The dispute matters because the area is governed by the 1974 agreement that followed the Arab-Israeli war of the previous year. The deal separated Israeli and Syrian forces, created a UN-monitored area of separation and placed territory east of the Israeli line under Syrian administration.

Syria condemned the renewed work on Sunday as a “flagrant breach” of the agreement and warned that Israeli military activity in the area threatened regional stability. Jordan, Qatar and Kuwait also issued condemnations.

Tensions along the frontier have increased sharply since the fall of Bashar al-Assad in December 2024.

Israel moved troops into the UN-monitored buffer zone after his government collapsed and has since maintained a military presence there, saying the deployment is necessary for its security. The move has drawn criticism from Syria and other governments, while the United Nations has said Israeli forces entering the separation area violated the 1974 agreement.

Turkey has become one of the main foreign supporters of President Ahmed al-Sharaa’s government in Damascus and has repeatedly criticized Israeli military operations in Syria as violations of the country’s sovereignty.

The latest dispute comes as Ankara and Israel increasingly find themselves on opposing sides over Syria’s post-Assad security order, with Turkey backing a stronger central government in Damascus and Israel seeking to prevent security threats from taking hold near the Golan Heights.
