Davey says far right in Britain now better funded and supported than in the past – UK politics live

2026-09-22 14:47

Davey says 'change to nature of politics itself' is needed

Davey said politics is broken.

But Conference, there is a bigger, more fundamental change we need to talk about.

A change to the nature of politics itself.

Because the things that have gone wrong in our country, the crises we face – the maternity crisis, the housing shortage, the sewage scandal, the cost of living, the sense that nothing quite works the way it should –

These are not the failings of the British people. Not for one second.

These are the failings of governments. Labour governments. Conservative governments. SNP governments in Holyrood.

Davey claims Lib Dems would raise tax allowance to £15,000, paid for by growth generated by rejoining EU's single market

Davey turns to tax.

Look what’s happened over the last few years – because of the lack of growth.

Desperate for another source of money, governments – both Conservative and Labour – have taken it out of people’s pockets.

How? By freezing the tax-free personal allowance.

Raising income tax by stealth.

Taking hundreds of pounds more out of the payslips of ordinary people, every year.

A stealth tax to pay the cost of the economic vandalism of Johnson and Farage.

When we were in government, our party did the opposite.

We raised the personal allowance by £4,000.

Taking more than 3 million people out of paying income tax altogether.

It was a profoundly liberal thing to do.

Rewarding hard work and aspiration. Helping the lowest paid the most.

Trusting people to spend their own money, not telling them the state knows best.

And here comes the big policy announcement.

So I’m announcing our plan to raise the tax-free personal allowance to £15,000.

Taking 2 million more people out of paying tax altogether, and giving most taxpayers a £680 tax cut.

Real money, back in people’s pockets.

Now – you might have heard Robert Jenrick say something similar at Reform’s conference. In between trying to defend Farage’s latest funding scandal.

But here’s the big difference.

Jenrick didn’t say this, but Reform’s figures show they’d pay for it by ripping £22 billion of crucial support away from disabled people.

Think about that. Reform say you can have a tax cut, but only if they get to punish disabled people at the same time.

We say no. We can cut taxes and support the most vulnerable people in our country.

Because we will pay for our tax cut fairly and honestly.

Not with more borrowing. Not with fantasy spending cuts that never materialise. Not by hiking your taxes by stealth elsewhere.

We will pay for it from the dividends of growth. The growth that comes from our bold new partnership with Europe.

Here is Peter Walker and Alexandra Topping’s story on this.

And here is an extract from the story, with some of the detail not set out in the speech.

Addressing the party’s annual conference in Brighton, the Lib Dem leader said the proposal, which would eventually increase the threshold for paying income tax to £15,000 and raise the level at which people pay the 40p higher rate by about £6,000, would be paid for entirely by increased growth.

This growth, the Lib Dems argue, would be created through rejoining the EU’s single market and customs union.

As making a deal with the EU would take time, the actual tax cuts would only happen in the final year of a parliament, potentially as late as 2034, costing £17bn. In the interim years, thresholds would be raised by inflation, thus keeping levels effectively stable.

There would also be a smaller increase to the 45p additional rate of tax, currently payable above £125,000.

Davey turns to special needs, and his disabled son, John. He says he and his wife were told John would need a wheelchair all his life. But they worked themselves on his mobility, with support from therapists, and now he can ride a tricycle.

I’m an MP. Emily is a councillor and a lawyer. We’ve had a lot of support from family and friends.

But even so, we have had to fight for John, every step of the way.

And I know that, for most people who aren’t so lucky, the fight is much harder.

I see them every week in my constituency surgery. Like the parents fighting for their kids with special educational needs.

Battling a system that’s supposed to be there for them and their child. But instead it beats them down and makes them feel hopeless. Trapped. Powerless.

Ask any parent in that position – and I know there are lots of you here in this hall – and they’ll tell you.

We don’t want charity. We don’t want a system that does everything for us.

We know our children best, and we love them like no one else can ever love them.

What we want is a system that empowers us and our children, so we can help them thrive.

That’s what real care is all about. It doesn’t just manage people – it unlocks their potential.

That’s what I’m fighting for. A liberal country. A caring country.

Davey is now talking about AI. This passage from the speech was briefed overnight.

Can we leave it to Trump to save us all? No!

Can we leave it to Mark Zuckerberg? To Sam Altman? To Elon Musk? No!

But I’m afraid the plan Andy Burnham has set out – a partnership with Trump’s Department of War – doesn’t come close to meeting the scale of this threat.

It only risks making us more dependent on a Trump administration in the pocket of the tech bros.

So it’s up to us to rise to this challenge, friends.

We need a global treaty to press pause on super-intelligent AI now, before it’s too late.

We need a new International AI Safety Agency – like we have for nuclear – to stop anyone from developing this technology recklessly.

A liberal solution – because the problem is fundamentally one about power. It’s too concentrated, and we must break it up.

Davey says liberalism 'rooted in seeing the value of every human being"

Davey says it is important to understand why people are attracted by far-right politics.

People are fed up.

They see a politics that promises change and fails to deliver.

Their bills keep rising. Their kids still can’t afford a home of their own. Their loved ones still wait too long for a GP appointment.

When people stop believing politics can change their lives, they get cynical. Angry. And they’re not wrong.

When our young people can’t see a future for themselves in our communities, they stop looking. And they turn to their screens instead.

Screens that offer the illusion of excitement. The illusion of connection. The illusion of belonging.

But screens that only deepen the sense of emptiness and loneliness that no amount of scrolling can ever fill.

Is it any wonder the populists have found something to exploit?

Their whole agenda is to tear everything down. And when people are desperate for change, “tear it all down” starts to sound like the only honest thing left.

Davey critcises Kemi Badenoch for excluding from the Conservative party anyone “who cares about human rights or climate action”.

And he says liberalism can take on populism because it means appreciating the worth of every individual.

Real Liberalism is rooted in seeing the value of every human being – not just the ones who share our appearance or our beliefs.

A free society is messy. It’s full of disagreement.

And as Liberals, we don’t erase our differences. We confront them.

The way people across our country overcome their differences every day.

Not by shouting past each other, but by building real human connections.

Person to person. Neighbour to neighbour. Street by street.

Because when that person you disagree with stops being just another little box on your social media feed, and becomes a human being with a face, a home and a life –

Davey says the Lib Dems are in “the fight of our lives”.

But they are “already winning”, he says.

I look around this hall and I see so many councillors, so many council leaders, so many Members of parliament – in Westminster, Holyrood and Cardiff Bay.

And all of you who’ve helped get them elected.

We’re even winning in Devon – whatever you might have heard!

Davey accuses Farage of promoting 'hatred and intolerance' to 'make himself richer' and gain power

Davey says Nigel Farage must be stopped from making Britain like Donald Trump’s America.

This is a far-right movement that is given oxygen, and heat, and fuel by politicians and parties who claim to be serious contenders to lead our country.

Cynical politicians like Nigel Farage.

Not just willing, but eager to fan the flames of hatred and intolerance to make himself richer. To get himself into power.

Trying to create a nation of division and fear – because that’s what keeps him going.

Well Conference, that is not our country. That is not who we are.
