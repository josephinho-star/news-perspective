Personal loan repayment terms usually range from a few months to nearly a decade, depending on the lender, the loan amount and type, and the borrower's credit profile. Three to five years (36 to 60 months) are the most common terms, but you can find lenders offering seven or eight years (72 or 84 months), especially for larger loans being used for home improvement projects.

The interest rate on a long-term personal loan depends on the borrower's credit score and income, among other actors, althought APRs generally range from 6% to 36%.

You can get approved for longer repayment terms from banks, credit unions and online lenders. Borrowers may be attracted to the smaller monthly payment, even though the increased interest could raise the overall cost of borrowing.

For example, if you chose a three years term for a $10,000 loan at a fixed 12% interest rate with no fees, you'd pay about $332 a month. If you chose a seven year term, your monthly payments would drop to $177. But when all is said and done,, you'd have paid about $2,871 extra in interest .

A longer repayment term can be a good idea when the lower monthly payment makes the loan affordable and helps you avoid missed payments.

It may also make sense if you need to preserve cash for emergencies or essential expenses, like housing payments, healthcare or an auto loan. Compare the total interest and any fees, and see if you're allowed to make extra payments without penalty, and pay off the balance sooner

It's generally recommended to take the shortest term you can comfortably afford, but if there is an unavoidable expense — like a home improvement project or medical emergency — and you don't have the room in your budget, a longer term may be the right choice.

While a long-term loan can give you more breathing room, there are drawbacks.

Smaller monthly payments. Spreading repayment over a longer timeframe usually reduces the individual amount due each month.

Larger loan amounts. Credit cards and short-term loans are better suited for financing smaller expenses. But you can get approved for a long-term personal loan for up to $250,000.

You can always repay the loan early. Most lenders have done away with prepayment penalties, so even if you take out a loan with a long repayment term, you can always make extra payments or just pay off the entire balance early.

Money available for other purposes. Lower monthly payments can leave more room in your budget for everyday expenses, long-term savings or surprise bills. Make sure the payment still fits comfortably alongside your other financial commitments.

More money paid in interest. A longer repayment term means interest has longer to accumulate, so you may pay more overall. The total cost depends on the rate, fees and repayment schedule.

Higher interest rate. Some lenders charge higher rates for longer repayment terms. When shoppig for a loan, compare the annual percentage rate (APR) and total amount you'll repay, not just the advertised interest rate or monthly payment.

It'll take longer to become debt-free. A longer term means you'll carry debt longer, which can make it harder to get approved for other forms or credit and leave you exposed if your finances change.

Fewer options. The number of lenders that offer terms of 84 months or longer is limited, and some only approve long-term financing for certain amounts or purposes, like home improvement projects. With fewer lenders to choose from, you may find it harder to get approved.

All the lender on our list offer loan terms of at least seven years. But there are many other factors to consider.

Your annual percentage rate (APR) measures the total cost of your loan, including interest and certain upfront fees. You'll usually need a credit score of at least 740 to get the best personal loan rates.

Some lenders charge an origination fee to cover the cost of processing and funding your loan. It can be as much as 10% of your loan total, and is usually taken out before you get your money. Some lenders charge originaition fees to all borrowers, others only apply them if you have bad credit. And there are lenders, like Discover, which don't have origination fees

Personal loans come as a lump sum. Make sure that the amount you're requesting is enough to cover what you need.

There are several factors to consider when shopping for a long-term personal loan—and this type of financing may not be right for everyone. Use these tips when comparing long-term personal loans:

Check your credit score before you apply. Lender requirements vary, but borrowers must typically have a FICO score of at least 650 to qualify for a personal loan. When shopping for a personal loan, look up your credit score and review your credit report to better understand how lenders will view your application. Lenders generally reserve the most competitive rates and terms for the most creditworthy applicants.

Evaluate how much you need to borrow. Long-term personal loans are usually for higher amounts than short-term loans—often up to $50,000 or $100,000. For that reason, this type of financing isn't a good fit for consumers who only need to borrow a small amount. Avoid borrowing more than necessary by choosing a lender that offers loan amounts that align with your needs.

Get prequalified and compare offers. Repayment terms for long-term personal loans typically range from 60 to 84 months—five to seven years. The longer the repayment term, the lower the monthly payment, but the more you'll pay in interest over time. Consider how much you can afford to pay each month and find a repayment term that fits your budget. Also determine whether each lender charges prepayment penalties if you pay off your loan early.

Submit your application. Long-term personal loans pose more risk to lenders because there is more time for the borrower to default. Lenders often charge higher interest rates on long-term loans than on short-term loans to compensate for this risk. When choosing a lender, compare available interest rates and other costs like origination fees and late payment charges.

Get approved and receive your funds. Long-term personal loans can take several weeks to process while short-term loans may be available within 24 hours. If you're in a hurry, look for lenders that offer fast approval and funding times.

What is a long-term personal loan?

A long-term personal loan is a personal loan that offers a longer amount of time to repay the balance. Many personal loan lenders give borrowers up to 60 months (five years) to repay their loan, but some offer longer terms (six years or more).

Keep in mind that because personal loans are a form of installment credit, borrowers must repay the balance in fixed, equal monthly amounts for a specified period.

How big of a personal loan can I get?

Personal loans can range in size from $500 to $100,000. Before you apply, consider how much you can afford to make as a monthly payment. Also, keep in mind that your creditworthiness may also determine the size loan you qualify for.

How does a personal loan impact my credit score?

As with any other form of credit, personal loans can impact your credit score positively or negatively. When you apply for a personal loan, a lender launches a hard inquiry on your credit report, which can result in a slight credit score dip at first. However, getting a personal loan can contribute to diversifying your credit mix, which may improve your credit score. Making on-time loan payments consistently can also improve your credit score over time.

How is my personal loan rate decided?

As with any other form of credit, your interest rate for your personal loan is decided based on factors such as your credit score, credit history, income, the loan's size and term. Keep in mind that when taking on a personal loan with a longer repayment term, you may be subject to higher interest rates.

What credit score is needed for a personal loan?

A credit score in the good to excellent range (a FICO score of 670 or higher) is generally needed for a personal loan but some lenders consider fair or poor credit scores as well. Just keep in mind that the lower your credit score, the higher your interest rate may be.

Can you get a personal loan with bad credit?

It's possible to get a personal loan with bad credit but you'll likely be subject to higher interest rates, which will make the loan more expensive.
