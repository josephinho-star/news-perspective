Turkey’s annual inflation rises slightly in October

2026-11-03 13:50

Turkey’s annual consumer price inflation edged up to 29.77 percent in October from 29.73 percent in September, while monthly inflation rose to 2.58 percent from 1.84 percent, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported, citing data released by the Turkish Statistical Institute (TurkStat) on Tuesday.

The independent Inflation Research Group (ENAG), meanwhile, calculated annual inflation at 45.12 percent, with consumer prices rising 2.68 percent month-on-month. The difference between the two annual estimates stood at 15.35 percentage points.

The Consumer Price Index (E-CPI) calculated by the Inflation Research Group (ENAG), increased by 2.68% in October 2026.

According to TurkStat, housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuel costs recorded an annual increase of 40.24 percent, while transportation prices rose 36.39 percent and food and non-alcoholic beverage prices increased 27.73 percent. Food prices alone rose 3.5 percent in October compared with the previous month.

The 12-month average inflation rate, which determines the ceiling for rent increases on residential and commercial properties, stood at 31.23 percent. This means rent increases for contracts renewed in November will be capped at that rate.

The latest figures come amid a longstanding dispute over the reliability of Turkey’s official inflation data. TurkStat has faced criticism from economists, opposition figures and independent researchers who argue that its figures fail to reflect the actual cost-of-living increases experienced by households.

ENAG, established in 2020 by a group of independent academics and economists, has regularly published alternative inflation estimates that are substantially higher than TurkStat’s figures. TurkStat previously filed a lawsuit against the group over its inflation calculations, but an Ankara court rejected the case in 2022.

Meanwhile, Turkey’s central bank kept its year-end interim inflation target at 24 percent while raising its year-end inflation forecast to 28 percent in its third inflation report published in August.

Last Friday, the central bank said higher energy and commodity prices driven by geopolitical tensions continued to pose upside risks to inflation, a week after it cut its policy rate by 100 basis points to 36 percent at its October 22 meeting, its first cut since January.

Turkey relies heavily on imported energy, leaving domestic prices exposed to swings in global oil and gas prices, while the war in the Middle East has pushed up energy and transportation costs across the region.
