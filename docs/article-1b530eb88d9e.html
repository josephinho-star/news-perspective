Norse Atlantic Airways files for bankruptcy and cancels all future flights

2026-11-27 15:40

Norse Atlantic Airways has filed for bankruptcy and cancelled all future flights after surging fuel costs caused by the Iran war ruined its finances.

The Norwegian airline said it was entering insolvency and posted a message on its website telling customers not to come to airports – including passengers stranded away from home. Its collapse came a day after it put flights on hold with a warning that its finances were critical.

The carrier operated low-cost, long-haul flights around the world, including from London Gatwick and the US to Europe and Asia. Flights from Gatwick and Manchester next month to Thailand are among those now scrapped, along with London flights to South Africa and the US.

The Norse board concluded operations were no longer viable after a year in which airlines across the world were hit by spiralling oil prices that doubled the cost of jet fuel as well as dampening consumer demand, after the Iran war began in February.

Rival airlines have predicted surging air fares and the collapse of weaker carriers.

In a statement to the Oslo stock exchange, Eivind Roald, the chief executive of Norse, said: “The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS has made the difficult decision to file for bankruptcy despite extensive efforts from the company and the group to find a solution.

“We are deeply sorry for the impact this has on our passengers, employees and partners.”

He said the owning group and Norse’s UK subsidiary – which was set up to comply with aviation rules after Brexit – were “working to find a solution for the remaining part of the business to continue operations”.

However, all UK flights were similarly cancelled and ticket sales halted.

In a message on its website, Norse advised passengers to contact their travel agents, flight bookers, insurers or credit card companies, but made clear those travelling were on their own and should ask airport information desks.

It said: “We know this will disrupt travel plans and may leave some passengers away from home. Customer service for both airlines is no longer available by phone, email, chat or social media.”

The abrupt end came after Norse announced it was on the brink of collapse on Thursday morning. On Friday morning, it reported that aircraft lessors had served termination notices for the 12 planes in its fleet.

Norse was founded in 2021, attempting to emulate the model of Norwegian Air, whose attempts to forge a new low-cost long-haul market ended with the pandemic.

Norse briefly revived the transatlantic routes using the same plane types, Boeing 787 Dreamliners.

However, it eventually hit similar financial turbulence and this summer told investors it was exploring a sale or merger, embarking on a $50m (£38m) cost-cutting programme, including shedding jobs and its Norwegian HQ.
