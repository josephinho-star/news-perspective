Hillary Clinton picks a foreign politician she believes is now the ‘leader of the free world’

2026-11-17 01:11

Former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton argued during an interview aired Sunday that if any politician qualifies as the current "leader of the free world," it would be Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney.

MS NOW host Rachel Maddow interviewed Clinton at the Temple Emanu-El Streicker Center in New York City and asked, "Do you think that there is a leader of the free world right now?"

"Maybe Mark Carney," Clinton said.

"That’s what I was going to ask," Maddow said. "He’s certainly articulating a vision of the free countries of the world and of — of the liberty-loving nations of the world that is about being stronger together."

The relationship between the United States and Canada has been strained following President Donald Trump's suggestions that Canada become the 51st U.S. state and his decision to impose tariffs on Canadian goods.

Clinton also argued that while some individual European leaders stood out, the continent as a whole was too disorganized.

"And, you know, there are some good individual European leaders, but Europe has got to get itself organized so that, you know, the sum of the parts adds up to some kind of a whole and be able to do a better job at making some tough decisions, particularly with respect to Ukraine," she said. "They need to release the $250 million of Russian assets that are frozen in those Belgian banks and use it to defend Ukraine, just for starters."

"So if they can begin to make some collective decisions, then I think you’ll see other leaders emerge," Clinton continued. "But there’s going to be, as you know, Rachel, some very consequential elections in Europe this year, like the presidency of France. And if a populist pro-Putin party emerges with a leader who is very close to Putin and very beholden to Putin, Marine Le Pen, becomes the president of France, then we have new problems that we’re going to have to deal with."

Earlier in the interview, Clinton argued that Trump had done "immeasurable damage" to America’s standing on the world stage with foreign policy that she described as "dangerous and incoherent.’

She also accused Trump of catering to "strongmen leaders."

"We are, unfortunately, seeing someone who admires strongmen leaders, thinks he is one, and believes in the old-fashioned concept of spheres of power," Clinton said. "And honestly, I don’t think it’s exaggerating to say that he’s perfectly fine with Putin doing whatever he wants with Ukraine and the rest of Europe."

"He’s perfectly fine with Xi doing whatever he wants with Asia, as long as he can dominate the Western Hemisphere, and that all of them are some kind of triumvirate that is going to decide what the rest of us are capable of doing and what kind of society we will live under," she added.

White House spokesman Davis Ingle told Fox News Digital, "President Trump is creating good-paying jobs, securing trillions of dollars in investments, and positioning our country to dominate the remainder of the 21st century. Meanwhile, Crooked Hillary Clinton is still whining about her spectacular 2016 election defeat. It’s time for Hillary to move on."

Morgan Murphy, a former White House official and former Pentagon press secretary, told Fox News Digital in response to Clinton's comments, "Canada's economy is chasing Mississippi's, and its armed forces couldn't scare the Salvation Army. It’s obvious Hillary still can’t admit the obvious: she lost [to] Trump, and he’s still the leader of the free world."

Theo Wold, a former White House deputy assistant for domestic policy under Trump, also criticized Clinton's comments.

"Canada’s PM Carney is nothing close to being the leader of the free world," Wold told Fox News Digital. "The Canadian economy is contracting; Canadian culture is at its weakest point as a consequence of mass immigration and proliferating crime and budget problems; separatist movements across the Canadian federation are gaining strength; and most of all, to the extent Canada projects power at all, it does so only from within the sphere of U.S. power projection and military might in the Western Hemisphere, making Canada at best a younger sibling piggybacking on America’s power projection around the world."

"Carney, pretending to be 2026’s Marshall Tito, used several high-profile speeches to criticize the America First movement and pitch a small state ‘non-aligned movement,’" he added. "There are no takers for this idiotic gambit outside of EU cultural elites in the dying capitals of Europe."
