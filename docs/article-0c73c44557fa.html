Metcalf denies trying to poke DeJean in eye; NFL won't suspend Steelers WR

2026-11-23 23:08

PITTSBURGH -- Steelers wide receiver DK Metcalf said Monday that he "can't remember" what occurred in the interactions with Philadelphia Eagles cornerback Cooper DeJean that led to Metcalf's disqualification in Sunday's loss.

Speaking for the first time since his ejection, Metcalf said he didn't try to poke DeJean in the eye.

"I was just telling him, stop pushing me," Metcalf said. "That's all I said."

Metcalf will not be suspended, the NFL said Monday, but the league is reviewing his actions from Sunday's game and he could be fined.

After the game, coach Mike McCarthy said officials told him Metcalf was ejected for poking DeJean in the eye.

Footage from the broadcast also captured Metcalf appearing to reach into DeJean's face mask after a third-quarter play. DeJean, though, said Metcalf "just missed." Metcalf was also flagged for unnecessary roughness earlier in the third quarter when he continued to block DeJean long after the play ended.

Asked if he thought DeJean provoked him, Metcalf said, "You got to ask him."

McCarthy said he had a "productive" conversation with Metcalf on Monday to address Sunday's incident.

"We talked through everything that happened and how we're looking to move forward," McCarthy said in his weekly news conference. "And obviously the importance of always being available for your teammates, being the responsibility of the 48 and obviously the actual process and everything that went on.

"I'm very comfortable with that conversation."

McCarthy declined to share if the team was going to levy any internal punishment on the receiver.

"Like anything, life's about learning situations," McCarthy said. "I do believe in second chances, so I will judge him based on my personal interaction with him. I did appreciate the way he communicated today."

At the time of his ejection, Metcalf had just one catch for 2 yards on four targets. So far this season, the Steelers' top receiver has 36 receptions for 434 yards and two touchdowns. He's also the only qualified receiver in the NFL this season catching less than 50% of his targets.

Metcalf, who said he exited the locker room in Philadelphia before his team was even off the field because he wanted to avoid the media, has been disqualified three times in his career. Speaking with reporters Monday, he didn't take accountability for his pattern of behavior.

"I think you got to ask the people that seemingly do that to me," Metcalf said when asked why he has struggled to maintain his composure at times in his career.

Though DeJean said after the game that he wasn't aware beforehand of Metcalf's history of being ejected, Metcalf said Monday that he "wouldn't be surprised" if the Eagles had a plan to agitate him.

He later added, "San Fran has done it my whole career when I was in Seattle, so it was nothing new to me." Asked if he wished he had done anything differently in Sunday's game, Metcalf said, "I just wish I would've been there for my team."

When asked if he'd heard anything about internal discipline from the team, Metcalf said, "You got to ask [general manager] Omar [Khan] that or anybody else other than DK."

Metcalf, who signed a $132 million, four-year extension last year after being acquired by the Steelers in a trade with the Seattle Seahawks, was suspended by the NFL for the final two games of the 2025 season for an altercation with a Detroit Lions fan. Metcalf also tried to poke Green Bay Packers linebacker Quay Walker in the eye in November 2025. He was flagged for unsportsmanlike conduct in the moment and fined $12,172 for fighting.

Since being drafted in 2019, Metcalf has been flagged for 56 total penalties. That's the seventh most in the league in that span and most among all players who aren't offensive tackles.
