Shams Charania reveals why Kawhi Leonard avoided suspension after Clippers probe

2026-09-15 17:47

Kawhi Leonard left Los Angeles with his contract intact and a path back to Toronto. The Clippers were left with a $30 million fine, five forfeited first-round picks and a damaged future.

ESPN insider Shams Charania said the imbalance was less about the NBA viewing Leonard as innocent than the evidence it found.

“They needed every form of evidence to void Kawhi Leonard’s contract or suspend him,” Charania said, in remarks edited for clarity. “Clearly, the league didn’t have it.”

“You have to have a stone-cold case,” he added. “Otherwise you’re dealing with a ton of real litigation and real arbitration through the NBPA.”

The investigation found that Leonard violated circumvention rules through the conduct of former representative Dennis Robertson, who acted on his behalf.

That was sufficient to order Leonard to pay $700,000, but not enough to risk an arbitration fight by suspending him or canceling his contract, explaining why he escaped the harshest penalties.

The contrast remains difficult to ignore. Steve Ballmer received a one-year suspension, several Clippers executives were sidelined and the franchise lost the draft capital it needs to recover from the Leonard era.

That cost began before the investigation. Los Angeles surrendered Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, Danilo Gallinari, five first-round picks and two swaps in the Paul George trade after Leonard made adding another star a condition of joining the Clippers in 2019.

The gamble produced one Western Conference finals appearance and no championships. Leonard remained exceptional when available, but injuries repeatedly removed him from postseason runs.

He now returns to the franchise he led to the 2019 title, while the Clippers begin another reinvention without control of five future first-rounders.

Toronto at least supplied a lifeline. Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks, one swap and two second-rounders give Los Angeles talent and flexibility. They do not erase the damage.

The Clippers made their own choices and earned their punishment. Leonard nevertheless received the benefits, avoided the harshest consequences and departed before the bill came due.

Charania explained why: The NBA did not find enough evidence to go further. The condition of the Clippers explains why that answer remains unsatisfying.
