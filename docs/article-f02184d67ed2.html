Locals of Michigan city with largest Muslim population in US call El-Sayed election victory 'needed' change

2026-11-20 23:51

Dearborn, Michigan, locals praised Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed’s election to the U.S. Senate, citing that the Great Lakes State "needed a change."

"I feel good because it's like we needed a change in Michigan… it's time for a change, it's time for a good change," said a Dearborn local who did not disclose his name to Fox News Digital. The city has the largest proportion of Muslims in the United States, at roughly half the population.

Jamal Jawad, owner of The Custard Company, was in the midst of opening his new shawarma restaurant called "Toum" in West Dearborn when he told Fox News Digital that the election was good for those who wanted affordable healthcare.

"It was good; Democrat. It was good for what people needed: money out of politics – into your pocket. Healthcare for all is good," Jawad said.

Their comments came after Dearborn Mayor Abdullah Hammoud told Fox News Digital that El-Sayed’s Nov. 3 victory over former Republican Rep. Mike Rogers in Michigan's high-stakes race was "monumental."

"It was monumental, and it's [truly] an American story where the brightest and best of us was elevated to one of the highest offices in the world — a member of the United States Senate — and where the people of Michigan elected an individual regardless of the direction in which he prays, but rather based on the direction in which he wishes to lead," Hammoud told Fox News Digital.

With the win, El-Sayed, a progressive Democrat and former Detroit health director, became the first Muslim American elected to the U.S. Senate.

"And so when he's hitting those three areas, he's dead on it… I think we are going to be in a good space with his support, and the diversity is good. We need diversity because we need to learn about people's culture as we're in a melting pot," she added.

Immigrants from Lebanon, Iraq and Syria began settling in the Detroit area in the early 20th century, drawn in part by growing demand for labor in the auto industry.

Dearborn residents embraced Muslim and Arab immigration to Michigan and rejected warnings about the area's changing demographics.

"Coming from somebody who served four years in the Army, I think it could be a good thing… there's a lot of people here who've served too," Ali Seif told Fox News Digital.

"I think Dearborn is one of those cities that looks kind of intimidating at first from the outside point of view. If you actually come in and see the city for itself, it's not that bad. You can grab a cup of coffee with your friends, grab some food. It's like any other city, but we just pray differently," Seif told Fox News Digital.

The nearby city of Warren, Michigan, another Detroit suburb, has also experienced demographic changes. The sale of a local church for conversion into the Al Ihsaan Islamic Center reflects one such change.

In Warren, several residents interviewed by Fox News Digital said they felt like they're being pushed out of their own hometown as Muslims immigrate to the area, expressing concerns about being the "next Dearborn."

Warren, Michigan's third-largest city after Detroit and Grand Rapids, grew rapidly during the post-World War II auto manufacturing boom, as war veterans moved to the area to work for Ford and General Motors. The blue-collar, white-picket-fence community incorporated as a city in 1957.

"I hear an opportunity for more Arabs to expand because most of the Arab population of the United States, which is not a big population, is mostly in Dearborn, Michigan, and I feel like they're kind of spreading their wings and expanding more by going to Ohio or Warren," Adam Seif told Fox News Digital.
