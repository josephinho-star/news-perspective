Go bigger with the best lab-grown diamond engagement ring and jewelry brands

2026-09-25 14:39

In the ever-evolving landscape of the diamond industry, a remarkable transformation is underway, ushering in a new era of sustainability, affordability, and ethical consciousness. At the forefront of this revolution stand lab-grown diamonds, a groundbreaking alternative to their natural counterparts.

Shoppers today are encountering a paradigm shift as they explore these diamonds, navigating a landscape defined by innovation and a departure from conventional norms. This change can be traced to the innovative production processes behind lab-grown diamonds.

Unlike traditional diamonds, these are born not from the depths of the Earth but within sophisticated machines that replicate the high-temperature, high-pressure conditions required for diamond formation. The result is a remarkably condensed growth period, ranging from as little as six weeks to at most, four months. This accelerated timeline aligns with the modern shopper’s desire for efficiency without compromising on quality.

After industry expert input and hours of researching what makes a lab-grown diamond worth the more modest investment, we selected our picks for the best lab-grown diamond brands you can shop right now.

What are the pros and cons of lab-grown diamonds?

Lab-grown diamond pricing — Are they cheaper?

Lab-grown diamonds are gaining popularity not just for their ethical and environmental benefits but also for their generally more affordable pricing compared to natural diamonds. This affordability is driven by several factors reshaping the diamond industry.

One significant contributor to the cost-effectiveness of lab-grown diamonds is the streamlined production process. Creating diamonds in a controlled environment proves more resource-efficient and less complex than traditional diamond mining, translating to reduced overall costs.

Environmental sustainability is a key consideration. Lab-grown diamonds, formed in controlled conditions, require less land disruption and have a smaller carbon footprint than their natural counterparts, appealing to environmentally conscious consumers.

Ethical considerations also come into play, as lab-grown diamonds eliminate concerns related to unethical mining practices and conflict diamonds. This ethical appeal aligns with the values of consumers seeking responsible sourcing.

Efficiency in the supply chain further impacts pricing. The streamlined process of lab-grown diamonds involves fewer intermediaries, leading to cost savings for producers and consumers alike.

Changing consumer perceptions contribute to the affordability of lab-grown diamonds. Increased awareness and growing societal acceptance drive demand, fostering a competitive pricing environment.

Are lab-grown diamonds ethical?

“There are so many loopholes in the Kimberly Process (KP),” Jhaveri said about the process meant to reduce the flow of natural conflict diamonds.

However, according to the Brilliant Earth website, another lab-grown diamond brand, the KP has limitations:

“[It’s] strict, narrow definition of what constitutes a diamond as sourced in conflict (only gems with profits used to fund wars against legitimate governments) can turn a blind eye to the blatant human rights abuses, violence, exploitation, and environmental degradation that stems from diamond-rich countries who are already active participants of the Kimberly process.”

Lab-grown diamonds evade this process entirely just by being grown in a lab rather than the Earth. No mining, no blood diamonds, no human lives in danger — what is yielded from lab-grown diamonds is just, simply, a diamond.

Traditionally, customers preferred seeing mined diamonds in person before purchasing, but the rise of online businesses has altered this dynamic. With technology being what it is and the ability to see color for what it is online today, people don’t need to see their lab-grown diamonds in person. In essence, the rise of lab-grown diamonds, as articulated by Jhaveri, is reshaping the diamond market, offering shoppers an opportunity to make choices aligned with their values, all while enjoying the beauty and symbolism of this timeless gem.

Why do people choose lab-grown diamonds?

The appeal of lab-grown diamonds extends beyond their eco-friendly origins. These gems, certified by reputable institutions such as GIA and IGI, adhere to the same rigorous criteria as natural diamonds. Jhaveri underlines this point, having stated, “There is no difference between how the GIA and IGI certify natural and lab-grown diamonds — it’s the exact same criteria.”

Discussing the qualities of a great lab-grown diamond, Daga emphasized the pursuit of perfection. She mentioned that customers today seek near-perfect grades, such as DIF (decorlored diamond, internally flawless) or F (moving down two grades in color) on the quality scale. The appeal lies in getting precisely what one desires at little to no ecological cost. The appeal for larger gemstones has also increased, according to Daga, thanks to the proliferation of Zoom calls and their becoming a regular part of many people’s lives.

As with any transformative innovation, shoppers are confronted with considerations regarding the resale value of lab-grown diamonds. Because one can get a lab gemstone triple the size of a natural one for the same price, resale value is less of a concern when purchasing today. “What people want is perfection,” Daga exclaimed. “And with lab-grown gemstones, they can get that.”

How are lab-grown diamonds made?

In sharing insights into the unique processes, Jhaveri likened it to making bread. “You know how you make bread out of yeast? You need that starter. A diamond, natural or lab-grown, comes from a diamond seed, which can also be lab-grown,” she says.

Lab-grown diamonds are created through two main processes: High Pressure-High Temperature (HPHT) and Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD). Both methods mimic the conditions under which natural diamonds are formed deep within the Earth.

High Pressure-High Temperature (HPHT)

The HPHT process starts with a small diamond seed, serving as the foundation for the growth of the lab-grown diamond. Subjected to controlled conditions, the diamond seed faces extreme pressure (5-6 GPa) and high temperature (approximately 1,500 degrees Celsius). This simulated environment mirrors the natural diamond-forming process that unfolds in the Earth’s mantle. Carbon atoms in a gaseous state are introduced, adhering to the diamond seed, facilitating the growth of the crystal layer by layer. This meticulous process typically spans several weeks to a few months, depending on the desired size of the diamond.

Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD)

In the CVD process, a gas mixture containing carbon is introduced into a vacuum chamber. Commonly used gases, such as methane, become ionized through methods like microwaves or lasers, breaking down the carbon molecules into individual atoms. These ionized carbon atoms settle on a substrate, often a flat diamond seed or another material, and crystallize to form a diamond layer. Notably, the CVD method allows for more precise control over the diamond’s growth and can result in a shorter production time compared to HPHT.

Following their creation, these diamonds undergo the standard processes of cutting, polishing, and grading, adhering to the same rigorous standards applied to their natural counterparts. Technological advancements have further broadened the possibilities, enabling the production of lab-grown diamonds in a variety of colors, sizes, and qualities, enhancing their versatility within the jewelry market.
