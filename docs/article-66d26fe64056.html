Former Takasbank chief among 3 jailed in Turkey’s investment fund scandal

2026-11-11 13:13

An İstanbul court on Wednesday jailed pending trial Yunus Arıncı, former chief of Takasbank, Turkey’s securities clearing and settlement bank, and two other financial executives in the country’s investment fund investigation, bringing the number of people in pretrial detention to 123, the ANKA news agency reported.

The scandal concerns suspected stock market manipulation involving investment funds that attracted nearly half a million investors. The crisis erupted in September when some funds could not meet withdrawal requests, prompting regulators to order the liquidation of 131 funds managed by seven asset management companies.

Arıncı was jailed along with Ömer Murat Gülkan, chairman of OMG Capital Advisors, and Ali Paslı, a founding partner of Adil Katılım Bankası.

Takasbank handles the exchange of securities and payments arising from trades on Borsa İstanbul and operates TEFAS, the electronic platform through which investors buy and sell investment funds. These functions make it a key part of Turkey’s financial market infrastructure.

Four other suspects were put under house arrest: Bulls Yatırım chairman Kemal Akkaya, Ruzy Madencilik chairman Nureddin Çevik, Lydia Holding chairman Enver Çevik and Maygold Otomotiv chairman Ayhan Yazkan.

The seven appeared before a judge after questioning by prosecutors from the İstanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office’s Terrorism Financing and Money Laundering Investigation Bureau.

Arıncı had previously been questioned as a suspect on November 1 and released under a travel ban and a requirement to report regularly to the police. He subsequently resigned from Takasbank and was detained again on November 7. He also formerly headed the State Supervisory Council, an inspection body attached to the presidency.

The latest arrests follow Monday’s court decision to jail Muhammet Erişen, deputy chief of the Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK), and Adil Katılım Bankası CEO Murat Yönaç in the same investigation.

The funds under scrutiny had accumulated more than $20 billion in assets in just three years, Reuters reported. Investors, including politicians, former officials and celebrities, sought high returns to protect their savings against inflation and a weakening lira.

Tera and Pusula are among the investment groups at the center of the scandal. Tera accounts for most of the assets in the funds being liquidated, making it central to efforts to repay investors. Both groups’ chairmen were jailed pending trial in September.

Investigators suspect funds were used to drive up share prices, increasing the apparent value of their holdings and misleading investors.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek said earlier this month that investigators had uncovered an alleged criminal organization extending into the senior financial bureaucracy, accusing participants of using bribery to undermine oversight.

The scandal has also prompted departures from senior regulatory posts. Capital Markets Board chairman Mahmut Sütçü submitted his resignation in October, citing health concerns.

On Wednesday BDDK chairman Şahap Kavcıoğlu said he had asked President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan to relieve him of his duties following allegations concerning himself and the agency. Kavcıoğlu has denied taking bribes or having investments in funds under investigation.
