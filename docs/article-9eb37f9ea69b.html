Pope Leo XIV to undergo lung surgery this week

2026-10-11 12:54

Pope Leo XIV announced Sunday that he would have surgery this week to remove a "localized nodule" in his lung.

Leo said the nodule was found at a "very early stage" with doctors recommending surgery.

"Following their advice, I have therefore decided to undergo an operation to remove it," Leo said during his weekly Sunday noon blessing in St. Peter's Square.

The surgery is due to occur at Rome's Gemelli Hospital, which is the primary location for popes to receive medical attention.

Leo has had no known previous health problems.

"I thank you for your spiritual closeness and entrust myself to your prayers," Leo said to the crowds on Sunday.

According to the American Cancer Society, a lung nodule is a small abnormal area, less than or equal to 3 cm, found during a CT scan of the chest or abdomen. These tests are often conducted as routine screenings for "trauma, heart disease, non-cancer related lung disease, or lung infection."

Most lung nodules are benign. The American Cancer Society said these nodules are "more often the result of old infections, scar tissue, or other causes" and that tests are needed to confirm it is not cancerous. He later posted the message about his surgery on social media.

The Vatican did not disclose whether the nodule was cancerous.

Vatican spokesperson Matteo Bruni said in a statement that "the operation is scheduled for the first days of this week and the estimated post-operative recovery time will be about one week."

Leo's next major international trip is scheduled for early November to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru. It's unclear if the surgery would affect his travels.

During his papacy, Leo has been outspoken about peace throughout the world. He spent part of his Sunday address renewing calls for peace in Ukraine.

"I make a heartfelt appeal to the parties to the conflict to allow the safe transport of grain across the Black Sea," Leo said. "The daily bread of millions of people cannot be held hostage by war."

On Saturday, the Pope also called for the death penalty to be abolished, saying on X that it is "inadmissible because it is an attack on the inviolability and dignity of the person."

"The common good can be safeguarded and the requirements of justice can be met without recourse to capital punishment: effective systems of detention have been developed that protect citizens while at the same time do not completely deprive those who are guilty of the possibility of redemption," Leo said.
