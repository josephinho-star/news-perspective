Israel, Greece sign €3 billion air defense deal amid tensions with Turkey

2026-08-31 14:11

Israel and Greece have signed a €3 billion ($3.5 billion) agreement to build a multilayered air defense system for Greece, the largest defense deal between the two countries, as they deepen military cooperation amid tensions with Turkey.

The agreement for the “Achilles Shield” was signed in Tel Aviv on Monday by Israeli Defense Ministry Director General Amir Baram and his Greek counterpart, Ioannis Bouras, the two countries’ defense ministries announced.

The system will combine defenses against ballistic missiles, aircraft and drones under a national command-and-control network.

The Israeli Defense Ministry described the agreement as the largest defense export deal in the history of Israel-Greece relations and one of the largest ever signed by Israel.

It said the system would draw entirely on Israeli technology and include David’s Sling and SPYDER interceptors made by Rafael Advanced Defense Systems, the BARAK MX system manufactured by Israel Aerospace Industries and air surveillance radars produced by IAI subsidiary ELTA Systems.

A separate €26 million agreement covers Rafael’s Drone Dome counter-drone systems for the protection of strategic sites.

The Greek Defense Ministry put the total budget for the program at €3.035 billion. It said 19 Greek companies would participate, accounting for more than 25 percent of the program, or €750 million, and that a production base with export potential would be established in Greece.

Greek Defense Minister Nikos Dendias said recent advances in ballistic missiles, drones, satellite communications, cyberwarfare and hybrid threats had rendered existing defense doctrines obsolete.

“Greece is the first country in the European Union to adopt a similar system,” he said.

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said the two countries shared strategic interests and regional challenges.

“At a time when actors with hegemonic ambitions are seeking to expand their influence and undermine stability in the region, Israel and Greece will continue to deepen their defense and strategic cooperation,” Katz said.

Although he did not name Turkey, the remarks appeared to allude to Ankara, whose relations with both Israel and Greece have been marked by longstanding tensions.

Turkey and Greece, both NATO members, have for decades been at odds over territorial waters, airspace, maritime boundaries in the eastern Mediterranean and the divided island of Cyprus. Although the two neighbors have recently sought to reduce tensions, Greece has continued an extensive military modernization program.

Relations between Turkey and Israel have also deteriorated sharply over Israel’s war in Gaza and the two countries’ competing interests in Syria.

On August 18 Israel struck the Abu Duhur airbase in northwestern Syria, saying the attack was intended to prevent Turkish troops from deploying there. Eight strikes damaged the runway but caused no casualties, according to Syrian state media.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s office said Syria had been on the verge of violating an agreed security “status quo” by allowing a Turkish military deployment at the base. Turkey condemned the strikes as a violation of international law.

Israel’s largest defense export agreement remains its sale of the Arrow 3 anti-ballistic missile system to Germany. The deal, first signed in 2023, was expanded in December to a total value of $6.5 billion.

Agence France-Presse, with reporting from Turkish Minute
