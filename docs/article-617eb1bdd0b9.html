France's Macron vows to hold accountable those who 'sought to bring down the Republic'

2026-10-18 19:16

French President Emmanuel Macron on Sunday voiced the public's "anger" and "shame" over rioting and looting during nationwide protests against rising living costs and poor conditions in schools. At least 1,800 people have been arrested.

"Those who looted will have to pay back for the damage, and those who sought to bring down the Republic will be held accountable," Macron said in a televised address to the nation.

He blamed "unrestrained violence" on "insurrectionist elements from radical far-left groups, joined by numerous criminals."

Macron has earlier held an emergency meeting with senior government officials, a day after masked groups set fires and attacked public buildings, including city halls, in several cities. He said he had instructed prefects to work with mayors to protect public buildings and businesses in the coming weeks, including by banning protests when necessary.

Flames engulfed the 19th-century City Hall in Limoges, central France, causing its roof to collapse and nearly destroying the building.

Hundreds of protesters gathered again on Sunday at the Place de la République in Paris amid a heavy police presence. Officers used tear gas as tensions flared sporadically in the neighborhood, with small groups of people trying to set bicycles and trash bins on fire.

"The right to protest is a fundamental right. Deliberately and collectively sowing disorder through violence is not," Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez wrote on X, citing the looting of hundreds of businesses in cities including Paris, Marseille, Lyon and Bordeaux.

About 550,000 people took part in protests across France, according to authorities, while organizers put the figure at about 800,000. Demonstrators answered calls from a labor union, student organizations and activists hoping to revive the "yellow vest" movement, which emerged in 2018 to protest social injustice.

Mass protests in France are often marred by violence on the fringes of otherwise peaceful marches, leading to clashes with police. The arrest tally reported Sunday approached the nearly 2,000 recorded nationwide during a single day of yellow vest protests in December 2018.

One person died of a heart attack, Paris police said in a statement, without providing details. The Paris prosecutor's office said it was investigating.

Dislodged cobblestones, shattered storefronts and broken glass littered the area around Paris' Place de la République on Sunday. Looting and damage were also reported in other tourist and shopping districts.

Paris police said they arrested 855 people in the capital region. The city's prosecutor's office said police kept 557 in custody.

"I am actually stunned by the level of destruction and violence that was expressed," said government employee Marie Peyreigne, 57, wondering what had led to the mayhem.

Parisians inspecting the damage photographed the partially dismantled base of the square's central monument, a statue of Marianne, a symbol of France.

Jean Foissy, 74, said he wanted to join the protest but hit "a wall of riot police and fires everywhere" and went home.

"I was very scared," he said. "I've been a Parisian since I was born, so when I got here, it almost made me want to cry because I find it so bad, it hurts me."

Margaux Eude, 26, said she supported the protest movement but "didn't think it would escalate like this."

"It's the troublemakers who came, and it breaks my heart because it delegitimizes the movement," she said.

Political leaders across the spectrum sought to parlay the fallout from Saturday's riots into political capital, ahead of next spring's presidential election.

Far-right leader and presidential hopeful Marine Le Pen criticized Macron's centrist government as "exhausted, disoriented and seemingly incapable of doing anything other than commenting on its own powerlessness" in a video she posted on social media Sunday.

Far-left leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon, for his part, accused Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu of "playing with fire," alleging that "by banking on degradation, you have only succeeded in uniting all youths and workers' unions."

Mélenchon, who joined the Paris march Saturday, insisted, "My message is clear: those who share our fight have no business in looting or destruction."

U.S. President Donald Trump posted "France is burning!" on his Truth Social platform, apparently linking the unrest to "the immigration of people from an entirely different world."

"Close your Borders and take control, before it is too late!" he wrote.

French authorities and police have not linked immigrants to the rioting and have repeatedly attributed some of the violence to "radical far-left groups" and other people who joined to loot.

"The scale of the damage is considerable and unprecedented," Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire wrote in an open letter. "Paris is a city of freedoms, debate and demonstrations. It must remain so. But it must also be a city where everyone can work and run a business safely."

Police said groups of hundreds of people formed unauthorized marches across central Paris on Saturday. "By late afternoon, a new phase began with the arrival of large numbers of people whose sole aim was to steal and loot, taking advantage of the unrest," police said.

The mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon, the three largest cities, requested a meeting with the prime minister in the coming days "given the extent of the damage and the seriousness of the situation."

In the southern port city of Marseille, some protesters briefly forced their way into the lobby of City Hall and a group of people wearing hoods and masks damaged a police station. In Lyon, a fire broke out inside a tourist office.

In Limoges, a group of "radical troublemakers" broke into City Hall while there were elected officials, employees, security personnel and members of the public inside, Maurice Barate, prefect of the Haute-Vienne region, said. "Despite the presence of so many people, they deliberately set the building on fire. It is an extremely serious and unspeakable act."

In eastern France, the Mulhouse prosecutor's office said an investigation has been opened after a 17-year-old girl was injured during the protests and lost an eye. On Saturday, another investigation was opened after a young protester lost a hand in Saint-Étienne.

The injuries come as police have drawn sharp scrutiny over their use of grenades and other riot-control weapons during recent demonstrations.
