Northern Cyprus ferry death toll rises to 10, with 18 still missing

2026-09-04 08:01

The death toll from a ferry disaster off northern Cyprus rose to 10 on Thursday after rescuers recovered another body, while 18 people remained missing.

The Filo Jet was carrying 267 passengers and crew when it capsized shortly after leaving the port of Kyrenia on the northern coast of the divided island around midday on Sunday.

Officials now say 239 people were rescued. Eight bodies were initially recovered, with another found on Wednesday and a 10th on Thursday.

Turkey’s Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz announced the latest recovery during a condolence visit to the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (KKTC), which is recognized only by Ankara.

“The body of one more person has been recovered and brought to the surface. The number of those who have died has risen to 10,” Yılmaz told a news conference.

Turkish Cypriot Prime Minister Ünal Üstel, who appeared alongside him, said Wednesday that officials believed those still missing had gone down with the ferry, which lies on the seabed at a depth of 514 meters (1,686 feet).

Yılmaz said more bodies were likely to be recovered in the coming days.

The Turkish naval vessels TCG Işın and TCG Alemdar, equipped with remotely operated underwater vehicles, have been conducting round-the-clock searches of the wreck site since Wednesday.

Before the two latest bodies were recovered, officials said all eight confirmed victims were Turkish nationals. Of the 20 people then listed as missing, 18 were Turkish nationals and two were residents of northern Cyprus.

Officials have not disclosed the nationalities of the two people whose bodies were recovered Wednesday and Thursday.

Investigators have so far been unable to retrieve the ferry’s voyage data recorder, or “black box,” which could provide a clearer indication of what caused the vessel to capsize.

At a court hearing in Kyrenia, the ferry’s second captain said the captain had delayed returning to port after the vessel began taking on water, according to local media.

Turkish survivor Sadık Sarıbuğa also described delays in turning back after it became clear that the ferry was in trouble about 15 to 20 minutes into the voyage.

“At first, the ferry began to sway and take on water. The seats came loose and the passengers told the crew to turn back. A man came over and said, ‘This happens all the time, nothing will happen,’” he told Turkey’s state-run Anadolu news agency.

His account echoed testimony from other survivors who said crew members had initially insisted there was no danger.

When conditions worsened, “they tried to turn around and that’s when the ship capsized,” Sarıbuğa said, adding that he and other passengers reached the upper deck by breaking a window.

The captain and eight other suspects were remanded in custody Monday and returned to court Thursday.

Police investigators told the court that the disaster could have been prevented if the captain had reduced speed and carefully returned to port after the ferry began taking on water.

The court ordered all nine suspects, who face charges of causing death through negligence, to remain in custody for another eight days as the investigation continues.
