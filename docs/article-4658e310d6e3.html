Türkiye targets 2027 drilling as it launches Pakistan offshore surveys

2026-11-20 10:54

Türkiye's Oruç Reis seismic research vessel will begin exploration for hydrocarbon resources in offshore fields in Pakistan, Energy and Natural Resources Minister Alparslan Bayraktar said Friday, adding that drilling was expected to start in 2027.

Last year, Türkiye state oil company Turkish Petroleum Corporation (TPAO) signed hydrocarbon exploration and production agreements with Pakistani companies including Mari Energies, Fatima, OGDCL, PPL, Prime and GHDL for three offshore and two onshore fields in Pakistan.

Bayraktar, who attended a ceremony to welcome the Oruç Reis at Karachi Port alongside Pakistani Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar and Petroleum Minister Ali Pervaiz Malik, said on the social media platform X that the vessel would carry out two- and three-dimensional seismic survey operations in the Indus and Makran basins to "uncover the potential there."

The vessel will spend about two months collecting data across 670 square kilometers (about 259 square miles), Bayraktar said.

"Based on the data we collect, we aim to begin drilling activity next year in the identified areas," he noted.

Dar said the survey would "deepen our understanding of the geological structures beneath the seabed and provide a stronger basis for Pakistan's future exploration."

He said that the economic activity would add "new strategic depth" between the two countries.

Bayraktar described it as a "strong turning point" in energy cooperation between the two countries, noting that it comes at a time of turbulence in global oil and gas markets.

"We are two nations with a history built on shared values and deep brotherhood," he stressed. He said the partnership is not just on paper. Licensing has been completed for five blocks, three offshore and two onshore, and field activity has begun, he said.

Last month, Bayraktar said the planned operations off Pakistan had been delayed by about six months due to the war in Iran.

The conflict, which began with U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran in February, has disrupted global energy markets and heightened the urgency of securing new oil and gas supplies.

At a time when the oil and gas markets are going through "a historic supply shock," Bayraktar said any finds would "directly contribute to our countries' energy supply security."

In October 2024, Türkiye began searching for oil and natural gas off the Somali coast under an agreement that allows it to drill in three areas, each measuring roughly 5,000 square kilometers.

Similar exploration agreements for oil and gas, as well as mining, were signed between Türkiye and Niger in July and October of that year.

Bayraktar said Türkiye wants to repeat its exploration and production success at home in Pakistan.

He recalled that in 2020, in the middle of the COVID-19 pandemic, Türkiye used the same vessels to discover the largest natural gas reserve in the Black Sea. He said it was also the largest offshore gas discovery in the world that year.

Those fields now produce about 10 million cubic meters of gas a day and meet the needs of about 4 million households, he said. Output is to reach about 40 million cubic meters a day by 2028, supplying 16 million households.

"We are putting the experience and engineering capability we gained in these activities at the service of our Pakistani brothers," he said.

The Energy and Natural Resources Ministry, meanwhile, also said two joint venture agreements were signed between MTAIC and Pakistani mining companies.

MTAIC is a venture of two Turkish state mining bodies, the General Directorate of Mineral Research and Exploration, known as MTA, and Eti Maden. Its Pakistani partners are Mari Energies Minerals and the Pakistan Mineral Development Corporation.

Under the agreements, MTAIC will start exploration at three sites in Pakistan: two for copper and gold and one for antimony.

MTA, MTAIC and the Geological Survey of Pakistan also signed a three-way memorandum of understanding to increase technical knowledge sharing in geology and mineral exploration and to develop joint projects.
