Cam Schlittler needed help this time — and the Yankees utterly failed him

2026-10-06 04:02

ST. PETERSBURG, Fla. — There might not be a more confident pitcher in the majors than Cam Schlittler, who does not doubt himself and did not doubt himself before Game 2.

Sure, he knew that he was about to face a lineup that excels at placing bat on ball. But he also knew he was tied for the third-most strikeouts in the majors this season.

Schlittler’s prediction was off the mark because Tampa Bay’s bats were constantly on the mark.

The Yankees ace of aces was not to blame for Monday’s 5-2 loss to the Rays in the division series at Tropicana Field, but he also was not able to play hero as he had in Game 1 of the wild-card series against the Red Sox.

In the game’s biggest moments, Schlittler needed the help of his defense, which let him down repeatedly.

After dominating Boston through 6 ¹/₃ scoreless, 10-strikeout innings, Schlittler only punched out two Rays while allowing four runs (two earned) on six hits and a walk in 4 ¹/₃ innings.

It started immediately, with the aggressive Rays swinging early in counts and making consistent, often loud contact in the first inning. Errors by Ryan McMahon (on a chopper) and Jazz Chisholm Jr. (an ill-advised throw to first after recording an out at second) scored one run, but Schlittler did well to limit the damage.

The game changed, though, in a four-run fifth inning during which the flame-throwing righty needed to throw pitches by Rays bats and could not.

A single by Jonathan Aranda (played conservatively by Spencer Jones, who might have had a chance at a diving catch) and a smashed double from Junior Caminero put runners on second and third without an out in a game that was then tied, 1-1. Schlittler needed one of those strikeouts. Unlike the Rays, he whiffed.

Liam Hicks managed to make contact — the softest imaginable, a tapper that traveled 5 feet in the air — but the slow roller to Chisholm was good enough to score the go-ahead run.

Next it was Chandler Simpson who fell behind 1-2 but kept fighting. Schlittler missed with a couple of 98 mph sinkers to run the count full before hitting the outside corner with a well-placed sinker. But Simpson, one-upping Hicks, chopped a swinging bunt 4 feet in the air toward George Lombard Jr. The rookie shortstop had a play at the plate and knew it, his eyes toward Austin Wells instead of the ball as he booted it on a backhand attempt to let in another run.

On a night Schlittler needed help and received none, Brent Headrick then entered and allowed two inherited runners to score.
