Australian politics live: One Nation denies journalists access to press conference in Melbourne

2026-09-08 05:59

Guardian Australia and ABC denied access to One Nation press conference

Guardian Australia and the ABC have again been refused entry to a One Nation press conference in Victoria.

One Nation’s Victorian leader, Warren Pickering, is due to address the media over allegations made against him by an ex-partner about alleged drug use.

Reporters from Guardian Australia and the ABC were escorted to the press conference at Crown Melbourne by hotel staff before they were asked which media outlet they were from and told to leave.

Pickering has rejected his ex-partner’s claims as “false”. According to The Australian, he said in a statement:

They are extremely hurtful and I just hope the public can see through them.

Reporters from The Australian, who broke the story, were not barred from attending the press conference.

Tributes for father and daughter killed in plane crash

A dairy company founder and his daughter have been identified as the two people killed in a light plane crash, prompting heartfelt tributes and condolences, AAP reports.

Ian Campbell, 56, and his daughter Hannah, 23, died on Monday morning when the aircraft crashed south of Brisbane, sparking a large grass fire.

Tributes to the father and daughter highlighted the family’s support for their local community and Campbell’s contributions to the agricultural sector.

The fourth-generation dairy farmer founded the Barambah Organics dairy farming company in Queensland and also ran Manana Pastoral in the Goondiwindi region, producing olive oil, lamb and beef.

Goondiwindi mayor Lawrence Springborg said the family had the support, prayers and goodwill of the community, and the pair would be greatly missed.

Campbell founded the Barambah Organics dairy farming company with his wife Jane. He told the ABC:

We feel their grief deeply and what makes it also so hard is that the Campbell family were so generous in their support of the local community.

The Campbell family had raised more than $70,000 for the Royal Flying Doctor Service and invested in agriculture, Mr Springborg noted.

National Farmers’ Federation president Hamish McIntyre said the loss of the father and daughter was an “absolutely heartbreaking tragedy”.

Mr Campbell was a highly respected figure in Australian agriculture and a long-time friend, he said.

Hannah was a bright young woman with her whole life in front of her.

Mr McIntyre said his thoughts were with the family, their many friends and everyone touched by “this devastating loss'’.

Two-thirds of households predict another RBA rate hike

Rising fuel prices, fears of further interest rate hikes and falling house prices have taken household confidence back towards the “deeply pessimistic” levels of last year, according to Westpac’s latest monthly survey.

Two-thirds of respondents now expect mortgage rates to rise, the survey showed, reflecting a near consensus among economists that the Reserve Bank will hike again this year and potentially as early as later this month.

Matthew Hassan, Westpac’s head of forecasting, said fears of higher rates were weighing particularly heavily on the mood of mortgaged homeowners.

As petrol prices push back towards $2.10 a litre, Hassan said “the mortgage belt is tightening up on spending plans”.

Westpac’s gauge of house price expectations dipped but showed more respondents expected prices to be higher over the next 12 months than lower. A little over 32% expect property values to decline over the coming year, with 20% expecting no change and 42% anticipating an increase (6% weren’t sure).

Westpac also today has joined the other major banks in predicting the RBA will hike again, picking the November meeting as the most likely time.

Luci Ellis, Westpac’s chief economist, said, the main reasons for changing the call “are the growing evidence of a more resilient household sector, and a larger-than-expected impetus from a datacentre focused investment boom”.

While the housing sector is playing out weaker than earlier forecast, the outlook for overall growth is now firmer and the tech sector is adding new cost pressures not evident previously.

Chalmers to release latest intergenerational report with AI a major feature

Jim Chalmers will release the latest intergenerational report on 21 September, which the treasurer described as “a snapshot of all of the changes we expect to see in our economy and in our society over the next 40 years”.

The government is required to release an IGR at least every five years under the charter of budget honesty, but can be more frequent (the most recent was in 2023).

In the words of Treasury officials, the reports “examine the long-term sustainability of current policies and how demographic, technological and other structural trends may affect the economy and the budget”.

Artificial intelligence is likely to be a major feature of this year’s report, given its potential to transform the way we work and live over coming years and decades.

That said, Treasury officials have already advised they expect AI will only help productivity growth return to historical levels, rather than unleash a new wave of prosperity.

The IGR will also look at the effects of an ageing population on the economy and the country’s finances. The last IGR highlighted how compulsory super had lightened the burden of paying for aged pensions.

The first IGR was delivered in 2002 under then treasurer Peter Costello, and this year’s report will be the seventh.

Butler refutes claim tobacco policies have ‘led to more Australians smoking illicit cigarettes’

Take three: Liberal MP Mary Aldred, who was on a Coalition-led parliamentary inquiry that recommended cutting the tobacco excise, has a go now to corner the government over the policy.

She again quotes NSW premier Chris Minns warning on the impact of the excise policy, and asks if the health minister will “admit Labor’s failed tobacco policies have led to more Australians smoking illicit cigarettes?”

The health minister, Mark Butler, says he begs to differ and that smoking rates have been going down despite the hiked up excise.

I don’t agree with that. The Institute of Health and Welfare, which has been the statutory authority under governments of both political persuasions for many, many years, says otherwise as well.

I think we should all be glad the authoritative body set up under statute, supported at least until today, by both major political parties, have said that smoking rates over the last three years have declined by about half a million on daily adult smoking rates.

Webster has another crack and asks why Mulino is “refusing to protect Australians …” which gets some more groans and Dick once again tells Webster to remove that part of the question completely.

Finally we get to the answer.
