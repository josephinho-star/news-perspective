Workers at the Arts Centre Melbourne have voted to go on strike for a full 24 hours this Friday, disrupting a one-night-only performance of US singer Macy Gray and the opening night of international dance act, Burn the Floor: Luminous.

Striking workers – including ushers, food and beverage attendants, stage managers, ticketing staff and more – will establish a picket line from 3.30pm and hold a rally from 6pm outside Hamer Hall, where Gray is scheduled to perform, on Friday.

The state government-run Arts Centre is Australia’s largest performing arts venue, employing around 900 workers, some 600 of whom are casuals. Hundreds are expected to join the strike.

The Media Entertainment and Arts Alliance (MEAA), the Community and Public Sector Union (CPSU) Victoria, and the Electrical Trades Union have been bargaining with the Arts Centre on a new enterprise agreement since March.

Pay remains the sticking point. The expired enterprise agreement saw wages increase by 1% every six months, and many of the lowest (Band 1) wages in the expired agreement are now well below the national minimum wage. More than a third of employees are classified as Band 1.

A spokesperson for the Arts Centre said all workers were paid at least the national minimum wage and that they had immediately adjusted the pay of workers whose wages had fallen below national baselines after the Fair Work Commission’s 2026 annual wage review.

The Arts Centre has offered workers a pay rise of 3% annually, the equivalent of the Victorian government wages cap. But workers say they need a 7% annual pay rise to make up for wage stagnation since 2022.

The decision to interrupt Macy Gray’s show has not been made lightly – but we are left with no choice until the government funds the Arts Centre properly.

Vicki Ward (Minister for Creative Industries) has the opportunity to sweep in and stop this industrial action by committing to paying workers fairly.

MEAA deputy chief executive, Adam Portelli, said:

Workers are at a point where they simply can’t afford to accept another agreement that leaves them falling behind.

This is about protecting the future of a major cultural institution as much as it is about workers’ pay.

‘They have let down Zomi’: protesters call for justice for slain aid worker

Outside the gates of Israel’s Canberra embassy, a lonely saxophone played as about 80 protesters gathered on Tuesday to demand an independent investigation into the death of Australian aid worker Zomi Frankcom.

Frankcom was killed by the Israel Defense Forces in April 2024 alongside other humanitarians while driving to deliver basic aid to the Gaza Strip.

The findings of an Israeli investigation last month suggested there was no suspicion of criminal misconduct surrounding her death despite a previous finding there had been failures in how the attack was carried out. No charges were laid.

A group, dressed in red to symbolise blood, passed between them a white, tightly bound cloth, designed to look like the dead body of a baby, AAP reports.

Mourning the life of his wife’s oldest friend, Karuna Ratna Bajracharya told the crowd:

I’m losing my faith in humanity when there is no justice.

I can see that when our leaders fail us, when justice fails, when international law fails.

The protesters called for the Israeli ambassador, Hillel Newman, to be expelled from Australia. At the time Newman said he felt deep sorrow for Frankcom’s family but refused to apologise for the strikes on aid vehicles.

Protesters say the response did not go far enough. Bajracharya, a lifelong Labor voter, said he was disheartened by the government’s sustained ties with Israel. He said:

They have let me down dramatically. They have let down Zomi Frankcom.

A letter to the minister in late August, understood to have been written by Australians previously onboard a Gaza-bound Global Sumud Flotilla, demanded an independent investigation into the IDF’s actions.

The group had not yet heard back, Jewish pro-Palestinian activist Anny Mokotow told the crowd. Before reading out the letter, Mokotow said:

We have to fight against this as much as possible. We do it for Zomi Frankcom and those who have died senselessly with her – for their bravery, generosity and selflessness.

Victorian inquiry into LGBTQ+ hate crimes hands down findings

Hate crimes against the LGBTQ+ community are under-reported, often not recognised as hate crimes by police, and frequently committed by “thrillseekers” enabled by “the relative permissibility of homophobic acts in society”, a Victorian government inquiry has found.

A legislative council inquiry into anti‑LGBTQ+ hate crimes in Victoria handed down its final report to parliament this afternoon. It included 41 findings and 35 recommendations to the state government to strengthen prevention and policing of hate crime related to gender or sexuality, and to fund LGBTQ+ health and community organisations and legal centres to better support victims and the wider community.

The inquiry found that there was significant mistrust of law enforcement among the LGBTQ+ community, which led to under-reporting of crimes against them.

It also found police often did not recognise or record the prejudicial element of the hate crime when it was reported. The committee recommended practical tools be developed to stop hate crime falling through administrative cracks in law enforcement, and an independent evaluation of Victoria police’s LGBTQ+ awareness training.

The inquiry was established after Guardian Australia reported in 2024 on a spate of more than a dozen, separate cases of gay men being allegedly attacked by people they met through dating apps.

Tributes for father and daughter killed in plane crash

A dairy company founder and his daughter have been identified as the two people killed in a light plane crash, prompting heartfelt tributes and condolences, AAP reports.

Ian Campbell, 56, and his daughter Hannah, 23, died on Monday morning when the aircraft crashed south of Brisbane, sparking a large grass fire.

Tributes to the father and daughter highlighted the family’s support for their local community and Campbell’s contributions to the agricultural sector.

The fourth-generation dairy farmer founded the Barambah Organics dairy farming company in Queensland and also ran Manana Pastoral in the Goondiwindi region, producing olive oil, lamb and beef.

Goondiwindi mayor Lawrence Springborg said the family had the support, prayers and goodwill of the community, and the pair would be greatly missed. Campbell founded the Barambah Organics dairy farming company with his wife, Jane.

We feel their grief deeply and what makes it also so hard is that the Campbell family were so generous in their support of the local community.

The Campbell family had raised more than $70,000 for the Royal Flying Doctor Service and invested in agriculture, Springborg noted.

National Farmers’ Federation president Hamish McIntyre said the loss of the father and daughter was an “absolutely heartbreaking tragedy”.

Campbell was a highly respected figure in Australian agriculture and a longtime friend, he said.

Hannah was a bright young woman with her whole life in front of her.

McIntyre said his thoughts were with the family, their many friends and everyone touched by “this devastating loss’’.

Two-thirds of households predict another RBA rate hike

Rising fuel prices, fears of further interest rate hikes and falling house prices have taken household confidence back towards the “deeply pessimistic” levels of last year, according to Westpac’s latest monthly survey.

Two-thirds of respondents now expect mortgage rates to rise, the survey showed, reflecting a near consensus among economists that the Reserve Bank will hike again this year and potentially as early as later this month.

Matthew Hassan, Westpac’s head of forecasting, said fears of higher rates were weighing particularly heavily on the mood of mortgaged homeowners.

As petrol prices push back towards $2.10 a litre, Hassan said “the mortgage belt is tightening up on spending plans”.

Westpac’s gauge of house price expectations dipped but showed more respondents expected prices to be higher over the next 12 months than lower. A little over 32% expect property values to decline over the coming year, with 20% expecting no change and 42% anticipating an increase (6% weren’t sure).

Westpac also today has joined the other major banks in predicting the RBA will hike again, picking the November meeting as the most likely time.

Luci Ellis, Westpac’s chief economist, said, the main reasons for changing the call “are the growing evidence of a more resilient household sector, and a larger-than-expected impetus from a datacentre focused investment boom”.

While the housing sector is playing out weaker than earlier forecast, the outlook for overall growth is now firmer and the tech sector is adding new cost pressures not evident previously.

Chalmers to release latest intergenerational report with AI a major feature

Jim Chalmers will release the latest intergenerational report on 21 September, which the treasurer described as “a snapshot of all of the changes we expect to see in our economy and in our society over the next 40 years”.

The government is required to release an IGR at least every five years under the charter of budget honesty, but can be more frequent (the most recent was in 2023).

In the words of Treasury officials, the reports “examine the long-term sustainability of current policies and how demographic, technological and other structural trends may affect the economy and the budget”.

Artificial intelligence is likely to be a major feature of this year’s report, given its potential to transform the way we work and live over coming years and decades.

That said, Treasury officials have already advised they expect AI will only help productivity growth return to historical levels, rather than unleash a new wave of prosperity.

The IGR will also look at the effects of an ageing population on the economy and the country’s finances. The last IGR highlighted how compulsory super had lightened the burden of paying for aged pensions.

The first IGR was delivered in 2002 under then treasurer Peter Costello, and this year’s report will be the seventh.
