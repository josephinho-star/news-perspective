Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos wins race to be next ECB president

2026-11-26 17:50

Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos has been chosen as the next president of the European Central Bank, to succeed Christine Lagarde, after winning support from the EU’s biggest countries.

The economist and monetary policymaker will become the first Spaniard to lead the ECB, defeating his rival candidate Klaas Knot, the former head of the Dutch central bank.

EU diplomats agreed two other appointments to the ECB’s executive board on Thursday: France’s Laurence Boone and Germany’s Markus Brunnermeier. The trio of appointments was a package deal, after France and Germany converged on choosing Hernández de Cos, who is expected to keep the ECB on the same path.

Following the talks European Council president António Costa confirmed the three appointments, saying: “This will pave the way for the formal appointment of the new ECB leadership by the EUCO [European Council] in December.”

The French president, Emmanuel Macron, also confirmed: “A consensus emerged on the nominations to the European Central Bank.”

The Spanish prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, who faces elections this weekend that he is predicted to lose, celebrated the victory of Hernández de Cos and the presence of Spaniards in other big EU roles, by hailing “a Spain that is leading in Europe as never before”.

Spain’s former deputy prime minister Teresa Ribera is one of the European Commission’s executive vice-presidents, with responsibility for a vast portfolio spanning the green transition and EU competition policy. The former Spanish economy minister Nadia Calviño leads the European Investment Bank.

Hernández de Cos, a former Bank of Spain governor, will become the fifth head of the eurozone’s central bank since it was founded in 1998. He is expected to take up his position after Lagarde stands down in February, during a time of political change and economic pressure in the 21-country eurozone.

Europe is entering a big election year, starting with Spain’s elections this weekend, with national votes to follow in France, Italy and (non-eurozone) Poland. European economies are under growing pressure from fierce Chinese competition, US tariff threats and soaring energy prices that are driving up inflation, as well as high debt in several member states.

One of his biggest challenges will be reining in surging prices: inflation in the eurozone hit 4% last month, up from 2.1% a year earlier.

Hernández de Cos, who is now serving as general manager of the Bank for International Settlements, is seen as supporting lower interest rates to boost growth.

Market analysts are closely watching what Hernández de Cos might say about France, where an embattled minority government is struggling to pass a budget. French debt is forecast to hit 122% of GDP next year, more than twice the limit allowed under EU fiscal rules.

Arne Petimezas, the director of research at AFS Group Amsterdam, said Hernández de Cos, who “leans dovish”, was the “continuity candidate”. He added: “Don’t expect de Cos to turn a blind eye to France and its fiscal predicaments. No carte blanche: bond buying will remain contingent on austerity and a path towards a lower debt ratio.”

The ECB’s approach to the French debt crisis will come under intense political scrutiny, especially after calls from the French far-right presidential frontrunner, Marine Le Pen, “to ease the burden of interest rates”.

Hernández de Cos is seen as a central banker in the mould of Mario Draghi, the celebrated former ECB president and Italian prime minister, who is credited with stabilising the eurozone at a pivotal moment in the debt crisis. Matthew Ryan, at the financial services firm Ebury, said Hernández de Cos was “likely to place greater weight on growth, financial stability and fragmentation risk than Klaas Knot, the alternative candidate and a genuine hawk”.

“We do not expect his appointment to alter the near-term rate path. The president sets the agenda but is ultimately one voice on the governing council, and rarely overrides the consensus.”

The contest was set in motion less than a month ago when Lagarde, the ECB’s first female president, announced that she intended to leave her post nine months early to speak her mind about world affairs and confront “the misrepresentation of the truth”.

Macron, who played a decisive role in appointing Lagarde, a former French finance minister, said EU leaders had welcomed her “decisive leadership during her term in office”.

Hernández de Cos’s appointment must still clear the European parliament and the ECB’s governing council, but these steps are seen as formalities.
