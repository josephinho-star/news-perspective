Cornell University announces full review of Greek life amid Jane Doe ‘Cornell 7’ investigation

2026-11-17 05:13

The future of Greek life at Cornell University is in question after the alleged gang rape of a student brought intense scrutiny to the Ivy League college’s handling of sexual assault cases.

Cornell University President Michael Kotlikoff announced Monday that the school will conduct a “full review” of its Greek life system in the coming weeks to “consider the future of fraternities and sororities” on the Ithaca, NY campus.

The potential overhaul follows backlash over the administration’s response to disturbing allegations detailed in a lawsuit filed by Jane Doe, accusing seven fraternity brothers of raping her at Cornell’s Chi Phi fraternity house in October 2024.

Kotlikoff said the university will implement various measures to “rebuild” students’ confidence in the administration’s ability to handle and prevent sexual violence.

“Sexual violence can have no place at Cornell: not on our campus, not in our community, and not in our culture. The responsibility to keep each other safe begins with me and extends to every part of our Cornell community,” Kotlikoff said in a statement.

“I know many in our community have lost confidence in the Cornell administration’s ability to prevent and respond to sexual violence. You have raised necessary questions about what the administration is doing to keep our students safe and where we have fallen short,” he said.

A shocking study of records by CNN revealed that just two out of 16 Cornell students found responsible for recent sexual assaults were expelled — while half the others only had to attend extra study.

The Ivy League has hired a new victim advocate in their Sexual Harassment and Assault Response and Education office, and a case manager to assist survivors, with more hires underway.

Incoming students will also receive in-person education at orientation on sexual assault, communication, and consent.

Cornell also tapped former US Deputy Attorney General Sally Yates to lead an independent review of the university’s ability to prevent and respond to sexual misconduct cases on campus — including specifically how it handled Jane Doe’s allegations.

“I commit to making changes, based on the report of the Yates independent review, to how we investigate and adjudicate sexual misconduct and share information with survivors and the community. We will strive to provide maximum allowable transparency around misconduct process outcomes,” Kotlikoff said.

The university will also expand options for campus social events not centered around alcohol, pilot a free late-night transportation program, invest in public venues for students to gather outside residential spaces, and reevaluate its drug and alcohol policies, the president said.

The “Cornell 7” case became national news after Doe filed a lawsuit in Manhattan Supreme Court two months ago.
