When others turned away, Türkiye stood with Sudan

2026-10-04 10:36

Sudan is currently navigating one of the most critical turning points in its recent history. The war, which has inflicted profound suffering on the Sudanese state, its legitimate institutions, and the Sudanese people, has presented global and regional actors alike with a profound test of their commitment to principles, responsibilities, and strategic partnerships.

At a time when many have chosen to hide behind the pretext of passive neutrality or to support projects aimed at fragmenting the country, Türkiye has demonstrated an exceptionally farsighted, proactive diplomacy and a unique model of interstate relations, proving that the shared bonds and history between Ankara and Khartoum run far deeper than temporary political fluctuations and are far more robust than the machinations of regional alliances.

At the institutional center of this complex process along the Ankara-Khartoum axis, the intensive diplomatic efforts of the Embassy of Türkiye in Khartoum stand out prominently. The recently concluded 25-month diplomatic period was not merely a traditional representational activity. Rather, it evolved into an intergovernmental fieldwork mechanism that elevated Turkish-Sudanese relations from conventional protocol frameworks to a “joint resistance alliance" and institutionally reinforced the constitutional legitimacy of the Sudanese state even under the darkest conditions. The scope of the strategic role assumed by Türkiye and the deep-rooted cooperation mechanisms between the two states can be analyzed within the following geopolitical and diplomatic axes.

At a time when questionable Western and regional media outlets and diplomatic mechanisms were attempting to fabricate an artificial “legitimacy crisis” and equate the Sudanese National Army with the rebel Rapid Support Forces (RSF) terrorist militias, Ankara demonstrated a legal and principled state stance by fully recognizing the legitimate institutions of the Sudanese state and the Sovereignty Council, providing them with unconditional support.

This institutional resolve has been manifested through the active presence of Turkish diplomacy on the ground, its refusal to yield to pressures to internationalize the crisis, and its nullification of “airspace closure” projects aimed at stripping the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) of their operational capabilities. The institutional and political support Türkiye has provided on international platforms such as the Organization of Islamic Cooperation and the United Nations has served as an impenetrable legal shield against international initiatives targeting the legitimate sovereignty of the Sudanese state.

One of the most defining pillars of Türkiye's policy toward Sudan is its continuous deployment of “humanitarian diplomacy” tools on the ground during the war. Despite the destructive effects of war, the near-collapse of logistical supply lines, and conflict environments where security risks reached their peak, Turkish state mechanisms have ensured the continuity of humanitarian aid through a strategic corridor.

Under the coordination of the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency (TIKA), the Turkish Red Crescent (Kızılay), AFAD (Disaster and Emergency Management Authority), and many others, massive humanitarian aid convoys and ships carrying basic food, shelter, and emergency medical supplies have been delivered to the field through institutional mechanisms between the two states. Beyond merely providing temporary logistical support, this aid has served as an institutional shield, strengthening Sudan’s social fabric against the socio-economic devastation.

At a time when security threats had reached their peak and regional instability had deepened due to the war caused by RSF militias, the Turkish Hospital in Nyala, Darfur, continued its operations without interrupting its services for even a single day, demonstrating the dedication of Turkish humanitarian diplomacy on the ground. At a time when many international organizations had withdrawn from the field, the continued collaboration of Turkish and Sudanese medical staff side by side transformed the solidarity between the two countries from mere protocols on paper into a living mechanism that saves human lives.

During the early stages of the war, as thousands of Turkish and foreign nationals were transported from conflict zones to safe provinces and then on to Türkiye, the Turkish diplomatic mission and Sudanese regular security forces demonstrated flawless operational cooperation. This process has once again proven that Turkish diplomatic missions are not merely administrative centers but institutional havens to which people can turn in times of crisis.

Despite the war conditions that led to the destruction of educational infrastructure and the closure of universities in conflict zones, the academic bridges between Turkish and Sudanese institutions have remained strong. Thanks to close coordination between the Sudanese Ministry of Higher Education, the Turkish Higher Education Council (YÖK), and relevant Turkish universities such as Tokat Gaziosmanpaşa University, the process for Sudanese students to pursue education through scholarships of the Presidency for Turks Abroad and Related Communities (YTB) or at their own expense has been streamlined.

The uninterrupted continuation of the student visa issuance process, despite challenging conditions, represents a strategic investment in Sudan’s human capital and the cultivation of personnel who will build the shared future of the two nations. These steps serve as a vital safety valve for preserving the intellectual capital that will spearhead Sudan’s post-war reconstruction efforts.

For Sudan to definitively break free from the current Western and regional blackmail trap, Türkiye-Sudan relations must evolve from crisis management to a phase of “comprehensive geopolitical engineering” conducted in coordination with rising global powers. In this context, the localization of the defense industry and the redesign of the regional security architecture are fully aligned with the vital interests of both states.

Türkiye’s groundbreaking unmanned aerial vehicle technologies, smart munitions, electronic warfare systems, and its sanctions-resistant domestic defense industry capabilities developed in recent years provide a critical multiplier effect in Sudan’s struggle for sovereignty. Aligning this expertise with Sudan’s domestic production capacity to meet the needs of its legitimate armed forces, and developing joint defense doctrines, constitutes the most effective deterrent against asymmetric threats and terrorist militias.

A three-dimensional geopolitical cooperation taking shape along the “Khartoum-Ankara-Moscow” axis has the potential to build a strong protective umbrella within the U.N. Security Council against biased draft resolutions that attempt to equate legitimate state authority with illegitimate entities on international platforms. The coordinated use of Türkiye’s growing network of institutional influence across Africa and Russia’s global veto power in the diplomatic arena will completely neutralize external pressures on Sudan.

During the postwar period of stability and economic development, the strategic security of the Red Sea is a shared priority for both countries. The revitalization of the historic island of Suakin, along with military and logistical hub projects and joint investments in energy and mineral resources, will reinforce military cooperation with an economic lifeline. The logistical facilities that Sudanese ports will provide to Turkish and Russian naval forces will not only strengthen the security of global trade routes but also cement Khartoum’s position in the regional equation.

International smear campaigns and false accusations directed against the Sudanese state are doomed to fail on the ground, thanks to the resolve of legitimate institutions and the awareness of the people. It is against this backdrop that the statement in the official farewell message published by Türkiye's ambassador to Sudan, Fatih Yıldız, “I have taken pride in representing Türkiye and the Turkish people, one of the few countries that did not abandon Sudan during its most difficult days," as his term came to an end, serves as a diplomatic testament to the unshakable solidarity between the two states.

The unwavering support provided by the Turkish diplomatic mission to Sudan’s official and legitimate institutions throughout the war has become a strategic bridge that strengthens the historic ties between the two countries. Ultimately, this enduring bond transcends the circumstances of the moment and affirms a shared commitment to standing together through both hardship and hope: long live the brotherhood between Türkiye and Sudan!
