Drake Maye injury: Is the Patriots' season over? Can Tommy DeVito carry the offense?

2026-11-16 21:14

The outlook for the New England Patriots' 2026 season has been dramatically altered. After suffering an injury on the Patriots' first drive against the Detroit Lions on Sunday, quarterback Drake Maye did not fly back from Munich with the team out of caution, as the team is concerned he might have sustained a fractured ankle.

The 5-5 Patriots haven't been among the AFC's elite in 2026, but were hoping to make a charge to the playoffs -- and Maye potentially finding his past runner-up MVP form was one of their silver linings.

When Maye was taking care of the football and extending plays, the Patriots were explosive and flashed potential to hang with the best -- as a Week 4 win at the Buffalo Bills showed. But when Maye was not buttoned-up with his decision-making (he entered Sunday with 11 INTs), the Patriots struggled to get by some of the league's lower-echelon teams, with close wins against the New York Jets and Miami Dolphins, who have won a combined three games this season.

Coach Mike Vrabel often says it's hard to win when a team's best players don't play well, and simply put, Maye is the team's best offensive player.

Entering Week 10, he ranked top five in the NFL in Total QBR (74.2), passing touchdowns (18), yards per attempt (8.4), rushing yards by a quarterback (219) and rushing touchdowns by a quarterback (3).

Now potentially without Maye for weeks or even the season, the question is can the team make it back to the playoffs?

ESPN Patriots reporter Mike Reiss answers some of the top questions about the trickle-down effect of Maye's injury and analyst Matt Bowen answers a fantasy question on what to expect if you have Patriots players in your lineup.

Is the Patriots' 2026 season over?

When Vrabel was hired as head coach in January 2025, he said the team's goals were to win the division, host home playoff games and compete for championships. Their odds of doing that without Maye are closer to zero, starting with the current standings -- they are 5-5 while the AFC East rival Bills are 8-1.

Here is the rest of the Patriots schedule: they are on the bye in Week 11 before a road trip to face the winless Los Angeles Chargers, then two huge home games in a span of five days -- vs. the Bills (Dec. 6) and the Minnesota Vikings (Dec. 10). Then they're at the Kansas City Chiefs and the New York Jets before finishing the year with home games against the Denver Broncos and the Miami Dolphins.

They have played poorly the past two weeks in blowout losses to the Packers and Lions, ravaged by injuries across the roster, but Maye's presence -- even as he battled through inconsistent play with turnovers -- gave them a sliver of hope.

Consider what happened Sunday: After New England's opening drive when Maye was injured on the final play, they totaled seven points and gained 82 yards over 10 drives, averaging 1.9 yards per play (not including kneel down). New England punted on eight of those drives, with seven three-and-outs.

Maye has been the driving force behind the passing game. Entering Sunday, Patriots pass catchers recorded 38.1% of their yards after the catch, the third-lowest total in the NFL behind the Texans (37.1%) and Giants (36.8%), per ESPN Research.

The defense has also mightily struggled as of late, giving the offense little room for error. Since Week 7, the Patriots are giving up the second-most yards per game (411.5), behind only the 1-8 Tennessee Titans.

With some of the NFL's elite teams on the horizon, life without Maye could look bleak.

What is Drake Maye's possible recovery timeline?

The recovery timeline for similar injuries have varied, depending on the severity.

In January, Broncos QB Bo Nix suffered a broken ankle and underwent surgery two days later. The initial recovery timetable reported was 12 weeks. In April, he underwent a clean-up surgery to fix bone spurs. Nix, who had a history of ankle injuries, was ready for training camp in July.

In September 2022, 49ers QB Trey Lance underwent surgery on his right ankle after suffering "a fibula fracture and ligament disruption" in a game. The injury ended his season and required a follow-up surgery in December of that year to remove hardware that was causing irritation. He was cleared to participate in OTA's the following April.

In 2024, 49ers linebacker Fred Warner said he had been playing most of the season with a fractured bone in his ankle.

As we learn more about the specific type of ankle injury Maye has, the timeline will become clearer.

What can the offense get out of Tommy DeVito?

Offensive coordinator Josh McDaniels has been through this before, most recently in 2016 when Tom Brady was serving a four-game NFL suspension and Jimmy Garoppolo and Jacoby Brissett stepped in to help the team to a 3-1 record. McDaniels' offense has flexibility to play to the specific strengths of the quarterback, with an obvious example coming with some designed runs for Brissett in '16 that never would have been called with Brady.

DeVito struggled Sunday when taking over for Maye, and Vrabel noted it was a tough position for him to be thrust into because backups don't receive many, if any, first-team repetitions during the week of practice.

"There were some things that he missed, but also not having a ton of live action since training camp, and really, I think he'll be ready to go coming out of the bye," Vrabel said. "I think that he'll be able to look at some things and understand, whether that's being able to transfer up into the pocket, get us in the right play, get the ball out quicker or look at protections or rotation, whatever that may be. I think that'll all improve."

DeVito had success with the deep ball in the preseason, and made improvements processing information on defensive coverages, so some of the things he has going for him are similar to Maye. And, of course, DeVito briefly caught fire with the New York Giants when thrust into a similar situation.

In eight career starts (all with the Giants), DeVito is 3-5 with a 26.4 QBR, 65.3% completion rate, 1,170 passing yards for 7 touchdowns, one interception and 34 sacks. He isn't as mobile as Maye, with only 36 rushes for 198 yards during those starts.

Vrabel reflected optimism in the backup QB they signed to two-year contract with a maximum value of $7.4 million in March.

"I have a lot of confidence in Tommy," Vrabel said Monday. "I know his toughness is going to be through the charts, and his competitiveness will be exactly what we need."

Could the Patriots explore other options at quarterback?

That is a longer-shot scenario in terms of signing someone to be the starter, mainly due to the intricacies of the offense and the time crunch. A more likely option initially is to utilize the 17-player practice squad to add a third layer of quarterback depth behind DeVito and rookie Behren Morton, the seventh-round pick from Texas Tech.

Another avenue to explore would be other teams' practice squads to see if there was a worthy option to sign to the 53-man roster in a backup role. There are 15 quarterbacks on practice squads, a group including Will Levis (Jets), Nick Mullens (Jaguars), Kyle Trask (Cowboys) Hendon Hooker (Titans), Jake Browning (Chargers) and Luke Altmyer (Lions).

"I don't see that being the case right now, but we'll have conversations and we'll discuss it and we'll look and see," Vrabel said Monday about the possibility of adding a quarterback.

How have injuries decimated the Patriots this season?

New England mostly had good fortune health-wise in its surprise run to the Super Bowl last year, and 2026 has been the other side of the coin. Vrabel noted in a Monday radio interview that it evens out over time.

It began in the spring when tight end Julian Hill, who they signed to be their No. 2 option and a blocking force, sustained a season-ending knee injury and veteran outside linebacker Harold Landry III had an undisclosed injury that landed him on the physically unable to perform list (where he remains). Then receiver A.J. Brown suffered a right high-ankle sprain in the season opener and hasn't looked the same since a strong training camp. They placed both starting guards on injured reserve, along with No. 2 receiver Romeo Doubs (shoulder) and No. 2 running back TreVeyon Henderson (illness), among others. And now possibly Maye.

Teams are allowed eight designations to return from IR and the Patriots have already used five of them and will likely max out all eight by season's end.

"We just have to find some consistency, hopefully, in the lineup with the guys that are coming in and out and everything else, guys that we've built up through the draft and the guys that know one system. But that sometimes always isn't going to be the case, and that's where we're at now," Vrabel said.

What should fantasy managers do with the Patriots' offense in fantasy football?

This injury will have a major impact on the Patriots' roster from a fantasy perspective. DeVito can provide some upside as a runner but he should only be played in deeper Superflex formats as a QB2.

WR A.J. Brown left Sunday's game with a hamstring injury, and he's played in only four games this season due to multiple injuries. When he returns, however, Brown's value declines, fitting in lineups as a WR3. DeMario Douglas now becomes a matchup-dependent WR3, while Mack Hollins is a bye week fill-in for deeper leagues. TE Hunter Henry was a top target for Maye, but I am anticipating a reduced amount of red-zone work here, which moves him down to the TE1/TE2 line.

In the backfield with TreVeyon Henderson on IR (illness), Rhamondre Stevenson could see a bump in volume if the Patriots are more run heavy, plus he can operate as an outlet/checkdown option for DeVito in the pass game, which keeps him in the RB2 range. -- Matt Bowen
