Shadow minister says Tories would ‘keep political activism out of classrooms’ – UK politics live

2026-10-06 12:15

Badenoch suggests Tories would not reverse Labour legislation making pensions triple lock less generous

In her interview with Times Radio this morning, Kemi Badenoch hinted that, if Labour has legislated to make the pensions triple lock less generous by the time of the next election, a future Tory government would not reverse that.

In his party conference speech last week, Andy Burnham said he wanted to change the triple lock uprating mechanism from 2030, with the savings used to part fund his plans to set up a National Care Service. The government later said it would legislate for this before the election.

The Tories are committed to the pensions triple lock in its current form. But, asked if she would repeal any legislation passed making it less generous, Badenoch replied:

I have said that the triple lock is our policy and it remains our policy. I don’t think going after pensioners is the right thing when there are loads of people who can work, who won’t work.

When we are going to reverse Labour’s policies, I say we are going to reverse their policies.

I have said that we are keeping this policy, but if they take that money and they spend it on something that we can’t get it back from, then it would be difficult for me.

What I’m not going to do is lie to your listeners just so I can get votes. I tell the truth and I say the triple lock is our policy and we’re keeping it.

But what I’m not going to do is to say something when I don’t know what the situation would be about what Labour has done with that money. We have to be very careful.

Shadow education secretary says Tories would legislate to stop teaching unions bringing politics into classroom

Laura Trott, the shadow education secretary, may have been speaking to a minuscule audience in the hall earlier (see 11.18am), but she gave a long speech that covered quite a lot of ground. Here are the main points.

Trott said the Tories would legislate to stop teaching unions bringing politics into the classroom. She spoke at length about pupils being exposed to unsuitable political content in the classroom and she said:

Today I’m launching Our Pledge to Parents which lays out my commitment to mums and dads. I will keep political activism out of the classroom.

And she singled out teaching unions for particular criticism.

We’ll properly enforce the rules on political impartiality for teachers and guest speakers.

And we’ll create a specific law around trade unions involved in education.

They must not bring politics into schools.

She claimed that pupils were being exposed to unsuitable ideas in the classroom.

Children as young as seven are being told they have white privilege.

Pupils are learning that a woman can have a penis, a man can be pregnant, and sex is assigned at birth.

Some have been told to rewrite negative headlines on migration to make them more positive.

And others have been taught that Britain is a racist country.

This is fiction being presented as fact ….

This is part of a trend of complex, controversial, contested issues being boiled down, laced with political bias and delivered in a place that children trust; schools.

If anyone has an agenda, it’s a network called Schools of Sanctuary.

Now you might not have heard of it, but it has now signed up over a thousand sixth forms, secondary schools, primary schools, even nurseries,

All calling themselves schools of sanctuaries for refugees.

The vision schools are signing up to is that the UK will be a place of welcome for all.

Except it’s more nuanced than that isn’t it?

We can’t welcome all migrants.

We don’t want to welcome all migrants, certainly not dangerous criminals.

Yet that is the simplistic political narrative they push on our children.

But when I wrote to the former education secretary [Bridget Phillipson] about this blatant political activism, do you know what she did?

Absolutely nothing.

She also condemned the role played by the National Education Union, England’s largest teaching union, in schools.

What do you think they debate when the NEU, that’s the National Education Union, get together?

The merits of teaching Latin perhaps?

The value of coding in maths?

The latest developments in pedagogy?

No.

Their recent resolutions include:

Defending the right to protest, opposing UK defence spending and embedding their own LGBT+ teaching materials in schools.

Friends, this doesn’t sound like a professional body that cares about teaching pupils.

It sounds like a campaigning organisation bent on politicising education.

And it is deeply entwined with politics, extreme politics.

Several NEU officials happen to also be directors of the Palestine Solidarity Campaign.

She said there was too much antisemitism in schools.

Parents … are being left with an impossible choice.

Sending their children to mainstream schools where Jewish children are heckled, bullied, singled out, or send them to Jewish schools, with prison-like securitywhere they’re under constant threat from outside.

She said under the Tories state schools would not be allowed to force young girls to wear the hijab. Referring to this story, she said:

You might have heard about the school in Barnet making the hijab part of the uniform for girls as young as 7.

Conference, be absolutely clear, we would not allow that under a future Conservative government.

She said the Tories and Labour had fundamentally different approaches to education.

Lucy Powell [the education secretary] says, education should be about what you can do, and not what you can’t.

Cartlidge says 'Iron Dome' just generic term for missile defence, like 'Hoover' meaning vacuum cleaner

James Cartlidge, the shadow defence secretary, has suggested that, when the Tories talked about setting up an “Iron Dome”, they were just using it as a generic term for missile defence – like describing a vacuum cleaner as a Hoover. They were not talking about the Israeli version, he claimed.

In an attempt to clear up the confusion caused by the Tories first saying they were proposing an Israeli-type Iron Dome, before Kemi Badenoch later said that wasn’t the plan (see 7.59am), Cartlidge told the Press Association:

The phrase ‘iron dome’ has come to be used a bit like the word ‘Hoover’ to generically refer to missile defence systems – and this is clearly a missile defence system.

Is it the exact same iron dome as Israel? No.

The Israeli iron dome is a short-range system. It’s to do with very near threats from the likes of Hezbollah.

It was actually originally designed for artillery and very unsophisticated weaponry, whereas what we’re talking about, Britannia Shield, is a bespoke UK system.

It’s just, if you speak to the media and the public, if you say ‘iron dome’, they at least know roughly what you’re talking about.

Katie Lam, the shadow communities secretary, also spoke this morning to a near-empty hall. She said she was launching a programme to develop “a new Conservative vision for local government”.

Explaining what that meant, she said:

That vision has to be informed by the wealth of experience that we have in our party.

Whether that’s councillors, former councillors, or just people who know about their area, and who have ideas about how it should be run.

It is widely assumed that, under Kemi Badenoch’s leadership, moderate, one-nation Conservatives have been more or less wiped out as a force in the party. But, in her Guardian column based on her time at the conference, Polly Toynbee suggests they might be more resilient than we think.

Here is an extract.

If nothing improves by next conference, the mirage of Badenoch’s rock-solid support will melt away. But what would replace her? Waiting in the wings there is a model of what a Conservative revival might look like – Prosper UK, launched in January.

It tiptoes, praising Badenoch loudly, yet it is running seven events at this conference. That included a small Sunday night rally for electoral reform. It was addressed by former West Midlands mayor Andy Street, now co-chair of Prosper UK with Ruth Davidson It is backed by a formidable array of what was once the One Nation group, now defunct in parliament – or dormant under Badenoch’s rightwards march. No MPs back Prosper UK in public, but the group claims a third of them do secretly, along with 30,000 declared supporters. David Gauke, vice chair, has crept back into the party – he thinks by accident when the membership computer processing his re-application didn’t register he’d been ejected in 2019. In returning, he joined a battalion of remainer luminaries of the centre-right who’d had the whip suspended at the same time, including Kenneth Clarke and Dominic Grieve.

Gauke travels the country speaking to Conservative associations and others. But though socially liberal, he’s dry as a bone on fiscal discipline, campaigning for low business taxes, a smaller state and enticing back non-doms – all Hayek, no Keynes. “No one speaks for some 7 million people centre or centre-right,” he says.

Laura Trott says Tories would 'keep political activism out of classroom' - in speech to near-empty hall

Laura Trott, the education secretary, has given a speech at the conference. This morning the events are on the main stage, which is the smaller venue. This afternoon events are in the larger symphony hall.

Trott’s speech was largely a long rant about supposed political correctness excesses in schools, with pupils being told that white people are privileged, women can have penises, Britain is racist etc. She said the Tories would “keep political activism out of the classroom”.

Trott is now taking part in a panel discussion including the Telegraph journalist and GB News presenter Camilla Tominey. Tominey is no leftie, but even she seemed to think Trott had laid it on a bit too thick. Tominey said that, following the supreme court judgment, she thought children were no longer being told women can have penises.

There aren’t many people watching – either in the hall or on YouTube, where there is a live feed.

But the Labour press office is tuning in. It has just posted this picture of the event on social media with the heading “People disguised as chairs latest” – a reference to a joke told by Jeremy Clarkson yesterday, when he was sarcastically commenting on how good it was to see so many people in the audience for his Tory conference session.

Air Marshal Greg Bagwell, former deputy commander of RAF Air Command, told Times Radio he did not think the Tories pledge of £10bn over 10 years would be enough to fund a missile defence system that would protect the UK, its territories and bases.

As the Press Association reports, Bagwell said:

It really is a question of definition of what capability you’re going to deliver here, and what you can realistically expect in terms of protection.

You might be able to protect certain key sites, certain strategic areas. London would be an example, but of course that’s not enough.

If you’ve got military bases all around the country, you’ve got power stations, you’ve got critical infrastructure dotted around the country, then you’re really going to have to try and cover the entire area, which is is challenging.

Bagwell said a full system could cost more than £80bn. He said that the US was talking about spending between $200bn and $1tr (£150bn to £750bn) for their “Golden Dome”.

Bagwell’s analysis matches what General Sir Richard Barrons told MPs in the summer. (See 10.23am.)

According to the BBC, Sidharth Kaushal, a senior fellow at the defence thinktank RUSI, said that while £10bn would not fund an Israeli-style “Iron Dome” missile defence system, it would “probably cover” what the UK “actually needs” including horizon radar and more E7 aircrafts.

How Tories would fund their £1bn-a-year 'Britannia Shield' plan

According to the Conservatives, a Tory government would fund the plan for a £1bn-a-year “Britannia Shield” with some of the money from the extra £10bn a year it would raise for defence.

Last month, the Tories published details of how that £10bn a year would be funded. Some £8bn of that would come from cuts to benefits – £4bn from cutting housing benefit, £3bn from reimposing the two-child benefit cap, and £1bn from the Motability scheme funding cars for disabled people.

The housing benefit cuts would partly be achieved by cutting local housing allowance (LHA) rates, which determine how much people can receive in housing benefit. The Tories have briefed that LHA rates would be set at 10th percentile of local market rates, not the 30th percentile.

The cuts would also involve social sector rents being cut.

How General Sir Richard Barrons told MPs ideal missile defence system would cost £120bn

Kemi Badenoch was asked earlier (see 7.59am) about GeneralSir Richard Barrons, the retired former head of Joint Forces Command, saying an Iron Dome-style missile defence system would cost up to £100bn.

The subject of integrated air and missile defence also links to the absolutely vital point that gets lost so often: the defence investment plan is the articulation of the SDR [strategic defence review] – it is the things that made the cut for the money available. Given the legacy holes in defence, there is at least another half of the iceberg that never made it into the DIP, because it was not even remotely affordable. There is a £60 billion to £80 billion hole in defence infrastructure—falling down airfields, ports, housing, the Reserve Forces’ and Cadets’ Associations. Most of that is not in the DIP, because it just could not get into the cut.

Integrated air and missile defence is the iconic thing that did not make into the review. There is about £1 billion in the DIP for it, and that buys improved command and control – you’ll know better that you’re going to be hit by a missile – some improvements for the Air Force ability to shoot down cruise missiles, and some improvements to the Type 45 so that it has a better capability against ballistic missiles.

Many people in the country assume there is a sort of iron dome over at least London, and that is not even remotely there. Even if you embrace the extraordinary technological change that some companies are producing in low-cost kills, whether directed energy weapons, DragonFire or cheaper missiles like Cambridge Aerospace and that kind of thing, the cost of an iron dome at entry level is probably £80 billion over 10 years—entry level. The IAMD [integrated air and missile defence] of my dreams is probably £120 billion over 10 years. But we got £1 billion, because it sits in that bit of the iceberg that never got to be remotely affordable. We should not kid ourselves that if we have an argument about the margins of the DIP, we are somehow fixing defence. That is just not true.

Badenoch says she is seeking tips for Tories from how 'heritage brands' recover from mistakes

In her interview on Times Radio earlier, Kemi Badenoch said she had been reading about how some “heritage brands” recovered after losing their reputation for tips that might help the Tories.

I’ve been reading a lot about heritage brands that lost their way. And it always happens when the brand stops looking after its core customers and starts doing things that are off-brand. And it takes some time to recover from that.

But that is what I’m doing. Refreshing the brand. I’m glad that I’m doing well. I know that people have started listening to the Conservative party again. I want them to start buying.

Badenoch says she does not understand why people are policing others’ identity.

I don’t understand why all of a sudden some people are policing other people’s identity?

I think identity is something very personal. If that’s how [Braverman] feels about herself, I think that’s fine.

But what I’m worried about is politicians running around telling some people that they’re not English.

I remember when I was younger watching English footballers, black and white. I can’t imagine people pointing to the black footballers and saying, ‘Well, you’re not really, really English.’

I think we need we need to be very careful with this, sort of conversation.

She says, on gender, she thinks men are men and women are different.

But identity is different. Her children are half white, half black.
