Victorian election 2026: Ben Carroll concedes to Jess Wilson, ending 12 years of Labor government

2026-11-28 10:29

Victorian Labor has suffered a historic defeat, ending 12 years of government, with the Coalition winning majority government and the Greens securing a record number of seats.

At 36, Wilson will become Victoria’s youngest leader since federation and the first woman to be popularly elected as the state’s premier – a feat neither Jacinta Allan nor Joan Kirner achieved.

In her victory speech, Wilson spoke directly to Labor voters who had abandoned the party and said her team would “work every single day to demonstrate that you were right to place your trust in us”.

“I know that for many of you this is the first time that you have ever voted Liberal. And for others, it was the first time you have ever given us your preference.

“I realise that you have lent us your vote. We don’t own it.”

Carroll has lost his seat of Niddrie in Melbourne’s north-western suburbs. Labor has held this seat for its entire 50 year history. It is the first time a Victorian premier has lost their seat at a state election.

In a speech to party faithful shortly after 8.30pm, Carroll took full responsibility for a defeat described by some Labor sources as an “absolute bloodbath”.

“This is a significant loss for Labor,” Carroll said. “It’s not the result we wanted, but it is a result we accept.

“As leader of the party, I take full responsibility. Democracy is both beautiful and brutal. Beautiful, because we get to witness the will of the people. And brutal, because it is so difficult and painful to lose.

“But Victorians have spoken. And their message is clear. They want change.”

The deputy Labor leader, Gabrielle Williams, said she was “deeply saddened” by the result and the party needed to learn from its mistakes.

“Clearly we have to be deeply humbled by the results and take the lessons from them,” Williams said.

“But I would go further and say we actually need to go back and have a look at the lessons from 2022 and where those warnings really began to emerge.”

As of 10pm the Greens had secured seven seats – holding Brunswick, Richmond and Melbourne and winning Pascoe Vale, Preston and Footscray from Labor and Prahran from the Liberals.

If voting trends from the first few hours of counting continued, it appeared possible the Greens might win more lower house seats than Labor, making it the official opposition.

Sarah Mansfield, the deputy leader of the Greens, told the ABC the early results were “really encouraging and I think a real vindication of that positive progressive agenda that we pushed forward this election”.

“This is an extraordinary election here, and what I think the fundamental message is there’s been a rejection of the two major parties, of the two-party system.”

Early in the night several former Labor seats were called for the Liberals, including Frankston and Mordialloc in the south-east, Hastings on the Mornington Peninsula and Box Hill and Ringwood, in the eastern suburbs. The seat of South Barwon also went to the Liberals, the first time the party has won the seat from Labor since the 1990s.

Point Cook, Sydenham, Werribee and Melton – traditional Labor strongholds in Labor’s west and north – also fell to the Liberals.

Federal Labor sources quickly began to distance themselves from the state election result. One described the result as the “perfect storm-of-shit moment in Victoria” that would not be repeated federally.

“There are upsides federally to the Liberals being in power and cutting and privatising stuff, and One Nation’s bluster and hollowness getting tiring,” they said.

Labor MP Steve Dimopoulous, who is seen by some as a potential leader of the party if he holds on to his seat, offered a sincere apology to Labor faithful.

“This is an absolutely unequivocal report card on the Labor government,” Dimopoulous said.

One Nation appeared on course to win at least two seats, having secured more than 20% of the primary vote: the regional district of Murray Plains, and Sunbury on Melbourne north-western fringe. Murray Plains was held by the Nationals, while Sunbury was held by Labor.

At One Nation Victoria’s campaign event, it was the federal leader Pauline Hanson who addressed the party faithful. The state leader Warren Pickering’s seat of Packenham has not yet been called.

Hanson congratulated Wilson and said “she had a big job ahead of her to clear up Labor’s mess.

“For Victoria’s sake, I wish her and her team well,” Hanson said.
