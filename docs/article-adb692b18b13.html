Protesters call for regime change in Iran following deadly crash that killed 13 oil and gas workers

2026-11-19 19:09

Efrat (Effie) Lachter, Fox News

Published Nov. 19, 2026, 2:09 p.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Hundreds of protesters poured into the streets of western Iran on Tuesday following a deadly bus crash that killed 13 oil and gas workers, with demonstrations quickly escalating into anti-government chants, with some protesters calling for the return of the Pahlavi monarchy.

The unrest erupted in Abdanan, a city in Iran’s Ilam province near the Iraqi border, where residents initially gathered to demand accountability for Saturday’s fatal accident before turning their anger toward the Islamic Republic’s leadership.

Videos obtained by Fox News Digital showed large crowds marching through the streets, chanting “Death to the dictator!”

Iranian-American journalist and foreign policy analyst Lisa Daftari said the demonstrations underscore the deeper political tensions facing Tehran. “Abdanan is a reminder that the Islamic Republic sits on top of a dormant volcano,” Daftari told Fox News Digital. “These uprisings start with different catalysts, and in this case it was a local grievance, but they always arrive at the same conclusion.”

“The slogans we hear are ‘Death to the Dictator’ and ‘Javid Shah,’ not complaints about a policy. They are an indictment of the entire system, top to bottom,” she said.

New reports Wednesday indicated that Iranian security forces arrested at least seven people, including a 15-year-old boy, during the unrest in Abdanan.

The Hengaw Organization for Human Rights said two detainees had been released, but the whereabouts of five others remained unknown. The Kurdistan Human Rights Network separately reported that security forces fired tear gas at demonstrators and that internet access was disrupted during the crackdown.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard-affiliated Fars news agency acknowledged the demonstrations, reporting that approximately 200 to 300 people initially gathered in Abdanan’s Vali-e Asr Square before the crowd grew to roughly 1,000.

Fars reported that the slogans initially focused on the deadly accident but subsequently took on a political character. Police were deployed as protesters moved into surrounding streets, according to AFP’s Nov. 17 report.

The protests followed Saturday’s overturning of a bus transporting workers from the Dehloran gas processing project. Thirteen people were killed and more than a dozen injured, according to Iranian media reports.

Euronews Persian reported on Nov. 17 that provincial traffic police identified driver negligence, irregularities involving the company’s bus contract, and inadequate road barriers as factors contributing to the accident. Prosecutors have announced legal proceedings against the company and provincial transportation officials, although responsibility has not been established in court.

Authorities warned against allowing the demonstrations to escalate.

“Individuals who seek to exploit this incident to disrupt public order or incite public sentiment will be dealt with firmly,” Abdanan prosecutor Amrollah Misaie said, according to Iran’s judiciary-affiliated Mizan news agency, as quoted by AFP on Nov. 17.

Daftari, the Foreign Desk editor-in-chief, argued that the Iranian government’s greatest challenge may ultimately come from within, regardless of its confrontation with Washington and Jerusalem. “Whatever happens in the military standoff with the United States and Israel, Tehran will still face its most serious adversary at home,” she said.

She also pointed to the protesters’ willingness to return to the streets following the deadly crackdown earlier this year, saying that “despite thousands being killed by the regime for protesting in January, we still see bravery on the part of the Iranian people to tell the world of their grievances and to stand up to their murderers.”

At the United Nations’ daily briefing on Tuesday, Fox News Digital asked the U.N. spokesperson whether the organization was monitoring the situation, particularly the actions of Iranian security forces on the ground. “Well, we’re keeping an eye on the situation, and we want to make sure that all peaceful protests are allowed to proceed without obstruction and without harassment of protesters,” the spokesperson said and added, “Certainly our human rights colleagues will continue to monitor the situation as we’ve done in the past.”

The demonstrations carry particular significance because Abdanan was previously a flashpoint during Iran’s nationwide anti-government protests earlier this year.

Reuters reported on Jan. 9 that it had verified footage showing demonstrators marching through the city during the broader uprising against the Islamic Republic.

The city also witnessed renewed tensions during mourning ceremonies for protesters killed in the January unrest. Reuters reported on Feb. 18 that witnesses and activists said security forces opened fire on hundreds of mourners gathered at a cemetery in Abdanan.

Tuesday’s demonstrations mark another outbreak of public anger in a city with a recent history of confrontations with Iranian authorities.
