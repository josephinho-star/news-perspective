Romania issues air alert over suspected drone flying from Moldova, Ukraine

Meanwhile, Romania has issued a air alert to residents in the eastern part of the country amid concerns about a potential drone identified in the neighbouring Moldova, likely flying in from Ukraine.

Prefect of Iași County, Constantin Dolachi-Pelin, told Agerpress news agency that the drone entered Romanian territory from the Republic of Moldova.

Two F-18 fighter jets were also scrambled to respond to the incident, Romanian defence ministry said.

The warning also forced a brief suspension of flights at Iași international airport.

Putin, Trump discussed Ukraine war, bilateral relations in first call ahead US peace envoys visit

Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump spoke by phone for an hour on Tuesday, days after US envoys travelled to Moscow and Kyiv in a renewed push to revive stalled peace talks over Ukraine.

According to Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, the two leaders discussed the war in Ukraine, with Trump stressing his desire to achieve a breakthrough in peace efforts during his presidency.

Trump’s special envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff visited Moscow and Kyiv over the weekend, but the meetings failed to produce a breakthrough.Russia resumed large-scale attacks on Kyiv on Monday night after a brief lull that coincided with the US visit to the Ukrainian capital.

Ushakov said Putin gave Trump an “objective and detailed assessment” of developments on the battlefield. Putin has repeatedly sought to portray Russian forces as making gains at the front, while at times claiming the capture of Ukrainian territory that remains outside Russian control.

According to Ushakov, Trump argued during the call that ending the war in Ukraine could pave the way for a broader restoration of US-Russia relations.

Ushakov also said Putin stressed to Trump that Russia had “no hostile plans whatsoever” towards Europe.

Trump has in recent days insisted that Putin would not attack Nato territory, playing down concerns in European capitals that Russia could eventually test the alliance with some form of military incursion.

Putin, Trump discusses weekend envoy talks on Ukraine, Kremlin says

Meanwhile, we have just heard from the Kremlin on a phone call between US president Donald Trump and Russia’s Vladimir Putin.

The pair talked about the visit of US peace envoys to Moscow and Kyiv over the weekend, and prospects for their bilateral relations.

Kremlin claims that Trump would like to see US-Russia ties restored during his presidency, while Putin assured him that he had no hostile plans towards Europe.

'We will defend our country's sovereignty,' Hungary's PM Magyar says after move to expel Russian diplomats

Hungarian prime minister Péter Magyar commented on his government’s decision to expel ten Russian diplomats from the country.

In a very brief statement echoing what his foreign minister, Anita Orbán, announced earlier (11:09), he said:

“The Hungarian government has ordered ten diplomats from the Russian Embassy in Budapest to leave the country. They carried out activities in Hungary that are unacceptable for diplomats. We will defend our country’s sovereignty.”

He did not comment on Russia’s response after Moscow threatened with “harsh and painful” actions in retaliation (12:22).

Ukraine 'racing against time' to prepare for winter as allies must prepare for 'protracted' conflict, allies say

Back to the videoconference on Ukraine (15:29), Germany’s defence minister Boris Pistorius urged partners to help Kyiv prepare for winter as it faces “race against time,” as “we can expect Russian air raids to increase again once temperatures drop and the damage will be even greater.”

A senior Pentagon official Elbridge Colby also warned that allies “must be prepared for a protracted conflict and for Ukraine’s requirements to persist in the months and years ahead.”

“Europe’s greater assumption of responsibility for its own defence is fully consistent with a strong and viable Ukrainian defence, provided that Europe continues to act with requisite seriousness and urgency,” he said.

Colby also said Europe “must not slacken” in its remilitarisation efforts.

“Support for Ukraine contributes to Europe’s security by helping to rebuild Europe’s military strength and industrial capacity. But words are not enough. Real money and industrial capacity must be put to work. Again, I underline that this is happening, but we must not slacken. The United States stands ready to continue supporting this effort.”

Separately, Germany’s Pistorius also warned that Ukraine’s supporters face being “targeted by Russia” with “hybrid means to sow uncertainty in order to erode our social cohesion, democratic our democratic principles and weaken our willingness to support Ukraine.”

He recalled the recent attempted drone attack at Leipzig airport in this context, but insisted Germany was “unwavering” in its support to Ukraine.

“These attempts will not produce the intended outcome,” he said.

UK summons Serbian chargé d'affaires over Mladić funeral

The UK has joined the growing chorus of countries criticising Serbia for the pomp and the participation of state officials in yesterday’s funeral of Bosnian Serb war criminal Ratko Mladić.

In a short statement, the UK government said that:

“The UK’s Special Envoy to the Western Balkans, Dame Karen Pierce DCMG, summoned the Serbian Chargé d’Affaires to condemn the involvement of Serbian state and military officials in the funeral of Ratko Mladić.”

Europe minister Lord Wood of Anfield said that Mladić “presided over crimes that are among the worst committed in Europe since the second world war,” and so “the denial and whitewashing of his crimes is an affront to international law and directly undermines regional stability and trust.”

“The thoughts of the UK Government are with Mladić’s victims and their families. They deserve respect, compassion and accountability, not efforts to rewrite proven history. Political leaders and public institutions have a responsibility to look to the future, not to the past, and to act in ways that support regional reconciliation, not to reopen old wounds.

The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia was established by the UN Security Council and Serbia is obliged to respect its judgments.

That is why today we summoned the Serbian Chargé d’Affaires. Glorification of a convicted war criminal directly undermines trust, fuels division and is incompatible with the process of reconciliation within Bosnia and Herzegovina and the region.

The UK remains firmly committed to international justice, accountability, and lasting reconciliation across the Western Balkans and will continue to work with partners across the region and internationally in support of peace, justice and a secure and prosperous future for all communities.”

Germany to deliver more Patriot missiles to Ukraine

Germany will deliver Patriot air defence missiles to Ukraine from its national stocks, defence minister Boris Pistorius.

“I decided to deliver urgently needed PAC-2 interceptor missiles from Bundeswehr stocks to Ukraine,” Pistorius said at a videoconference of defence ministers from countries supporting Ukraine, in comments reported by Reuters.

Pistorius said Germany would also step up the provision of air-to-air missiles and would continue to deliver drones and long-range ammunition to Ukraine.

'No place in Europe for the glorification of war criminals,' EU's Kallas says

EU’s foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas ramped up pressure on Serbia, condemning “the public glorification afforded to a convicted war criminal in Belgrade” after yesterday’s funeral of Ratko Mladić.

Kallas said the ceremony was “an affront to the memory of the victims, their families, and all those who suffered during the conflicts in the Western Balkans.”

“True reconciliation cannot be built on revisionism, the denial of genocide or the honouring of perpetrators.

Serbia’s path into the European Union requires an unequivocal commitment to fundamental European values and historical truth. There is no place in Europe for the glorification of war criminals.”

Several other EU foreign ministers also condemned the memorial service and put a question mark on Serbia’s accession path, including Austria’s Beate Meinl-Reisinger, Lithuania’s Kęstutis Budrys,andSweden’s Maria M Stenergard.

Separately, Bosnia and Herzegovina’s foreign minister Elmedin Konaković ordered two Serbian diplomats to leave the country in protest at Serbian government officials taking part in the funeral. Earlier, he also withdrew Bosnian diplomats from Serbia (12:48).

European countries say they would support restrictions on trades with West Bank settlements

The foreign ministers of Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Iceland, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Spain, Sweden and the UK, have issued a statement saying they would support or consider restrictions on trade in goods with Israeli settlements in the West Bank that are illegal under international law.

The countries confirmed “their intention to introduce national and/or support European restrictions on trade in goods with settlements which are illegal under international law, or that they are actively considering these and other measures, in accordance with their national procedures,” the nations’ foreign ministers said in a statement.

“The government of Israel must immediately halt the expansion of settlements and civilian administrative powers, ensure accountability for settler violence, and investigate allegations against Israeli forces,” it added.

The (relatively new) UK foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, has been speaking in the House of Commons, setting out the government’s position.

Taking a much stronger stance than his predecessor, Yvette Cooper, Miliband said the government would now take the official stance that Israel’s occupation of the West Bank was unlawful in its entirety, while announcing an import ban on goods from illegal settlements in the occupied territories, among a number of other policies.

Ukraine can buy Patriot air defence system parts from EU's support loan, von der Leyen says

The European Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen, has said that Ukraine will be able to buy crucial parts for Patriot air defense systems through the next €3.2bn (£2.7bn; $3.7bn) disbursement from the EU’s support loan.

“Together with Nato secretary general Rutte, I welcome the agreement by Member States on the derogation for Ukraine to purchase crucial products for Patriot air defence systems,” she said in a statement.

Ukraine heavily relies on advanced air defence systems, mainly US Patriot interceptors, to protect its skies from Russian ballistic missile attacks. But this year, supplies of Patriot missiles have run out, leaving Ukraine vulnerable to strikes.

After a brutal summer of deadly Russian missile and drone attacks, Ukraine last week requested an exemption from Buy European procurement rules to acquire US-made Patriot interceptors using the EU’s €90bn support loan.
