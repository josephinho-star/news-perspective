Dealbook Summit 2026 live updates: Altman says 'hard to imagine the AI industry without Jensen'

2026-11-18 19:47

Artificial intelligence, trade tensions, and the need to create trust have been recurring themes at the New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday.

Andrew Ross Sorkin, founder and editor of DealBook and co-anchor of CNBC's "Squawk Box," has been sitting down in New York with prominent business, economic and political leaders to discuss a wide range of newsmaking issues.

The conference comes as OpenAI rival Anthropic is about to make its public debut on Thursday, and it follows a series of widespread tech outages that have grounded airplanes, snarled email, and stymied banking transactions, among other disruptions — a dramatic reminder of society's dependency on these services.

In discussing the balancing act that tech leaders are confronting trying to push ahead with AI innovation while attempting to guarantee public safety, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said, "The race dynamic in the fog of war, I think, is a bad thing. I think this is a time where more caution, not less caution, is warranted."

Skydance co-CEO David Ellison emphasized the need to rebuild trust in media organizations.

"In a world, you know, where very quickly you're not going to be able to believe your eyes, we think having institutions that earn trust every single day is incredibly important," he said.

Meanwhile, Canada Prime Minister Mark Carney expressed some frustration over trade relations with the U.S. He said a new deal "should have been done six months ago, but we're not going to sign something that doesn't make sense for Canada."

He said he's pressing on in forging other trade partnerships. "We got a lot to do, so we would like to do the deal. But if the U.S. doesn't want to do the deal, we're busy," he said.

Key guests yet to come Wednesday include SpaceX founder and CEO Elon Musk, Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella.
