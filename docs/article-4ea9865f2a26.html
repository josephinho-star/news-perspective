At least 31 killed as fuel tanker crash ignites large fire on Cairo highway

2026-11-26 00:39

A crash involving a fuel tanker ignited a huge fire on a highway surrounding Cairo on Wednesday, killing at least 31 people, Egyptian authorities said.

The blaze on the ring road, a key artery that encircles the Egyptian capital, also injured at least 21 people and left more than two dozen cars and motorcycles charred, according to the latest figures released by the health ministry.

The stretch of the highway where the crash occurred was in Waraq, a densely populated neighbourhood in Giza, part of the Greater Cairo area, the ministry said.

At the scene, rescue workers were pulling people out of burning cars. An Associated Press video showed firefighters working to put out the fire spreading among the cars. Hundreds of people gathered near the scene in the Warraq district of Imbaba, a Cairo suburb.

The Egyptian president, Abdel Fatah al-Sisi, expressed his condolences to the victims in a statement. The public prosecutor’s office said DNA investigations were under way to identify the victims.

The health minister, Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, said the injured were taken to nearby hospitals and that he would head to the scene. He said the death toll was preliminary, raising concerns it could rise further.

Deadly traffic incidents claim thousands of lives every year in Egypt, which has a poor transportation safety record. Speeding, bad roads and poor enforcement of traffic laws are the main reasons behind most of the crashes.

In January, 21 people were killed and 16 were injured in a multicar pileup on a road near the southern province of Beni Suef.
