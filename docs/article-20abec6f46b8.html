Judge declares mistrial in Lindsay Clancy murder case after jury fails to reach unanimous verdict – as it happened

2026-09-04 20:36

In a dramatic conclusion to a trial that has gripped, saddened and divided the nation, the judge declared a mistrial in the case of Lindsay Clancy, the Massachusetts mother who admits to killing her three children in 2023. The trial collapsed without a verdict after the jury deliberated for roughly 38 hours but could not reach a consensus over whether Clancy was criminally responsible for the killings. The nine women and three men had twice already told the judge earlier this week they couldn’t reach a unanimous decision and had been sent back to deliberate further. But today, they came back once more, declaring that “with a heavy heart” they could not “and will not be able to” reach a consensus. After a last-ditch appeal to the state’s highest court from the defense was denied, Judge William Sullivan declared a mistrial. He thanked the jury, which was split 11-1, for their efforts and hard work in serving on such a difficult case, and set a status hearing for 29 September.

The Plymouth county district attorney said it would not make a decision on whether to pursue a retrial today. Tim Cruz told reporters outside the courthouse that this case was “not about the healthcare system or how women are treated in it”. The case, he said, “was and has always been about getting justice for those three little babies. We’re here at this courthouse today because those children are not.”

Clancy’s defense attorney Kevin Reddington placed blame on the mistrial on the holdout juror that prevented a verdict, but said Clancy would be ready for another trial, should it come in the fall. Reddington claimed he had “won” the case and “crushed” the prosecution’s arguments (reminder: we don’t know in which direction the jury was split), and said Clancy is a “brave” and “wonderful” person whose life is “a tragedy”. He said he hoped the case would spark change for how women experiencing postpartum mental health issues are treated in the United States.

Reddington also said other countries are “light years ahead” of the United States in treating women who speak about their postpartum mental health issues in a way that keeps the children with them in a safe environment while they get treatment.

Reddington also said he hoped the case might spark changes in society when it comes to postpartum health care.

This case highlighted “the woeful deficiency that women have when they are pregnant, when they have babies, and God forbid when they are on medication because of postpartum psychosis,” he said.

Asked if he would do anything differently in a second trial, Reddington said: “Why would I? I won the case.”

A reminder that we don’t know how the jury was split.

He also said he’d want the same 11 out of the 12 jurors, and finding jurors who weren’t aware of the case would be the real challenge.

I don’t know if we will ever be able to get jurors that aren’t aware of this case anywhere. New York, Washington, Georgia, Japan, India, I don’t know anywhere.

Clancy 'will be ready' for potential retrial in fall, defense attorney says

Asked about previous comments that client Lindsay Clancy was not emotionally ready for another trial, Reddington said, “Judge Sullivan is indicating that we are looking at sometime in the fall … She will be ready.”

Clancy will continue to stay in Tewksbury state hospital, he added.

Clancy's defense lawyer says he 'crushed' the prosecution's case

Lindsay Clancy’s defense attorney Kevin Reddington is now speaking to reporters outside the courthouse.

“Obviously this is not a time for gloating, bragging, expressing victory. It’s a very sad case,” he said.

He said he “crushed” the prosecution’s case, adding: “I’m representing a woman who is a fantastic, wonderful individual. This woman is so brave, so awesome.”

She sat there through seven weeks of the trial, where nobody had anything bad to say about her except the two prosecutors.

Cruz said he acknowledged that postpartum depression impacts the lives of many women, but argued that the evidence suggests Lindsay Clancy was “in control of her actions when she committed those homicides”. (A reminder that Clancy’s defense argued that she suffered from severe postpartum psychosis, which is different to postpartum depression).

The prosecution “did our part”, Cruz went on, adding that he’s proud of the work they did in the case. He particularly praised assistant district attorneys Jennifer Sprague and ShananBuckingham, who “are both mothers themselves, and went home to their families after each long day in court.”

They embraced the most difficult trial assignment with utmost professionalism and always with the thoughts of Cora, Dawson and little Callan on their minds.

Cruz went on to acknowledge that this has been “an emotional trial for everybody”.

This has been emotional for the Duxbury community. It’s been emotional for you members of the media.

He goes on to centre the children, Cora, Dawson and Callan in his remarks, arguing that the case “is not about the healthcare system or how women are treated in it”.

This was and has always been about getting justice for those three little babies. We’re here at this courthouse today because those children are not … Our priority has always been to fight for Cora, for Dawson and for Callan.

Prosecution says decision on retrial won't be made today

Speaking outside the courthouse, Tim Cruz, the Plymouth county district attorney, has said in a statement that there will be no decisions made today regarding a retrial.

I’ll be meeting with our team and we will address this case further at the appropriate time in the context of an official court proceeding. So you will not get an answer on that question today.
