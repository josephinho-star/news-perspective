Lecornu also confirmed that France will release 10 million barrels of diesel from its strategic stocks to try to ease tight supplies amid surging energy prices stemming from the wars in Iran and in Ukraine.

He said: “Last Friday, President (Emmanuel) Macron convened an emergency meeting of the G7, which decided to mobilize strategic reserves in a coordinated manner. As part of this effort, we will make 10 million barrels of diesel from our reserves available to distributors in France.”

Lecornu said the move “will automatically lower prices at the pump by an estimated amount ranging from 0.12 to 0.18 euros per liter of diesel.”

A formal arrest warrant has been issued against Germany’s former top spy, with a judge ordering him to be held in pre-trial detention a day after he was arrested on suspicion of treason and spying on behalf of an overseas intelligence agency.

August Hanning, 80, is accused of buying secret documents from a former colleague and using them to advise a foreign intelligence service, according to the country’s office of the federal prosecutor.

Wednesday’s formal arrest warrant followed Hanning’s arrest on Tuesday, along with that of his former office head, Manfred D. It has sent shock waves through Germany’s political and intelligence community, with analysts calling it potentially the country’s biggest espionage scandal since the end of the cold war.

A lawyer for Hanning denied he had been involved in any wrongdoing on Wednesday, telling the conservative daily newspaper Die Welt: “While it is true that he was in possession of confidential documents, the allegation that he passed on information contained therein to the detriment of Germany, let alone for payment, is incorrect.”

West should 'learn from the men of Ancient Greece', says Rubio in Athens speech

US secretary of state Marco Rubio called for the nations of the West to return to their civilizational roots and restore the values that drove the rise of Europe and North America to global dominance for centuries in a speech in Athens.

Rubio said: “It has become fashionable today to believe that enlightened foreign policy means prioritizing the ‘global community’ over one’s own people. And I think here, too, we have much more to learn from the men of Ancient Greece. They knew that there was nothing selfish or wrong about loyalty to your nation, nor anything selfish or wrong about fighting to defend its interests above all else.”

Rubio also urged Europe to uphold its traditional values.

“Today, we find ourselves at a crossroads.

“The next decade will write the story of the next century. The course we take will determine whether the West maintains its place at the head of the world, or resigns itself to a future of atrophy, and servitude, and decline. We will either choose to act now or lose the choice to act at all.”

France summoned Iran’s ambassador over what foreign ministry spokesperson Pascal Confavreux described as an Iranian disinformation campaign targeting France’s secondary school student protest movement.

The envoy was told of the “absolutely unacceptable” nature of false content disseminated online by official Iranian foreign ministry accounts, Confavreux said in a statement, adding that Tehran’s October 6 summons of the French ambassador was baseless.

“These undignified provocations must cease,” he said.

Iran on Tuesday summoned France’s envoy in Tehran over the ongoing student protests, accusing Paris of the “violent treatment” of demonstrators and of extensive human rights violations, according to Iranian state media.

Back to the protests in France and the administrative court has suspended the ban on gatherings around 45 schools in Haute-Garonne, Occitanie, seeing it as an attack on the freedom to demonstrate, BFMTV reported.

The ban “is neither appropriate nor proportionate and whose necessity is not established, constitutes a serious and manifestly illegal infringement of the freedom of demonstration and the freedom of assembly,” the administrative court of Toulouse said in its decision.

Russian missiles and drones struck Ukrainian cities including Kyiv, Pryluky and Kremenchuk overnight on Tuesday. Two people were killed in Kyiv, while a strike destroyed an apartment block in Pryluky, in Ukraine’s northern Chernihiv region, killing at least nine people including three children.

Following the attack on a teacher in La Ciotat who was doused with gasoline during student protests, three 16-year-olds will appear before the Marseille juvenile court in February 2027, newspaper Liberation reported.

More from the Greek capital where visiting US Secretary of state, Marco Rubio, has been dominating the news.

In a day packed with talk of energy and defense, gas ‘corridors’ and trade, counterterrorism and US military bases - all earning Greece the encomium of being the region’s “pillar of stability” according to Rubio – it is the foreign policy chief, himself, who has made the news.

Rubio’s decision to use the trip to deliver a major address “on democracy and its future” from the ancient Pnyx has imbued the visit with an air of excitement.

Until last night it was far from sure that the US secretary of state would have access to the setting because of security reasons.

Only hours before more than 1,500 protestors, appalled by the war in Iran, had marched through the streets of central Athens waving banners declaring him persona non grata.

Excerpts of Rubio’s address released by the State department suggest he will focus on the shared values that have united Americans and Europeans in what has also been described as a rallying cry for “a great renewal of the Western spirit.”

But there are also fears the US secretary of state may use the setting to make a speech in the spirit of the blistering attack on Europe issued by Vice President JD Vance at the Munich Security conference last year. The speech is scheduled to take place in the next half hour.

Poll shows majority of French people supports high school protests, demands, but uncertain about their future

A new Elabe poll for BFMTV shows that 55% of French people support the high school protests.

While 35% oppose the movement, 10% say they have no clear view.

Support for the students’ demands is even higher, with 72% considering them legitimate – a view shared by majorities across the political spectrum, from left to right.

However, the public is far more divided over what should happen next: 55% believe students have made their point and should end the protests, while 27% think they should carry on.

'Impunity must end,' Le Pen says, as she offers her support to police over student protests

French far-right National Rally leader Marine Le Pen offered her strong support for the police, saying “impunity must end,” and “those who vandalise our schools, and their parents when they are minors, must pay for the damage they cause.”

“The French people should no longer have to finance the vandals,” she told reporters on a visit to Cournon-d’Auvergne.

She also said the police should have the support of the nation, and “not face constant accusations from those in power,” as she chose to use the protests to criticise the French president, Emmanuel Macron.

“While the far left spreads false accusations to stoke violence, Emmanuel Macron once again chooses to undermine those who protect the French people,” she said.

Le Pen leads the presidential polls ahead of next year’s election, with 33-34% of vote share in the first round, well ahead of Édouard Philippe and Jean-Luc Mélenchon at around 16-18%.
