Russia’s ambassador to the United States, AlexanderDarchiev, has died “suddenly” at the age of 66 after just one and a half years in the job, Moscow’s foreign ministry announced on Wednesday.

The ministry called Darchiev an “outstanding diplomat, an experienced leader and a kind colleague”. Moscow did not give more details on the death.

Darchiev previously served as Russia’s ambassador to Canada between 2021 and 2024. He was appointed to the US role in March last year, shortly after Donald Trump returned to power, and several years into Moscow’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

Darchiev was a specialist in Russia-US relations and was the director of the foreign ministry’s North America department between 2014 and 2020.

In 2022, when Russia attacked Ukraine, Darchiev called the move a “necessary measure”.

Marco Rubio, the US secretary of state, has emerged as a superfan of the University of Florida’s football team, spending more than $1.2m of taxpayers’ money on frequent personal trips to watch them play, a report published on Wednesday claims.

Ethics watchdogs have questioned the necessity of the country’s top diplomat shuttling so often between Washington DC and Gainesville, where his son Anthony has a walk-on role as a running back for the team known as the Gators.

An investigation by the Washington Post found that Rubio has attended at least 10 games in the last two seasons, including five this fall, and that of 23 total trips made to Gainesville on military aircraft, 15 were “personal” in nature.

The outlet said that while Rubio is prohibited from flying commercially, because of security protocols that also cover the secretary of defense and the FBI and CIA directors, his use of government transport “appears to significantly outpace that of his predecessors”.

Using published defense department rates listing the cost per hour of different kinds of military aircraft, the Post calculated that the flights alone have cost more than $1.2m.

Most recently, on 7 November, Rubio arrived in Gainesville onboard an air force C-37, a plane used frequently for the VIP transport of high-ranking government officials, and was seen on the sidelines as the Gators lost 23-7 to Oklahoma. Anthony Rubio played no part in the game.

White House threatens House Democrats with criminal investigations ahead of oversight sweep

The White House’s top lawyer, Will Scharf, has sent a letter to Democratic congressman Robert Garcia, the incoming chair of the House oversight committee, threatening Democrats with criminal investigations if they launch oversight inquiries purely “deployed to harass and aggrandize” members of the Trump administration that could verge into “blatant criminality”.

This comes as Democrats have vowed to investigate cabinet members and issue subpoenas to allies in the president’s orbit. Scharf, however, said that the White House is monitoring “abusive and capricious threats that are now proliferating from Democrat legislators who seek to abuse the legislative process to intimidate and to interfere with the lawful functioning of government”.

Punchbowl News was first to report the news of the letter.

The House oversight committee, the lower chamber’s main investigative body, is able to investigate government offices or individuals; however, its inquiries must serve a valid legislative purpose. The committee cannot act as a formal law enforcement or criminal trial agency.

In his letter, Scharf accused Garcia and other colleagues of displaying “malicious and improper features” in their promises to conduct investigations in the new Congress. The White House counsel listed several statements from incumbent and incoming Democrats which, he claims, are personal grievances as opposed to furthering a “legitimate legislative task”.

“Issuing unlawful subpoenas with the intention of depriving citizens oftheir constitutional rights and privileges may warrant further investigation,” Scharf added.

Garcia, however, remained undeterred in response.

“It appears Donald Trump has something to hide. Let’s be clear,” the top Democrat said in a statement. “Congress has wide latitude to conduct investigations and oversight, an authority inherent in the power to legislate.”

He added that the administration “continues to weaponize the government” with threats of “unconstitutional attacks” on lawmakers.

“We are a co-equal branch of government and will not be deterred by baseless threats from a weaponized [justice department] or from the president’s lawyers,” Garcia said.

The United States could lose its measles elimination status this week, an event likely to cement health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr’s legacy of meddling in vaccine policies amid the worst outbreak of the virus in the US in 35 years.

If the US loses its measles-free status, it would join Canada and several European and Asian nations that had eliminated the disease only for it to return.

“That is a tremendous step backwards,” said Dr Paul Offit, director of the Vaccine Education Center, an attending physician in the division of infectious diseases at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, and a prominent critic of Kennedy.

A panel of eight independent experts will consider the epidemiological evidence in Washington DC, part of a group called the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), a regional branch of the World Health Organization (WHO). The PAHO director will make the final call.

“Independent of what PAHO says, measles is now endemic in the US. Measles has a foothold in the US,” said Offit. “We’re going to see it this year, we’re going to see it next year,” because the US “critically decreased the percentage” of people vaccinated.

The US is in the midst of its worst measles outbreak since 1991 with more than 5,400 cases nationally, according to the Johns Hopkins University International Vaccine Access Center tracker. Just this year, six people have died in Pennsylvania, a child died in New York on election day and an infant died in Wisconsin days later. Nearly every US state has an active outbreak.
