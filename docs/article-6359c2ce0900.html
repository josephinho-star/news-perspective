FCC signs off on SpaceX’s plan to put up to 1 million data center satellites into space

2026-11-25 17:44

In an announcement on Wednesday, the FCC says it authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million AI chip-equipped satellites as part of the Elon Musk-owned company’s “Starmind” initiative. With several others also pursuing the idea of putting AI data centers in space, including Google and Blue Origin, this is the first approval the agency has granted for a full “constellation” of AI satellites.

The FCC’s approval requires SpaceX to deploy at least one satellite by 2033, and meet the 1 million satellite milestone by 2040. The FCC also deferred SpaceX’s request to dispose of satellites located above 600km in space “graveyard” orbits, rather than having them burn up in the Earth’s atmosphere, saying the company hasn’t provided “sufficient information” about its disposal process plans.

SpaceX has described its Starmind project as a way to “capture solar energy in space to power” AI capabilities on Earth. The company says Starmind will transmit data to Earth using high-speed lasers that connect to Starlink. It plans to produce chips for its satellites at an upcoming Terafab facility.

Google has already taken its first steps toward putting compute into space, with the launch of a “Project Suncatcher” AI server satellite last month. Jeff Bezos’ Blue Origin will carry out the first dedicated heavy-lift launch for Project Suncatcher in 2029.
