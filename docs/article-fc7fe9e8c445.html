Turkey bans Anyma concert after ‘satanic’ imagery claims

2026-09-03 14:36

The İstanbul Governor’s Office has banned electronic music producer and DJ Anyma’s concert scheduled for September 12 after social media users linked visuals from his performances to “satanic” rituals and symbols.

The concert was part of Anyma’s 2026 “AEDEN” Global Tour and was scheduled to take place at the Ataköy Marina Arena in İstanbul. Anyma is the stage name of Italian-American electronic music producer Matteo Milleri, whose performances combine electronic music with large-scale audiovisual productions. His official website had listed İstanbul as a tour stop on September 12.

The cancellation followed a social media campaign targeting images used in Anyma’s stage shows. Some people described figures and symbols appearing in the performances as references to satanic rituals and called for the concert to be canceled. Turkish media reported that the İstanbul Governor’s Office subsequently decided not to allow the event to proceed.

There has been no detailed public statement from the governor’s office explaining the decision in full, according to reports. The “satanic” characterization has largely appeared in social media posts and media coverage surrounding the cancellation rather than as an independently established description of Anyma’s artistic concept.

The decision is the latest in a longer series of concert and festival restrictions in Turkey. Local officials have cited public safety, public order, environmental concerns and social sensitivities in various cases, while some cancellations followed campaigns by conservative or religious groups against particular artists or performances. Turkish commentators have described the pattern as extending back to at least 2022.

In May 2022 the Eskişehir Governor’s Office imposed a 15-day ban on outdoor events including concerts and festivals, citing security concerns. The decision forced the cancellation of Anadolu Fest, a music festival scheduled to take place in the city. The governor’s office later told a court that it considered the festival a security risk, citing a terrorist attack in nearby Bursa.

In August 2022 the Burhaniye District Governor’s Office in the western province of Balıkesir refused permission for the Zeytinli Rock Festival, citing public safety and health, public order, environmental protection and complaints about security, traffic and environmental problems at previous festivals. The festival had been scheduled for August 17-21.

That same year, two concerts by Iranian musician Mohsen Namjoo in Bursa and Konya were canceled after conservative groups campaigned against his Turkey tour, accusing him of using Quranic verses in his songs. Namjoo denied the accusations and said he had never mocked or insulted the Quran.

In February the Beşiktaş District Governor’s Office canceled concerts by Russian deathcore band Slaughter to Prevail and Polish metal band Behemoth at the Zorlu Performing Arts Center while also banning other ticketed events at the venue and the greater Zorlu Center complex for two days. The district governor’s office said the performances were incompatible with “our societal values.”

In September 2025 İstanbul officials canceled French singer Enrico Macias’s planned concert after calls for protests over his support for Israel. Officials cited concerns that demonstrations could lead to legal problems and grievances.

A month later İstanbul officials canceled British singer Robbie Williams’ planned concert, citing public safety concerns after calls for protests over his past performances in Israel and his family background.

The repeated cancellations have started debate over the broad grounds used to restrict concerts and festivals in Turkey. A review published by the Institute for Democracy and Education described the Anadolu Fest ban in 2022 as the beginning of a larger pattern, while noting that subsequent decisions have used grounds ranging from public order and security to environmental concerns and religious sensitivities.
