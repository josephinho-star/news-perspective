Rob Lowe accuses Hollywood of blacklisting Ben Shapiro’s ‘Confession,’ defends decision to star in right-wing movie

2026-11-19 19:34

“We got turned down by every single film festival,” he said. “Summarily frog-marched out of any conversation.”

Lowe, 63, described the movie’s rejection as “all political,” explaining it was a “really interesting assessment of not only where our world is, but where Hollywood is as well.”

“People are definitely at a place where a lot of people cannot see a work just for its work,” he said in the podcast.

“They have to do the math of what does it mean and what will people think, and does that mean I’m on this side or does that mean.”

Lowe shared that Shapiro’s media company, the Daily Wire, eventually distributed the movie, which wasn’t initially supposed to happen.

The actor admitted people thought he was “crazy” for accepting the role alongside James Franco and Nick Nolte, also noting that it’s “very different from anything” he’s ever done.

He recalled reading the Shapiro-written script, calling the latter and saying, “This is fantastic.”

“And he was like, ‘Well, do you think you know anybody, 50-ish leading man who would not be scared just because I wrote it?’ And I go, ‘Yeah, me.’ And he goes, ‘You’re good with it?’ I said, ‘I’ve worked with the most left-wing writers in the world. Why wouldn’t I also work with the most right-wing writers in the world?'” he continued.

Lowe explained he was “old enough and established enough” to take on the role, saying, “People know me well enough to know that I’m not an ideologue at all.”

“I’m not telling anybody how to live, how to think on either side of the aisle. I’ve campaigned for Republicans. I’ve campaigned for Democrats,” the “West Wing” star added.

“For every Jane Fonda I have a Ben Shapiro. For every Jimmy Kimmel I’ve got Bill O’Reilly,” Lowe continued.

The “St. Elmo’s Fire” star clarified that “what’s lacking” in some people’s lives is others with opposing views despite not agreeing.

The Václav Marhoul-directed movie was released to the public on Nov. 11 after premiering at the Warsaw International Film Festival in October.
