Europe 'faces window of vulnerability until 2030,' Dutch chief of defence warns

The chief of defence of the Dutch armed forces, Gen Onno Eichelsheim, said that Europe urgently needs to learn from the lessons of Ukraine, as Europe is “facing a window of vulnerability until 2030” as it races to rearm itself.

Speaking at the Euronews Defence and Space Summit, he said there is “a gap between today’s threats and an armed force that will only be ready several years from now.”

“Within that window, we must ensure that strong deterrence is in place, and if deterrence fails, we have to fight,” he said.

Eichelsheim also spoke about the need to make the right decisions on procurement and industrial cooperation and work together, not in solely national interests, to move at pace required. “We don’t need 27 different drone programmes,” he said.

“The success of European defence cooperation will not be measured by the number of programmes we create, but by the equipment we put in the hands of our soldiers,” he said.

He said the EU’s tools “only work if we use them in the right way,” whereas the European defence matchmaking programmes at the moment are “like Tinder, with all the same frustrations.”

“To make a match, you need a sufficient number of participants on the platform and once a match is made, someone must take action. And it is clear that member states, my own included, are not putting all of our project projects on that platform.

Furthermore, the information provided is often not detailed enough to enable meaningful matches, just as on Tinder. Profiles do not always reflect all reality, and even when you do get a match, someone has to respond.”

At one point, fearing that he’s taking this analogy a bit too far, he rushed to assure the audience that he does not, actually, spend that much time on Tinder and is happily married.

But he continued with his serious point:

“Too often we still begin by asking what share of defence industry contracts can my own country secure? Only after we have established that we look at partners, and only then do we accept attempts to make defence cooperation work. So we must reverse that logic.”

Ukraine struggling to cover funding gap as war costs surge with drones, missiles, PM says

Meanwhile, Ukraine’s total war costs will reach $155bn this year and the government is struggling to close a $27bn funding gap to ensure it can still conduct combat operations at the start of 2027,prime minister Sergii Koretskyi said.

Koretskyi said the government planned to cover $7bn of the gap by postponing expenditure, reallocating money from other parts of the budget and cutting some costs, and was in talks with partners on how to cover the remaining $20bn, Reuters reported.

It is the first time Kyiv has released an estimate of its annual war costs, meaning there is no comparable figure for 2025, but officials have said that the cost of the conflict has risen as the use of missile and drone technology has increased.

Russia's hybrid threats no longer 'an eastern flank problem' as they 'move closer to our red lines,' Lithuanian chief of defence says

Lithuanian chief of defence Raimundas Vaikšnoras issued a similar warning about Russia’s escalating actions in Europe.

“We often imagine that war begins with tanks crossing the border. We wait for a clear Article 5 scenario.

But Russia is already operating below [the threshold for] Article 5. In Lithuania, we see indirect these threats as drone attacks in our airspace, sabotage and arson, cyberattacks, GPS jamming, the use of illegal migration as a tool …

For years, it was seen as an eastern flank problem. Not any more. We see the same methods increasingly used across Europe. The purpose is clear to test our resiliency, find our weak points and challenge ally unity.

So the question is, if this is happening now, what is next?

Russia will continue to test how far it can go if there is no effective response. Each successful test creates space for the next one.

More pressure on critical infrastructure, energy network and our societies and attempt to slow down Nato decision making process before conventional conflict even begins.

Today, Russia operates below of Article 5. Tomorrow it may test how close it can get to that line.”

He warned that “we should not assume” that Russia will remain at the current threat level.

“It will test our limits. It will move closer to our red lines, and it may eventually test whether these lines can be erased.”

Vaikšnoras warned that meanwhile “all European procurement systems and legal frameworks still often work at peacetime speed.”

“If it takes years to test, buy and field new technologies, we risk buying yesterday’s technology for tomorrow’s wars,” he warned.

Five dead, 14 injured after overnight Russian strikes on Ukraine

Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine faced “a difficult attack” from Russia overnight with “nearly 190 attack drones, as well as ballistic missiles.”

“The main target was energy infrastructure in the capital and the Kyiv region. Residential buildings were also damaged. Work is ongoing to restore electricity and water supplies everywhere they are currently unavailable.”

“In the morning, a drone struck a grocery supermarket in Ivankovychi. First responders are extinguishing the fire. Kyiv is also under drone attack. A warehouse building was hit, and one person was killed. There were strikes on critical infrastructure in the Zhytomyr, Mykolaiv, Kirovohrad, and Rivne regions. There was also damage in the Odesa, Sumy, Kharkiv, Kherson, and Cherkasy regions.

As of now, fourteen people have been injured across the country as a result of all these strikes. Tragically, five people have been killed, including a child.”

Russia issues rare warning to Nato over Kaliningrad

Russia has warned Nato it would be ready to resort to nuclear weapons if the alliance attempted to cut off Kaliningrad, a Russian exclave that borders the Baltic Sea, Reuters reported.

In a document sent to Nato, seen by Reuters, and first reported by Lithuanian media, Moscow accused the alliance of a “dangerous and reckless course” that entailed “high risks of the outbreak of a direct armed conflict”.

The document said this included the possibility “of Russian strikes against decision-making centres in the alliance’s member states right from the outset of the conflict”.

“Russia will be ready to use the entire arsenal of forces and capabilities at its disposal, including nuclear weapons, in order to defend its territory should Nato countries undertake any attempt aimed at isolating the Kaliningrad Region from the rest of the country,” said the text, classed as a “non-paper” — diplomatic jargon for an informal document.

Nato spokesperson Allison Hart confirmed Russia had sent a non-paper to the alliance but did not disclose its contents.

“We have sent a response, noting that Nato is a defensive Alliance and none of our activities or exercises pose a risk to any part of Russia. We strongly denounce the threat of force, including any irresponsible nuclear rhetoric. We call on Russia to end its unprovoked war in Ukraine,” she said.

Lithuania dismissed the Russian warning “completely baseless and seen as a measure of psychological pressure,”Deividas Matulionis, national security aide to president Gitanas Nausėda, told local media, as reported by LRT.

Brexit 'the biggest lie of the last 30 years,' Macron says

Meanwhile, with Brexit and the question of Britain’s relationship with the EU back in the spotlight in the UK, it’s worth picking up some fresh comments from France’s Emmanuel Macron, who is now on a state visit in Spain.

Speaking with students at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid last night, Macron took aim at Brexit calling it “the biggest lie of the last 30 years.”

In a long defence of Europe’s unique integration process – where, according to Macron, even nationalist leaders have come to appreciate the bloc’s role – he described Brexit as a “disastrous exception” to the rule.

“Brexit is the biggest lie of the last 30 years.

Those who said, ‘We’ll be richer,’ eight points of GDP less 10 years on. We’ll solve the immigration problem; it’s more complicated than before. And we’re going to be a huge success in international trade, it’s even worse.”

We will hear from him again later today, as he is expected to take part in a press conference with Spain’s Pedro Sánchez early afternoon.

Russia moves towards mass attacks on energy infrastructure, Ukraine's PM says

Russia has moved to mass attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure,Ukrainian prime minister Sergii Koretskyi said, after overnight strikes on energy facilities in the capital and other regions, Reuters reported.

“This was the first time such a massive and combined attack had taken place since the end of the last heating season,” Koretskyi said on Telegram.

'It will be a dark winter for Europe,' Belgium's defence minister warns

Belgian defence minister Theo Francken said “there will be more hybrid threat actions” in the coming months, as he starkly warned “it will be a dark winter for Europe.”

Speaking at Euronews’s Defence and Space Summit, Francken said hybrid warfare “is not something that Russia [has been] doing for two or three years,” but for much longer and it has “expertise” in this area, often making it difficult for western countries to investigate them and attribute responsibility.

He particularly worried about Russia’s increased use of jet-powered drones.

“We need to scale up our air defences and we need to have more stocks, scale up production. Everything has to go faster,” he said.

He said Belgium used to be “the worse pupil in class” two years ago with low defence spending, but it has since improved and ramped it up its planning.

He said Europe got too used to its comfortable welfare state protections at the cost of adequate spending on defence, and this is now changing.

“Europe has been very good in investing in soft power, in social security. And it’s beautiful to have a pension system, to have social welfare systems [for] when somebody gets sick, when somebody lost his job – we have everything. But when there’s war or there’s hybrid threats, we don’t have anything. So yeah, we’re in a really difficult position.”

Debris had been found at the premises of one of the western embassies in the centre of Ukraine’s capital, Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko said this morning, citing preliminary information.
