100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade postponed: What to know

2026-11-25 21:46

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade has been postponed ahead of an expected nor'easter in New York City, marking the first time the parade has been rescheduled due to weather.

The parade has been moved from Thursday to Saturday due to a forecast of strong winds and heavy rain, according to The Associated Press.

All the balloons that were filled up on Wednesday will be deflated and returned to New Jersey for storage until Saturday.

"With severe weather expected on Thanksgiving Day and in close partnership with our city partners, the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will now march on Saturday, November 28th," Macy's Thanksgiving Parade Executive Producer Will Coss told reporters.

Coss noted that organizers have spent two years preparing for Saturday's event.

"Our team is now working through the complex operational logistics required to bring the Parade to life on Saturday, and we will share additional details as they are finalized," Macy's said in a statement. "We are incredibly grateful to the thousands of volunteers, performers and partners who make this extraordinary tradition possible. We can’t wait to celebrate 100 years of Parade magic together on Saturday."

Spectators typically watch workers fill up the balloons the night before the parade, but there won't be an "inflation night" on Friday, he said.

"It's the only inflation we support here in New York City," Mayor Zohran Mamdani joked.

Macy's was working with school bands from outside the city who were in town so they could perform in Saturday's parade, Coss said.

"It will be spectacular. I promise you. It will be the parade that we set out to deliver, so I look forward to sharing it with everyone," he said.

A Thanksgiving storm is expected to hit New York City and the tri-state area with heavy rain, damaging winds and coastal flooding in some places expected.

An area of low pressure developing off the Southeast coast will push northward into the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast beginning Wednesday night. The storm is expected to intensify through Thanksgiving Day before clearing out on Friday, according to the FOX Forecast Center and the National Weather Service.

The iconic parade draws thousands of spectators along the 2½-mile route, which begins in Manhattan's Upper West Side to Herald Square on 34th Street, the site of the Macy's department store.

Mamdani said the parade is part of the city's iconic culture, but that public safety could not be compromised.

"I know that many New Yorkers, myself included, as well as millions around the world, were looking forward to the parade being held tomorrow," he said. "Generation after generation has watched SpongeBob and Spider-Man soar down our streets, and has taken in Garfield floating down Sixth Avenue. And I know that this change of plans will cause inconveniences."

"Yet safety has to come first," he added.

Along the route, there will be an expanded police presence, and "the NYPD is fully prepared for an event of this magnitude, just as they have been prepared for the 99 parades that preceded this," said NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch.
