Marine Corps legend under investigation related to his time as high school instructor

2026-11-20 19:18

A highly decorated Marine Corps hero is under criminal investigation over allegations from his time as a high school instructor — as rumors swirl about his questionable conduct toward female students, according to reports.

Sgt. Maj. Bradley Kasal recently became the subject of a Naval Criminal Investigative Service case on undisclosed grounds, and officials only revealed it stemmed from his time serving as a JROTC instructor at Nevada’s Basic Academy of International Studies, CBS News reported.

Kasal led the public high school’s junior military program from 2018 to 2025, after retiring from his 34-year Marine Corps career, during which he became widely known for his 2004 battlefield heroics in Iraq.

A widely circulated photograph captured Kasal being helped from a Fallujah house where he nearly died defending wounded comrades, standing his ground and returning fire despite being riddled with bullets and shrapnel.

Kasal’s bravery earned him the Navy Cross — the Navy’s second-highest honor — while the photograph of his rescue was cast in a bronze statue called “No Man Left Behind,” which stands at two Marine bases.

But that legacy could be in jeopardy as stories from his time as a high school instructor began to emerge after his investigation was revealed.

Former students recalled a fiercely dedicated leader who nevertheless appeared to cross boundaries at times.

Kasal allegedly gave extra attention to some female students outside of school, including trips to Starbucks, rides home and even invitations to stay at his cabin, CBS reported.

“He would mentor us on how to be a proper leader — which is his job — but he would go a bit beyond this,” said one of Kasal’s former JROTC cadets, 22-year-old Damien Greenwell.

“If someone couldn’t eat, he would pay for their food. If someone didn’t have a ride home, he would help them out,” Greenwell told CBS News. “It’s awesome, it’s great and very commendable.”

“But if you’re regularly getting Starbucks for a girl … it looks kind of weird … and with driving people home on a consistent basis, it looks bad,” he added. “You need to have a certain kind of boundary between you and students.”

The girl told CBS that Kasal would counsel her on her home issues and even on relationships with boys.

Kasal even offered to adopt her, the former student said.

She said Kasal once texted her about the offer, saying, “I’m not asking as charity. I’m asking because your [sic] wanted.”

His conduct towards students was also reportedly known by school higher-ups.

A district employee told CBS that Kasal was once warned about not being alone with cadets.

Other JROTC cadets, however, said they never saw anything questionable out of Kasal and credited him with their outstanding time in the program.

Daniel Gillian told CBS Kasal’s leadership “gave me a lot of structure and discipline” and helped him “become a better person overall.”

Gillian acknowledged Kasal would sometimes take certain students out for coffee and give them rides, but that it never seemed to be anything more than him being a “nice guy.”

The Naval Criminal Investigative Service typically withholds information about ongoing investigations, which can range from homicide and sexual assault, to fraud, terrorism and espionage.
