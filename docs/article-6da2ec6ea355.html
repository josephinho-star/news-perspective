California braces for Thanksgiving flood nightmare as monster tides threaten to swamp coastal streets

2026-11-21 17:38

California is piling up sandbags ahead of Thanksgiving as some of the highest tides in years threaten to send seawater spilling onto coastal streets.

In San Diego, crews are shoring up sand barriers and clearing storm drains ahead of exceptionally high king tides expected Monday through Thanksgiving Day. In the Bay Area, where residents slogged through knee-deep water during Wednesday’s record rain, the US Coast Guard is warning of the highest predicted king tides in 18 years.

“We went through the forecast for next week, and we are anticipating high tides and rain that this city has never seen before,” Mayor Daniel Lurie said.

Downtown San Francisco recorded 2.31 inches of rain Wednesday, breaking a Nov. 18 record that had stood since 1918, ABC7 reported. The station aired footage of people wading through knee-deep water and drivers trying to get through flooded streets.

The Coast Guard says Bay Area water levels could hit 7.5 to 8 feet from Nov. 24 to 26, with low-lying roads, waterfront trails and beach parking lots vulnerable to flooding. It urged boaters to check mooring lines and dock cleats before the water rises.

“Give the ocean plenty of space, never turn your back on the water, and never drive through flooded coastal roads,” said Lt. Rachel Davis, a spokesperson for Coast Guard Sector San Francisco.

In Southern California, San Diego’s preparations come after a bruising week on the water. City lifeguards made more than 175 rescues last week, including 22 on Saturday, as waves reached up to 20 feet in some areas along the city’s 17-mile coastline.

“We ask that beachgoers do not test their skill or abilities in the ocean during large swell events,” said Daniel Orloff, lifeguard chief for San Diego Fire-Rescue. He also urged people to steer clear of cliffs, coastal bluffs and spots where water can surge ashore.

Low-lying stretches of Mission Beach, Pacific Beach and Ocean Beach could flood, and roads near Frontage Road and Pacific Coast Highway in the Midway area could close, the city said.

A coastal flood advisory runs from 4 a.m. Sunday to 4 a.m. Tuesday, with a coastal flood watch from Monday night through Thursday afternoon.

King tides occur when the gravitational pull of the moon and sun produces exceptionally high tides. This time, San Diego officials expect a broad wave of warm water moving across the Pacific, known as a Kelvin wave, to amplify the effects of El Niño.

The National Weather Service said Thanksgiving-week tides could break records and warned that a larger swell arriving at peak tide could bring significant coastal flooding.

San Diego crews are using heavy equipment to reinforce sand berms along beaches and Mission Bay while stormwater workers clear channels, monitor pumps and stage equipment near flood-prone streets.

The city is offering up to 10 free prefilled sandbags per household while supplies last and arranging deliveries for older residents and people with disabilities.

The latest threat follows an October pounding of coastal flooding that prompted a luxury Laguna Beach resort to move guests upstairs.

“We are putting no guests on the first floor,” a Surf & Sand Resort employee told the Post at the time. The employee said the hotel was taking the precaution as waves battered the beachfront property.

The Bay Area’s highest tides of the season are still ahead. The Coast Guard projects water levels could reach 8.2 feet or higher from Dec. 22 to 25, with another round of unusually high tides expected in January.
