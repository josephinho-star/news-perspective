Stream It Or Skip It: ‘Habeas Corpus’ On Netflix, Where A Law Professor And Her Students Try To Reopen The Case Of A Man Convicted Of Murder

2026-09-23 20:30

In Netflix‘s new Brazilian legal drama Habeas Corpus, a law professor who quit litigating due to a violent courtroom incident, puts together a team of students to help get a murder conviction overturned. The most tenacious member of that team has a secret connection to the professor and that fateful case.

HABEAS CORPUS: STREAM IT OR SKIP IT?

Opening Shot: A lawyer very dramatically makes her closing argument, telling the jury that they should convict the defendant. But as she closes her argument, she’s shot in the shoulder.

The Gist: Eight years later, that lawyer, Inez Franco (Marjorie Estiano), is now a law professor because she cannot set foot in a courtroom because of PTSD from that incident. She talks to her class about the case against Jonathan Ferreira dos Santos (Hipólyto), who was convicted of a double murder and given 47 years in prison. The woman suspected of arranging the murders, Renata Vargas (Paula Cohen), is set to go on trial, represented by Inez’s old firm and her ex-boyfriend, Murilo Villar (Vladimir Brichta).

Her aim is to gather a team of students to investigate and once they have enough evidence, file a writ of habeas corpus to reopen the case. One of the students vying to be on the team is Marina (Any Gabrielly), a young mother who lives in the favelas of São Paulo. She’s trying to juggle her efforts to show that she’s worthy of being on the team with the care of her son, but she often leaves her son home alone to work on the case. But her reasons for wanting to be on the team go beyond getting justice for Jonathan; she has a connection to the last time Inez was in a courtroom.

What Shows Will It Remind You Of? Habeas Corpus reminds us of legal dramas like The Rainmaker.

Our Take: There’s nothing particularly revolutionary about Habeas Corpus, except for perhaps the idea that legal dramas can be just as entertaining when another country’s court system is examined. But it’s a solidly-written drama with fine performances by both Estiano and Gabrielly, and a twist at the end of the first episode that makes this case even more intriguing.

The rest of the members of the team are more stock characters, including a couple, a hard-driving overachiever, and the son of the managing partner of Inez’s old firm, who is basically there as a spy. But the relationship between Inez and Marina — and the secret reasons why Marina wants to be on Inez’s investigative team — should drive the dramatic tension as the team probes Jonathan’s case.

There are some hiccups in the first episode that jump to some conclusions; we’re not quite use why Murilo takes on Jonathan’s case other than a way to keep it away from Inez, and we’re not sure how Jonathan is convinced to switch his representation over to Inez and her team. But all of the intrigue that goes along with Inez’s quest and her team, not to mention how she’s going to be able to get herself back inside a courtroom again, is certainly enough to keep the tension high in this series.

Performance Worth Watching: Marjorie Estiano is dynamic as Inez, but we also appreciate Any Gabrielly’s more understated performance as Marina. Like we said above, the two performances mesh well.

Sex And Skin: Some slight nudity to show Inez’s scar eight years after being shot.

Parting Shot: We see how Marina is connected with the day in court where Inez got shot.

Our Call: STREAM IT. Habeas Corpus is a solid legal drama with two very watchable lead performances. It also feels universal, even if you’re not really familiar with Brazil’s legal system.
