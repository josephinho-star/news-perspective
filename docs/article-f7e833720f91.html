Trump delivers message to Putin: ‘Settle the war’ - Europe live

2026-09-22 14:49

'Settle the war,' Trump says is his message to Putin

Meanwhile, Trump is arriving at the UN HQ right now.

Our colleagues on the US blog will cover him in more detail here, but I will pick up all his Europe-related comments here.

After very brief doorstep comments – almost exclusively mocking CNN - he answers a reporter’s very loud question by saying his message to Putin is: “settle the war.”

Trump dismisses AI warnings as 'hoax' and pushes to rebrand it 'super intelligence'

Back to New York, Trump is now talking about his attempts to rename artificial intelligence as “super intelligence,” as he dismisses warnings about AI as “a hoax” (allegedly coming from the same people as “Russia hoax,” “Ukraine hoax” and climate change) and declares he will “not stifle AI growth.”

He says “whoever wins SI, wins.”

“We’re leading now over China by a lot, and everyone else, and we’re going to keep it that way.”

EU agrees to drop two Russian billionaires from sanctions list

Meanwhile, the EU has agreed to drop two Russian billionaires, Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman, from its sanctions list, while extending restrictions on nearly 3,000 individuals and companies for the next three years (11:15, 16:13).

With an end-of-day deadline looming it seems Latvia blinked in a standoff with France about the de-listings it had sought to prevent. The Baltic state decided to abstain on the de-listings, according to diplomats.

France stunned EU member states earlier this month when it called for Usmanov, a Russian-Uzbek national, to be removed from the list on “national security” grounds, reported to refer to a prisoner exchange deal involving French nationals and Azerbaijan.

Luxembourg then asked for the same treatment for Fridman, the Russian-Israeli magnate, who is suing the Grand Duchy for $16bn for freezing his assets.

Latvia lodged a last-minute objection to the compromise plan that will see the two men removed from the list in exchange for extending the sanctions for three years, rather than the usual six months.

Without an agreement sanctions on nearly 3,000 people and entities accused of supporting the war on Ukraine would have lapsed at the end of Tuesday 22 September. That outcome was never on the cards, but it created a tense standoff over who would move first.

Quickly going through a number of issues in short succession, Trump also says he is going to “continue to seek to end the disastrous war between Russia and Ukrainee.”

He says he signed into law “a powerful new law” on sanctions, and “if necessary, I will have to use them.”

“It’s time to stop the killing,” he says.

He pays tribute to the First Lady’s work on “securing the safe return of many displaced Ukrainian and Russian children back home to their families.”

On Nato, Trump pointedly praises the alliance’s secretary general Mark Rutte and says Nato is “growing stronger than ever before” as member states ramp up their defence spending.

Much to Rutte’s relief, no doubt, he doesn’t go into all sorts of grievances he has with the alliance.

“I look forward to working with the wonderful people of Denmark and Greenland. It’s been a great experience, amazing experience, and we’ve really formed a bond in doing it.”

That’s one way to phrase the period in which he repeatedly threatened to take control of Greenland through whatever means necessary, but hey, it’s the outcome that counts, right?

US will build two 'very major' military bases as part of new Greenland deal, Trump says

Trump is now speaking about the “northern frontier”, which seems to be his new phrase for Greenland.

He says the US has reached “an unprecedented agreement” on this, with the Kingdom of Denmark, “an incredible, beautiful country.”

He says the deal – to be formally signed shortly after his speech – “gives the US permanent control over security and all of the needs on that territory.”

“We will have the complete ability to do what is necessary to defend the American continent, and also Europe and other places. No US adversary will ever be permitted to establish a military presence in Greenland any more, or make sensitive investments there without our express written approval.”

He says the US will build two “very major” military bases in Greenland.

“By expanding America’s footprint in the Arctic and North Atlantic, this deal also protects Europe in addition to America and greatly protects them."

Trump is currently talking about Iran, but he makes an assertion that Iran “made a missile that was capable of hitting Europe, and they were very proud of it.”

“I hope the Europeans realise that,” he adds.

But no further comment on the European angle to this story at least for now, despite his (well-advertised) frustrations with Nato. Let’s see if he comes back to it later.

A US fighter jet reportedly crashes near US air base in Germany

Elsewhere, I am also keeping an eye on reports coming from Germany that a US F-16 fighter jet crashed near a US airbase in Spangdahlem in western Germany.

One person was injured in the incident, n-tv reported, via local authorities, with local residents reporting “a large plume of black smoke” in the area.

The aircraft involved in the incident has not been confirmed.

EU still locked in dispute over delisting two Russian billionaires from sanctions

As time runs out ahead of an end-of-day deadline, EU diplomats are still in dispute over delisting two Russian billionaires, Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman, from the sanctions list.

Ambassadors from the 27 member states were due to resume talks around now (1600 CET) in the search for a compromise, amid a stand off between Latvia and France.

As we are waiting for Trump’s address, let’s get a quick update on the EU’s ongoing talks about its Russia sanctions list (11:15) from our Brussels correspondent, Jennifer Rankin.

Friedrich Merz misses out on UN general assembly debate as he fights fires at home

Thinking of big names appearing at the UNGA this week, one name is missing from the list: and that’s Germany’s Friedrich Merz.

Last week he cancelled his plans to go to the US amid growing problems domestically, which only got worse at the weekend after his conservative party suffered a drubbing at the polls in two state elections.

As our Berlin correspondent Deborah Cole noted:

“Merz cancelled a planned trip to New York to speak before the UN general assembly, already anticipating he would need to be in Berlin to grapple with the state elections’ aftermath.

Now, more than ever, he will have his work cut out for him after just 16 months in office.”

Instead, Germany will be represented by its foreign minister, Johann Wadephul, who will speak later this week.
