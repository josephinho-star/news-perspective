Chris Van Hollen rules out 2028 presidential bid as his challenge to take Senate leadership gains support – US politics live

2026-11-13 20:57

Van Hollen says he won't pursue 2028 presidential bid

Senator Chris Van Hollen, who is challenging Chuck Schumer to lead upper chamber Democrats in the new Congress, said today that he will “no longer” pursue a 2028 presidential bid.

“I believe the size and scale of these majorities provides an opportunity for the United States Senate to answer the call with the American people,” he said at the What’s Next post-election conference in Denver, where dozens of Democratic lawmakers, strategists and political insiders have gathered to discuss the party’s future after the monumental wins in the midterms.

Bobby Pulido won his third Latin Grammy award on Thursday night and announced his retirement from the music industry. It happened just days after the star Tejano singer – and Democrat – flipped a Republican-held congressional district in the November midterm elections.

He won the award in the category for best Tejano album before saying in Spanish during his acceptance speech: “I’m retiring from music, and I leave with so much gratitude.

“I love you so much, and we’ll see each other soon, because there’s a need to represent – and to get to work and use the voice for something else. Thank you.”

Among those to congratulate Pulido for his Latin Grammy victory was top US House Democrat Hakeem Jeffries.

“Felicidades,” or congratulations, Jeffries wrote in a social media post reminding users that Pulido had “crushed” Republican incumbent Monica De La Cruz nine days earlier. “And what a month for Congressman-Elect Pulido.”

One of Pulido’s campaign strategies was to perform at dozens of quinceañeras – girls’ 15th birthday parties in Mexico and along its border with the US – that are somewhat similar to American sweet 16s.

Early in the race, De La Cruz had derided Pulido’s artistry, saying their electoral contest between them wasn’t “about who you want performing at your niece’s quinceañera. It’s about who you trust with your family’s future.”

But Pulido turned the dig into support, countering that he would “be honored to stop by and celebrate” at quinceañeras planned by families in his and De La Cruz’s district.

His first and still biggest hit song was Desvelado, meaning “sleep-deprived”, which had been played more than 646m times on Spotify as of Friday.

Pulido’s 1995 album of that name went platinum. His Para Que Baile Mi Pueblo – “so that my people dance” – and the first volume of Una Tuya y Una Mía (“one of yours and one of mine”) won best Tejano album at the 2022 and 2025 Latin Grammy awards.

He won a third Latin Grammy award in that category during Thursday’s ceremony at the MGM Grand Garden Arena in Las Vegas with Una Tuya y Una Mía Vol 2.

Two days before his triumph, Pulido told the celebrity news and politics website TMZ that he would not bring the Grammy to Capitol Hill if he won it.

“This is a place for work,” Pulido said to the outlet. “It’s a much bigger responsibility to be a public servant than it is to be a singer, for sure, no doubt

“Your decisions really affect people, and as a singer you can influence people in a different way, but it’s not the same.”

But she didn’t explicitly say whether she would support Senator Chris Van Hollen yet, either.

Per my earlier reporting, Zohran Mamdani officially announced the 90-day reprieve he secured for Snap-accepting bodegas and convenience stores nationwide to have time to implement new agriculture department rules.

The extension, Mamdani said, starts today as a result of conversations he had with Donald Trump and Brooke Rollins, the agriculture secretary.

The new guidelines threw the lives of “more than a million New Yorkers” and “tens of milllions more across America” into “chaos”, the mayor said at a press conference on Friday.

“People already reckoning with a shortage of affordable places to buy what they need for their home, soaring costs at the register, and food deserts across the five boroughs, now feared that the few places they could shop would no longer be able to accept their benefits,” Mamdani said.

Given the tight timeframe to adjust their stock and meet the new requirements, small retailers ran the risk of going out business if they couldn’t accept Snap benefits, the mayor added. “For so many of our fellow Americans, whether they shop at a general store in Michigan or a convenience store in Missouri, would have struggled to use their benefits and feed those they love.”

On 2028 run Slotkin says she is 'looking at it seriously', won't vote for Schumer in leadership contest

Speaking to reporters in Denver today, Elissa Slotkin, a Democratic senator from Michigan, said she was looking at a 2028 presidential run “seriously” but has not “made any decisions”.

“I think what I’ve consistently felt, and I still really feel, is I want to be a part of the next generation of leaders going forward that does things differently and understands who they serve,” she said today. “Whether it’s me or somebody else. I just want to be a part of that next generation.”

While Slotkin did not yet say that she would back Chris Van Hollen’s bid for Senate majority leader in the next Congress, she noted that she has had productive conversations with the Maryland Democrat and was happy another lawmaker was in the race. Slotkin did, however, confirm that she would not be supporting Chuck Schumer.

“We need a new generation of leadership that thinks differently about how to exercise oversight and power, and you know, I didn’t support Nancy Pelosi for speaker of the House,” Slotkin added. “I’ve been on record on these things long before most of my peers, and more importantly than that, inside the Democratic caucus, I’ve been pushing and agitating … the desire to have a strategic plan for Democrats is a central theme for me.”

Incoming Democratic senator from Minnesota backs Van Hollen in leadership battle

Peggy Flanagan, the senator-elect from Minnesota, said today that Chris Van Hollen is her choice for Senate majority leader.

“Minnesotans are demanding change. Democrats need to deliver. That starts with new voices in our leadership,”she wrote on X. “I believe @ChrisVanHollen is the leader and fighter we need to bring about real change, so no American is left behind.”

Flanagan said on Thursday, before Van Hollen had announced his candidacy, that she thinks it’s time for Schumer to leave the leadership role.

“I really appreciate Chuck Schumer’s years of leadership. I like Chuck Schumer,” she told the Guardian. “I simply think it’s time for new voices. And I think that is the message that was sent in this last election. I heard clearly from folks across the state that they are ready for new Senate leadership as well.”

Mamdani secures deal with Trump to delay new Snap rules for bodegas and corner stores around the country

Zohran Mamdani, the New York mayor, has struck a deal with Donald Trump to secure a 90-day delay on new rules for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (Snap), so that bodegas and corner stores across the country have time to comply, according to a source familiar.

The US agriculture department implemented the new guidelines, which retailers had to meet by 4 November, and require stores to stock at least seven varieties within each staple food category: dairy, grains, protein, and fruits or vegetables.

Retailers would also have to ensure that they offer perishable items (that require refrigeration after being opened) in three of the staple food categories.

Several advocates were concerned that the new rules would mean those relying on Snap would be unable to afford groceries at their only accessible store. According to the Food Action Research Center, bodegas, convenience stores and small grocery stores comprise roughly 70% of Snap retailers.

According to the source, the reprieve came about through Mamdani’s direct negotiations with the president. The New York mayor also sent a letter to Brooke Rollins, the agriculture secretary, earlier this month to request an extension. More than 100 bodega owners have reached out to Mamdani’s office with concerns about the new rule. According to the New York department of city planning, there are more than 10,000 bodegas and corner stores across the five boroughs.
