FAA administrator urges ‘lay off’ Sean Duffy for the flight stoppage: ‘Blame me’

2026-11-18 19:14

The head of the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) took responsibility for the nationwide ground stop that affected more than 12,400 flights on Friday, urging people to “lay off” Sean Duffy, the transportation secretary.

“I take full responsibility for it,” Bryan Bedford, the FAA administrator, said at an event featuring aviation officials and lawmakers on Wednesday, Politico reported.

“I wish people would lay off the secretary,” Bedford added. “He’s got a lot of work to do, but I’m the guy they hired to get this done. So blame me if we need to blame somebody. Blame me.”

The FAA administrator’s comments arrived as Duffy, the top transportation official in the US, faces scrutiny from former officials and lawmakers over his handling of the tech outage crisis, which left tens of thousands of travelers stranded over the weekend.

In a press conference on Friday, Duffy refused to accept responsibility or apologize for the crisis, blaming Democratic lawmakers and his predecessors for not providing the funding to upgrade outdated systems.

The FAA said it grounded all flights after a newly installed router flooded communication systems, causing issues between aircraft systems and radar feeds.

Trump administration officials have emphasized that the tech outage at the FAA was not caused by a cybersecurity attack and was unrelated to other disruptions.

Jay Clayton, director of national intelligence, testified on Wednesday that there was no indication the outage was the result of a malicious actor or connected to similar disruptions that have affected banks and major companies. He said the outages were caused by patches – modifications to systems to address issues such as security vulnerabilities.

But lawmakers, including some Republicans, have grown frustrated with Duffy after he and the White House asked Congress to provide $50bn in funding to upgrade the air traffic system, more than double what was originally estimated.

Some GOP lawmakers told reporters the justification behind the large sum of money was thin.

But Bedford argued the investment was necessary on Wednesday, claiming Donald Trump and Duffy believed the outage was a “call to action” to “fix the system” and “return to the gold standard”.
