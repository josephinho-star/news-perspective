LSU's Lane Kiffin hands Tennessee bulletin board material, claims he forgot he ever coached the Vols

2026-11-19 19:27

Year one in Baton Rouge has taken its toll on Lane Kiffin. Such a toll, in fact, that the LSU head coach has let his team's upcoming trip to Knoxville to take on No. 9 Tennessee sneak up on him.

That's what Kiffin is claiming, at least, with some interesting comments about essentially forgetting about the fact that he used to be the head coach of the Volunteers.

"This is going to sound crazy, but this season has been so much," Kiffin explained earlier in the week. "It's just been a lot. As we were getting ready and started watching film and stuff, it actually didn't cross my mind that I used to be there (Tennessee), and fans and how they were gonna be. I feel like we've been in so much toxic environments and things so far that it'll just be more of it. I really hadn't thought much about it."

It has been 17 years since Kiffin's one-season stint in charge of the Vols before he resigned after a 7-6 campaign to take the head coaching gig at USC.

Seventeen years is a long time, especially in the wild world of coaching, but Kiffin voluntarily telling Tennessee fans he has completely erased his time in Knoxville from his memory feels like an odd choice.

Two days later, Kiffin gift-wrapped another piece of bulletin board material for the Vols and their fanbase.

Speaking on the weekly SEC coaches teleconference, Kiffin explained that he didn't believe the atmosphere inside Neyland Stadium on Saturday would be filled with as much hatred as what he and the Tigers felt at Ole Miss in Week 3 of the season.

"I'm not challenging Tennessee," Kiffin said on the SEC coaches teleconference on Wednesday, according to On3's Grant Ramey. "But I just don't think you can top that (Ole Miss atmosphere) — just the feeling of what that was like and the anger and hatred because it was so recent."

Kiffin can say he's not challenging Tennessee fans with the statement, but that is exactly how the Vols' fanbase is going to take it, regardless of the fact that Saturday won't mark his first return trip to Knoxville wearing a different team's logo.

Kiffin's return to Oxford earlier this season to take on the Ole Miss program he abandoned in December ahead of its College Football Playoff run made for one of the most memorable scenes and atmospheres in recent college football history.

While the wound Kiffin left at Ole Miss was much fresher than the one left behind in Knoxville, there will still be plenty of emotional fans wearing orange and white in Neyland Stadium on Saturday.

It feels important to note that Neyland Stadium holds approximately 37,000 more fans than Ole Miss's Vaught-Hemingway Stadium.
