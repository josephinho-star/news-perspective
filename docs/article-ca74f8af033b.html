NYC subway shooter who mistakenly wounded bystander in freak twist learns his fate

2026-11-16 20:50

An Upper East Side subway shooter who wounded an innocent straphanger in a bizarre chain of events on a crowded train landed 15 years behind bars Monday.

Steven Sylvester, 36 — who faced up to 25 years in prison before copping the plea deal — admitted to Manhattan Supreme Court Justice Kathryn Paek that he shot the 32-year-old bystander while aiming for another man on the northbound No. 4 train in Manhattan on June 2, 2024.

Sylvester had been physically fighting with his girlfriend on the train around 2:25 a.m. when a good Samaritan first tried stepping in between the couple to break up the altercation.

The 35-year-old do-gooder grabbed Sylvester from behind and was struggling to hold him back when the train pulled into the 86th Street and Lexington Avenue subway station — and Sylvester pulled out a gun and fired a shot toward the man.

Sylvester’s single shot missed his target, who was only a few feet away — but the bullet ricocheted off a subway window, striking a second man who was just entering the train car at the time, according to court papers.

The victim suffered a bullet wound to his left hand and grazing wounds to his torso.

Sylvester fled the scene but was arrested four days later.

The shooter — who originally faced attempted murder among other charges — pleaded guilty to attempted assault in the first degree and criminal possession of a weapon as part of a plea deal.

“Steven Sylvester jeopardized the safety of countless train passengers when he opened fire on a crowded subway train, attempting to shoot a New Yorker who intervened to protect others,” Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg said in a statement.

“His decision to fire a weapon in such a confined space resulted in significant injuries for an innocent bystander.

“The outcome of this case reflects the gravity of this conduct and our unwavering commitment to holding accountable those who commit acts of violence within our transit system,” Bragg said.

Sylvester will remain remanded in city custody before his upstate prison sentence begins Jan. 25.
