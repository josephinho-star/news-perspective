FBI’s ‘Most Wanted’ cartel kingpin ‘Chapo Isidro’ captured in Mexico military operation — sparking massive gunfight

2026-11-21 09:53

A ruthless drug lord – one of the FBI’s 10 most wanted fugitives – was captured in a joint US-Mexican military operation on Friday night, sparking a gunfight between cartel members and soldiers that sent innocent civilians running for safety.

Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “Chapo Isidro” and alleged leader of the Sinaloa-based Meza-Flores Transnational Criminal Organization (TCO), was arrested and awaits extradition to the US to face alleged drug trafficking charges, Mexico’s Defense Ministry announced Saturday.

His second-in-command, only identified as Germán N, aka “Serafín,” and the cartel’s alleged financial operator, José N, were also captured in the operation.

Armed guards escorted the three handcuffed men into a building after their capture, according to video shared by the defense ministry.

Flores has evaded capture for over a decade before FBI officials placed a $5 million bounty for his arrest for his role with the “heavily armed and violent” group, whose merciless resume includes “gun battles, kidnapping, murder, torture, and extortion to maintain control of their territory.”

The drug lord’s capture coincided with a reported attack on federal forces by suspected gang members in the city of Guasave in the northwest corner of the crime-riddled state of Sinaloa, the State Public Security Secretariat said Friday.

“The SSPSinaloa reports that, this Friday, November 20, 2026, an attack on federal personnel occurred in the municipality of Guasave. The public is asked to stay safe indoors and avoid the area, as well as to refrain from traveling on roads,” the Public Security Secretariat office said.

Two low-flying Mexican military helicopters sprayed a barrage of bullets down on the suspected drug traffickers during the clash with the cartel in what appeared to be a residential area, according to video shared by TV Azteca.

The fighting forced residents to hide throughout the duration, according to the outlet.

Gunshots were heard inside Guasave’s Kuroda Park as Mexican Pacific League baseball teams Algodoneros de Guasave and Venados de Mazatlán were in the middle of the action.

Se informa que, derivado de la coordinación entre organismos de inteligencia de la Secretaría de la Defensa Nacional y de los E.U.A., realizada en el marco de la cooperación bilateral con ese país, el 20 de noviembre del presente año, Fuerzas Especiales de la Guardia Nacional (Lobos) y del Ejército (Murciélagos), en coordinación con elementos de Fuerzas Especiales del Ejército comisionados en la Secretaría de Seguridad Pública del Estado de Sinaloa, detuvieron en el municipio de Guasave, Sin., a Fausto Isidro N (a) “Chapo Isidro”, jefe de la organización delictiva “Beltrán Leyva” y aliado al “Cártel del Pacífico” facción “Mayos”, a Germán N (a) “Serafín”, segundo al mando de esa organización delincuencial y a José N, operador financiero de este mismo grupo delictivo.

(a) “Chapo Isidro”, cuenta con una orden de aprehensión en México por delincuencia organizada y por cometer delitos contra la salud, y una orden de extradición a los E.U.A. por asociación delictiva para distribuir droga en territorio estadounidense; además, se encuentra en la lista de los 10 fugitivos más buscados por el F.B.I.

La operación para estas detenciones, fue realizada por fuerzas militares de élite, las que una vez ubicado con precisión el objetivo, mediante el empleo de diversos ingenios tecnológicos, la desarrollaron en 3 fases, de la forma siguiente:

Arribo al área del objetivo: se realizó utilizando el sigilo y la sorpresa por diferentes vías de acceso terrestre al área de la detención.

Establecimiento de la seguridad y captura del objetivo: esta se realizó a inmediaciones de la ubicación de los detenidos, mediante la colocación de un dispositivo de seguridad interna y externa, para evitar la huida o la llegada de refuerzos, habiendo sido agredidos con disparos de armas de fuego y explosivos, por lo que la fuerza de detención ingresó a la casa habitación para capturar a las personas indicadas.

Evacuación del objetivo: confirmada la identidad de los detenidos, se procedió a su evacuación a la Ciudad de México, en una aeronave de la Fuerza Aérea Mexicana, para ser puestos a disposición de las autoridades ministeriales competentes.

Derivado de esta detención, se registraron bloqueos en los municipios de Ahome, Guamúchil, Guasave y Navolato, para lo cual, en coordinación con autoridades locales, se activó el plan antibloqueo, movilizando fuerzas de seguridad para restablecer el tránsito en las vías de comunicación y proteger a la población.

Players with the Algodoneros de Guasave called for time as popping sounds rang across the field, according to video from the game broadcast.

In a chilling moment, all personnel on the field sprinted towards their dugouts to escape any bullets as fans screamed and rushed out of the stands.

“There is an off-field situation,” one of the game’s commentators said on the broadcast.

“There is a national security situation and we’d better pause and we’ll be reporting any developments in the game,” he added.

Mexican Pacific League officials canceled the remainder of the game.

“In light of security-related incidents that have occurred over the last few hours in the city of Guasave, the Liga Mexicana del Pacífico Banamex — in coordination with local authorities and with the goal of safeguarding the well-being of our fans—has decided to cancel the game between the Venados de Mazatlán and the Algodoneros de Guasave at Kuroda Park,” the league announced.

None of the fighting took place inside the stadium, the league confirmed.

Blockades tied up multiple highways as trucks were set alight to block entry into the city of roughly 300,000 people, the Associated Press reported.

Flores was expected to be transferred to prosecutors in Mexico City after his arrest, TV Azteca reported.

The FBI sought the suspected drug lord for multiple counts of Drug-Trafficking Violations and Use and Possession of a Firearm, accusing him of flooding the US with deadly drugs and other crimes.

He was originally indicted by a grand jury in Washington, DC, in May 2012, before a federal arrest warrant was issued for him in November 2019.

His alleged crimes included Conspiracy to Manufacture and Distribute Five Hundred Grams or More of Methamphetamine, Distribute Five Kilograms or More of Cocaine, Distribute One Kilogram or More of Heroin, and Distribute One Thousand Kilograms or More of Marijuana for Importation into the United States; Use and Possession of a Firearm.

The TCO are allegedly responsible for killing multiple law enforcement officers and members of other cartels in the Sinaloa State, US officials said.
