Why Anthropic's hot IPO is bringing out sellers — plus, a silver lining of the down market

2026-11-18 19:46

Every weekday, the CNBC Investing Club with Jim Cramer releases the Homestretch — an actionable afternoon update, just in time for the last hour of trading on Wall Street. Stocks are struggling to mount a sustained rally despite Nvidia's strong earnings, upbeat forecast, and positive revision of its cloud industry backlog. As we said earlier , Nvidia's report had something for both the bulls and bears. While the guidance was impressive and the stock is historically cheap on an earnings basis, the quarter didn't change Jim Cramer's view that investors should take some shares off the table, if they haven't already, as a matter of capital preservation . A down session for the S & P 500 would be its fourth straight and would extend the pullback from its all-time closing high to more than 6%. That's still pretty far from a correction, defined as a decline of at least 10% from recent highs. For the week, the S & P 500 has dropped about 3%. Some of the pressure over the past few days may be tied to a bit of selling by investors making last-minute room for their Anthropic allocations ahead of Wednesday night's initial public offering (IPO) pricing and Thursday's first trade. These types of moves tend to happen around IPOs as the market absorbs the new supply. A silver lining to the bearish sentiment and continued selling pressure is that this could be the push needed to get the S & P Short Range Oscillator into oversold territory. We have kept our powder dry, waiting for this signal to get more opportunistic and begin deploying our 16% cash position. An attempt at a midday rebound fizzled . The timing of the move coincided with a pretty quick drop in oil prices. West Texas Intermediate crude traded slightly above $84 per barrel around 11 a.m. ET, but fell to around $82.50 about an hour later. This quick drop in WTI, the American oil benchmark, brought down Treasury yields despite Federal Reserve Christoper Waller's call on Wednesday to "shock people" with a half-point interest rate hike. We often point out the linkage between oil prices and the stock market because energy costs affect so many parts of the economy, including inflation expectations, input costs, and consumers' discretionary spending. While stocks were unable to maintain their lift, the drop in oil and bond yields stuck. Palo Alto Networks reports quarterly results after the bell and CEO Nikesh Arora will be on "Mad Money," fresh off the earnings call. On Tuesday, Arora posted on X about all the tech outages lately: "What the industry tried to warn enterprises about 6 months ago is here. The events of this week so far are not a coincidence. AI is making it easy for hackers to attack multiple customers," he said. Club stock Palo Alto rose more than 8%. On Thursday morning, we'll get earnings from Walmart , Alibaba, and Bath & Body Works. It's a quiet day for economic data, with just the Philadelphia Fed's business outlook and the government's weekly jobless claims scheduled before the bell.
