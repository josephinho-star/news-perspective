Greens would nationalise Thames and SE Water without paying compensation, says deputy leader – UK politics live

2026-10-03 10:38

Millward calls for immediate takeback of 'failed' water companies

She calls for national takeover of South East Water and Thames Water, without compensation for shareholders. She tells conference:

Burnham’s 10 year plan of gentle threats and vague notions of control is not enough. Too many of our water companies have already failed.

Right here in Sussex, we too often find ourselves driving to pick up bottled water because nothing is coming out of our taps.

Not a single major reservoir has been built in this country since the beginning of privatisation 35 years ago.

And so, today, the Green Party is announcing that we would, immediately - not in 10 years, but before Christmas, bring South East Water and Thames Water back into public ownership at no cost to the tax payer.

We have all the tools we need to make it happen. Forget compensation for shareholders and creditors, these are insolvent companies.

Earlier she had laid out the issue:

We see the sewage being pumped into our river by private profit making companies - and we see just how far wrong we’ve gone.

Water - the very stuff of life - uniquely found on this planet in the whole wide universe - the stuff we’re made of - it replenishes us, cleans us, connects us… and we — sold it.

Those are the main keynote speeches today at the Greens party conference, from deputy leaders Rachel Millward and Mothin Ali who both opened their speeeches with a recap of the party’s significant membership growth particularly among young people.

There are now more than 200,000 members across England and Wales, making the Greens the third-largest political party.

“Meeting so many of you on action days around the country, getting more Greens elected, sharing our message on climate, inequality, immigration, trans rights. It’s been a year of hope,” said Millward.

While the PM at Labour conference last week announced a 10-year plan to renationalise water companies, Millward said this delay was a mistake.

She called for an immediate buyback of ‘failed’ water companies Thames Water and South-East, amid a broader call for people to take greater power of natural assets to stop tech billionaires like Elon Musk and Peter Thiel and AI CEOs who are “hoarding the world’s wealth, polluting our planet” . She said they were fuelling the climate disaster with energy-guzzling data centres and profiting off misinformation and online division.

“Andy Burnham talks a lot about reversing 40 years of neoliberalism, but we can’t just look back. We stand on the cusp of unprecedented, technological, and social change.”

Meanwhile Mothin Ali used his speech to call for greater celebrations and protections for “multiracial, multicultural Britain” noting that the country was only growing more diverse and was stronger for it.

He said the rising levels of racist violence and intimidation though warranted the Burnham government calling a “national emergency”. As I noted earlier, there are scheduled anti-immigration protests to take place on the south coast today.

There needs to be more done to “protect everyone’s equal right to belong, and to make diversity work for us”, said Ali

Live and let live. Now, that’s a simple phrase that I’ve always lived by. But in a diverse democracy, it’s something that’s profound. You don’t have to become your neighbour. You don’t have to worship. You don’t have to vote like them. You don’t even have to agree with them. But you do have to recognise their equal right to insist and to belong… We have to speak to people we disagree with. We have to listen seriously to genuine concerns about immigration, housing, public services, and security. And we need to distinguish between legitimate political argument from the denigration of whole communities.

Ali says democracy “means very little without economic security” and launches into announcing a job guarantee for young people, if the Greens were to be in power.

There are 1 million young people in the UK – roughly one in every seven British people aged 16-24- who are categorised as a Neet (not in education, employment or training).

He notes this crisis, and says current policies – threatening to withold or cut welfare payments like universal credit if people don’t accept short-term work placements – are poor youth strategies making the problem worse.

Across London, Wales, young people are struggling from Cardiff to Birmingham, from Leeds to Leicester, from Liverpool to Sheffield. They’re forced to compete with a handful of jobs. We need to do better.

So the Greens solution, he says, is a youth jobs guarantee.

He says that the Greens in power would launch a scheme “providing a guaranteed job for every 18- to 24-year-old lasting up to two years, which start by introducing them into the areas that are worst-hit by usual unemployment”. He doesn’t list which areas these are and detail is scant on how these jobs would be made and funded, where they’d come from, how they would be implemented.

He says the new jobs would be aligned with “our critical national missions: a stronger care economy, a green transition, homes for all, and a sustainable food system, a nature recovery”.

Mothin Ali then goes on to say the Greens could become the “most inclusive political movement in modern Britain”, saying the party wants to be the place for people to feel like they belong and are empowered.

He’s gearing up to announcing the party’s economic policy plank of promising jobs for young people – giving them financial security.

Whether you’re a black boy sitting in a park, or whether you’re a Jewish family towards a synagogue, or whether you’re a Muslim in a mosque.

Or someone seeking safety after fleeing from war and persecution.

There should be no street, no neighbourhood, and no place in this country where you can’t live your life in peace.

Belonging should never be a condition on race, religion, accent, or background.

So what do we do? When people feel powerless, give them meaningful power. When they feel unheard, create places where they genuinely have a voice.

Ali to Braverman: 'I'm brown, Muslim and as English as a cup of tea'

He also singles out Suella Braverman, former Tory Home Secretary and now a Reform spokeswoman, who said on Question Time this week that she doesn’t consider herself to be English because of her Asian heritage.

She said she identified as British Asian but not “an English person” because her parents were born abroad – sparking widespread outrage.

“A message to Suella Braveman,” said Ali.

“I’m brown. I’m Muslim, but I’m as English as a Yorkshire cup of tea.”

Ali: 'Can we build a country confident enough to live with difference?'

The Greens’ other deputy leader Mothin Ali is delivering his remarks now, praising the party’s growth and noting that the young greens are “the biggest youth political movement in the country.”

People making the journey to the green party because they see we offer a real alternative.

He says people are looking for a different kind of politics that recognises how multicultural the UK is.

Britain is becoming more diverse, not less. Can we build a country confident enough to live with difference? To protect everyone’s equal right to belong and to make diversity work with us all.

Millward sketches out the scenario of “hyper scaled data centres that guzzle energy to make a mockery of climate progress.”

"I feel the despair of young people who tell me they are terrified, that they don’t want to live in a future of climate wreckage and fake everything.

Take a breath. Look around you. We love it here. We don’t want your post human reality. We want this world, the soil between our toes, the trees, that give us shelter and shade, the food we grow with our own hands, the tastes of the ground it comes from. It might seem crazy, but friends, we have all the power that we need.”

Millward’s walked out to ‘You’ve Got The Love’ by The Source blasting over the speakers.

She says the party’s membership growth has been incredible this past year – “and it’s just the beginning” – noting the campaigning by members in local races across the country.

She launches into criticism of “tech oligarchal overlords” and “the handful of men who control too much for the rest of us”.

“They are hoarding the world’s weath, polluting the planet, and now trying to buy our democracy.”

We’re expecting Millward to call for the Burnham government to immediately bring water companies back into public ownership.

She’ll suggest that a “10-year plan of gentle threats and vague notions of control is not enough.” This was laid out by Burnham at Labour conference last week.

Polanski called for rent brakes to give people 'breathing space'

The Greens’ deputy leaders are due to address members on Day 2 of their conference in Brighton shortly, and we’ll have a live stream of those remarks in the top of our blog.

But yesterday, party leader Zach Polanski used his conference speech to introduce a key new policy, calling for a “three-year emergency brake on private rents”.

No rent rises above CPI wage growth or 2%, whichever is lower. No sudden hikes. No evictions under the guise of rent rises. No more uncertainty, no more fear.

That emergency brake would give renters vital breathing space. Let wages begin to catch up with the rents that have outstripped them for far too long.

And in the long term, we’ll put in place a national Fair Rents Guarantee – to keep rents affordable for everyone, forever. That doesn’t just mean cheaper homes, it doesn’t just mean government savings.

It will mean that people can stay in the places they love, close to their family and friends. It will mean young people can take those first amazing, exhilarating steps into independence and have full lives waiting for them on the other side.

Young families able to live around the corner from the in-laws, not crammed into their spare room.

Anti-migrant demonstrations planned in Southampton and Dover

We can also expect anti-migrant demonstrations to be taking place on the south coast this afternoon with police on alert in Southampton and Dover.

A group called South Coast Patriots has organised the protests, and are expected to be met by counter-protesters from the local Southampton Stand Up To Racism group.

Police have issued a ban on face coverings in the area.

Farage has 'not been believed' on safety fears, says Tice

Meanwhile, Reform leaders this morning have been speaking about the fact that the man acccused of murdering former party spokeswoman Ann Widdecombe has also been charged with a terror plot against Nigel Farage.

Deputy leader Richard Tice told the BBC Radio 4’s Today programme his party leader has “not been listened to” over his safety concerns.

Farage has repeatedly raised the threats against his personal security before, often in the context of justifying or explaining why he’s received significant financial donations.

Tice said: “(Farage’s) security and the risks to it, he’s talked about over over many times, many occasions, over many years.

“And I think what’s particularly upsetting for those of us in and around him is sometimes he’s not being believed, not being listened to, and you know we have just got in a sense, deep regret over everything that’s happened over a long period of time.”

Police charged Joshua Kerry, 28, with the terror plot on Friday. He already stands accused of the brutal murder of Widdecombe, 78, a former Reform representative and Tory minister, who died on 8 July at her home in Devon.
