O’Reilly suggests building technology like Iron Dome to prevent hackers: ‘They’re not going away’

2026-11-19 19:32

Conservative commentator Bill O’Reilly said there should be a bipartisan effort to develop technology like the Iron Dome to protect the U.S.’s technology infrastructure against hackers.

“Both parties are going to have to say, look, we have to develop technology just as we did with the Iron Dome and the space weaponry, all that, to protect our infrastructure from hackers, because they’re not going away,” O’Reilly said during an appearance on NewsNation’s “Katie Pavlich Tonight.”

O’Reilly’s suggestion comes as Americans are recovering from widespread technology outages in the past week that have impacted flights, banking systems and more.

On Nov. 12, Microsoft faced global outages that impacted its platforms Outlook and Teams. The incident, which lasted about a day, was “introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change,” the company said.

A day later, the Federal Aviation Administration issued a rare nationwide ground stop that lasted more than four hours. Authorities attributed the incident to an overload of data on routers. Thousands of flights were delayed and canceled on Friday, impacting operations through the weekend.

Customers of several banks including Chase, Bank of America and Wells Fargo had trouble accessing their accounts Monday. A spokesperson from JPMorgan Chase told The Hill the cause was “a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems” and denied anything “cyber-related.”

Speculations flared over whether the outages were linked to a cyberattack, but Trump administration officials, including Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, have repeatedly said that is not the case.

In a Fox News interview, Mullin noted that he could say with “98 percent certainty” that the outages were not linked to a cyberattack but said “SI” or super intelligence could have been the reason.

Chase said Thursday on the social platform X that “third parties who provide services to the firm were the target of a cyber attack.”

“Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses,” the company added.

Raising concerns over hackers during the NewsNation interview, O’Reilly said, “There are millions of them. And they’re mercenaries. They’re criminals. They’re awful. And they can destroy lives. They can destroy countries.”

On Tuesday, the National Security Agency (NSA) faced a network outage, which disrupted the operations of NSANet, a classified internal network used by employees.

“They got into the NSA stuff today,” O’Reilly said. “The hackers got in. Now, don’t tell me that’s a functional thing. It’s not. These are targeted things.”

O’Reilly emphasized that when President Trump returns from his trip to China, where he took part in a rare three-way talk with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping, “he has to bring in all his tech people and security people and figure this out.”
