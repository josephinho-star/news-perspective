Turkey’s top court finds no rights violation in İmamoğlu’s pretrial detention

2026-11-26 10:44

Turkey’s Constitutional Court ruled Thursday that the pretrial detention of suspended İstanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu did not violate his rights, leaving one of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s leading rivals behind bars 613 days after he was jailed in a municipal corruption case, the ANKA news agency reported.

The full court found his complaint about the length of his detention admissible and voted 14–1 to find no violation. It rejected his other complaints unanimously.

İmamoğlu claimed that his detention violated his right to liberty and security as well as his rights to stand for election and participate in political activity.

The court’s written reasoning is expected later.

Police detained İmamoğlu on March 19, 2025. A judge ordered him jailed pending trial four days later on charges including bribery, bid-rigging and leading a criminal organization. He was suspended from office that day, and his detention sparked widespread protests across Turkey.

İmamoğlu denies the charges and says the prosecution is aimed at keeping him out of the presidential race. The government denies interfering in the judiciary.

İmamoğlu’s lawyers filed the application with the Constitutional Court on May 13, 2025, seeking his release and arguing that his detention was unlawful and had exceeded a reasonable period.

They said restrictions on access to the investigation file and shortcomings in judicial review violated his right to liberty.

The corruption trial involves 436 defendants. Earlier this month, the trial court ordered the release of İmamoğlu’s lawyer Mehmet Pehlivan and seven other defendants while keeping the mayor and 37 others in custody. The next hearing is scheduled for January 4.

İmamoğlu has also challenged his detention at the European Court of Human Rights, which in March asked Turkey whether there was reasonable suspicion to justify holding him and whether his detention served a political purpose.

Human Rights Watch and two other organizations urged the Strasbourg court to consider whether the case forms part of a wider pattern of politically motivated prosecutions.

İmamoğlu won the Republican People’s Party’s (CHP) presidential primary in March 2025. He left the CHP in August and joined the opposition New Party, which named him its presidential candidate.

Özgür Özel and 90 lawmakers founded the New Party in July after a court removed Özel as CHP chairman and reinstated his predecessor, Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu.

On Wednesday his lawyers said they would file a separate Constitutional Court application after Turkey’s highest administrative court upheld the revocation of his university degree.

Turkey’s constitution requires presidential candidates to have completed higher education.

The next presidential election is scheduled for 2028.
