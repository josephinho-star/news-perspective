Kremlin rejects Erdoğan’s call to scrap UN Security Council veto

2026-09-22 14:49

The Kremlin on Tuesday rejected President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan’s call to abolish permanent seats and veto power on the United Nations Security Council as the Turkish leader prepared to address the General Assembly in New York.

Russia supports adding permanent members to the council but wants the current five to keep their vetoes, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said.

Peskov described permanent membership and veto power as “very important elements” of the UN’s structure.

Erdoğan set out his position in a Bloomberg opinion article published during his visit to New York.

The article expressed a policy position rather than presenting a formal proposal or timetable for changing the UN Charter.

Erdoğan argued that the council still reflected the balance of power after World War II.

He said adding more permanent members would expand the existing privilege instead of ending it.

Erdoğan proposed a council with no permanent members and no vetoes.

Under his proposal, the General Assembly would elect all council members for fixed terms.

Countries seeking another term would have to win the support of a majority of UN members again.

The Security Council currently has 15 members. China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States hold permanent seats and veto power.

Changing that structure would require approval from two thirds of the General Assembly and ratification by two thirds of UN members, along with all five permanent council members.

Russia’s opposition therefore makes Erdoğan’s proposal impossible unless Moscow changes its position.

Elsewhere in New York, Erdoğan called for closer ties with the United States at a Turkish American business event Monday.

He said bilateral trade exceeded $38 billion in 2025 and reached $28 billion during the first eight months of 2026.

Erdoğan said the two countries’ $100 billion trade target was achievable.

He called for cooperation in nuclear energy, liquefied natural gas, civil aviation, advanced technology, defense and joint production.

US Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack also said the dispute over Turkey’s Russian S-400 air defense system would be resolved.

Barrack gave no timetable or details about a possible agreement.

Turkey’s purchase of the S-400 system led Washington to impose sanctions and remove the country from the F-35 fighter jet program.

During meetings that began Monday at the Turkish House, Erdoğan met the leaders of Vietnam, Libya, Kuwait, Kosovo, Lebanon and Georgia.

His talks with Vietnamese President To Lam focused on trade, investment and defense cooperation.

Erdoğan discussed Libya’s political stability, energy and defense ties with Presidential Council Chairman Mohamed al-Menfi.

His meetings with Kuwaiti Crown Prince Sheikh Sabah Khaled Al-Hamad Al-Sabah and Kosovo Prime Minister Albin Kurti covered trade, regional security and defense cooperation.

Erdoğan told Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam that an international initiative should replace the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon.

He discussed trade, transport and regional security with Georgian Prime Minister Irakli Kobakhidze.

Erdoğan was also due to address the UN General Assembly later Tuesday, with Gaza and Palestinian statehood expected to be central subjects.

He was scheduled to attend a UN climate event Wednesday before continuing his New York program.

No bilateral meeting between Erdoğan and US President Donald Trump had been announced at the time of publication.
