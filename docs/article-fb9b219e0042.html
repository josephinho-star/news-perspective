Denmark ratifies Trump security deal over Greenland after months of diplomatic tensions

2026-11-19 10:41

Danish lawmakers on Thursday approved a security agreement with U.S. President Donald Trump covering Greenland, seeking to end months of strained diplomatic tensions over the island's future.

Trump and the prime ministers of Denmark and Greenland signed the agreement in September. It allows Washington to expand its military presence on the island, paving the way for two new bases and the construction of a "Golden Dome" missile defense system.

Thursday's approval follows a protracted period in which Trump repeatedly threatened to take control of the self-governing Danish territory. Tensions reached fever pitch in January as the president repeatedly insisted that the U.S. needed Greenland for its national security, at times declining to rule out using military force to acquire it.

A parliamentary tally showed 109 Danish lawmakers unanimously backed the agreement on Thursday, per Reuters. Greenland's parliament ratified the deal earlier this month. It is not subject to a vote in the U.S. Senate.

The Danish government can now formally notify the U.S. that both parliaments have consented to complete the ratification process.

The deal supplements an existing 1951 defense agreement. It also maintains Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination.

Trump's proposed Golden Dome is a multibillion-dollar initiative designed to shield the U.S. from a range of aerial and missile threats. It was rolled out in May last year and is often compared with Israel's "Iron Dome" system.

Trump's threats alarmed Greenlanders and other Europeans and opened a rift between the U.S. and NATO, of which Denmark and the U.S. are both members. Greenland has maintained that it is not for sale, and the European Union earlier this year united to rebuke Trump.

— CNBC's Kevin Breuninger & Anniek Bao contributed to this report.
