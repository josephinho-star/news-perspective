Princess of Wales speaks about impact of cancer diagnosis in BBC film Heal

2026-11-21 14:42

The Princess of Wales has spoken candidly about the impact of her cancer diagnosis in a BBC documentary to be aired later this month.

“Cancer does change you,” Catherine, 44, says in the trailer for the 30-minute programme. “It changes your body. It changes you physically, but there’s a much, much deeper part to what a cancer diagnosis shifts in you.”

The film records Catherine taking on the Three Peaks challenge – climbing the three highest mountains in England, Scotland and Wales in 24 hours – and meeting with other people undergoing cancer treatment. Described by the BBC as a “highly personal film”, the documentary, titled Heal, will be shown on BBC One on 29 November.

The trailer emerged as it was reported that King Charles will voluntarily pay “mansion tax” on relevant royal household properties, despite crown exemption. The high-value council tax surcharge, often referred to as a mansion tax, will take effect in 2028 and apply to properties valued at more than £2m.

Catherine was diagnosed with an undisclosed form of cancer in early 2024, for which she received chemotherapy, and by January 2025 announced she was in remission.

She was treated at the Royal Marsden hospital in London, a world-leading oncology centre, with funds from the distribution of the documentary, which will also be broadcast in other countries, going to the Royal Marsden cancer charity.

When people successfully finish cancer treatment at the hospital they often ring a bell in celebration. “I didn’t ring the bell,” Catherine says in the trailer, speaking to her brother, James Middleton, as they are walking together.

“Maybe this is your ringing the bell,” he says, in reference to her undertaking the Three Peaks challenge and making the programme.

When she completed the challenge in June, 18 months after announcing that the cancer was in remission, she became the first royal to have achieved the feat – climbing Ben Nevis, Scafell Pike and Yr Wyddfa in less than 24 hours, having started on a Saturday evening.

In the programme, which will be introduced by the presenter Kirsty Young, Catherine talks about how she has learned through her recovery to find “a sense of hope and joy alongside uncertainty”.

Kate Phillips, the BBC’s chief content officer, said she hoped the documentary “will resonate with BBC viewers and provide comfort and support to those navigating their own health challenges”.
