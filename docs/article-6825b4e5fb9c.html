Victorian Greens call for major tech companies to compensate residents affected by existing datacentres

The Victorian Greens have called on the government to require major tech companies to compensate residents affected by existing datacentres for noise, pollution and falling property values through a levy.

It comes as cabinet meet today to consider restrictions on the industry, amid increasing community opposition to the expansion of datacentres across the state. The debate has seen the country’s largest electricity distributor, Jemena, halt connections to new datacentres, the Age reports.

In a letter to the artificial intelligence and digital economy minister, Anthony Carbines, the party has laid out its “urgent demands to protect communities from Labor’s reckless datacentre rush”.

Among the demands, the Greens also want an immediate moratorium on new AI datacentre approvals and construction in the absence of legislation, and prohibitions on new datacentres on Melbourne’s green wedges and other land of significant agricultural importance.

The Greens’ AI and datacentres spokesperson, Dr Sarah Mansfield, said:

Datacentre corporations and the global tech giants stand to make billions in profit, while Victorian families bear the costs – noise, pollution and falling property values.

If these corporations damage people’s homes, health or quality of life, they must be forced to compensate them in full.

Cabinet has an opportunity today to finally put communities ahead of tech-bro billionaires. Anything short of compensation, a moratorium and proper exclusion zones will be another capitulation to the datacentre lobby.

Victorian premier plans to pass work from home laws this week

Victoria’s premier, Ben Carroll, says the government will introduce amendments to its work from home bill and pass it through parliament this week.

Before she was deposed as leader, Carroll’s predecessor Jacinta Allan had announced plans to legislate the right to work from home two days a week for those who can “reasonably” do so and had secured bipartisan support for the bill.

But one of Carroll’s first acts as premier – after a media blitz by business groups – was to announce he would push back the bill’s commencement from 1 September to July 2027. The bill has then been sitting towards the bottom of the upper house notice paper after passing through the lower house.

This afternoon, he posted on social media that he had made some “sensible amendments” to the bill and expects it to pass this week:

We’re going to pass the right to work from home laws this week. Work from home works. That’s the message I’ve heard loud and clear. But these laws need to work for business too, because the reality is, if it’s not practical for employers, the laws won’t last. So I’ve made some sensible amendments to these laws to make them practical and affordable for businesses, while preserving the right for workers.

Carroll has previously signalled he was open to amendments that would ensure businesses don’t have to foot the bill for home office equipment and other costs.

Light plane plunges into Queensland paddock, sparks grass fire

A light aircraft has plunged into a rural property in Queensland, sparking a fast‑moving grass fire and a massive emergency response, with grave fears held for the people on board, AAP reports.

The plane went down on a paddock south of Brisbane after 11am on Monday, with shocked residents raising the alarm as smoke billowed across paddocks.

Crews were called to a grass fire just after 11am and arrived to find plane debris scattered on the Chambers Flat property and flames racing through dry grass across an area about 300 by 400 metres, authorities said.

At least a dozen fire trucks and multiple crews worked to contain the blaze, which burned close to a farmhouse before being brought under control about 12.50pm.

No homes were damaged.

Police have cordoned off the crash site and have yet to confirm how many people were on board or whether anyone has been injured.

Paramedics and firefighters remain at the scene as investigators work to piece together what caused the accident.

The Australian Transport Safety Bureau has been notified and will investigate the crash.

Arts Centre Melbourne workers take industrial action for the first time

Workers at Australia’s largest performing arts venue, Arts Centre Melbourne, have taken industrial action for the first time in its history, with multiple work stoppages delaying performances last week, and more expected to come.

Ushers, food and beverage attendants, stage managers, ticketing staff, programmers, electricians, technicians, and other unionised employees of the VVictorian government-run Arts Centre walked out of the building as audiences took their seats for five shows last week.

The workers are calling for a 7% annual pay rise to make up for wage stagnation since 2022. The previous enterprise agreement saw wages increase by 1% every six months, and many of the band 1 wages in the expired agreement are now below the national minimums. The Arts Centre had to increase affected workers’ pay after the recent minimum wage review to ensure they were not paid less than the national baseline. The Arts Centre told the Guardian none of its workers were paid below minimum wage, andhas offered a pay rise of 3% annually, the equivalent of the Victorian government wages cap.

Members of MEAA, the Community and Public Sector Union Victoria, and the Electrical Trades Union have been bargaining with the Arts Centre on a new enterprise agreement since March.

Footage posted to social media by the Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance (MEAA) last week showed audiences applauding a worker announcement explaining that the delay to the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra’s performance of How to Train Your Dragon 2 was caused by industrial action.

A union petition that had nearly 3000 signatures at the time of writing called on the Victorian government to “increase funding to Arts Centre Melbourne and give workers a real pay increase that reverses years of real wage decline”, linking worker pay to the broader issue of cultural funding.

A spokesperson for Arts Centre Melbourne said the organisation respected workers’ rights to take lawful industrial action, and continued to bargain in good faith with union representatives to reach an agreement that was “fair and financially sustainable outcome that is consistent with the Victorian Government Wages Policy”.

Arts Centre Melbourne has reached agreement with unions on a range of detailed conditions within the proposed Enterprise Agreement. The main outstanding issue is wage increases … During this period of industrial action, our priority is to minimise disruption for presenters, artists, and audiences, while maintaining a safe working environment for our team.
