2026-09-07 05:59

The shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, says Sydney house prices are “plummeting so fast that they are on track for the biggest crash in 40 years” in a question to the government.

Light plane plunges into Queensland paddock, sparks grass fire

A light aircraft has plunged into a rural property in Queensland, sparking a fast‑moving grass fire and a massive emergency response, with grave fears held for the people on board, AAP reports.

The plane went down on a paddock south of Brisbane after 11am on Monday, with shocked residents raising the alarm as smoke billowed across paddocks.

Crews were called to a grass fire just after 11am and arrived to find plane debris scattered on the Chambers Flat property and flames racing through dry grass across an area about 300 by 400 metres, authorities said.

At least a dozen fire trucks and multiple crews worked to contain the blaze, which burned close to a farmhouse before being brought under control about 12.50pm.

No homes were damaged.

Police have cordoned off the crash site and have yet to confirm how many people were on board or whether anyone has been injured.

Paramedics and firefighters remain at the scene as investigators work to piece together what caused the accident.

The Australian Transport Safety Bureau has been notified and will investigate the crash.

Arts Centre Melbourne workers take industrial action for the first time

Workers at Australia’s largest performing arts venue, Arts Centre Melbourne, have taken industrial action for the first time in its history, with multiple work stoppages delaying performances last week, and more expected to come.

Ushers, food and beverage attendants, stage managers, ticketing staff, programmers, electricians, technicians, and other unionised employees of the VVictorian government-run Arts Centre walked out of the building as audiences took their seats for five shows last week.

The workers are calling for a 7% annual pay rise to make up for wage stagnation since 2022. The previous enterprise agreement saw wages increase by 1% every six months, and many of the band 1 wages in the expired agreement are now below the national minimums. The Arts Centre has offered a pay rise of 3% annually, the equivalent of the Victorian government wages cap.

Members of MEAA, the Community and Public Sector Union Victoria, and the Electrical Trades Union have been bargaining with the Arts Centre on a new enterprise agreement since March.

Footage posted to social media by the Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance (MEAA) last week showed audiences applauding a worker announcement explaining that the delay to the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra’s performance of How to Train Your Dragon 2 was caused by industrial action.

A union petition that had nearly 3000 signatures at the time of writing called on the Victorian government to “increase funding to Arts Centre Melbourne and give workers a real pay increase that reverses years of real wage decline”, linking worker pay to the broader issue of cultural funding.

A spokesperson for Arts Centre Melbourne said the organisation respected workers’ rights to take lawful industrial action, and continued to bargain in good faith with union representatives to reach an agreement that was “fair and financially sustainable outcome that is consistent with the Victorian Government Wages Policy”.

Arts Centre Melbourne has reached agreement with unions on a range of detailed conditions within the proposed Enterprise Agreement. The main outstanding issue is wage increases … During this period of industrial action, our priority is to minimise disruption for presenters, artists, and audiences, while maintaining a safe working environment for our team.

Enforcement and compliance focus in tobacco war, Chalmers says

Nationals MP Llew O’Brien is demanding a yes/no answer on whether the government will rule out any reduction in the tobacco excise.

“The government’s priority is to deal with this as an enforcement issue, a compliance issue, a law enforcement issue,” Jim Chalmers says.

In answer to objections from the opposition about the relevance of his answer, the speaker, Milton Dick, says he can’t force the treasurer to say yes or no.

Jim Chalmers is quoting the shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson – who was just booted from the chamber for being rowdy – saying he would “like to slow the growth in the price of housing”.

“Then he says he wrote a whole book about it,” Chalmers says.

And we’re back to another demand the government join the Coalition to “crush the organised crime gangs”.

The health minister, Mark Butler, talks about the opposition increasing the tobacco excise by far more than Labor did when they were in power.

“And they trousered every single dollar,” he says.

He says Queensland and South Australia have put extra enforcement powers in place and there’s a “completely different picture” to that playing out in NSW and Victoria.

Taylor says tobacco excise cut would ‘crush’ organised crime

Now to the tobacco excise wars, sparked by the explosion in illegal tobacco.

Opposition leader Angus Taylor talks about the firebombing of small businesses and the need to “crush the organised crime gangs”. The opposition wants to cut the excise cut on cigarettes, so they would be more competitive on price.

Leader of the house Tony Burke says “the last thing this parliament should ever do is outsource its health policy to organised crime”.

It has to be dealt with through law enforcement, he says, adding:

They know all too well that the exact impact of what they are proposing would be an increase in tobacco consumption in Australia.
