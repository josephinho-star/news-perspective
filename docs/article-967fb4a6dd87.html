Remembrance ceremonies held across the US to honor victims of 9/11

2026-09-11 14:35

The United States was marking the 25th anniversary of the September 11 terrorist attacks on Friday, with memorials and remembrance ceremonies taking place across the country to honor the nearly 3,000 people killed in the attacks.

In New York City, the national September 11 memorial and museum’s annual commemoration ceremony got under way on Friday morning. Family members of victims gathered at the 9/11 Memorial Plaza as the names of those killed in the 9/11 attacks and in the 1993 World Trade Center bombing were read aloud.

The 25th commemoration ceremony began with the first moment of silence at 8.46am, marking the moment when the first passenger plane hijacked by al-Qaida jihadists struck the north tower of the World Trade Center. This year seven moments of silence were planned in total. These include the annual six marking the times when each of the twin towers was struck and then fell, as well as the times of the attack on the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia, and the crash of Flight 93 in Pennsylvania.

And a seventh moment of silence, which was added to the ceremony this year, was to honor the thousands of people who “have died from the health effects of 9/11 and its aftermath” in the years since the attacks. Working on rescue and recovery and cleaning up for months amid the toxicity created at Ground Zero and in lower Manhattan has been linked to a wide swath of cancers and respiratory diseases.

Current and former New York City leaders and other political figures attended the ceremony, including the city’s mayor, Zohran Mamdani as well as former mayors Michael Bloomberg, Rudy Giuliani and Bill de Blasio.

Joe Biden and Jill Biden also attended, along with Barack Obama and Michelle Obama; George W Bush and Laura Bush; and Bill Clinton and Hillary Clinton.

The US vice-president, JD Vance also attended the New York ceremony, as well as Howard Lutnick, the commerce secretary.

Donald Trump and first lady Melania Trump are attending the ceremony at the Pentagon, where American Airlines Flight 77 crashed after being hijacked. That ceremony began at 9am ET. The US president is expected to speak, and the Trumps will participate in a wreath-laying ceremony.

At the Pentagon ceremony on Friday morning, Condoleezza Rice, the former secretary of state who was in the White House on the day of the attacks, used her remarks to focus on how ordinary people responded.

“People gave blood. They filled houses, worshipped, prayed. They took care of the vulnerable, children, the elderly, the infirm. They comforted families of the fallen,” Rice said.

She noted the response also extended beyond the US, pointing to air traffic controllers who worked to land diverted planes, Nato’s decision to invoke Article 5 for the first time, by which an attack on one member is considered an attack on the collective, and Canadians who took in stranded travelers, sometimes for days.

“Heroism and compassion were commonplace, but uncommon in the depth and the breadth of their generosity and their kindness,” she said.

Pete Hegseth, the defense secretary, used his remarks at the Pentagon ceremony to link the 9/11 attacks directly to the ongoing US war with Iran, framing both as part of a continuing religious and civilizational struggle.

“The struggle against evil continued, and it continues now” he said. “It will continue until judgment day. Our only answer here on Earth is eternal vigilance.”

He also warned that “enemies plot to curb our power and kill our citizens, including from Islamic terrorists abroad and at home”.

He went on to credit Trump with dismantling Iran’s military capabilities, saying “the United States killed their leaders” and vowed that “we’re still sending terrorists where they belong, to hell, and tankers to the bottom of the ocean, where they belong.”

He added: “We are ensuring that Iran will never have a nuclear weapon.”

Hegseth closed by invoking scripture, telling the audience that “the world will always have war and rumors of war”, before calling for a new generation of “strong, brave warriors who will say, when it matters most, ‘send me’”.

Earlier on Friday morning, a large American flag was unfurled against the side of the Pentagon building, echoing one draped that way on the day of the attacks.

On Wednesday night in New York City, a drone show featuring 2,977 lights, one for each person killed in the attacks, illuminated the skies over New York Harbor, before forming the outline of the twin towers.

Friday’s memorial ceremonies come just days after the Mamdani administration released more than 170,000 documents related to the health concerns, air quality and the city’s response to the attacks, including records suggesting that New Yorkers were misled about the safety of the air quality after the attacks.

In Virginia, the national 9/11 Pentagon memorial will be closed to the public from 3am through noon on Friday for the Pentagon’s 25th anniversary 9/11 observance ceremony, with the exception of any victims’ families, survivors or survivors’ families.

In Pennsylvania, a ceremony is also planned at the Flight 93 national memorial, where United Airlines Flight 93 crashed after being hijacked, beginning at 8.30am ET. The morning observance will include a reading of the names of the passengers and crew members, followed by the ringing of the bells of remembrance. The afternoon ceremony is set to feature “remarks, keynote speakers, music and concludes with a public wreath-laying ceremony at the Wall of Names”, according to the National Park Service.

On Saturday, another ceremony will take place at the Flight 93 national memorial, where 40 candle lanterns will be carried to the Wall of Names and placed beneath the names of each passenger and crew member.

Additional ceremonies and memorial events are scheduled on Friday across the country as communities mark 25 years since the attacks.
