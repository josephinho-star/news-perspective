Breanna Stewart brings World Cup MVP momentum back to Liberty for playoff push

2026-09-18 00:00

Breanna Stewart’s confidence in her game is easy to see, so when she was asked Thursday, before the Liberty practiced at Barclays Center, if her FIBA World Cup MVP had a bit more meaning since she hasn’t been the focal point of the WNBA MVP conversation, her answer reflected that.

But more than anything, during her 10 days in Berlin as part of the Team USA squad at the World Cup, her focus was on winning. Stewart was an instrumental part in helping the U.S. win its fifth consecutive FIBA World Cup title, which included toppling France in the gold medal game.

“There was enough talk about all the things, about it [being] France’s time and whatever,” Stewart said. “I wanted to do whatever I could to win that game. And if that made me MVP, then that made me MVP. But really, just happy to be bringing back the gold.”

With the World Cup behind her, Stewart’s focus shifts back to the Liberty and the home stretch of the WNBA season. The Liberty have clinched a playoff berth, but their seeding in the postseason is far from solidified with four games left in the regular season and a date with the league-leading Minnesota Lynx right out of the break.

Stewart — who scored 22 points in the gold medal game against France and averaged 12.8 points, 7.3 rebounds and 2.8 assists per game during the tournament — found there are parallels between what she took away at the World Cup and the stretch of four games in seven days that the Liberty have to end the regular season.

“Knowing how important these four are, and Chris [DeMarco, head coach] talked about it a little bit [Wednesday], but the mindset and mentality of doing whatever it takes,” Stewart said. “I think that sometimes with USA Basketball or with national team, you’re constantly just, like, going, right? You have to give 1,000 percent, and that’s the same here.

“It’s like, don’t save it, because we have a deep enough team. We have people that can come in and do things just as well.”

The Liberty’s busy final week of the regular season won’t be an easy one, with the Lynx being the first team they’ll face while also having two games against the Atlanta Dream to close things out. The Liberty are two games back of the Dream and seven games back of the Lynx.

The Liberty, who have had plenty of ups and downs this season and were 4-3 in seven games going into the break, could still finish as high as third or as low as eighth depending on how things shake out.

“I think it’s all about who finds their chemistry first after a break and we had many players home, it’ll be a challenge,” Leonie Fiebich said of Friday’s game against Minnesota.

“We know that we have a great team here, but we had some up and downs during the season, and just, like, finishing strong,” Marine Johannes added.

The Liberty will go into Friday with DeMarco anticipating every player to be available, though he warned that things could change. Outside of injured forward Satou Sabally, the Liberty had everyone on the court for practice in Brooklyn.

“It’s nice to have the whole team together again, that’s for sure,” DeMarco said.
