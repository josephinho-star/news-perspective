Zelenskyy denounces Trump’s decision to lift diesel sanctions on Russia as ‘weak’ – US politics live

2026-10-09 22:01

Zelenskyy denounces Trump's decision to lift diesel sanctions on Russia as 'weak'

Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, denounced Donald Trump’s decision to suspend sanctions on Russia, to let the US buy diesel fuel from the nation that kills Ukrainian civilians daily, in an interview with Axios on Friday.

Zelenskyy, who had traveled to Washington to plead with US lawmakers to pass tougher sanctions on Russia last month, reportedly told Axios on Friday that Trump’s decision, was “not fair and not honest” and “looks like a happy birthday present for Putin”.

“It looks absolutely terrible,” he added.

Ukraine’s president also criticized Trump’s decision in a statement on social media, in which he wrote:

Peace never comes through weakness. Today, representatives of the U.S. Congress were in Kyiv – a bipartisan delegation – and I am grateful to them for visiting Ukraine at such a difficult time. They asked: what is needed to end the war? The answer must be strong. There must be sanctions under the Lindsey Graham bill, there must be support for Ukraine in protecting lives, and there must be pressure on Russia for its war. And today, there was a conversation between the leaders of America and Russia. A conversation that leads to something different. …

Any easing of sanctions against Russia without a clear and lasting de-escalation agreement with Russia is an obvious weakness. It plays into Russia’s hands – allowing it to kill more, wage war for longer, have even less respect for America, and inflict even greater losses and damage on the world. …

Gifts to Putin will not bring peace or any benefit to the civilized world. Russia will “repay” the diesel with further terror and perfidy. Allowing Russia to sell petroleum products is an investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged. What is needed is real de-escalation with Russia on a reciprocal basis. We count on America’s fair support for our defense of life, for our defense of people in Ukraine – and on the United States having a correspondingly strong conversation with Russia. A strong one, not a weak one.

'I want to thank president Putin', Trump says, after agreeing to lift sanctions on Russian diesel ahead of midterms

As he left the White House on Friday for a rally in New York state, Donald Trump extended his thanks to Russia’s president, Vladimir Putin, for agreeing to sell diesel fuel to the US in return for the lifting of US sanctions on fuel exports from Russia, even as Russia’s deadly war on Ukraine continues.

When a reporter asked Trump to comment on the response from Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who said “that you are weak”, Trump ignored the question and turned to the rest op the press corps to boast about the deal.

“I think you heard about the oil deal with Russia. That is a big deal,” Trump said.

“That is massive amounts of oil coming into our country. And I want to thank president Putin, to be honest with you. We have massive amounts of oil coming into our country, and it’s diesel which is what we want, so: thank you,” the president said.

“Zelenskyy said you are weak because of it,” the reporter told Trump. “He just criticized you.”

“Who said that?” Trump leaned in and asked.

“Zelenskyy, the Ukrainian president, just said you are weak because of the deal you made with Putin,” the journalist replied.

Trump stepped away and called on another journalist instead of responding.

As Republican senators met Zelenskyy to discuss tougher sanctions on Russia, Trump struck deal with Putin to buy diesel

Donald Trump’s surprise announcement on Friday, that the US will buy diesel from Russia, and is lifting sanctions for six months to enable Russia to export diesel freely, came as two Republican senators, Kevin Cramer of North Dakota and Roger Wicker of Mississippi, were in Kyiv meeting with Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, to discuss implementing the tough new Russia sanctions law signed by Trump just three weeks ago.

“We can only protect ourselves from this terror by working together, and we discussed how the United States can help save lives,” Zelenskyy wrote on social media, with video of the meeting also attended by two Republican congressmen, Dan Crenshaw and Marc Veasey, both of Texas.

“Of course, we expect the Lindsey Graham Act to be implemented,” Zelenskyy added. “This is a matter of political will, and it is now important to show leadership and fully cut off the financial flows sustaining Russia’s war machine.”

Trump says he struck a deal with Putin for Russia to sell diesel to US, weeks after signing Russia sanctions law

Just weeks after signing a new law that gave him the authority to impose tariffs on countries that buy fuel from Russia despite US sanctions, Donald Trump announced on Friday that the US will now be buying fuel from Russia.

Writing on social media, Trump said that he stuck a deal with Russia’s president, Vladimir Putin, “wherein it was agreed that Russia will immediately supply over 300,000 Tons of Diesel Fuel to the American and Global Marketplace,” to be followed by another 4.5m tons of diesel fuel exports.

A short time later, the US treasury’s office of foreign assets control announced that, at Trump’s direction, it had lifted sanctions on the sale of Russian diesel to the global market, “including importation into the United States for six months.

Trump claimed that the deal, which suspends an effort to punish Russia for its full-scale invasion of Ukraine, would soon drive down the cost of fuel in the US, which has been a concern to voters casting ballots in the midterm elections.

The Lindsey Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, signed into law on 18 September, authorized the president to impose tariffs on countries “facilitating Russian oil sanctions evasion”, but also gave him broad authority to waive any sanctions or tariffs. Those provisions had led some Democrats to oppose the legislation, arguing that it delegated too much authority to Trump.

Vance says he's doesn't know if execution live stream 'is actually going to happen'

More Republicans decry Pentagon's plan to livestream military execution

More Republican lawmakers have criticized the Pentagon’s plan to livestream the execution by firing squad of Nidal Hasan – the US army psychiatrist who was sentenced to death after he murdered 13 people at the Ford Hood army base in Texas in 2009.

Senator John Curtis, a Republican from Utah, began his plea for defense secretary Pete Hegseth to reconsider broadcasting the execution by invoking Seneca – the ancient philosopher who warned that Rome’s “bloody spectacles corrupted the people who watched them”.

“A civilization erodes when it cultivates an appetite for executions as a form of entertainment,” Curtis said in a post on X, cautioning that livestreaming the event “risks feeding the appetite”.

He added: “The victims deserve justice administered with solemnity. Broadcasting a death diminishes us and is not an effective deterrent.”

Meanwhile, Rob Bresnahan – a GOP congressman who is facing a competitive re-election battle for his central Pennsylvania district in the upcoming midterms – said the execution “shouldn’t be a live streamed spectacle” and the victims and their families’ pain “deserves respect, not a public show”.

Trump administration announces new sanctions on International Criminal Court

The Trump administration today announced a set of new sanctions on the international criminal court, a global court which investigates and prosecutes individuals accused of atrocities and other crimes against humanity.

“America never joined the court. We don’t need that court, and we don’t want it,” said secretary of state Marco Rubio in a video on Friday. “They want to prosecute border patrol agents that are protecting our nation’s borders, and they want to prosecute our elected leaders that are ensuring our national security … President Trump will never allow the international criminal court to prosecute Americans.”

The new sanctions target the court as an institution, blocking any ICC assets in the US and giving US companies and individuals six months to wind down any business with the court.

“Either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC. And we expect our allies, many of whom are part of the ICC and that rely on American service members for their defense, to rein in this rogue court,” Rubio added.

In a statement, the ICC said the sanctions are an “assault on the rule of law and on the very foundations of the international legal order”.

The administration’s announcement today comes after Navi Pillay, a former ICC judge, won the Nobel Peace prize – an award that Donald Trump has long-coveted.

She joked with reporters on Friday that “if you clap a little bit more, I’m going to share this prize with Trump.” Last year’s winner, María Corina Machado, the Venezuelan opposition leader, drew criticism for handing her medal to Trump. However, the Nobel committee acknowledged that the honor could not be transferred.

A reminder that in 2024, the ICC issued arrest warrants for Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former defense minister Yoav Gallant, for alleged war crimes. A move that the Trump administration has repeatedly condemned.

Last year, Donald Trump imposed personal sanctions against ICC officials deemed to be involved in investigations of Americans or Israelis.

At the White House today, Donald Trump mentioned the upcoming execution of Nidal Hasan.

“Today you can do almost anything. Nothing happens,” Trump said. “But we’re changing that … in fact, we’re giving a man the death penalty who killed 14 soldiers a couple of years ago.”

Hasan – a former the US army psychiatrist – actually shot and killed 13 people, 12 of whom were soldiers, at the Fort Hood base in 2009.

Trump, however, did not comment on the Pentagon’s decision to livestream the execution – which is set to be the US’s first by firing squad since 1945, as well as the first military execution since 1961 and the first ever to be broadcast for people to watch online.

Jack Reed, the Senate armed services committee ranking member, has also condemned the Pentagon’s decision to livestream the execution of the Fort Hood shooter.

On Friday, Reed called the decision “a barbaric spectacle”, adding: “This is yet another stunt by Secretary Hegseth to distract from his tenure’s repeated failings which have only weakened our national security and standing in the world.”

Reed called on the Donald Trump administration to “reconsider this abhorrent move” and said that a livestream would be a “gift to anti-American terrorist recruiters”.

A survivor of the 2009 Fort Hood mass shooting said that he supports the execution of the gunman but was concerned about the Pentagon’s decision to livestream it.

Speaking to the BBC on Friday, Alonzo Lunsford, a former US army sergeant who was shot seven times at the military base in Texas, said: “To show the whole thing without it being censored for the general public, for me personally, I don’t agree with that, but it’s not about my personal belief.”

He added that it was “not something you should view for entertainment purposes”.

Lunsford’s comments come after defense secretary Pete Hegseth announced on Thursday that the execution of US army psychiatrist Nidal Hasan by firing squad will be a public event.

The UN has condemned the Pentagon’s decision, saying that the live stream of an execution “is tantamount to a public execution and would breach the absolute prohibition of torture”.

Trump establishes committee to investigate Fed governor Lisa Cook

Donald Trump has set up a committee to investigate Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook as part of his latest efforts to fire her.

The “committee of inquiry” will investigate “false statements in connection with one or more mortgage instruments” that the White House says Cook made.

“As president, it is my job to ensure the laws are faithfully executed, including by firing subordinates who cannot be trusted to tell the truth and follow the law,” Trump said in a statement.

This latest move by Trump comes just months after the supreme court ruled that Cook, a Joe Biden appointee, was entitled to remain as Fed governor as she fights Trump’s unproved allegations against her.

Last August, Trump fired Cook on social media, accusing her of mortgage fraud, which involves misrepresenting a property as a primary residence to secure better loan terms. Cook denied the allegations and sued the administration, arguing she was fired without cause.

Trump’s establishment of a committee to investigate Cook also comes just days after the Federal Reserve’s inspector general found that there was no basis for the justice department to reopen its criminal investigation into the central bank’s renovations in Washington DC.

Mullin says Mamdani's comments after New York ICE shooting are 'dangerous and incite hostility'

At a press conference today, Markwayne Mullin – the Department of Homeland Security secretary – addressed the shooting by an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agent of a man in the Marble Hill neighborhood of the Bronx, New York during an enforcement operation on Thursday.

The man was conscious and hospitalized, authorities said, but the shooting quickly sparked angry protests from local residents and elected officials.

Officials on Friday identified the man as Oscar Belgal, 28, who they said was the subject of an ongoing DHS investigation and was allegedly involved in the shooting of border patrol officer two years ago. On Thursday federal immigration agents attempted to arrest Belgal, officials said, but he allegedly refused orders to exit his vehicle, before accelerating toward the officers who fired shots that struck Belgal “superficially in the neck”. After he stopped his vehicle officers administered first aid and took him to the hospital, an Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO) director.

Early reports indicated that there was a five-year-old child in Belgal’s car, in the residential area where agents fired multiple rounds, according to the city’s mayor, Zohran Mamdani. The child was physically unharmed, he said.

Later at a press conference, Mamdani demanded from the Trump administration that ICE stop its enforcement actions in New York City. “ICE is rotten to its core,” he said.

Today, however, Mullin said that agents weren’t aware there was a child in the car, but placed blame on Belgal. “He knew he had a child in his car,” Mullin said. “That outrage should be with him for endangering the five-year-old.”

He added that body cameras were worn by the ICE agents, and officials will release more information as the investigation moves forward.

Speaking today, Mullin also slammed the mayor’s comments, adding that his words were “dangerous and incite hostility”.

The homeland security secretary said that he spoke to the Mamdani several times on Thursday night, and said they mayor wanted official to release Belgal.

“President Trump actually got involved and said that we would release him to New York’s finest,” Mullin said. “If you abolish ICE, who’s going to take the criminals off the streets of your own town?”

Broadcasting an execution could cause post-traumatic stress disorder in the viewing public, mental health experts have said after Hegseth’s announcement that the firing squad execution of Nidal Hasan – the former US army psychiatrist who killed 13 people at Fort Hood – would be livestreamed.

Dr Simon Duff, a forensic psychologist at the University of Nottingham in the UK, said that while there was not a lot of research into the impact of watching an execution, studies examining journalists’ experiences of witnessing violence suggested evidence of trauma response.

“Certainly if people are not resilient they probably shouldn’t [watch], but if they have some underlying psychological driver towards viewing an execution they will likely do so regardless of the potential for harm,” Duff said.

“Recent events suggest that executions do not always go to plan,” he said, referring to the failed execution by lethal injection of Christa Pike in the US. “That possibility needs to be acknowledged, and the likelihood that this would increase the trauma for viewers.”

Neil Greenberg, a professor of defence mental health at the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience at King’s College London, said trigger warnings did not routinely lead to people avoiding media they may find distressing.

“Instead they are likely to suffer anticipatory anxiety before the ‘trauma’ is shown and some people will then become distressed,” he said.

“It is likely that people who have pre-existing mental health problems will be especially at risk of becoming distressed or experiencing a deterioration in their mental health.

“My advice would be not to watch the event at all and I would hope that mainstream media would not transmit highly traumatic images [or] sounds,” Greenberg said.

Public executions were once common around the world, including in the UK, but only two out of 17 countries that carried out executions last year did so publicly.
