NYC nurse still wearing scrubs killed in caught-on-camera hit-and-run crash

2026-08-30 22:22

A speeding hit-and-run driver killed a 28-year-old nurse walking home from working the overnight shift at a nearby Brooklyn hospital on Saturday, according to officials and video of the crash.

Jiayi Zheng — still wearing her forest green scrubs — was walking along the crosswalk at 55th Street and Third Avenue when the operator of a black Cadillac sedan drove through a red light and barreled into her around 7:30 a.m. in Sunset Park, disturbing footage shows.

Zheng was sent flying in the air before landing on the median, just a block away from where she worked at NYU Langone-Brooklyn Hospital as a medical- surgical unit nurse.

Medics rushed her to the same hospital, but she succumbed to her head and neck injuries.

The heartless driver — who police sources said had Pennsylvania plates — never stopped.

“I heard the bang. Her bag was over here, and they left her there,” witness Antonio Perez told News 12 Brooklyn.

“The car just kept on, it never stopped for nothing at all.”

The deadly crash unfolded on a perilous roadway, according to Sen. Andrew Gounardes, who represents the neighborhood.

A Department of Transportation redesign plan for the dangerous stretch of roadway was presented in 2023 but never went into effect.

“While the investigation proceeds, I am once again calling on @NYC_DOT to work with the community to make 3rd Ave safer,” Gounardes wrote on X. “Just over a year age we lost two elderly neighbors to a similar crash, and 3rd Ave remains one of the most dangerous corridors in Brooklyn. Enough is enough.”

Zheng is the ninth person killed by a hit-and-run driver in the last two weeks across the Big Apple — and 11th killed while walking or biking the two-mile stretch of Third Avenue that runs under the Brooklyn Queens Expressway since 2014, according to advocacy group Transportation Alternatives.

“New York City streets should be the envy of the world, but we are the envy of no one today,” Ben Furnas, their executive director, said in a statement.

Zheng’s colleagues remembered her as a “loving nurse and hard worker” in the medical-surgical unit, News 12 reported.

“We are deeply saddened by the passing of Jiayi Zheng, a nurse at NYU Langone Hospital-Brooklyn,” a spokesperson for the hospital said in a statement to The Post.

“Our nurses play an essential role in caring for others with compassion and dedication, and this loss is being felt across our entire community.”

The driver has yet to be caught as of Sunday afternoon.
