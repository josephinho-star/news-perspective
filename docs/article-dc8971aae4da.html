Elon Musk claims ‘I’m not Maga’ and says he regrets vast sums to back Republicans

2026-11-19 01:07

Elon Musk denied being Maga on Wednesday, despite aligning himself intensely with Donald Trump’s Make America Great Again movement since 2024.

“I’m not Maga,” the world’s richest person told the New York Times Dealbook summit, which he attended via video call from China, where he is one of several US business leaders who have accompanied Trump on his trip to Asia.

“I’m not sure what Maga means, but if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican party, then I certainly don’t have that,” the chief executive of SpaceX and Tesla added.

Musk famously declared at a 2024 campaign rally in Madison Square Garden: “I’m not just Maga. I’m dark, gothic Maga.”

In a 45-minute interview with Andrew Ross Sorkin, a New York Times columnist, Musk also said it would be good for Washington to have split control between the two parties, and that he thinks “there are positives and negatives in both”.

He also accused Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, of “lying” about restrictions on access to his firm’s Starshield satellite system; praised Jensen Huang, the ousted Nvidia chief executive; and said that “no one I met at Anthropic set off my evil detector”.

Asked about his previous negative comments about Anthropic, Musk said that before he agreed to rent computing power to the AI firm: “I went to met with a lot of people at Anthropic. I didn’t just move forward with the deal.”

“My interaction so far with the Anthropic team is that they seem like good people and they seem principled,” Musk added. “They seem diligent and they seem seem to care about the future.”

Musk donated at least $250m of his own money to help Trump win the election in 2024, and recently spent nearly $500m in the US midterms to support Republicans, many of whom lost.

Musk admitted at the Dealbook summit that his spending had “failed”, and that he felt “pretty foolish” for spending such a sum, the most that anyone has ever spent in a political cycle when they themselves did not run for office. “It certainly shows that American elections are not just about money,” he said. “If I could rewind the clock, I would have spent less money.”

After Trump took office for the second time in 2025, Musk served for 180 days heading the so-called “department of government efficiency” (Doge) within Trump’s administration, before stepping down following a very public falling out with the president.

That culminated in Musk posting on his social media platform in June of that year: “Time to drop the really big bomb: @realDonaldTrump is in the Epstein files. That is the real reason they have not been made public.”

The two men later reconciled. During his time leading Doge, Musk frequently appeared in the Oval Office and at cabinet meetings alongside the president, often sporting hats that read “Make America Great Again” or “Trump was right about everything”.
