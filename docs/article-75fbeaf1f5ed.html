China’s Moonshot and Z.ai bring AI model subscription race to Tmall’s retail shelves

2026-09-07 08:00

By packaging tokens and coding plans like everyday retail goods, AI labs are opening a new front in their push for sustainable revenue

China’s artificial intelligence developers are competing for paying users on a new consumer front: Alibaba Group Holding’s Tmall, where AI subscription plans are now being sold alongside smartphones, skincare and luxury fashion brands.

Beijing-based Z.ai, also known as Zhipu AI, kicked off the trend last week by opening an online storefront selling subscriptions to its GLM Coding Plan. A day later, Tmall launched an “AI Space Station”, a central marketplace where users can buy AI subscriptions, top up tokens and purchase specialised packages from Alibaba Cloud, Z.ai, MiniMax and DeepSeek.

Early market signals have been positive. Within 48 hours of Z.ai opening its storefront, transactions involving AI tokens and subscription products across Taobao and Tmall jumped by more than 160 per cent, a Tmall spokesperson told the SCMP on Monday.

By putting access to models on digital retail shelves, Chinese developers are turning software subscriptions into everyday retail products and could become less reliant on direct website sales.

Poe Zhao, a China technology analyst and founder of Hello China Tech, said Tmall gave developers a more efficient channel to acquire paying users, but with a trade-off, as it made them “more dependent on traffic controlled by the platform”.
