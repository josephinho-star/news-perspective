Owner of LA celebrity hotspot breaks down in tears as he reveals KBBQ restaurant is drowning in $1M debt

2026-11-24 19:22

The owner of a popular celebrity hotspot broke down in tears as he revealed his Korean BBQ restaurant is drowning in $1 million in debt.

Jongpil Kim, of MUN Korean Steakhouse in Los Angeles, has served a whole host of star-studded guests since opening the doors in 2021, including K-pop icons from the eight-member boy band ATEEZ.

The upscale eatery describes itself as being a “perfect blend of tradition and modernity with premium meats, flavorful banchan, and an inviting atmosphere.”

But its financial struggles were recently laid bare in a video circulating on social media as Kim welcomed entrepreneurs looking to partner with MUN.

In the clip, shared on the Instagram page of Korean chip brand Sarang Snacks, founders Hochun Lee and Ian Emlet turned up in the hopes of partnering with the restaurant.

Dad-of-two Kim welcomed the duo and offered them a meal before eventually opening up about his money woes, claiming sales had been cut in half.

“We are still paying back what we couldn’t pay in COVID, little by little,” he said, tearfully adding that MUN still owed $1 million as he wiped his eyes.

“I leave the house at 6am, I drive for Uber, I go to the restaurant at 4pm, I get home around 1am. Watching my kids sleep is when my stress finally goes away. That’s why I do it. I do it for my kids.”

An emotional Kim, who left his office job in Korea six years ago at age 43, paused again to wipe his face before revealing that he has no backup plan should the restaurant fail.

“Honestly, there is no plan B. If the business closes, we can’t stay in America. No matter what, the business has to survive,” he explained.

Kim has since spoken to the California Post about the debt and delved further into his financial situation.

“It is true that our business has accumulated over $1 million in debt due to a series of unforeseen challenges,” he told The Post.

The businessman then pointed to a series of factors including “prolonged construction during the pandemic, back rent, taxes, and lower sales caused by external crises such as overseas wars and the LA wildfires.”

Kim described his journey at the restaurant as “deeply challenging,” adding “yet it has also brought so many happy memories with our guests.”

He thanked the community for its support before sharing what the future might look like for MUN.

“Going forward, our main focus is paying down our remaining obligations, keeping our doors open, and giving back through great food and genuine Korean hospitality,” Kim concluded.

“I feel deeply fortunate to have received unconditional love and support from so many people.”

In light of their video gaining momentum online, Lee and Emlet set up a GoFundMe page in the hopes of raising funds for MUN Steakhouse.

It urged readers to donate to support Kim and his family, who “have nothing left to go back to in Korea.”

It has already substantially exceeded its target, having so far raised more than $158,000 of its requested $130,000.
