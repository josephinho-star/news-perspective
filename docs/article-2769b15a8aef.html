The Shane Gillis attack article is just another ‘bad boyfriend gotcha’ miss

2026-11-26 00:37

Shane Gillis’ ex-girlfriend wants everyone to know he’s a really bad boyfriend. And an insecure jerk.

Grace Brassel told the world in a very long, excruciatingly self-pitying essay, detailing the rise and fall of their great love affair which lasted about a year.

“After our time together, how do I think he feels about women? He doesn’t like them much,” she wrote in The Cut.

According to Brassel, there were mean comments disguised as jokes, infidelity and an O.J. Simpson estate sale. But most of all there were celebrities. And when there weren’t celebrities, she noted her disappointment.

“I wanted the party to be perfect since he said Bradley Cooper and Gigi Hadid would be there. They weren’t,” she wrote of a Halloween bash she and Gillis hosted.

The pair started dating after she dropped into his DMs.

The comedian, who is 12 years her senior, flew her out to Austin. He wined her, dined her and bought her an $800 dress for Olivia Culpo and Christian McCaffrey’s wedding. She was enthralled with the perks of dating one of the world’s most famous men.

“Shane was rich, he had a cool car and his house was lined floor to ceiling with awards,” she wrote.

Within two months the pair were living together and partying with other celebs so much, she couldn’t keep up her day job of social media manager. So she quit to become his live-in, stay-at-home girlfriend. Brassel ignored “red flags,” including the time he “spent thousands of dollars at O.J. Simpson’s estate sale because he thought it was funny,” she wrote. “I saw him as my best friend, so I didn’t think too much about it . . .”

(Wait, we’re supposed to hate this guy?)

But she lost herself in his frat boy universe. Fights happened. He cheated. She threw a matcha at the wall and in another instance punched a hole in a wall.

Brassel was no friend to walls. Nor to herself in that essay.

In the moment, burping all that out probably felt deliciously satisfying. She likely believed she was adding real value to a massive cultural moment, providing definitive proof that the controversial joke about slutty gymwear in Gillis’ “Saturday Night Live” monologue wasn’t just a joke. It was coming from a place of deep, sincere misogyny. She wrote that backlash against his “lazy” monologue brought her “closure.”

But the piece missed the target entirely. Instead, the nuclear revenge bomb detonated on her. Brassel looked sad, bitter and immature. Most of all, she came across as untrustworthy.

This isn’t a defense of Gillis.

Rather, it’s a defense of keeping complicated, private relationships, well, private.

Brassel is young. She was clearly swept up into this world of money, celebrity and partying. She was on the arm of the coolest dude. Then suddenly she wasn’t.

And the hangover has hit her hard.

But why didn’t any of her friends step in to warn her that publishing such an intimate piece was so terribly foolish and shortsighted? And that it most certainly wasn’t going to make her more appealing to future partners. Let alone someone of prominence who can provide the flashy, monied, celebrity rich life she clearly enjoyed.

Brassel claimed it took her a “year to find” her “own voice again.” But she can’t stop using that voice to bash Gillis.

This is reminiscent of the Jonah Hill episode back in 2023 when his ex-girlfriend Sarah Brady posted their private texts on Instagram, showing that he was controlling and mean. But the gross breach of trust and the betrayal made Brady look unstable, sad and desperate considering Hill had moved on with another woman.

The inclination to overshare is powerful in our narcissistic culture, where people are rewarded with the affirmation from strangers for broadcasting “their truth” or trauma. But it’s poisonous to our actual interpersonal relationships.

And now, I await Gillis’ version. And when I say his version, I mean a complete and detailed list of items he bought from the O.J. collection.

Perhaps Gillis is a terrible boyfriend. Maybe he did make Brassel feel small. Maybe he did cheat on her. But nothing was as damaging as his short, courteous response to her 3,019-word attempt to cancel him.

“Grace and I had some good times together,” Gillis said. “Breakups are tough, and I wish her the best.”
