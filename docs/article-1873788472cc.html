EU, Indonesia sign landmark trade deal after 10 years of talks

2026-11-22 08:39

The European Union and Indonesia officially signed a comprehensive trade and investment protection agreement on Saturday in Brussels after 10 years of negotiations, the European Commission said in a statement.

The deal will likely come into effect in early 2027 as the European Parliament still needs to sign off and both the EU and Indonesia need to complete internal procedures.

The deal is part of the bloc's efforts to diversify its trade and offset the impact of U.S. President Donald Trump's tariffs.

EU chief Ursula von der Leyen and Indonesian Minister of Economic Affairs Airlangga Hartarto attended a signing ceremony for a pact that Brussels said is projected to increase European Union exports to Southeast Asia's largest economy by almost 40% within 15 years.

"This is one of the most ambitious deals the European Union has concluded in Asia," said von der Leyen, the European Commission's president.

EU trade chief Maros Sefcovic and Indonesia's minister for trade Budi Santoso were also in attendance.

"Together, we represent a market of 730 million people," von der Leyen added, thanking Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto, who she said will visit Brussels on Dec. 11.

Negotiations for the deal were concluded last year, almost a decade after they were launched, as part of a wider EU push to diversify trade as it faces challenges from the United States and China.

The Indonesia-European Union Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA) and the ancillary Investment Protection Agreement (IPA) will open investment in strategic sectors such as electric vehicles, electronics, and pharmaceuticals, Brussels said.

The pact removes tariffs on more than 98% of European exports to the world's fourth most populous country, including food, agricultural and industrial products and will help save over 600 million euros ($658 million) a year in customs duties, according to the EU.

Around 90% of Indonesian exports to the EU will be tariff-free, Airlangga said, noting that it is expected to benefit the country's top shipments to the bloc, including palm oil, footwear and textiles.

"The benefits for our exporters are concrete and measurable," he said, hailing a "new chapter" in economic relations with the EU.

Palm oil and deforestation had posed stumbling blocks to the negotiations, and the pact includes mechanisms for cooperation on regulatory and sustainability issues as well as a dedicated protocol on palm oil to allay some of the related concerns.

Brussels said the deal will boost economic growth, with the EU's gross domestic product (GDP) projected to be 16 billion euros higher and Indonesia's 27 billion euros higher by 2042 than they would be without the agreement.

The European Parliament has to greenlight the pact and Indonesian authorities complete the ratification process before it can provisionally come into effect.
