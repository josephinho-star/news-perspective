Stock futures slip as traders await key inflation report, assess tech sell-off: Live updates

2026-11-25 02:51

U.S. stock futures were marginally lower on Tuesday night, following a slide in tech stocks during the regular session that drove the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite lower. Traders also looked ahead to fresh inflation data.

Dow Jones Industrial Average futures lost 68 points, or 0.1%, while S&P 500 futures declined 0.2%. Nasdaq-100 futures traded down 0.3%.

In Asia, South Korea's Kospi plunged 4.49% on Wednesday, while Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.41%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was down 0.41%.

Ambarella plunged 37% in U.S. extended trading after the designer of low-power AI vision and video chips issued fourth quarter revenue guidance of $70 million to $75 million. That fell massively short of the $112 million analysts polled by LSEG were expecting.

Wall Street is coming off a losing session for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, after tech stocks gave back some of their gains from the prior session as Nvidia sank and Anthropic fell below its IPO price. The broader index fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq slid 1.5%. On the other hand, the 30-stock Dow which has less exposure to tech, climbed more than 400 points, or 0.9%.

The choppy trading action was just the latest in a volatile month. Even before the ouster and reinstatement of Nvidia's Jensen Huang, and Democrats' sweep in the midterm elections, investors were deliberating the future of the artificial intelligence build-out amid a worsening macroeconomic outlook.

"Let's remember that the sell-off in the AI ecosystem predated the news about Huang and Nvidia," Liz Ann Sonders, chief investment strategist at Schwab Center for Financial Research, told CNBC's "Closing Bell" on Tuesday.

"The concerns about capex, the data center build-out, the spread between the cost of capital and return on invested capital, the bifurcation that's happening in the market now between sort of profitable segments of the market and unprofitable," she added. "I think that is the bigger, more aggregate story."

Traders are turning their attention toward the personal consumption expenditures price index for October, due on Wednesday morning. Economists polled by Dow Jones see the headline reading rising by 0.3% for the month and 3.8% annually. Weekly jobless claims are also due, arriving a day earlier than usual as U.S. markets will be closed on Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday.
