Turkish pilots begin Eurofighter flight training in UK

2026-09-09 14:36

Turkish Air Force pilots have begun flight training on Eurofighter Typhoon fighter jets in the United Kingdom after completing an introductory course in aviation terminology, Turkey’s Defense Ministry announced on Wednesday.

The training is part of an agreement under which Turkey will purchase 20 newly built Eurofighter Typhoons from the UK.

“Our Air Force Command pilots, who began their training in August under the Eurofighter Typhoon procurement agreement signed with the United Kingdom, have successfully completed their terminology training and begun flight training,” the ministry said in a statement on social media.

The ministry had previously said the flight training would last eight months and be followed by a three-month instructor pilot course, allowing Turkish personnel to train additional pilots after the aircraft enter service.

Turkey and the UK signed an agreement worth up to £8 billion ($10.7 billion at the time) in October 2025 for 20 new Typhoons, with the first delivery expected in 2030.

A separate training and support agreement signed in March provides for the training of 10 Turkish pilots and nearly 100 ground crew technicians in the UK. The program covers aircraft mechanics, avionics, weapons and mission systems and includes simulators, spare parts and long-term maintenance support.

The Eurofighter is produced by a four-nation consortium comprising the UK, Germany, Italy and Spain. BAE Systems will carry out the final assembly of Turkey’s aircraft at its Warton facility in northwest England.

Ankara has also been negotiating with Qatar and Oman to acquire secondhand Eurofighters that could enter service before the newly built aircraft arrive. Defense Minister Yaşar Güler said after the UK agreement was signed that Turkey planned to purchase 12 aircraft from each country, potentially bringing its Eurofighter fleet to 44.

Turkey has been seeking advanced combat aircraft to modernize an air force centered around aging F-16s. The country was removed from the US-led F-35 program in 2019 after taking delivery of Russia’s S-400 air defense system, which Washington said posed a security risk to the stealth fighter jet program.

Ankara views the Eurofighters as a transitional capability while it develops the domestically produced KAAN fifth-generation fighter jet. Turkey is also seeking to return to the F-35 program and has raised the issue in talks with Washington.
