Court ruling throws wrench into Trump’s bid for offshore mining

2026-11-17 23:53

A court ruling this week is throwing a wrench into the Trump administration’s bid to open up mining off the coast of American Samoa.

Judge Jill Otake, a Trump appointee, ruled that the administration did not adequately consider the potential impacts of offshore mining during its Endangered Species Act review.

Her ruling noted that the National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS) “only considered the preliminary effects of the lease sale and did not even attempt to consider” other activities such as actual mining.

She wrote that the agency’s “cramped, expedited, informal consultation is apparently unprecedented” and vacated the letter authorizing the lease sale to move forward.

The ruling does not appear to explicitly vacate the lease sale itself, however, and it’s not immediately clear whether the Trump administration will attempt to move forward with the sale, which had been scheduled for Thursday.

A spokesperson for the Marine Minerals Administration said that the agency, which would carry out the lease sale, “is aware of the opinion” in an email to The Hill. “We are currently working to address the issues raised in the court’s opinion.”

However, in a separate but related case challenging the lease sale itself rather than the review that underlied it, Otake wrote that if she vacates the review but the Trump administration moves ahead with the lease sale, “any bidders for the leases will be on notice that the Court has determined that NMFS violated the Endangered Species Act …regarding the proposed leases.”

David Henkin, an attorney with Earthjustice who is representing groups seeking to block the potential offshore mining, said that taken together, the judge’s statements are a warning to potential bidders that “if you buy those leases knowing that they haven’t complied with the Endangered Species Act, do not be surprised if the court later sets aside voids, vacates, throws into the garbage can those leases.”

Henkin also more broadly took issue with the potential mining itself, calling it a “highly speculative and risky endeavor with our oceans, on which we rely to protect us from the worst effects of global climate change, when the minerals that these companies want to bring from the bottom of the ocean are available already from terrestrial sources.”

The Trump administration has sought to advance offshore mining, announcing the American Samoa lease sale — the first of its kind — last month.

At the time, acting Marine Minerals Administration Director Mike Olsen said the U.S. needed minerals, saying in a written statement that “Critical minerals are no longer just commodities; they are a vital strategic asset in global competition, essential for reaching our energy efficiency goals, strengthening our national defense, and securing domestic supply chains.”
