Everything to know about the 9/11 25th anniversary ceremony at Ground Zero

2026-09-11 09:00

The first of seven moments of silence will come at 8:46 a.m., followed by the sound of thousands of houses of worship throughout the city tolling their bells.

Families of victims of the 2001 and 1993 attacks will read the names in pairs, stopping only to honor the following moments of silence of the ceremony:

8:46 a.m. — the moment hijacked Flight 11 struck the North Tower;

9:03 a.m. — the moment hijacked Flight 175 struck the South Tower;

9:37 a.m. — the moment hijacked Flight 77 struck the Pentagon;

9:59 a.m. — the time the South Tower fell;

10:03 a.m. — the moment hijacked Flight 93 crashed near Shanksville, Pennsylvania; and

Despite the backlash, Hizzoner pledged to be at Ground Zero and “proudly honor the families, the survivors, the first responders forever impacted by that horrific terror attack by standing alongside them at this year’s 9/11 commemoration.”

Similarly, Gov. Kathy Hochul promised to be in attendance despite a Port Authority police union asking her to stay way over her decision to illuminate One World Trade Center alongside her proclamation recognizing January as Muslim American Heritage Month.

Most of the living presidents — Joe Biden, Barack Obama, George W. Bush and Bill Clinton — are also expected to be in attendance.

A security fence will be installed by the US Secret Service on the east side of the West Side Highway from Liberty to Fulton Street, as well as around the perimeter of the 9/11 Memorial site, NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said during a security briefing Wednesday.

Vehicular traffic will be closed south of Canal Street to Battery Place and from Broadway to West Street starting Friday.

Traffic will be allowed southbound on Broadway, but there will be a ban on all trucks.

The Hugh Carey Tunnel will remain open, with cars being allowed access to the FDR Drive.

The No. 1 train will also bypass the World Trade Center Cortlandt station.

Invited guests permitted to attend the Ground Zero ceremony will need to go through security screening stations.

The Memorial Plaza will open to the public starting at 3 p.m.

The annual 9/11 Memorial & Museum Tribute in Light will illuminate the Big Apple’s skies as the sun sets on Friday and will last until dawn on Saturday morning, and will be aired in a live national broadcast on Disney-affiliated channels and streamers.

Dozens of other iconic buildings throughout the five boroughs — and across the US — will also illuminate their facades and rooftops in blue to coincide with the art installation.

The 9/11 Memorial & Museum will commemorate the 25th anniversary of the terror attacks with its annual ceremony. Paul Martinka for NY Post

This year will be the first time the ceremony will include a seventh moment of silence for those killed by 9/11-related illnesses. Bolivar Arellano/NY Post

Presidents Joe Biden, Barack Obama, George W. Bush and Bill Clinton are expected to attend the ceremony. Paul Martinka for NY Post

Mayor Zohran Mamdani has pledged to attend the ceremony despite intense backlash from politicians and surviving first responders. Matthew McDermott for NY Post
