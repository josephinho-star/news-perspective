Gérard Depardieu drops appeal against sexual assault conviction

2026-11-16 12:10

The French actor Gérard Depardieu has dropped his appeal against a conviction for sexually assaulting two women on a film set, and has for the first time apologised to the victims.

“I was found guilty. I hear it and I accept this conviction,” Depardieu, 77, said in a written statement. “I address my deep and sincere apologies to those who suffered.”

The actor, who has made more than 200 films and TV series, wrote: “For a long time on film sets, I hid behind provocation and ribald humour.”

He said he accepted that remarks and “some of my gestures” were not “without consequences”, particularly because he held a powerful position in the entertainment industry.

Depardieu said he had never intended to hurt the women he encountered but had “come to understand that intention changes nothing about the harm caused”.

He said times had changed and that he belonged “to a world that no longer exists”.

At a trial last year, Depardieu was found guilty of sexually assaulting a 55-year-old set dresser and a 35-year-old assistant director during the shooting of the feature film Les Volets Verts (The Green Shutters) in Paris in September 2021.

His suspended sentence will now stand and his name must be added to France’s sex offender register.

The set dresser, named in the media as Amélie, said outside the Paris appeal court on Monday: “I waited for years for this apology.” She said she would have preferred if Depardieu had attended court to deliver it in person.

The assistant director, named in the media as Sarah, said: “The monster of cinema has fallen.”

Depardieu’s conviction last year was a turning point for the #MeToo movement in France. He became the biggest figure in the French film industry to be convicted of sexual assault after years of criticism of the country’s reluctance to take women’s claims of abuse seriously.

Depardieu had previously denied all allegations of sexual assault. He told last year’s trial that the media had used allegations against him to damage his reputation.

He attacked the #MeToo movement as well as women who had staged protests outside venues during his concert tour at the time of the allegations. “This movement is going to become a terror,” he said.

The trial also set an important legal precedent when the judge ruled that Depardieu’s defence lawyer, Jérémie Assous, had been “excessively harsh” to the two women in court, saying they were not real victims and were liars.

Assous had said the women were working for the cause of “rabid feminism” and called their lawyers hysterical, “abject and stupid” and described them as having an “unbearable voice”.

The judge awarded the complainants compensation for being further victimised by Depardieu’s defence during the trial, ordering Depardieu to pay each woman €1,000 (£835).

The court had heard there was a “system of impunity” around Depardieu, with film industry insiders staying silent to keep cashing in on his name.

Depardieu has appealed against a separate decision requiring him to face trial on charges of raping and sexually assaulting the actor Charlotte Arnould at his Paris home in 2018. Appeal judges will rule this month on whether to uphold his appeal or allow the trial to go ahead next year.

In an open letter to Le Figaro in October 2023, Depardieu denied all allegations and said any encounter with Arnould had been consensual. “Never, ever, have I abused a woman,” he wrote.

In December 2023, when Depardieu was under formal investigation for rape and sexual assault in the Arnould case and also facing scrutiny over sexist comments revealed in a TV documentary, the French president defended him, saying “he makes France proud”.

Asked at the time about stripping Depardieu of a state award, Emmanuel Macron said: “You will never see me participate in a manhunt … I hate that type of thing.”
