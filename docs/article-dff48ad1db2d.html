Erdoğan downplays $18 billion fund crisis, says no risk to financial system

2026-09-25 11:54

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan said Turkey’s investment fund crisis posed no risk to the wider economy, although regulators are liquidating 131 funds with reported assets of about $18 billion and 455,758 investors.

Speaking to journalists after his visit to the United Nations General Assembly in New York on Thursday, Erdoğan described the problem as confined to a small part of the capital markets. He promised that everyone responsible would face legal action and said his government would make changes to prevent a repeat.

Turkey’s Capital Markets Board (SPK) has ordered the liquidation of the 131 funds run by seven asset managers. The regulator counted 455,758 individual investors, rather than accounts, in the funds. Treasury and Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek valued their assets at $18.3 billion, but the reported value does not establish how much investors will get back.

The SPK extended the liquidation deadline from three months to six months.

İşbank will oversee the sale of funds managed by Tera, while state-owned Ziraat Bank will handle funds run by the other six firms. Investors are due to receive proceeds as assets are sold, in proportion to their holdings. Şimşek chaired talks on liquidation and payments on Thursday, with further talks expected Friday.

The crisis spread through the stock market after fund managers Pusula and Tera said they could not meet some investors’ requests to withdraw their money. The funds held large stakes in shares that were hard to sell without driving down their prices. Some sold shares they could still trade to raise cash, adding to a selloff that sent Istanbul’s main stock index down more than 8 percent last week, analysts told Reuters.

Main opposition New Party leader Özgür Özel rejected Erdoğan’s assessment, calling the crisis “the fraud of the century” and claiming it had affected 2 million people, a broader figure than the regulator’s count of 455,758 investors in the funds. He called for investigators to examine transactions dating back to the fund’s founding in 2021, rather than limiting the inquiry to transactions since 2024. Özel also alleged that people in Erdoğan’s ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) had profited from the funds and called for their gains to be recovered and paid to investors.

As the fund run shook the stock market, rumors spread that Şimşek, who is overseeing the government’s response, would resign. Şimşek denied the rumors on Thursday. “The claims circulating about my resignation are baseless,” he wrote on X, urging people to disregard what he called speculation targeting Turkey’s economic stability.

The investigation has also turned to people who invested in the funds before the funds became available to the public. Turkey’s Financial Crimes Investigation Board (MASAK) sent prosecutors records of those early investors and reviewed transactions involving 117 executives of firms linked to the inquiry. It said it had stopped attempted transactions totaling nearly 2.5 billion lira, about $51 million, by September 21.

Police detained six more people on Friday after investigators identified 12 who had traded in a Pusula fund while access to it was restricted. Five of the 12 had faced legal action, and police were seeking one more person.

Separately, a court ordered 11 more people jailed pending trial on Friday, bringing the total jailed in the fund investigation to 45, Sözcü daily reported. They included Erkan Kilimci, a former deputy governor of the Turkish Central Bank who later served on the board of Tera. Prosecutors are examining suspected market manipulation and transactions involving the funds. The allegations have yet to be tested at trial.

In a filing on Turkey’s Public Disclosure Platform (KAP) on Friday, state-owned Emlak Katılım Bank said it had applied for approval to buy shares in Katılımevim, Birevim and İktisat Katılım Bank. Those are proposed purchases of companies, separate from İşbank’s and Ziraat Bank’s work to liquidate investment funds. The transfers need approval from the Ministry of Treasury and Finance, the SPK and the Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK). Emlak Katılım disclosed no price or plan for covering any losses tied to the fund crisis.

Questions about oversight predate the selloff. Former MASAK deputy chief Ramazan Başak raised concerns about Tera in a July 2025 submission to the Presidential Communication Center (CİMER) and a January criminal complaint, according to documents examined by Cumhuriyet columnist Barış Terkoğlu. Başak was detained in November after Tera complained about his public warnings and was detained again in April on accusations of spreading false information and manipulating the market, Terkoğlu wrote. Başak’s complaint also repeated an unverified claim that Tera chairman Emre Tezmen had described himself as close to Şimşek and claimed they had business ties in Britain.

The Ministry of Treasury and Finance rejected the alleged business link on Thursday, saying Şimşek had no partnership or commercial relationship with Tera or its owners and executives before or during his time in public office. It said someone invoking his name to suggest influence would not establish such a relationship.

Opposition lawmaker Ümit Özlale from the New Party accused senior bureaucrats and politicians of enabling the crisis, an allegation that has not been established. The New Party submitted a bill seeking to recover investors’ money without using public funds or imposing new taxes. The Republican People’s Party (CHP) has called for a parliamentary investigation.

Erdoğan’s remarks downplaying the crisis came after access to three articles examining the people behind companies in the inquiry was blocked in Turkey on national security and public order grounds, according to the Freedom of Expression Association (İFÖD).
