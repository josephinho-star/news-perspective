Anthropic links Turkish company to Malaysia election manipulation platform

2026-09-11 14:41

US artificial intelligence developer Anthropic has linked a Turkish cybersecurity company to a platform designed to manipulate political debate in Malaysia through fake social media accounts, fabricated news and false intelligence reports.

The 154-page report, published on Thursday, examined attempts to misuse Anthropic’s Claude models between December 2025 and August 2026.

Anthropic said it removed an account that used Claude to operate a commercial election manipulation network consisting of about 1,000 fake accounts on X, a fake news website and fabricated dossiers.

The company said it found “clear links” between the network and BBS Bilişim Teknolojileri, an İstanbul-based company that operates as BBS Technology.

BBS sold access to the system as an influence service, while the people operating it described it as a “military-grade, AI-driven, real-time political operations ecosystem,” Anthropic alleged.

The platform used census, election and voter data to profile all 222 Malaysian parliamentary constituencies according to race, religion and attitudes toward the monarchy.

Its fake accounts were programmed to appear authentic before joining political campaigns and to evade social media platforms’ detection systems.

One dashboard showed a request for 1 million artificial views in support of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s account, although Anthropic said figures recorded by the operator could not be independently verified.

The people behind the network also produced dossiers containing claims about an opposition politician and civil society groups that Anthropic found had no factual basis.

A fake outlet named Malaysia Pulse scraped reports from Malaysian news organizations, rewrote them with Claude and published them under fabricated bylines.

The website also republished material from Russian and Chinese state-linked outlets after removing information identifying the original sources.

Anthropic did not identify who bought the service or name the opposition politician targeted by the fabricated claims.

The company behind the operation sought a contract with the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission, but Anthropic found no evidence that it secured one.

Anthropic rated the campaign Category Two on a six-level scale, meaning its material appeared on several platforms but showed no evidence of reaching authentic online communities.

BBS denied any involvement, knowledge or approval and announced an internal review on Friday.

The company said its name, digital assets or information belonging to former affiliates may have been used without its knowledge and pledged to file a criminal complaint if its review identifies those responsible.

BBS describes itself as a provider of cybersecurity services to public institutions and companies in banking, energy, insurance, aviation and telecommunications.

Malaysia last voted in November 2022 and must stage its next general election by early 2028 unless parliament is dissolved sooner.

Anthropic said it banned the accounts and organization tied to the operation and used the findings to build new systems for detecting similar campaigns.
