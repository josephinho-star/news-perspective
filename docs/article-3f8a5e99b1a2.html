Julius Randle rips Nets teammates for showing ‘no pride’ in embarrassing loss: ‘A lot of excuses’

2026-11-20 01:32

Julius Randle has been in the NBA for 13 seasons, including three All-Star appearances as a member of the Knicks.

Well before he said in a post-game news conference Wednesday night that he was embarrassed about the Nets’ lack of effort in a blowout loss to the Bulls, his frustration and anger were already evident.

As seen and heard in a since-viral video taken from behind the bench at the end of the third quarter, Randle admonished his teammates for several seconds for showing “no pride” in their latest defeat, which he later called “one of the worst games I’ve ever been a part of” in his career.

“Just a little frustrated. … It was tough,” Randle said after the Nets fell to 3-11 entering Friday’s home game against Orlando. “Like I said, I’ve been in the league for 13 years, and it just felt like five individuals out there. I was just trying to somehow, some way, relay to the group that winning is the most important thing, not individuals.

“So we gotta find a way to get connected as a group and put that first. But I have to find a way to be better too, and I put that on me.”

With seven first-round picks over the past two seasons, the Nets still are rebuilding, but Randle and former NBA champion Michael Porter Jr. are among the veterans on hand to help coach Jordi Fernández avoid such alarming performances.

Wednesday’s loss — with Julius Erving and other former ABA champion teammates on hand — marked the third consecutive game in which the Nets got walloped in the first half.

They coughed up at least 70 points through two quarters in those losses to Memphis, Philadelphia and Chicago — the first time that’s happened in their franchise history in the NBA.

Randle had spoken after the loss to the 76ers about the importance of “punching first,” but the Bulls connected for a franchise record 15 3-pointers in the first half alone, and they shot 21-for-46 on 3s in their 30-point win.

“They were living in our paint at will, getting into the paint and spraying the basketball. … I felt like we didn’t stop them at all from getting in the paint,” Randle said. “As a man, you gotta look yourself in the mirror and come into work and figure out what matters.

“If winning is what matters, then you’ll do the necessary things to win. But I think it’s not there right now.”

Fernández said he has appreciated the efforts of Randle and Porter, who both average more than 20 points per game for the season, although Porter managed just five on 1-for-7 shooting in 27 minutes against Chicago.

“They’ve been amazing in practice. They work every day, and it’s not pulling teeth at all,” Fernández said.

“Now, I need to see it on the court. Obviously, as starters and veterans, they’re more responsible, but I’ve been very happy with them. I know they can be a big part of us doing things better. No excuses here. I think from the beginning both of those guys, when things don’t go well, they put it on themselves, as [I do] myself. That’s what you’re supposed to [do] as a leader.”

The 31-year-old Randle added that he’s “always going to hold myself accountable and figure out ways or find ways to do more. I think [that’s true] when you find ways to win, but when you lose, you find excuses. It seems like a lot of excuses right now.”

Fernández also used phrases such as “selfishness” and “not playing hard” and “guys pointing fingers” to describe Wednesday’s no-show.

“Obviously, I’m the one in front right there, and I don’t want my team to play like this,” he said.

The third-year head coach acknowledged that an immediate priority must be to fix the defensive effort, which also featured 18 second-chance points by the Bulls in the first quarter.

“Obviously, we all feel embarrassed for the performance,” Fernández said, “but you gotta watch and learn and move on.”

Joshua Jefferson, Drake Powell and Ben Saraf were recalled from Long Island of the G-League. Paolo Banchero (ankle) and Jonathan Isaac (knee) will be out for Orlando.
