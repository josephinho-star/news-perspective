Chiefs and Bills take care of business ahead of the NFL’s biggest regular-season game

2026-11-22 23:46

Sunday in the NFL felt a little like November in the SEC for the Kansas City Chiefs and Buffalo Bills. You know how SEC teams like to schedule a late-season cupcake as a tune-up before the big rivalry game? Well, the Chiefs and Bills don’t get to pick their opponents, but whoever put together the Week 11 schedule did them both a favor.

When I wrote about the Week 11 betting lines Thursday, Buffalo was favored by 16.5 points and Kansas City by 15.5. Both covered those numbers. The Bills beat the Miami Dolphins, 26-9, while the Chiefs demolished the Arizona Cardinals, 34-7.

Of course, the analogy only works if the cupcake leads into a big rivalry game. And that’s exactly what’s in store for NFL fans on Thanksgiving night, when Patrick Mahomes and the 8-2 Chiefs head to Buffalo to face Josh Allen and the 9-1 Bills. The Bills have never hosted a game on Thanksgiving, and for their first one, they get the biggest game of the NFL regular season.

Sure, Chiefs-Bills doesn’t have the history of Steelers-Browns, Cowboys-Eagles or Packers-Lions. But try telling someone in Buffalo that Kansas City isn’t a rival. Mahomes and Allen met in the playoffs four times in five seasons, twice with a trip to the Super Bowl on the line. The Chiefs ended the Bills’ season all four times. And I’m guessing nobody in Buffalo needs me to explain what "13 seconds" means.

Kansas City certainly looked ready Sunday. Travis Kelce had a vintage Kelce game, catching six passes for 74 yards and two touchdowns, both before halftime. Mahomes threw for 324 yards and three scores, and rookie running back Emmett Johnson added 105 yards and a touchdown on the ground. That’s three straight games with at least 34 points for the Chiefs.

Buffalo, meanwhile, won by 17 on a day when Allen threw two interceptions and didn’t run the ball once. How often do you get to say that about a Bills win? Allen still threw for 304 yards, and Miami managed three field goals. I wouldn’t count on Mahomes being quite that accommodating.

Thanksgiving night could decide where the Chiefs and Bills meet in January

The Bills have won five straight regular-season games against the Chiefs. A sixth would put Buffalo at 10-1, two games ahead of Kansas City. If the Chiefs win, they’ll close out Thanksgiving night tied with Buffalo at 9-2 and holding the head-to-head tiebreaker. That’s a pretty big swing for a Bills team trying to earn the AFC’s No. 1 seed for the first time in the Josh Allen era.

Allen has won all three of his regular-season trips to Arrowhead and lost all three of his playoff games there. Mahomes has won in Buffalo in January, too, beating the Bills 27-24 in the divisional round after the 2023 season. But both times these two played for the AFC championship, Buffalo had to go to Kansas City. A win Thursday gives Allen a much better shot at making Mahomes get on a plane in late January.

And if you’re wondering what happened the last time Buffalo did earn the No. 1 seed, the 1993 Bills won the AFC championship game at home, 30-13. The team they beat to get to the Super Bowl? The Kansas City Chiefs.

Of course, the Bengals would prefer to host the AFC championship game themselves. They’re 7-2 heading into Monday Night Football against Washington, and I suspect they’ll be rooting for Kansas City on Thanksgiving. A Chiefs win would give Buffalo its second loss, and the Bengals don’t get a shot at the Bills in the regular season. They do, however, get the Chiefs at home on Dec. 13.

And yes, the Broncos and Jaguars are in the mix for the No. 1 seed, too. Jacksonville even went into Cincinnati and beat the Bengals. But when I ranked my Super Bowl contenders at the halfway point, I had three AFC teams in the top tier. If it’s my money, I’m still betting the AFC runs through Kansas City, Buffalo or Cincinnati.

So if the Chiefs win on Thanksgiving and the Bengals keep winning, Chiefs-Bengals on Dec. 13 might be the biggest game of the NFL regular season. That gives me about two weeks to ask my editor if changing "Bills" to "Bengals" and running this article again counts as plagiarizing myself.

Chiefs-Bills headlines a much better Week 12 schedule

Week 11 didn’t look particularly good on paper, and most of Sunday’s early games looked about the same on TV. If the NFL wants to wash the taste of those lopsided matchups out of our mouths, though, Week 12 ought to do it.

The Packers visit the Rams on Wednesday night. The Bears and Lions open Thanksgiving with a game that could help decide the NFC North, and we get Seahawks-49ers on Sunday afternoon. I’d happily watch any of those three, and none is the game I’m looking forward to most. That would be Chiefs-Bills on Thanksgiving night.

The only downside is that three of those four games will be over before we even get to Friday. Then again, am I really going to complain about only having to wait four days to see Mahomes and Allen play each other?

Ask me again Sunday at 1 p.m. when I’m trying to decide which game gets the main TV in my living room and Saints-Bengals is probably my best option.
