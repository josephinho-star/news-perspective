Wild scenes of celebration in Belgrade as Vučić concedes Serbian election – Europe live

2026-10-25 22:43

'The door to the EU is open, and we are ready to move forward together,' EU enlargement commissioner says

EU enlargement commissioner Marta Kos is clearly hoping for more engagement from the new government in Belgrade.

In her congratulatory message on X, she said:

“Dear Serbia, you have made your voice heard! After nearly two years of protests for democracy, the rule of law and the fight against corruption, Serbians turned out in historic numbers. Congratulations to the Student List on your victory!

These are the very values at the heart of our European Union. A new chapter can begin for Serbia. The door to the EU is open, and we are ready to move forward together.”

“Today in Serbia is a vivid reminder that the will of the citizens and their right to freely decide on their future are the foundation of every democratic change.

I congratulate the representatives of the Student List and firmly believe that a new chapter lies ahead for Montenegro and Serbia, based on respect and open dialogue, without unnecessary escalation of tensions.

The citizens of both countries deserve relations that will be measured by concrete results of cooperation, and not by the depth of diplomatic misunderstandings.”

Montenegrin president Jakov Milatovic also joined in:

“I congratulate the Student List on their electoral victory, and the citizens of the Republic of Serbia on their clearly expressed democratic will. I am convinced that this electoral result will open a new chapter in the relations between Montenegro and the Republic of Serbia, based on friendship, sincere dialogue, and commitment to a shared European future.”

The change of government in Serbia could indeed significantly shift the dynamic in the western Balkans, although again we should not necessarily expect an immediate move against Russia (17:54).

Council of Europe’s secretary general Alain Berset has also offered his congratulations.

“Congratulations to the Student List of Serbia on your electoral result.

Serbian citizens have made their democratic choice. We welcome former President Vučić’s acceptance of the result and commitment to a peaceful democratic transition.

Council of Europe stands ready to work with Serbia’s democratically elected leadership to strengthen human rights, democracy and the rule of law.

Your perseverance, energy and commitment to European principles and Serbia’s future have brought you this far. You can count on our support.”

'We stand ready to support reforms ... and work to advance Serbia's future in the EU,' von der Leyen and Costa say

European Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen and European Council president António Costa have congratulated the Student List on their victory in tonight’s election in Serbia.

“Today, the people of Serbia turned out in record numbers to shape their country’s future. Congratulations to the Student List on its victory in the parliamentary elections.

We stand ready to support reforms on the rule of law, media freedom, the fight against corruption and strong democratic institutions, and to work together to advance Serbia’s future in the European Union.”

As explained earlier, it may not be quite that straightforward when it comes to the EU… (16:48).

Vučić’s gambit backfires as Serbia will need to host second elections to choose his presidential successor

By the way, Vučić’s decision to step down as president to contest this election now puts him in a very awkward position.

Because, erm,the Serbian people will now have to choose his successor as the head of state, and looking at the results tonight, he may not like the outcome of that process either.

After his resignation, a close Vučić ally – the outgoing speaker of the parliament and former prime minister Ana Brnabić – has temporarily stepped into the role as acting president.

But once the new parliament forms, the new speaker will have to decide the next steps for recruiting the full-time replacement.

The process is complicated and CRTA has a good explanation on all possible dates here, but essentially the vote needs to take place before the end of the year.

Despite some similarities, Student List's win in Serbia is slightly different than Tisza's in Hungary – snap analysis

When thinking about today’s opposition win in Serbia, there is an understandable temptation to compare it to Hungary and the fall of Viktor Orbán in April – and Magyar clearly plays on that in his reaction, too (22:48).

And there are some undeniable similarities in how the two countries visibly grew tired of their increasingly authoritarian leaders.

The opposition forces in both countries – Tisza in Hungary and the Student List in Serbia – have some things in common, for example their sort of catch-all character as they formulate their demands in the broadest possible terms to attract all sorts of voters, while relentlessly focusing on corruption and demands of accountability for political leaders.

On a technical level, they both focused on a door to door campaign, making a point of covering thousands of kilometres across the country, and reaching out to voters directly, instead of being a sort of talking shop with ideas. They also both adopted elements of national folks and symbolism for their campaigns.

But there are also very substantial differences.

The Serbian Student List does not have a single strong leader figure in sharp contrast to Péter Magyar’s strong position in Tisza. It is based on a grassroots group of people with little prior experience of frontline politics, and not on a former insider who switched sides, with all sorts of upsides and downsides that come with that. For one, we do not even know who could be their PM.

The Student List’s path to a parliamentary majority was also technically more complicated or, put it simply, required a larger win. That is largely down to the mechanics of the Serbian electoral system, which – unlike the setup Viktor Orbán had tailored in Hungary with the intention of helping his own party, not the opposition – does not offer the same disproportionate winner’s bonus.

Finally, the underlying issues demanding immediate attention are possibly much bigger and more complicated to resolve in Serbia than in Hungary, particularly without the option of getting some EU funds flowing quickly after the vote to buy you some good will and time. Hungary’s Tisza is much more openly pro-European than the Student List.

But Magyar clearly and, at least to an extent, rightly sees the two movements and their victories as linked: earlier this morning, he even posted a slightly cheesy post reminiscing the times he and his parents spent at an unnamed Serbian restaurant in Budapest, “which has since closed down.”

“Fortunately, you can still find great ćevapi in plenty of places, whether Sarajevo-style or Serbian-style,” he added, posting a picture of himself with the said ćevapi.

(There are some sensational Serbian places in London, too. Just saying.)

Hungary's Magyar congratulates the Student List on election win

Hungary’s prime minister Péter Magyar – who came to power ousting Viktor Orbán after 16 years in power – is among the very first European leaders to offer his congratulations to the Student List.

“I have just spoken by phone with the leaders of the Students’ List @studentblokade, who won the elections in Serbia, to congratulate them on their victory. I assured them of Hungary’s support.

I was delighted to hear how much strength our victory in April had given them during this difficult struggle.”

Earlier tonight, he cheekily said:

“In Serbia, @avucic has conceded defeat. The Students’ List has won an absolute majority in the elections. Celebrations have begun in the streets across Serbia. Sound familiar?”

'We will remember this moment for the rest of our lives'

“I think this is a moment we will remember for the rest of our lives,” Sara, 24, says as she smiles with her friends.

“It’s simply the realisation of our dream. And it’s very strange that on the very streets where the police beat us, on the streets where we retreated countless times in the face of the gendarmerie, we are now standing here as the victors.”

“I think this is a victory for everything we stood for, for all the values we promoted through the student movement. And I’m very glad that we’ve proven that Serbia only wins when the students win.”

"After 14 years, we finally feel freedom again,” Nemanja, 29, says when we start chatting amid celebrations in Belgrade.

“We feel that justice will come and that this will become a beautiful and good country, where people can get ahead through their own knowledge and abilities.”

'A victory of courage over fear, of hope over indifference,' Student List's Saičić says, but adds 'tomorrow, the work begins'

Speaking at the Student List’s election night party, chemist Radomir Saičić had one of the strongest speeches as he thanked all who cast their votes in today’s election.

But he said “tonight is about more than just the results: it is about the people who kept believing when things where at their hardest, who refused to give up, to believe that nothing could ever change.”

He calls it “a victory of courage over fear;of hope over indifference; and of conviction that Serbia can, and must, be different.”

He says the victory “is not a personal trophy; does not belong to any of us,” and brings about “great responsibility” for all those in the new parliamentary majority.

“Tonight we have shown that change is not only possible, but that there is a will for it. Now we must prove ourselves worthy of the trust we have been given. …

Tonight, we celebrate in the way Serbia knows to do best: with song, dance, embraces, and tears of joy. And tomorrow? Tomorrow, the work begins.”

In pretty moving scenes, some people offer handshakes, high fives and even bear hugs to police officers as they stand aside from blocking the streets, despite earlier concerns about a potential mobilisation of security forces.

The crowd is now moving towards Slavija square, where they held their final pre-election on Thursday.

Crta’s projection is very similar to that of Ipsos/CeSIR (21:43):it puts the Student List at 51.2% with 134 seats in the new parliament, well clear of the 126 required for the majority.

Vučić’s list is in the second place with 40.4% of the vote and 106 seats.

10 seats are expected to go to minority groups, including three seats each for Hungarians and Albanians.
