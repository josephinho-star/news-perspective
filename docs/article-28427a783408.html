Sacramento’s booze zone experiment is about to get much bigger

2026-09-02 01:36

Sacramento could soon have six more places where event-goers can legally take their drinks outside, and city officials are betting the booze-fueled crowds will be good for business.

It’s the city’s third round of action on Entertainment Zones in a little more than a year.

The concept made its Sacramento debut in 2025, when the city allowed to-go alcoholic drinks to be sold and consumed in outdoor public areas during the Rainbow Festival and Terra Madre Americas event at the Convention Center.

Now officials want to spread the party beyond the central city.

The proposed zones would cover parts of downtown, Broadway, the Arena area and Oak Park:

9th and I Street: 9th to 10th Street, I to J Street

14th and Broadway: 9th to 19th Street, X Street to Burnett Way

24th and Broadway: 20th to 28th Street, X Street to 1st Avenue

24th and K Street: 23rd to 25th Street, Jazz to Kayak Alley

Arena: 3rd to 7th Street, J to L Street

Oak Park Triangle: 34th to 36th Street, 2nd to 4th Avenue

City staff say the zones are meant to turn commercial corridors into busier destinations, drawing residents and visitors while helping nearby businesses.

And there’s a financial angle: The city says letting bars and restaurants participate could encourage customers to stick around longer and spend more.

“Cities that thrive have a vibrant social economy. Participation in activations by bars and restaurants could provide significant benefits to businesses, including increasing the length of stay per guest and dollars spent per guest,” the staff report said.

Officials said they received requests for new zones in downtown, midtown, Broadway and Oak Park.

The city says the expansion could boost revenue for bars and restaurants, spill economic activity into neighboring businesses and make events more enjoyable for residents and visitors.
