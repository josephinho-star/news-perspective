Nets’ free-throw problem on full display in loss to 76ers

2026-11-17 23:56

The Nets have been a bottom five team in the NBA this season from the free-throw line, and they certainly left too many points at the stripe in Monday’s loss to the 76ers.

The Nets shot just 22-for-34 for a 64.7 percent conversion rate in their 12-point defeat. For the season, Brooklyn is ranked 26th in the league at 74.8 percent through its 3-10 start.

“Once again, I don’t want to go to the players and be like, ‘We gotta make free throws,’ because then you go there and you’re nervous,” Nets coach Jordi Fernández said.

“Obviously I don’t want to shoot 64 percent. … If we shoot better, we probably have a bigger chance. But I know our guys are good shooters and they’re gonna make free throws. We gotta concentrate and make sure that we believe that all of them are going to go in.”

Nolan Traore, Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle were a combined 19-for-21 from the line, but Egor Dëmin, Day’Ron Sharpe, Moritz Wagner and Danny Wolf combined to miss nine of 10 attempts.

Randle cut his lip in a face-to-face collision in Monday’s fourth quarter with Philly’s Jaylen Brown, who temporarily left the game to get his face bandaged. Brown also suffered a hamstring injury after returning to the floor.

“I’m fine. It feels terrible, to be honest. But I’m fine,” Randle said. “I just went to trap him and he turned, and he didn’t see me. Just kind of collided, but it’s fine.”

NBA legend Julius “Dr. J” Erving and former Nets coach Kevin Loughery will highlight the alumni present for a ceremony at halftime of Wednesday’s game against the Bulls honoring the Nets’ 1974 and 1976 ABA championship teams as part of the franchise’s 60th season.

Others scheduled to attend include: Brian Taylor, Bill Melchionni, George Bucci, Jim Eakins, Rich Jones, Jim O’Brien, Billy Schaeffer, Al Skinner and Chuck Terry.

Nets rookie Mikel Brown Jr. launched a mentorship program called “Mikel’s Crew” with a basketball clinic Tuesday with Brooklyn students from El Puente Beacon Leadership Center at MS 50 in Williamsburg.

“I feel like that’s just what it’s all about: influencing the youth and the community the right way,” Brown said. “That’s just how I grew up. I was influenced, I had a lot of mentors and people in my corner that kind of shaped me into who I am today.”
