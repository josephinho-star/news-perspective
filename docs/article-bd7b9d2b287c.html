Turkey, Qatar sign mutual defense agreement

2026-11-14 13:01

Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defense agreement in İstanbul on Friday aimed at adopting a “unified position” against an external armed attack on either country.

The announcement followed talks between Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani.

The agreement seeks to strengthen collective defense and regional security, according to a joint statement.

The two countries also agreed to consult on security threats and expand cooperation through joint military exercises, defense industry projects and personnel training exchanges.

The pact will “add a new dimension to the brotherly ties between the two countries,” Erdoğan said during the meeting, according to his office.

Qatar’s ruler traveled to İstanbul after attending the United Nations COP31 climate summit in southern Turkey.

Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed a regional defense pact in August committing them to come to one another’s aid if any of them came under attack.

Turkey’s parliament ratified that pact last week.

The pact links Turkey, which has the second-largest military in NATO, with Pakistan, the Muslim world’s only nuclear-armed state, and Saudi Arabia.

Turkey has said the pact is not aimed at any country and is open to other states, including Egypt.

Qatar, Indonesia, Pakistan and Turkey also signed a defense cooperation memorandum called “Badr” in Doha in October to expand military cooperation.

Erdoğan and the Qatari emir also discussed the Iran war.

Turkey and Qatar would continue working together “to prevent a renewed outbreak of war in the Gulf region and to promote dialogue and peace,” Erdoğan said.

Their talks also covered Israel’s attacks on Gaza, the humanitarian situation in the Palestinian territory, efforts toward normalization in Syria and developments in Libya, according to the Turkish presidency.
