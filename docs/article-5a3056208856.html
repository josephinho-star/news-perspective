The FBI is changing how it fights crime and protects Americans in their communities

2026-11-18 10:00

When President Donald Trump took office in January 2025, America faced one of the highest threat environments in its history. Years of open borders, soft-on-crime policies and a government that turned its back on law enforcement had left our communities vulnerable and our law enforcement officers without the support they deserved.

Under Trump’s leadership, we set out to reverse that tide. We rebuilt the FBI around its core mission: protecting the American people. We changed our priorities, revitalized partnerships, overhauled our technology and transformed the Bureau into a more aggressive, effective crime-fighting organization.

The results: Over the last 21 months, this FBI made 71,650 violent crime arrests, a 114% increase compared to the final 21 months of the previous administration. We seized 4,616 kilograms of deadly drugs, up 32%, including enough fentanyl to potentially kill more than 350 million Americans.

We identified or located 5,801 child victims, up 25%, many of whom were trafficked, exploited or abused. We made 6,744 arrests tied to foreign terrorist organizations, an increase of 1,057%, and disrupted more than 1,300 terrorists. We arrested 10 fugitives from the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, including alleged murderers, rapists and other violent criminals. That is 2.5 times the number arrested during the previous four years combined.

And through the historic, first-ever Most Wanted Fraudsters list, led by Vice President JD Vance and the White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, we arrested six alleged fraudsters in three-and-a-half months who were collectively responsible for $2.1 billion in alleged fraud.

These results were no accident. They were the product of deliberate decisions to change how the FBI operates, where we deploy our resources and how we work with our law enforcement partners.

First, we put violent crime back at the center of the FBI’s mission

On my first day as director, we made crushing violent crime a top priority. Previously, it barely ranked among the Bureau’s top 10 priorities. We changed that immediately. Today, "Crush Violent Crime" is more than a slogan. It drives our operations, our resource decisions and our commitment to the American people.

We then put our people where they could make the greatest impact. This FBI moved nearly 2,000 personnel out of Washington and back into the field. Agents, analysts and FBI teams solve cases in the field, in neighborhoods and alongside local officers who know their communities, not from conference rooms at headquarters. We shifted our focus away from Pennsylvania Avenue and back to Main Street America.

Second, we rebuilt and strengthened our law enforcement partnerships

The FBI cannot succeed alone. Our greatest strength comes from working shoulder to shoulder with the men and women of state, local, tribal and federal law enforcement.

Through our 59 Homeland Security Task Forces, 200 Joint Terrorism Task Forces, 30 Safe Trails Task Forces and other partnerships, we have strengthened coordination across agencies and delivered major joint operations.

Operation Summer Heat, our three-month violent crime surge, brought law enforcement together to target dangerous criminals nationwide with more than 9,000 violent crime arrests, a 3% increase from the record number of arrests in summer 2025. Operation Steadfast Promise deployed 500 FBI personnel to combat violent crime on tribal lands, representing the largest commitment to Indian Country in Bureau history — with surges like Operation Not Forgotten, where this FBI executed 118 arrests with Indian Country partners — up 123% from last year’s operation and bringing us to over 1,000 Indian Country arrests in 2026.

We added 2,770 Task Force Officers across the country by eliminating wasteful administrative bureaucracy that saw a 14-month onboarding process shrink to less than 30 days, allowing our agents to partner directly alongside state, local, tribal or other federal law enforcement officers.

We then put our people where they could make the greatest impact. This FBI moved nearly 2,000 personnel out of Washington and back into the field.

We also expanded our capabilities to confront emerging threats. Through our first-of-its-kind counter-unmanned aircraft systems (counter-UAS) school at the FBI’s Redstone arsenal, we equipped local law enforcement partners to detect, track and respond to dangerous drones. During the 2026 FIFA World Cup, those efforts helped lead to the seizure of more than 700 drones.

And our reach extends beyond America's borders. We increased foreign transfers of custody to 84, up 71%, and extraditions to 157, up 121%. These are dangerous fugitives and alleged criminals who can no longer evade American justice simply by crossing an international border.

Third, we modernized the FBI to meet today's threats

For too long, outdated technology and bureaucratic processes slowed the Bureau's ability to deliver results. We changed that. The FBI increased its use of superintelligence by 605% compared to 2024, putting this FBI on a course to save nearly 640,000 labor hours, the equivalent of about 79,000 workdays, by synthesizing data and streamlining administrative paperwork. In less than two years, our Laboratory Division eliminated a four-year DNA backlog involving 1.8 million samples.

We transformed our headquarters’ operational command and control center, standing up the first-ever "Director’s Strategic Information Group" — cutting DNA processing and producing finalized reporting in just 20 minutes, compared to what took hours in the old system. We also achieved compliance with mandatory insider threat requirements for the first time since the policy took effect in 2021.

And through Operation Blackout, our campaign against transnational scam centers, we are dismantling sophisticated criminal networks that prey on Americans. Our efforts have resulted in shuttering dozens of scam compounds overseas, more than $17 billion in seizures and prevented over 11,000 Americans from being scammed out of nearly $700 million in cyber-enabled fraud schemes.

These changes are delivering results where they matter most: in American communities. The nationwide murder rate has fallen 30%. Violent crime is declining across American cities. And during a historic summer that included America 250 celebrations and the 2026 FIFA World Cup — 6.8 million fans, with most of those at the 11 venues throughout America, the equivalent of 78 Super Bowls in 39 days — the country had zero major security incidents at those events.

This is what happens when law enforcement has clear priorities, the right resources, strong partnerships and leadership willing to put the mission first. None of this would be possible without the extraordinary men and women of the FBI and our law enforcement partners who put themselves in harm's way every day. They deserve the credit for turning our priorities into results.

Americans have a fundamental right to feel safe in their homes, on their streets and in their communities. Under President Trump, we have restored the FBI's focus on delivering that security.

We have made tremendous progress, but our work is far from finished. Criminals will continue to adapt. Threats will continue to evolve. And this FBI will continue to meet them head-on.
