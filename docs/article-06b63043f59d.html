Sophie Cunningham says fans thank her for standing up for women and girls as fame brings security concerns

2026-11-18 19:43

Indiana Fever guard Sophie Cunningham says her rising profile has changed how she moves through public life, bringing both messages of support from fans and new concerns about her privacy and safety.

"I can’t go anywhere anymore without people saying, ‘Thank you for sticking up for women. Thank you for sticking up for girls,’" Cunningham told Maxim in an interview published Wednesday.

Cunningham said the attention has become difficult to avoid, even away from basketball. "I have security now. I can’t really go anywhere without being approached," she said, adding that she has had to take her "privacy and protection much more seriously."

Her comments come after a season in which she became a prominent voice in the debate over transgender athletes in women’s sports. In an ESPN interview published in July, Cunningham said she wanted to protect "young girls in a locker room, or young girls in sport who shouldn’t have to go against biological men." She also said she had received criticism but had never said she hated transgender people.

Cunningham recently said she hired security to travel with her around the clock after discussing concerns about stalkers on her podcast. She said the unwanted attention had spread beyond the places where fans had previously recognized her.

Cunningham’s remarks also follow a series of controversies involving fans who publicly supported her position. At a July game in Seattle, two teenage girls held signs thanking Cunningham for "speaking up for girls." The Storm later apologized after minority owner Celeste Keaton confronted the fans; the WNBA suspended Keaton for five home games and fined her.

Weeks later, OutKick reported that security at an Atlanta game told fans wearing shirts about transgender participation in women’s sports to cover them or leave. The WNBA said the security staff had acted improperly and apologized.

The Fever’s season ended with a playoff loss to the Las Vegas Aces. Looking ahead, Cunningham said she could see herself in several roles—including, someday, WNBA commissioner—because she believes she understands both the players’ perspective and the league’s business side.

Her comments in Maxim add a personal dimension to the public attention: she said she wants fans to feel they can approach her, but is learning to set boundaries as her fame grows.
