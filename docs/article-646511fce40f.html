House passes stopgap bill that pauses changes to federal science grant-making process

2026-09-01 20:45

A Trump administration effort to give political figures control over a large swath of U.S. science funding is off the table until at least December.

On Tuesday, members of the House of Representatives voted 370 to 48, with 14 members not voting, to adopt a Senate appropriations bill that will continue funding federal agencies until December 11.

As part of the stop-gap measure, the Senate agreed in August not to adopt proposed rules from the Office of Budget Management that would give politically appointed bureaucrats authority over federal research grants. This would break from the current system where independent panels of peer reviewers, experts in the field, assess grant requests for funding from the National Institutes of Health, the National Science Foundation and other governmental agencies.

The rule change has met an outpouring of opposition from the scientific community. Thousands of researchers, including several Nobel laureates, used the public comment period on the proposed rule to say it would politicize research and destroy U.S. leadership in global science.

Whether the proposed rule change goes further could hinge largely on the outcome of the midterm elections, which are to be held in November: Democrats, who are currently favored to win control of the House, have broadly opposed the change.
