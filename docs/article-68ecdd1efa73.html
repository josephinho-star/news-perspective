The NFL Rookie Progress Report: How all 257 draft picks are faring with their new teams

2026-08-28 21:58

The NFL's cut-down deadline is Sunday, and the 2026 NFL season is less than two weeks away. But before 32 NFL rosters get pared down to 53 players each, we're doing our annual inventory of the most recent draft class through training camp.

We asked our NFL Nation writers to size up how all 257 draftees have fared since being selected. They have updates on every player who went off the board, from No. 1 pick Fernando Mendoza to Broncos linebacker Red Murdock, this year's "Mr. Irrelevant." Who is in line to start for their team? Who is outplaying their draft position in the preseason and could make an early impact? And which high picks are falling behind?

Rookies are grouped by team below, and each team is listed in the order by which it made its first selection in April. That means we begin with the Raiders' class, before hitting the Jets and Cardinals and so on.

Round 1 (No. 1): Fernando Mendoza, QB. Mendoza might not be the Week 1 starter, but signs of him being a franchise quarterback are there. Throughout training camp, players and coaches have praised the Heisman Trophy winner's improved confidence. He's unafraid of targeting tight windows. On top of that, he has a firmer grasp of the system, resulting in better command of the offense. "That's hard to do as a young guy," center Tyler Linderbaum said. "You're dealing with a lot of new things, a whole new offense. So seeing the growth in his command and operation, it's a good starting point."

Round 2 (No. 38): Treydan Stukes, S. Stukes has picked up momentum lately. He performed well against the Texans in the second preseason game and played mostly with the first-team defense during the final week of training camp.

Round 3 (No. 67): Keyron Crawford, edge rusher. Crawford has flashed at times during training camp while working mostly with the second-team defense. He's currently behind Maxx Crosby, Kwity Paye and Malcolm Koonce, but the Raiders like to rotate their edge rushers in games. So Crawford should get some playing time this season.

Round 3 (No. 91): Trey Zuhn III, G/OT. The Raiders view Zuhn as an interior offensive lineman, but he has played right tackle in preseason games. It seems like Las Vegas is prepping Zuhn to be a swing tackle this season.

Round 4 (No. 101): Jermod McCoy, CB. McCoy's first preseason game was rough, allowing two touchdowns. But he has looked much better since, especially in man coverage against the Texans. The Raiders will continue to be patient since McCoy hasn't played football in more than a year due to a knee injury, but there's confidence that he'll eventually be a reliable outside corner.

Round 4 (No. 122): Mike Washington Jr., RB. The Raiders are expected to rely on Washington quite a bit, especially with Ashton Jeanty's recent ankle injury. Washington's performance this preseason shows he could handle the extended workload.

Round 5 (No. 150): Dalton Johnson, S. Johnson will be a depth piece in the secondary, with Las Vegas using him as a nickel in practice lately.

Round 5, (No. 175): Hezekiah Masses, CB. Masses has received a fair number of first-team reps throughout training camp. There is not a ton of veteran experience among the Raiders' cornerbacks, so Masses has an opportunity to make a difference.

Round 6 (No. 195): Malik Benson, WR. Benson has been a reliable pass catcher for both Kirk Cousins and Mendoza. Will that translate into the regular season? Only time will tell, but Benson has set himself up to get playing time.

Round 7 (No. 229): Brandon Cleveland, DT. Cleveland might have a tough climb to make the initial 53-man roster and could start the season on the practice squad. -- Ryan McFadden

Round 1 (No. 2): David Bailey, Edge. Bailey looks like the real deal. His first-step quickness is exceptional, and he flashes surprising power on his bull rushes. If there's a knock, it's that he needs to show more awareness in run defense. Bailey will start in the base defense and play in several sub packages. "He's everything that we expect him to be," coach Aaron Glenn said.

Round 1 (No. 16): Kenyon Sadiq, TE. He missed virtually the entire training camp due to a setback from offseason hernia surgery. Sadiq is expected to be ready for Week 1, but the lost time will hurt his ability to make an early impact. When healthy, he's a versatile and explosive player who can line up anywhere, including the backfield. Offensive coordinator Frank Reich will have fun utilizing Sadiq, describing him as "a high-horsepower guy," but it will take a while.

Round 1 (No. 30): Omar Cooper Jr., WR. Many assumed Cooper would be the No. 3 receiver behind Garrett Wilson and Adonai Mitchell, but that's not a lock. Cooper lost ground in camp and is now engaged in a battle with Isaiah Williams for that job. Cooper's explosiveness isn't dazzling, and his route running needs refinement. He has lined up mostly on the outside, but he's capable of playing the slot.

Round 2 (No. 50): D'Angelo Ponds, CB. Ponds was expected to compete for the nickel job, maybe even an outside corner position, but he missed the entire camp with a calf strain. It's fair to wonder if he will be active in Week 1, considering the lost time. It has been a frustrating summer for Ponds.

Round 4 (No. 103): Darrell Jackson Jr., DT. Jackson has been a pleasant surprise who has played his way into the defensive line rotation.

Round 4 (No. 110): Cade Klubnik, QB. He doesn't have the biggest arm, but the organization loves his intangibles, which is a big reason he's the odds-on favorite to be the No. 2 quarterback.

Round 6 (No. 188): Anez Cooper, G. He went from third team to second team after the first preseason game. Cooper will make the 53 but could be inactive on game day.

Round 7 (No. 228): VJ Payne, S. Payne is expected to return soon from an early-camp injury but will be at the bottom of the 53-man roster if he's not put on the practice squad. -- Rich Cimini

Round 1 (No. 3): Jeremiyah Love, RB. When he was healthy, Love was everything the Cardinals imagined -- fast, quick, strong, a great route runner, great hands. His teammates raved about him and all the facets of his game. When he finally made his debut against the Raiders in the second preseason game, he ended up with a high ankle sprain and hasn't practiced or played since. He won't play again this preseason and might not practice until Week 1.

Round 2 (No. 34): Chase Bisontis, G. Bisontis was put on injured reserve with a torn left MCL that he suffered during the preseason game against the Raiders. Before that, Bisontis was progressing each week, according to coach Mike LaFleur. He was likely going to be a backup but could've been inserted into the starting lineup if needed.

Round 3 (No. 65): Carson Beck, QB. Beck is dealing with sore ribs after taking a hit during the Hall of Fame Game on Aug. 6 and hasn't practiced or played since. He was making steady growth before then, showing flashes of his arm and touch during training camp. He might be back soon, but his injury stunted his training camp progress.

Round 4 (No. 104): Kaleb Proctor, DT. Proctor was put on season-ending IR with a torn meniscus that he suffered during OTAs.

Round 5 (No. 143): Reggie Virgil, WR. Virgil has impressed during training camp and has put in enough good tape to likely make the team as a bottom-of-the-depth-chart receiver.

Round 6 (No. 183): Karson Sharar, LB. Sharar dealt with injuries during OTAs and minicamp but has recovered and is starting to find his footing.

Round 7 (No. 217): Jayden Williams, OT. Williams worked his way up to the second-team offensive line throughout camp, impressing his teammates and coaches. -- Josh Weinfuss

Round 1 (No. 4): Carnell Tate, WR. Tate has impressed with his surprisingly large catch radius and consistent hands-catching ability. His ability to work the full route tree has helped him earn quarterback Cam Ward's trust. The Titans have started to move Tate across the formation rather than always having him on the ball. "He looks real good," Saleh said. "There's still a lot of things that he has to learn with regard to any game that's happening out there on the perimeter."

Round 1 (No. 31): Keldric Faulk, Edge. Faulk has earned an early spot in the defensive end rotation and should get plenty of snaps when healthy, as he is currently day-to-day due to a shoulder injury. The 6-foot-5, 274-pound Faulk has worked diligently in training camp on keeping his pad level low.

Round 2 (No. 60): Anthony Hill Jr., LB. Saleh has Hill working exclusively at Mike linebacker to allow him to better digest the defense and play fast. The results are showing. "He's done a nice job," defensive coordinator Gus Bradley said. "We've got him at one position right now ... because there's quite a bit on that plate. But I've seen him as far as handling the defense, addressing the defense, taking charge of the defense."

Round 5 (No. 142): Fernando Carmona, G. Carmona has taken hold of the starting right guard spot. The coaches feel he embodies the nastiness they want the offensive line to play with this season.

Round 5 (No. 165): Nicholas Singleton, RB. Singleton ran well, especially between the tackles, when healthy. He missed a week of practice due to injury, but he'll definitely have a spot on the roster.

Round 6 (No. 184): Jackie Marshall, DT. Marshall returned from an oblique injury and got right back into the interior defensive line rotation.

Round 6 (No. 194): Pat Coogan, C. Coogan has earned the backup center spot. The coaches like his football IQ and communication skills.

Round 7 (No. 225): Jaren Kanak, TE. Kanak landed on injured reserve after tearing his pectoral muscle in the preseason opener against the 49ers. -- Turron Davenport

Round 1 (No. 5): Arvell Reese, LB. Reese has stood out throughout the summer. His physical style of play fits well with what the Giants are going to do, and he should be an instant asset for a run defense that struggled last season. Reese has been plugged in immediately as a starter at inside linebacker next to veteran Tremaine Edmunds. "Yeah, I mean, [Reese is] a freak, man. ... That's a big middle, bro," safety Jason Pinnock said. Reese will also be a valuable piece in Dennard Wilson's "positionless defense" as a rusher on passing downs.

Round 1 (No. 10): Francis Mauigoa, G. Mauigoa fits in nicely on the Giants offensive line. He has been the starter at right guard since Day 1 of camp, where he has proven to be a big help with his nasty style of play, specifically in the run game. New York seems to have big plans for Mauigoa. The only downside is he missed some time this summer with a stinger, which comes after he was diagnosed with a herniated disc in his back earlier this year. It's something to keep an eye on moving forward.

Round 2 (No. 37): Colton Hood, CB. It has been an up-and-down summer for Hood, but he is still competing with Deonte Banks, Greg Newsome II and Paulson Adebo for playing time at one of the two outside spots. It's likely that Hood starts the season as a backup, but he'll get a chance at some point. His competitiveness has flashed, which has made life difficult for receivers but also led to some penalties.

Round 3 (No. 74): Malachi Fields, WR. The Giants traded up because they were infatuated with his size, physicality and ability to make contested catches. That's exactly what they've seen this summer. Fields will likely start as the Giants' No. 2 receiver opposite Malik Nabers, in part because of his size and ability to block.

Round 6 (No. 186): Bobby Jamison-Travis, DT. Jamison-Travis is probably the rawest of the three sixth-rounders and could end up on the practice squad.

Round 6 (No. 192): J.C. Davis, OT. The Giants are very happy with what they've seen from Davis at left tackle, especially when he didn't allow a pressure in the second preseason game against the Dolphins despite playing the whole way.

Round 6 (No. 193): Jack Kelly, LB. Kelly is playing inside linebacker after being a smallish edge in college and will likely have some special teams value as a rookie. -- Jordan Raanan

Round 1 (No. 6): Mansoor Delane, CB. Although Delane missed a large portion of fully padded practices in camp recovering from a right shoulder injury, he was quite impressive in his preseason debut against the Buccaneers' starters. The Chiefs are hoping Delane can be a suitable replacement for All-Pro Trent McDuffie, who was traded to the Rams this offseason. Delane is projected to be a starter in the Week 1 "Monday Night Football" opener against the Broncos.

Round 1 (No. 29): Peter Woods, DT. Woods was stellar early in camp, demonstrating that his athleticism, power and quickness should pair well alongside star pass rusher Chris Jones. But Woods sustained a left ankle injury right before the preseason opener, which has limited him in the first two exhibition games. Woods is not projected to be a starter, but he should be a factor as a rotational defender on third-down snaps. The Chiefs believe Woods can create pressure on the quarterback by winning one-on-one matchups next to Jones, who is often double-teamed.

Round 2 (No. 40): R Mason Thomas, Edge. Thomas offers something no other Chiefs edge rusher has -- blazing speed. That trait should get Thomas on the field sooner rather than later on clear pass-rushing snaps. He has done well in camp and showed in the preseason he's capable of pressuring the quarterback with a variety of moves.

Round 4 (No. 109): Jadon Canady, CB. Canady projects to be the Chiefs' nickel defender at some point, but he'll likely spend his rookie season learning behind Chris Roland-Wallace.

Round 5 (No. 161): Emmett Johnson, RB. Johnson can break tackles and is a positive in the pass game, making him a part of the Chiefs' backfield rotation.

Round 5: (No. 176): Cyrus Allen, WR. Allen was the biggest winner in camp, and his ability to be a quality route runner should make him one of the Chiefs' top answers against man-to-man coverage.

Round 7 (No. 249): Garrett Nussmeier, QB. Nussmeier is not afraid to zip the ball into tight coverage. He'll use this season to essentially redshirt and learn before eventually becoming Patrick Mahomes' long-term backup. -- Nate Taylor

Round 1 (No. 7): Sonny Styles, LB. Styles has been mostly working with the starters since camp began and they have had him wear the green dot at times. In two preseason games, he has played like a rookie still learning to play at the NFL level. He'll have to adjust to taking on guards and centers when coming downhill, but the Commanders drafted him because of his athletic ability and length. His speed shows up a lot in coverage, from closing on receivers to how deep he can drop. Said safety Jeremy Reaves of Styles: "Man, we got us one."

Round 3 (No. 71): Antonio Williams, WR. Williams will play early for Washington, though the addition of Stefon Diggs allows him to ease into his role. The Commanders like what they've seen from Williams, particularly in the slot. And one coach said he had some "great plays" in the second preseason game at Detroit.

Round 5 (No. 147): Joshua Josephs, Edge. After a strong summer, Josephs will make the team. In addition to special teams play, he provides depth as a pass rusher and is someone to watch as he develops.

Round 6 (No. 187): Kaytron Allen, RB. Allen should make the roster given the combination of injuries at the position and his displays of good vision and power.

Round 6 (No. 209): Matt Gulbin, C. Washington needs depth at center, so Gulbin will probably make the roster as someone to develop for the future.

Round 7 (No. 223): Athan Kaliakmanis, QB. If Washington keeps three quarterbacks, Kaliakmanis would likely be the third because of his strong arm and ability to play from the pocket. -- John Keim

Round 1 (No. 8): Jordyn Tyson, WR. Tyson began camp on a high note, working immediately with the 1s and developing chemistry with the first-team offense. But the Saints won't see him for at least the first several weeks of the season after he injured his hamstring in a joint practice session against the Jaguars. The Saints handled him cautiously earlier this summer due to previous hamstring issues in college, but the injury occurred after he landed awkwardly while making a catch in practice.

Round 2 (No. 42): Christen Miller, DT. The Saints brought Miller along slowly due to injuries. (He had hamstring issues in minicamp and a toe injury in camp.) The Saints have been playing him with the second and third teams when he has been available. Miller said he can play anywhere on the defensive line. He showed promise in his 17 snaps against the Jaguars but left with a shoulder injury and did not play in the second preseason game.

Round 3 (No. 73): Oscar Delp, TE. Delp scored his first NFL touchdown in the preseason opener against the Jaguars and played 13 snaps in the second preseason game before injuring his hamstring. Delp wasn't going to leapfrog Juwan Johnson or Noah Fant in terms of targets or playing time when healthy, but it's unclear if he'll miss time at this point.

Round 4 (No. 132): Jeremiah Wright, G. Wright dealt with a shoulder injury earlier in camp, but he'll be a key backup in the interior behind right guard Cesar Ruiz.

Round 4 (No. 136): Bryce Lance, WR. Lance was considered a developmental wideout when he was drafted, but he has gotten opportunities due to attrition. His recent reps with the first team in practice have shown that the Saints will be able to find a role for Lance this season.

Round 5 (No. 172): Lorenzo Styles Jr., S. Styles is expected to miss time with a calf injury, according to coach Kellen Moore. His path to the roster was already tough, and this injury will be a setback.

Round 6 (No. 190): Barion Brown, WR. Brown had a very impressive camp and will be able to contribute right away as a receiver and kick returner. He did not play in the second preseason game, showing that the team already considers him important enough to be cautious with him.

Round 7 (No. 219): TJ Hall Jr., CB. Former undrafted rookie Jayden Price has grabbed all the attention in camp, leaving Hall with a practice squad spot at best right now. -- Katherine Terrell

Round 1 (No. 9): Spencer Fano, OT. The Browns wanted Fano to earn his role as the franchise left tackle, and he has quickly won the coaching staff over with his daily training camp battles against star edge rusher Jared Verse. Fano will be Cleveland's blind-side protector on an entirely new offensive line in Week 1. "The guy's extremely athletic, he plays hard, those things were not going to be the question," offensive coordinator Travis Switzer said. "It's just refining the technique, which he's shown he can do, and he's going to continue to do that."

Round 1 (No. 24): KC Concepcion, WR. Concepcion has been a dynamic addition to the Browns' offense. Coach Todd Monken is using Concepcion in a role similar to the one he created for Zay Flowers in Baltimore, with plenty of motion and a variety of touches. Monken envisions a versatile role for Concepcion as a rookie -- possibly as a punt returner, too. "We've got to find a number of ways to get him the ball with his explosiveness, his quickness, his contact balance," Monken said. "He's going to be a real weapon for us."

Round 2 (No. 39): Denzel Boston, WR. Browns assistant GM Glenn Cook said he didn't envision a scenario in which Cleveland would get to draft both Concepcion and Boston, but the Browns aren't regretting double-dipping at the position. Boston's contested-catch ability and reliable hands earned him a starting role alongside Concepcion as the "X" receiver.

Round 2 (No. 58): Emmanuel McNeil-Warren, S. The Browns eased McNeil-Warren into a defense that asks its safeties to take on a multitude of roles, but he is primed for a significant role in a scheme that frequently uses three-safety lineups.

Round 3 (No. 86): Austin Barber, OT. The Browns drafted Barber to develop him into a future starting tackle, but they have given him reps at guard and have been impressed by his versatility. He could be a top reserve as a rookie.

Round 5 (No. 146): Parker Brailsford, C. Brailsford was poised to be the Browns' backup center but did not play in the preseason because of injury.

Round 5 (No. 149): Justin Jefferson, LB. Jefferson should make the roster but could be a healthy scratch on game day. He's not a starter and doesn't appear to be penciled in a top special teams role.

Round 5 (No. 170): Joe Royer, TE. The status of Royer is in question as he has missed camp for personal reasons. He could be placed on the reserve/non-football injury list to preserve a roster spot for someone else.

Round 6 (No. 182): Taylen Green, QB. Green received few reps in camp, as the Browns are effectively using his rookie season as a redshirt year for development. But his physical traits are tantalizing, and Monken has praised Green's recall of the offense.

Round 7 (No. 248): Carsen Ryan, TE. Injuries at tight end and fullback have forced Ryan to take on multiple roles, but Monken has been pleased with his growth. Ryan is on track to be Cleveland's No. 3 tight end. -- Daniel Oyefusi

Round 1 (No. 11): Caleb Downs, S. Expectations have been high since the Cowboys moved up a spot to take him, and Downs has done nothing but meet every standard. He has shown he can play in the slot and cover shifty receivers while also being a willing run defender. He can also play on the back end. Considering how much the Cowboys will use their sub packages, he will be a starter in Week 1. "He has a streak on the field," defensive coordinator Christian Parker said. "Obviously, you don't know that until you're in those competitive environments with somebody, but he definitely has a fire to him and an edge."

Round 1 (No. 23): Malachi Lawrence, Edge. The Cowboys knew Lawrence would need more development than Downs and are prepared to be patient. There has been growth throughout the summer and the preseason games, but the best thing Lawrence will have is the ability to learn from veterans like Von Miller. The Cowboys like Lawrence's strength at the point of attack and believe he can be a healthy part of their pass-rush rotation. "Malachi's a guy I see getting better steadily every single day," coach Brian Schottenheimer said. "You do that over these next four, five, six weeks, and I think he'll be ready to go come the regular season."

Round 3 (No. 92): Jaishawn Barham, LB. He could make this draft class special. Before suffering a groin injury in camp, Barham was everywhere and earning first-team snaps. He maintained that level of play when he returned against Arizona in Week 2 of the preseason. He plays with a nasty demeanor and can pursue well. Barham could end up as a starter later in the season.

Round 4 (No. 112): Drew Shelton, OT. Like most Day 3 rookies, he needs to add strength. Shelton got bullied a few times in practice and games but can play either tackle spot. The Cowboys believe he can develop into a keeper.

Round 4 (No. 114): Devin Moore, CB. A groin injury limited Moore for a spell in camp, but his size (6-foot-3, 200 pounds) and speed give him the edge for one of the final cornerback spots.

Round 4 (No. 137): LT Overton, DT. He enters a crowded room with high-end and solid veterans ahead of him, but Overton showed he has the size, strength and movement skills that could eventually get him into the rotation.

Round 7 (No. 218): Anthony Smith, WR. He has had good moments in training camp practices and preseason games but maybe not enough to crack the initial 53-man roster. Still, Smith has special teams value and speed the Cowboys want to develop. -- Todd Archer

Round 1 (No. 12): Kadyn Proctor, G/OT. Proctor has long been slotted in as the Dolphins' starting left guard despite playing left tackle at Alabama. He has solidified himself as one of the team's five best offensive linemen since being selected. Proctor faced criticism for his work ethic during the draft process, but Dolphins coach Jeff Hafley said he loves the rookie's commitment through the first few months of his career. "Proctor's been outstanding -- I can't say enough about him," Hafley said. "The buy-in has been outstanding."

Round 1 (No. 27): Chris Johnson, CB. Like Proctor, Johnson was expected to make an immediate impact. He has cross-trained at all three cornerback positions and while it's unclear who the Dolphins' starters will be, Johnson is effectively guaranteed to be one of them. His coaches and teammates have praised his football IQ and instincts, and there have been flashes this summer when he looked like a lockdown corner. "The thing that's impressed me the most about him is just the poise he plays with out there," defensive coordinator Sean Duggan said. "For a rookie, the moment's never too big. He always seems under control."

Round 2 (No. 43): Jacob Rodriguez, LB. The Dolphins were excited to land Rodriguez, and he has validated that excitement this offseason. His tendency to force fumbles has translated to the practice field, and he has proved himself capable of making plays sideline to sideline. It shouldn't be a surprise if he starts in Week 1.

Round 3 (No. 75): Caleb Douglas, WR. This pick was widely considered a reach by draft pundits, but general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan might get the last laugh. Douglas looked like the Dolphins' best receiver early in camp, though that's not a particularly high bar to clear. His production tapered over the past two weeks, but he is listed as a starter and should get every opportunity to establish himself as Miami's lead receiver.

Round 3 (No. 87): Will Kacmarek, TE. Kacmarek wasn't a flashy selection, but he proves his value every time the pads come on. Sullivan called him an "upper-echelon" run blocker already, and Kacmarek has been a mauler for a Dolphins offense that wants to establish the run this season. He will see plenty of action as a rookie, especially if he continues to develop as a pass catcher.

Round 3 (No. 94): Chris Bell, WR. Bell was a late-Day 1 talent who fell to the end of the third round because of a torn ACL suffered in December. Despite starting training camp on the active/NFI list, Bell has since returned and was cleared for full contact entering the final week of the preseason. There's a path for Bell to be one of the team's best receivers in 2026.

Round 4 (No. 130): Trey Moore, Edge. Moore was a training camp standout but will undergo surgery on an ankle injury suffered in the second preseason game. He is expected to miss significant time.

Round 4 (No. 138): Kyle Louis, LB. Louis projected as an NFL safety but the Dolphins see him as a role-specific linebacker. He is versatile enough to play both positions, and Miami might have to utilize that skill set this season.

Round 5 (No. 158): Michael Taaffe, S. Safety is the Dolphins' thinnest position, and Taaffe should get playing time out of necessity.

Round 5 (No. 177): Kevin Coleman Jr., WR. The Dolphins' third rookie wide receiver has come on strong over the past few weeks. Coleman, just like his counterparts, has an opportunity to make an impact.

Round 5 (No. 180): Seydou Traore, TE. Traore is an intriguing player to watch after the catch and should make the initial roster despite some focus drops this summer.

Round 6 (No. 200): DJ Campbell, G. Campbell will likely make the roster as a depth and developmental piece, but he isn't expected to push for early playing time.

Round 7 (No. 238): Max Llewellyn, Edge. Llewellyn has flashed a few times at camp and should make the roster as a developmental pass rusher, but don't be surprised if he pushes for playing time. -- Marcel Louis-Jacques

Round 1 (No. 13): Ty Simpson, QB. Simpson entered training camp in a competition with 2023 fourth-round pick Stetson Bennett IV to back up Matthew Stafford. Bennett got the majority of the No. 2 reps early in training camp, but Simpson impressed in his first preseason game, completing 21 of 25 passes for 190 yards with two touchdowns and no interceptions. After the game, McVay said he thought Simpson had "good command" of the offense. In his second preseason game, Simpson played the second half but got just three series; he completed 8 of 12 passes for 73 yards. And he capped off his preseason with 119 passing yards on Thursday night.

Round 2 (No. 61): Max Klare, TE. Klare was drafted to a team with a crowded tight end room, as the Rams will likely carry five tight ends, including Klare. Los Angeles ran 331 plays in 13 personnel (three tight ends) last season which, according to ESPN Research, was more than double any other team.

Round 3 (No. 93): Keagen Trost, OT. Trost left the preseason opener with a hamstring injury after starting at right tackle. He was on pace to be the Rams' backup at the position. Sean McVay said he's hopeful Trost will return before the start of the season. "He's a mentally tough, resilient guy," McVay said.

Round 6 (No. 197): CJ Daniels, WR. Daniels is expected to make the Rams' roster, with McVay describing the sixth-round pick as "a mature rookie" during training camp.

Round 7 (No. 232): Tim Keenan III, DT. Keenan totaled a sack and six tackles in the Rams' preseason games. -- Sarah Barshop

Round 1 (No. 14): Olaivavega Ioane, G. Ioane will be the Ravens' starting right guard when the regular season begins after working with the first team since his first practice. Coaches and teammates have praised Ioane for carrying himself like a veteran. "He's very smart. He understands the game up front," Ravens offensive line coach Dwayne Ledford said. "He has some really good feet that allow him to do a lot of things up front that, being his size, is pretty special."

Round 2 (No. 45): Zion Young, Edge. Young has impressed the coaches with his physicality. He is expected to back up Tavius Robinson as the strong-side linebacker but should have the opportunity to start next season. When asked about Young's best trait, tight end Josh Cuevas said, "It's his willingness to do anything."

Round 3 (No. 80): Ja'Kobi Lane, WR. He is primed for a big role in the offense after being the star of training camp. By making highlight plays nearly every day this summer, Lane has positioned himself be the No. 3 receiver behind Zay Flowers and Rashod Bateman. "He's effortlessly good," fellow rookie wide receiver Elijah Sarratt said.

Round 4 (No. 115): Elijah Sarratt, WR. He has flashed at times this summer, but Sarratt could be a game-day inactive because he doesn't provide much of a special teams presence.

Round 4 (No. 133): Matthew Hibner, TE. He has the edge as the team's No. 3 tight end and should be a core special teams player.

Round 5 (No. 162): Chandler Rivers, CB. It looks like Rivers will be the backup slot cornerback and contribute heavily on special teams.

Round 5 (No. 173): Josh Cuevas, TE. He's been closing on Hibner for the No. 3 tight end spot and has been solid on special teams.

Round 5 (No. 174): Adam Randall, RB. The Ravens usually don't carry four running backs on the 53-man roster, but Randall could be active on game days if he can show an ability to play special teams.

Round 6 (No. 211): Ryan Eckley, P. The first punter taken in this year's draft has not disappointed, averaging 49.8 yards in his first two preseason games.

Round 7 (No. 250): Rayshaun Benny, DT. If Benny fails to make the 53-man roster, he has impressed enough to land on the practice squad.

Round 7 (No. 253): Evan Beerntsen, G. He's buried so far down the depth chart that he has played a total of eight snaps in the first two preseason games. -- Jamison Hensley

Round 1 (No. 15): Rueben Bain Jr., Edge. Bain will start, and the team is thrilled with what it has seen so far. Whether it was channeling Reggie White with a hump move on third down that forced a Justin Fields incompletion, or the club/swipe move he put on teammate Luke Goedeke during one-on-ones, Bain has the stuff to make an impact right away, even with his recent AC sprain. But it's also his attitude and what he does when no one's watching that have quickly earned the respect of his teammates. "Just seeing the hunger and determination in his eyes, it's good to have on your side," defensive tackle Vita Vea said.

Round 3 (No. 66): Tyler Onyedim, DT. Onyedim has been in the defensive line rotation this preseason. While Eyioma Uwazurike looks to get most of the tackle snaps that once went to John Franklin-Myers, Onyedim has flashed in the pass rush with his quickness off the snap and length. So pass-rush situations will be where he makes the quickest impact, and his playing time will increase as he builds up his strength at the point of attack.
