Mozambique opposition leader goes on trial for terrorism over deadly election protests

2026-11-17 15:19

Mozambique's opposition leader Venâncio Mondlane has gone on trial for charges including incitement to terrorism and civil disobedience over deadly protests that followed the country's disputed 2024 elections.

He denies any wrongdoing, while his party has described the trial as politically motivated.

Ahead of Mondlane's appearance at the Supreme Court, security was tight, with authorities fearing a fresh wave of demonstrations by his supporters.

Police have appealed to citizens to follow the proceedings calmly and avoid actions that could threaten public order.

Mondlane arrived at the Supreme Court early on Tuesday with "great peace of mind", and was greeted by dozens of supporters.

"As you can see, I arrive calm, confident in the work of my legal team," he told journalists gathered outside court, amid a heavy police presence.

The trial is widely seen as a major test of Mozambique's political stability, nearly two years after disputed elections triggered some of the country's deadliest political unrest in decades.

"The trial is a real test for Mozambique. It is is an important opportunity to demonstrate that the rule of law applies equally to everyone," said Luis Nhachote, a political analyst.

Mondlane, who challenged the presidential election results that declared Daniel Chapo of the ruling Frelimo party the winner, accused the authorities of widespread electoral fraud and called for nationwide protests and work stoppages.

The demonstrations continued for months, with rights groups accusing security forces of using excessive force against protesters.

Amnesty International, citing local civil society organisations, said more than 300 people were killed during the unrest, which was one of the country's worst post-election crises since independence.

Mozambican police put the death toll at 96, including 17 police officers.

European Union election observers also reported irregularities in the October 2024 vote, raising questions about the credibility of the results.

Prosecutors accuse Mondlane of encouraging demonstrations that disrupted public services, damaged property and threatened public security.

The charges include publicly glorifying criminal acts, incitement to collective disobedience, public instigation to commit crimes and offences linked to terrorism.

He could face up to 24 years in prison if convicted of the most serious charge.

Mondlane has rejected the accusations, insisting that his calls for demonstrations were aimed at demanding electoral transparency rather than encouraging violence.

A heavy deployment of police patrolled outside the Supreme Court and major streets in Maputo before the trial began.

In a statement issued on Monday, Mozambique's police appealed to citizens to "preserve tolerance and peaceful coexistence," adding that the right to demonstrate must be exercised strictly within the limits of the law.

Police also pledged to protect people and property while acting preventively and proportionately in accordance with the rule of law.

The court has only granted access to selected media organisations, sparking criticism from journalists and press freedom campaigners.

Court officials say the restrictions are necessary because of limited seating capacity in the courtroom, but critics argue they undermine transparency in a case of significant public interest.

Live broadcasts of the trial have been banned by the authorities, citing the fear of unrest.

The proceedings were expected to be heard by five judges, with witnesses called by both the prosecution and defence.

Political observers warn that the trial could reignite tensions in a country still struggling to recover from the violence and deep political divisions that followed the 2024 elections.
