Rams win Battle for LA as Chargers’ winless nightmare hits new low

2026-11-01 23:54

For nearly 30 minutes Sunday afternoon, the Los Angeles Rams looked like a team determined to give away another football game. Fortunately for Sean McVay, the Los Angeles Chargers were more than willing to take that burden off their hands.

In just the second regular-season Battle for LA at SoFi Stadium, the Rams overcame a dreadful first half, forced three turnovers and scored 21 unanswered points to defeat their winless roommates, 24-9, earning consecutive victories for the first time this season.

The Rams (4-4) are back at .500 after a season that appeared to be slipping through their fingers just two weeks ago. Meanwhile, the Chargers (0-7) remain the NFL’s punching bag, with Justin Herbert’s interception problems becoming as inevitable as a dog who barks at the mailman when they see him from the window.

It wasn’t pretty early, but the Rams eventually reminded their crosstown rivals who owns the house.

The afternoon began with an intriguing wrinkle on special teams. The Rams elevated kicker Josh Karty from the practice squad to handle kickoffs alongside veteran Brandon McManus, and the experiment initially looked brilliant.

Karty’s opening kickoff bounced awkwardly in front of the returner, limiting the Chargers to a nine-yard return and pinning them at their own 23-yard line.

Unfortunately, the Rams offense didn’t start as sharp as their new kickoff specialist.

After forcing a three-and-out, Matthew Stafford threw an ugly interception to Tarheeb Still, who jumped a sideline route intended for Konata Mumpfield. A Tyler Higbee false start had turned a manageable third-and-1 into third-and-6, another example of the self-inflicted mistakes that have haunted McVay’s team.

The Chargers couldn’t capitalize, however, and Herbert returned the favor with an interception to Quentin Lake, who nearly took it to the house before being stopped deep in Chargers territory.

For most of the first half, Stafford and the Rams offense looked completely lost. Five possessions produced zero first downs, while the Chargers built a 9-3 lead behind three field goals.

Then, with halftime approaching, somebody finally flipped the switch.

Stafford connected with Tutu Atwell on a gorgeous 40-yard strike down the left sideline, breathing life into an offense that had been flatlining for nearly two quarters.

The Rams marched 66 yards in five plays, needing just 1:36 before Kyren Williams punched in a 1-yard touchdown to give Los Angeles a 10-9 lead. A lead they would never relinquish again.

Blake Corum emerged from halftime running like a man possessed, carrying five times for 55 yards on the opening possession of the third quarter. His 23-yard touchdown run capped a 74-yard drive and pushed the Rams’ advantage to 17-9.

Suddenly, the Chargers looked overwhelmed.

Even another costly Karty kickoff penalty, his second of the afternoon for a ball bouncing short of the landing zone, couldn’t rescue Herbert and the Chargers offense.

Herbert, who entered Sunday leading the NFL with eight interceptions, threw his second of the afternoon directly to Trent McDuffie, who dropped into zone coverage and made the quarterback pay for never seeing him.

Two plays later, Stafford found Puka Nacua on a quick screen. Nacua followed his blockers, broke a tackle and raced 18 yards into the end zone.

The Rams led 24-9, and the Battle for LA was effectively over.

Two weeks ago, the Rams were 2-4, sitting in last place in the NFC West with their Super Bowl aspirations hanging by a thread.

Now they’re 4-4, winners of two straight, and suddenly have a pulse again. They’ll await Monday night’s Bears-Seahawks result to see how the division standings shake out.

For the Chargers, the nightmare continues. At 0-7, their playoff hopes are essentially on life support, and the approaching NFL trade deadline could force some uncomfortable organizational decisions.

Selling veterans for future draft capital might be more sensible than pretending this season can still be saved.

Herbert’s second interception, his NFL-leading 10th of the season, was the final dagger.

McDuffie read the quarterback perfectly, dropping into coverage and intercepting a pass that Herbert never should have thrown.

Two plays later, Stafford connected with Nacua for an 18-yard touchdown, turning an eight-point advantage into a 24-9 lead the Chargers never threatened.

Did you see that?

The Chargers came within inches of stealing the lead on the final play before halftime.

Herbert’s desperation heave bounced off Johnston and into McConkey’s hands at the goal line, but the Rams stopped him just short.

One more yard could have changed the afternoon entirely.

Instead, the Rams dodged a bullet.

The Rams head to the nation’s capital during election week to face the Washington Commanders, looking to extend their winning streak to three games and climb above .500 for the first time this season.

The Chargers return to SoFi Stadium as the designated home team against the Houston Texans, still searching for their first victory.
