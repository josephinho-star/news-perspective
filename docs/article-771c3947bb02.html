Yankees could be in trouble with catcher market drying up

2026-11-27 01:34

The Yankees can take a marker to their offseason whiteboards.

Cross out Tyler Stephenson, who agreed to return to the Reds. Put an “X” through Gary Sánchez, who will sign with the Marlins. Already erased was Ryan Jeffers, who surprisingly accepted the Twins’ qualifying offer.

Try it freeThe names, while still plentiful in number, grow less intriguing by the day. After spending two seasons without a true righty-hitting option at catcher, the Yankees still are searching for Austin Wells’ complement.

“If we can find a good-defending, right-handed-hitting option to level up, that’d be great,” Yankees general manager Brian Cashman said earlier this month at the GM Meetings, “but that’s also slim pickings.”

The pickings are even slimmer now. The one catcher still on the free-agent market likely to command a multiyear and not-insignificant contract is Carson Kelly. It is likely that the caliber of catcher the Yankees add will depend upon their pursuits of bullpen, center field and third base help. If they land reliever Adrian Morejon, for example, they probably would reach into the bargain bin elsewhere (unless they bring in a catcher through trade).

With less than a week to go before signings and trades are expected to freeze, here are some of the righty-hitting catcher options who are still available through free agency:

Carson Kelly: The best still on the board, Kelly likely expects more playing time and a better contract than the Yankees are willing to give. But a veteran, well-regarded catcher with a lifetime .822 OPS against southpaws would fit well.

Austin Hedges: One of the premier framers in baseball, the 34-year-old always has been defensively excellent but not much of an offensive threat. Last season, he did hit lefties well (.747 OPS).

James McCann: A veteran and former Met who has settled nicely into a part-time/backup role in the twilight of his career, McCann’s defense has slipped, but he has hit decently — although last season, he was stronger against righty pitching.

Kyle Higashioka: Another for the run-it-back Yankees? Higashioka, now 36, has seen his pop time slow, and he caught just seven of 39 attempted base stealers last season, but he remains agile behind the plate and hit lefties well last season before suffering a flexor strain in July.

Elias Díaz: His defense still was solid during his age-35 season with the Royals and Rangers this past year and he posted an .820 OPS against southpaws. Do the Yankees want a slugger with nice pop but poor on-base skills?

And possible trade candidates:

Iván Herrera/Pedro Pagés: The Cardinals are deep at catcher, where Leo Bernal likely will start next season after a strong debut and with top prospect Rainiel Rodríguez on the way. They could afford to part with the bat-first Herrera or glove-first Pagés. Herrera mostly served as DH last season, struggling particularly behind the plate with holding opposing base runners, but he owns an .863 OPS against lefties in his five seasons. Pagés has not hit much in his three years but is a strong defender.

Freddy Fermin/Luis Campusano: After top prospect Ethan Salas got his feet wet last season, the Padres have a surplus of catchers. The 31-year-old Fermin is defensively strong but owns only a .599 OPS over his past two seasons. Campusano’s all-around defense has made strides, particularly his throwing arm, and he is coming off a strong season in which he hit .265 with a .795 OPS.

Agustín Ramírez: With Sánchez landing in Miami as a complement to Joe Mack, would the Marlins shop Ramírez? Maybe not; they could store the former Yankees prospect in the minors and work on his defense, which has been weak. But the 25-year-old slugger, who went from the Yankees to the Marlins in the Jazz Chisholm Jr. trade, has a strong offensive reputation and has posted a .755 OPS against lefties in his two major league seasons.
