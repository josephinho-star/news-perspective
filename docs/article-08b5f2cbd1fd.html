Stock futures rise as traders see whether Nvidia can extend rally from Jensen Huang’s return: Live updates

2026-11-24 07:40

U.S. stock futures were up Tuesday morning after Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO sent the chipmaker's shares soaring and sparked a rebound in the artificial intelligence trade.

Futures tied to the Nasdaq-100 futures were 0.19% higher. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures advanced 65 points, or 0.13%, while S&P 500 futures gained 0.26%.

In Asia, the Taiwan Weighted Index was up over 9%, with key chip stocks TSMC and Hon Hai Precision Industry closing in on their 10% price limits.

South Korea's Kospi jumped over 7% on Tuesday, with heavyweights Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix seeing gains of about 8% and 10% respectively.

Japan's Nikkei 225 also rose 4.05%, also powered by chip related stocks, and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was also up 1.62%.

Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index was down 0.39%, while mainland China's CSI 300 was 0.57% higher in early trade.

Nvidia surged 31% during Monday's regular session, leading a rally in chip stocks. The Nasdaq Composite closed 6.5% higher, the S&P 500 finished up 4.7% and the Dow Industrials rose 3% Monday.

It was the best session for the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 since April 9, 2025 when President Donald Trump announced a reprieve on tariffs.

The tech sector of the S&P 500 soared 9%, powered by sharp gains in semiconductors, memory names and neoclouds. Advanced Micro Devices added almost 7%, while Micron Technology surged 19%. Neocloud stock CoreWeave jumped 22%.

The market action was a sharp reversal from Friday's steep sell-off, which saw the S&P 500 and Dow close in correction as the AI trade suffered and Nvidia tanked.

"The shocking decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO helps to remove a key level of uncertainty from the AI trade, which has been suffering in recent weeks," said Glen Smith, chief investment officer at GDS Wealth Management.

"Stock market corrections can sometimes end as quickly as they begin and we believe the bottom in stocks is likely in," he added.

Looking ahead to Tuesday's session, on the economic front, consumer confidence data is set to be released.
