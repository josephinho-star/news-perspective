UK sanctions firms over Israeli West Bank settlements

2026-11-17 18:27

The UK has sanctioned companies it said are involved in building settlements in the occupied West Bank, in the first package of measures since the government announced a tougher approach to Israeli actions in the territory.

The government said the sanctions against eight construction and property development firms would disrupt their flows of finance and ability to support building in the West Bank.

The construction of settlements, which are illegal under international law, has risen sharply in recent years. Settler attacks on Palestinians and their property have also surged.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar called the sanctions a "hostile act targeting private, law-abiding companies".

Criticising the move on X, he wrote that Israel - as it has done in the past - will respond with "countermeasures", and that "decisions will be announced in due course".

"Israel will not bow to external pressure or change its policies as a result," Saar said, adding that they will "consider new ways to strengthen Jewish communities" in the West Bank, and "protect those involved in developing and building".

Israel previously condemned UK sanctions related to West Bank settlements, accusing the Labour government of "systematically acting against the State of Israel".

The Palestinian foreign ministry welcomed the sanctions and called on other countries to follow suit.

It comes two days before the deadline for bids for construction tenders in the highly sensitive E1 area east of Jerusalem.

The plans, which have been condemned internationally, would in effect divide the West Bank in two and cut it off from East Jerusalem.

UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband has said the E1 development would render a two-state solution "unviable" by "cutting through the heart of Palestine".

The sanctions also target Tzav 9, an Israeli activist movement which the UK government says has organised and carried out blockades to prevent aid entering Gaza.

Responding to the announcement, the group said: "Tzav 9 is a movement of abiding citizens from all the political spectrum, and we've always protested peacefully."

It comes after the UK expanded its human rights sanctions regime last month, enabling the government to sanction individuals and companies involved in violations of international humanitarian law anywhere in the world.

The government is also developing separate legislation to ban the import of goods from Israeli settlements and the provision of advertising services for settlement properties in the UK.

Miliband said: "Protecting the two-state solution is the North Star of this government's approach.

"That is why my message on E1 is clear: do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from the destruction of the two-state solution.

"The status quo in the West Bank and Gaza is unacceptable.

"We are committed to working productively with a new Israeli government and the Palestinian Authority to advance lasting peace and security for Israelis and Palestinians alike."

In September, the UK government announced that it would ban the import of goods from Israeli settlements, as well as acting against companies and individuals providing services for settlement expansion.

At the time, Miliband said the move marked the beginning of a "new approach" to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, which "not only calls out injustice and suffering, but acts".

That announcement prompted a furious reaction from Israel, which said it would shut the British consulate in East Jerusalem in retaliation.

Last month, most diplomats left the consulate - which handled UK relations with Palestinians in Jerusalem as well as in the West Bank and Gaza - but the UK insisted that the building would not be closing and would operate as a "UK mission".

Israel has built about 160 settlements housing 700,000 Jews since it occupied the West Bank and East Jerusalem during the 1967 Middle East war. An estimated 3.3 million Palestinians live alongside them.

There has been a surge in attacks by settlers on Palestinians and their property in the West Bank since the start of the Gaza war, which was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October 2023.
