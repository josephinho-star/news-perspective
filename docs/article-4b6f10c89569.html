UK sanctions firms over Israeli West Bank settlements

2026-11-17 13:10

The UK has sanctioned companies it said were involved in building settlements in the occupied West Bank, in the first package of measures since the government announced a tougher approach to Israeli actions in the territory.

The government said the sanctions against eight construction and property development firms would disrupt their flows of finance and ability to support building in the West Bank.

Settlements are illegal under international law, and there has been a rise in violence towards Palestinians in the West Bank by settlers in recent months.

It comes two days before the deadline for bids for construction tenders in the E1 area east of Jerusalem.

The plans, which have been condemned internationally, would in effect divide the West Bank in two and isolate East Jerusalem.

UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband has said the E1 development would render a two-state solution "unviable" by "cutting through the heart of Palestine".

The sanctions also target Tzav 9, an Israeli activist movement which the UK government says has organised and carried out blockades to prevent aid entering Gaza.

It comes after the UK expanded its human rights sanctions regime last month, enabling the government to sanction individuals and companies involved in violations of international humanitarian law anywhere in the world.

The government is also developing separate legislation to ban the import of settlement goods and the provision of advertising services for settlement properties in the UK.

Miliband said: "Protecting the two-state solution is the North Star of this government's approach.

"That is why my message on E1 is clear: do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from the destruction of the two-state solution.

"The status quo in the West Bank and Gaza is unacceptable.

"We are committed to working productively with a new Israeli government and the Palestinian Authority to advance lasting peace and security for Israelis and Palestinians alike."

In September, the UK government announced that it would ban the import of goods from Israeli settlements, as well as acting against companies and individuals providing services for settlement expansion.

At the time, Miliband said the move marked the "beginning of a new approach" to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, which "not only calls out injustice and suffering but acts".

The announcement prompted a furious reaction from Israel.

Israel has built about 160 settlements housing 700,000 Jews since it occupied the West Bank and East Jerusalem during the 1967 Middle East war. An estimated 3.3 million Palestinians live alongside them.

There has been a surge in attacks by settlers on Palestinians and their property in the West Bank since the start of the Gaza war, which was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October 2023.
