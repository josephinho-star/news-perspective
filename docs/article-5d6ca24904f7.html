Fantasy football: Tight end sleepers who can fuel your playoff push

2026-11-27 23:51

We are right in the thick of the fantasy football playoff push, when every win matters and every point can be the difference between a postseason berth and watching from the sidelines.

With the Thanksgiving games wrapped up, you already know you need every edge you can find, especially if you had to play against Jahmyr Gibbs, Javonte Williams or DeVonta Smith. When top skill players erupt, chasing raw volume elsewhere rarely catches up, which makes streaming and attacking soft defensive coverage at tight end one of the sharpest ways to swing your matchup.

Keep your eye on Commanders tight end Chig Okonkwo as a sneaky upside play who can steal the spotlight. Though everyone else is locked in on Arizona’s Trey McBride and his target volume in this game, Okonkwo has the kind of matchup profile that plays up nicely in a crunch-time lineup.

The Cardinals have struggled mightily against tight ends, ranking 28th in DVOA against the position while surrendering 54.5 receiving yards per game and the ninth-most fantasy points per game to tight ends. If you need a ceiling swing without paying top-of-the-board prices, Okonkwo is squarely in play.

With Juwan Johnson (hamstring) out, Saints tight end Noah Fant is another prime target, with a revenge-game narrative against his former Bengals squad adding extra intrigue — provided his calf cooperates. Cincinnati ranks 30th in DVOA against tight ends, giving up an average of 80.4 yards per game and the eighth-most fantasy points per game to the position. Should Fant also sit out, Oscar Delp steps into immediate fantasy relevance.

If those routes are blocked or you just need a steady pivot, look to Texans tight end Dalton Schultz. An illness limited Schultz to a partial session Thursday, though he is trending toward being good to go for Sunday. You should not bank on massive yardage totals, yet Houston draws a Ravens defense sitting last in DVOA against tight ends.

Baltimore allows roughly 55 receiving yards per game to the position. But the real key to playing against the Ravens is that they have given up seven touchdowns to tight ends across 11 games this season, making Schultz a viable TD-hunting option.
