Turkey detains 15, freezes assets in fund probe

2026-09-21 14:24

Turkish police detained 15 people on Monday and were seeking 10 others over alleged manipulation of shares in Katılımevim, which provides interest-free financing plans for home and vehicle purchases, while prosecutors also restricted transactions involving executives and officials linked to nine financial companies.

The İstanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office opened proceedings against 25 people after Turkey’s Capital Markets Board (SPK) found signs of suspected market manipulation in Katılımevim shares, Justice Minister Akın Gürlek announced.

The latest operation is part of an investigation into fund managers and investment companies that began after some funds failed to meet investor withdrawal requests and selling spread to Turkey’s main stock index last week.

Prosecutors froze asset transfers and other financial transactions by executives and officials at companies tied to the Pusula, Tera, Hedef and Bulls financial groups, along with investment and real estate company Ufuk Yatırım.

Banks, notaries and public agencies responsible for land, ships and aircraft were instructed to prevent the transfer or disposal of assets under investigation without the prosecutor’s approval.

The restriction also covers transactions that could reduce the assets of board members, authorized signatories, their spouses and first-degree relatives.

Prosecutors asked Turkey’s Financial Crimes Investigation Board (MASAK) to examine bank and cryptocurrency transactions made since 2024 by managers of Pusula, Tera, Hedef, Bulls and their subsidiaries.

The review covers the managers’ relatives to determine whether money or cryptocurrency was transferred abroad through their accounts.

Investigators found that Pusula Holding Chairman Serdar Turhan transferred $15 million to a Swiss account on August 24, while its subsidiary Pusula Portföy’s Chairman Muhammed Yarız transferred 25 million euros to an account at Edmond de Rothschild Bank in Switzerland on September 1, according to reports citing a MASAK review.

The transfers took place before funds began missing investor repayment deadlines on September 15 and 16.

Turhan and Tera Chairman Emre Tezmen were among executives taken into custody in earlier operations, along with Tera managers Alper Öztürk, Emre Alkin and Kerem Alkin.

Police later detained Tera Yatırım board members Bülent Uygun and Abdulkadir Özkan along with Doğa Sigorta Chairman Nihat Kırmızı.

Four people have been jailed pending trial in the investigation, while courts have barred 51 people from leaving Turkey.

The SPK in a decision published Monday extended the maximum period for liquidating 131 investment funds from three months to six months, citing their portfolio structures and market conditions.

The regulator said the process could finish earlier and that the rest of the liquidation rules had not changed.

The regulator had halted purchases and redemptions in funds managed by Tera, Pusula, Hedef, Atlas, A1 Capital, Pardus and Bulls and ordered them wound down after the withdrawal crisis.

The funds managed about 891 billion lira ($18.3 billion at current exchange rates), and had about 353,000 investors when the intervention began.

İşbank is overseeing the liquidation of six Tera funds, while state-owned Ziraat Bank is handling the other 125 funds.
