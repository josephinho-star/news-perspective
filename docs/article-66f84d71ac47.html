Ministers ‘fail to make case’ for Heathrow third runway, MPs report says

2026-11-26 11:00

Ministers have “failed to make the case” for a third runway at Heathrow, according to a damning report by MPs.

The Commons transport committee said on Thursday that the detailed policy papers laid out by the government showed that expanding the London airport would fall short of pledges on the economy and climate.

The chair of the committee, Ruth Cadbury, said plans for the third runway “should be grounded” and the government needed to “go back to the drawing board”.

Heathrow is seeking to build a third runway and expand its terminals at a cost of about £33bn to almost double passenger numbers, adding up to 276,000 flights a year.

The committee was tasked with examining the government’s Heathrow expansion national policy statement (HENPS) – but Cadbury there was a “complete disjoint” between the plans and a lack of wider aviation strategy.

The report found that the case was not made across the four tests ministers had set for expansion – that it deliver growth across the country, without harmful impacts on climate, noise and air quality.

The report said that while the government was designating the runway as a critical national project, the economic growth projections in modelling commissioned by the Department for Transport were a fraction of previous estimates – a 0.05% boost to GDP rather than o.5% – and did not justify the designation.

It said that expansion was “not currently compatible with the UK’s legally binding climate targets, and it seems impossible for the additional carbon dioxide emissions it will generate to be offset elsewhere in the economy”.

MPs said that the case for “potential compatibility rests too heavily on delivery of technologies” such as greener fuel and carbon capture which was “nowhere near the necessary trajectories”.

The committee said it “did not agree that reversing hard-won improvements in local air quality is an acceptable consequence” and the statement did not take account of World Health Organization guidelines or emerging pollutants.

It added that the government still needed to set out how commitments relating to noise mitigation were “secured and delivered”.

The report also said it was “highly concerning that there are no clear, committed, funded plans in place to improve surface public transport to an expanded Heathrow”, concluding: “Expansion should not go ahead at any price, or at any cost to society or the environment.”

Cadbury said: “Our report should be a wake-up call for ministers. We found that the government should have set out its overall strategy for aviation before publishing the draft HENPS. It did not do so and now it has failed to make the case for Heathrow expansion.

“Plans for a third runway should be grounded for now and the government should go back to the drawing board before proceeding any further.”

The government under Keir Starmer had strongly backed a third runway, particularly the then chancellor, Rachel Reeves. However, Andy Burnham has historically opposed the third runway and the scheme appears in doubt.

Responding to the committee’s report, Heathrow’s chief executive, Thomas Woldbye, said the third runway still had strong support.

“The overwhelming weight of evidence and support remains strongly behind Heathrow expansion. Trade unions, business groups, airlines, regional airports, senior cabinet ministers and the majority of MPs back expansion because they recognise it is vital to creating quality jobs and supporting long-term economic growth across the UK,” Woldbye said.

Heathrow on Wednesday released polling showing three-quarters of Labour MPs backed the expansion plan.
