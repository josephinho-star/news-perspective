Erdoğan, Aliyev vow unity after tensions between allies

2026-11-09 09:44

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and Azerbaijani President Ilham Aliyev pledged Sunday to preserve their countries’ alliance, presenting a united front after a public dispute over headscarf restrictions in Azerbaijani schools.

Speaking in the Azerbaijani city of Shusha, the leaders stressed their shared history and enduring ties following weeks of friction between the close allies.

The dispute followed Azerbaijan’s adoption in September of school uniform regulations prohibiting clothing deemed incompatible with secular education. Although the rules do not explicitly mention Islamic headscarves, schools have used them to bar girls wearing hijabs from classrooms.

The restrictions sparked protests in Azerbaijan and criticism from figures in Erdoğan’s Islamist-rooted Justice and Development Party (AKP). His government has lifted longstanding headscarf restrictions in Turkey.

Presidential adviser Oktay Saral demanded that Azerbaijan reverse the restrictions. Aliyev’s ruling New Azerbaijan Party condemned the comments as interference in the country’s internal affairs.

Baku also barred 31 Turkish nationals, including opposition politicians, from entering the country over social media posts. Azerbaijan’s Foreign Ministry accused them of making statements targeting the country’s sovereignty and constitutional order and inciting religious hatred.

Saral was subsequently removed from his post, but Aliyev did not attend an October 30 summit of the Organization of Turkic States in Ankara despite a personal invitation from Erdoğan. Foreign Minister Jeyhun Bayramov attended instead.

Erdoğan arrived at Fuzuli International Airport on Sunday as Azerbaijan marked Victory Day, commemorating its capture of Shusha during the 2020 war with Armenia.

Aliyev and his wife, Mehriban Aliyeva, welcomed Erdoğan and his wife, Emine, at the airport, where a guard of honor was assembled.

“With the permission of Almighty God, our unity will endure forever,” Erdoğan said during joint statements to the press.

“This unity of hearts, which we describe with the motto ‘One Nation, Two States,’ is an everlasting torch that draws strength from our shared past and illuminates our future.”

Aliyev said the alliance — “an important factor across a vast geographical area” — remained unshaken.

“Together, Azerbaijan and Turkey are even stronger,” he said, adding that Erdoğan’s visit “shows the whole world our unity.”

“I am confident that this unity will last forever.”

Analysts say the dispute reflects deeper differences over Baku’s diplomatic independence and its close ties to Israel.

“Azerbaijan-Turkey tensions stemmed from the fact that Baku does not see itself as the younger brother in this alliance and insists on relations between equal partners,” Elkhan Shahinoglu, head of the Baku-based Atlas Research Centre, told Agence France-Presse.

“The hijab dispute is a symptom of a deeper rift,” Gönül Tol, an analyst at the Washington-based Middle East Institute, wrote on X.

“Tensions have long simmered over a number of issues including Azerbaijan’s sovereignty, Ankara’s influence in Baku, diverging policies toward Israel, and Turkey’s rapprochement with Armenia.”

Azerbaijan maintains strong military and economic ties with Israel, which supplied it with weapons during the 2020 war over Nagorno-Karabakh.

Those ties contrast with Ankara’s increasingly confrontational stance toward Israel over the Gaza war. Erdoğan has accused Israel of genocide.

Ankara is also seeking to normalize relations with Armenia, having closed the land border in 1993 in solidarity with Azerbaijan. Turkish officials have linked progress toward normalization to Armenian-Azerbaijani peace efforts.

Azerbaijan celebrates Victory Day on November 8, marking its capture of Shusha in 2020 with Turkish backing.

The Turkic-speaking allies describe their relationship as “one nation, two states” and remain closely bound by military and energy ties despite the recent friction.

Turkey is a key transit route for Azerbaijani oil and gas exports to Western markets.

Azerbaijan is a secular, Muslim-majority former Soviet republic with a predominantly Shiite population.
