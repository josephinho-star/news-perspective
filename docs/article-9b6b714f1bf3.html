Socialist newcomers’ old words take new turn as they prepare to enter Congress

2026-11-28 11:00

As January approaches and newly elected lawmakers prepare to take their oaths of office, past statements could prove troublesome as they jockey for committee assignments with powerbrokers seeking to protect the image of their respective parties.

The midterm elections sent a new crop of lawmakers affiliated with the Democratic Socialists of America to Congress — in a few cases, unexpectedly. While some sought to moderate their image after the primary season, controversial remarks made on the campaign trail or years before their candidacies could follow them to Capitol Hill as they seek committee assignments and influence within the Democratic caucus.

Fox News Digital reviewed some of the most controversial remarks made by the incoming lawmakers — comments that could complicate their efforts to build influence in the new Congress.

Darializa Avila Chevalier: ‘Defund and abolish’

In an early test of his political sway, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani endorsed a duo of DSA-aligned Democrats in primaries for congressional districts encompassing portions of four of the city’s five boroughs.

Darializa Avila Chevalier, a left-wing graduate student at the City University of New York, was one such candidate. She defeated longtime Rep. Adriano Espaillat, chairman of the Congressional Hispanic Caucus, by a relatively small margin during a June primary contest. Before and after Chevalier’s primary victory, scores of controversial deleted social media posts made by the socialist hopeful garnered national media attention.

"No. It means ending policing full stop. Period. No more police at all ever," she responded to one user on social media in 2020 who asked her if abolishing the police only entailed getting rid of law enforcement "as we know it."

"F**k you. We’re gonna defund and abolish. You don’t get to water down our movements," she said in another post that year.

Though once in vogue, calls to defund or abolish the police have since become politically toxic, something House Democratic leadership will likely have in mind when determining what to do with Chevalier once she shows up in D.C.

Chevalier has also signaled an affinity for ideas outside the normal range of American political discourse.

"Seize the means of production," she wrote in 2019.

"The pyromania associated with anarchism is very intriguing to me," wrote Chevalier about a year later.

The socialist newcomer has also signaled a hostility to the Democratic establishment, an institution that now holds considerable sway over her political future.

"I have no nuance to add. F*ck Kamala Harris," Chevalier wrote in response to the then-vice president telling Central American migrants not to come to the United States illegally in 2021. She also reposted content criticizing the Democratic National Committee and other mainstream liberal institutions from the left.

In response to criticism, Chevalier has stated those posts no longer reflect who she is and that she has grown considerably since writing them.

Melat Kiros: ‘Inevitable’

Elected to represent Colorado’s First Congressional District as a Democrat, Melat Kiros, a socialist, has attracted criticism for her attempts to explain — and, in the telling of critics, justify — terrorist attacks.

In a June interview, speaking on the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks, Kiros stated that they were "inevitable in the sense that we destabilized a lot of the Middle East." On the Oct. 7 terrorist attacks in Israel, she told Twitch streamer Hasan Piker that they too were "an inevitable consequence of apartheid, of occupation, decades of occupation."

Calling an attack inevitable, however, does not necessarily equate to endorsing the attack itself.

Kiros, who was born in Ethiopia, has also accused Israel of genocide and called for the abolition of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) — both hotly debated positions within the Democratic Party.

Chris Rabb: ‘Lock those f***ers up’

Much like Chevalier, Chris Rabb is a DSA member who has railed against law enforcement and endorsed economic ideas considered by some to be radical.

"Would you like to eliminate cash bail and establish a wealth tax?" Fox News host Sean Hannity asked Rabb during an August interview.

"Absolutely," answered Rabb.

"Would you like to abolish prisons and abolish the police?" Hannity followed up.

"Well, what’s interesting about that statement is, long before there was any notion of policing, we had founders that did not even understand that term," Rabb said. "Policing is something that’s evolved over time. Ultimately, what we want is to have communities that are safe. And 99% of what police do [is] not going after the bad guys, all of that can be done in different ways by professionals who are trained to do so."

While Rabb generally supports reducing the number of criminals in prisons, calling America’s high incarceration rate "morally wrong," he makes one notable exception.

"But I will make an exception for all of those f***ers who are lawless MAGA thugs who have destroyed lives and communities," Rabb said on an October episode of Wajahat Ali’s podcast. "Lock all of those f***ers up. I know that seems a little hypocritical of me being on the left and being anti-incarceration, but lock those f***ers up."

Claire Valdez: ‘Militarized terrorist organization’

Elected alongside Chevalier on Mamdani’s New York City DSA congressional slate, Rep.-elect Claire Valdez has her share of controversial comments.

In January, for instance, she called ICE a "militarized terrorist organization that is destroying our country" and endorsed calls to disband the agency.

Valdez has also called for subjecting one of the largest industries in the United States to public ownership.

"We need to, like, nationalize the airline industry, but let’s start by abolishing PreCheck," she said during a May appearance on the B*tchuation Room podcast.

Fox News Digital reached Chevalier, Kiros, Rabb and Valdez with requests for comment on Tuesday but has received no responses as of publication.
