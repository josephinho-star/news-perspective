Sacramento mayor fined $29K for using campaign cash to pay for jewelry, meals in Hawaii and family’s phone bills

2026-11-21 01:35

Hawaii meals, Nordstrom shopping sprees and even the family cellphone bill — all allegedly paid for with campaign cash.

Now Sacramento’s Democratic mayor, Kevin McCarty, has agreed to pay $29,500 to settle a slew of campaign finance violations that also involved missing receipts, a mass mailer and two paintings that followed him from the state Assembly to City Hall.

The California Fair Political Practices Commission laid out eight counts in a proposed settlement involving two of McCarty’s former Assembly campaign committees and his 2024 mayoral committee. McCarty said he has already paid the fine.

The costliest violation involved AT&T. From January 2019 through 2022, more than $10,000 in campaign funds went toward buying or leasing cellphones and an iPad under McCarty’s name, according to the proposed settlement.

More than $2,100 covered cellphone expenses for his wife and children. The penalty alone came to $10,000.

Then there were the campaign credit cards.

Regulators flagged 11 purchases totaling nearly $4,000, including jewelry and clothing from Nordstrom, meals at Roasted Chiles in Hawaii and Lazy Dog, and Home Depot charges for a flooring measurement, paint and supplies. The spending drew a $5,000 penalty.

Other charges were reimbursed, but the commission found that at least $111.86 in Home Depot expenses was inaccurately reported and never paid back.

“I take responsibility for the mistakes made in my campaign’s financial reporting and compliance,” McCarty told The California Post. “These were errors, not intentional efforts to mislead anyone or misuse campaign funds. The expenses in question were reimbursed in a timely fashion, and the fine has been paid.”

McCarty said he cooperated with investigators and took steps to prevent a repeat.

The missing paperwork carried a hefty price of its own. McCarty’s Assembly committees racked up at least $70,000 across 179 expenditures between December 2018 and July 2022 without keeping the required receipts and invoices. The payments covered airfare, sporting events and purchases from Apple, Russian River Brewing and a nursery, among other vendors.

Most of those expenses appeared on campaign statements, but the committees were required to keep the supporting records for four years. The violations drew $9,000 in penalties. James Santos, the former Assembly campaign treasurer, was also fined in connection with four of the eight counts.

Even the art on the mayor’s walls made it into the settlement. McCarty’s Assembly campaign spent $5,500 on two David Garibaldi paintings that he later moved into his mayoral office. The campaigns failed to report the transfer, triggering a $1,500 penalty.

A reelection mailer sent to about 60,000 voters accounted for the remaining $4,000. McCarty’s Assembly campaign spent at least $32,000 on the mailing, which listed a consultant’s address and omitted the required disclosure identifying the committee that paid for it.

The settlement news came days after a separate blowup at City Hall. Fox News reported that a woman confronted McCarty during public comment Tuesday amid tensions over the city’s immigration policies.

“It’s time for your f—ing career to end,” she told him, according to the outlet.

“Do you want everyone to know your address?” she added. Her microphone appeared to cut out as she began reciting what she claimed was his home address. Officials called a recess as shouting erupted in the chamber.

The council resumed public comment about 20 minutes later. Earlier in the meeting, McCarty had announced that the council would postpone consideration of a $3.7 million Department of Homeland Security grant agreement amid questions about potential data sharing.
