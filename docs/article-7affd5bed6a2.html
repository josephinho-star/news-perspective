Police crackdown on Beşiktaş fans sparks outrage, tourism boycott of Georgia

2026-11-27 14:36

Georgian police have sparked widespread condemnation in Turkey over their treatment of Beşiktaş supporters who displayed Palestinian flags and banners during a Europa League match against an Israeli team on Thursday, with opposition politicians denouncing the violence, rival football clubs expressing solidarity and Turkish travel agencies canceling tours to Georgia in protest.

All 16 Beşiktaş supporters detained in Georgia’s Black Sea city of Batumi were released Friday following diplomatic and legal efforts undertaken on the instructions of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, the state-run Anadolu news agency reported.

Thursday’s match between Beşiktaş and Israel’s Hapoel Be’er-Sheva was suspended for approximately 30 minutes after Georgian police intervened in the stands, using pepper spray against supporters. Beşiktaş went on to win the match 2-1.

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan held talks with his Georgian counterpart, Maka Botchorishvili, while the Turkish Football Federation (TFF) condemned the treatment of Beşiktaş supporters inside and outside the stadium.

Özgür Özel, leader of Turkey’s main opposition New Party, said on X that the detention of Beşiktaş supporters who had unfurled a banner in support of Palestine was “unacceptable.”

“Showing solidarity with the Palestinian people and raising one’s voice against oppression is not a crime; on the contrary, it is a human duty,” Özel added.

He called on Turkish authorities to take diplomatic action to secure the supporters’ immediate release and hold those responsible for what he described as an unlawful intervention to account.

Müsavat Dervişoğlu, leader of the nationalist opposition İYİ (Good) Party, described the police violence against Beşiktaş supporters who had traveled to Batumi after their team was forced to play a home match abroad as “disgraceful,” calling on the Turkish Foreign Ministry to urgently intervene.

Several Turkish football clubs, including Beşiktaş’s İstanbul rivals Galatasaray and Fenerbahçe as well as Trabzonspor and İstanbul Başakşehir, also condemned the police intervention and expressed solidarity with the club and its supporters, with Fenerbahçe urging UEFA to reconsider its decision to relocate the match.

UEFA announced in October that the match would be relocated to Georgia after Turkish authorities informed European football’s governing body that the fixture could not be held in İstanbul due to security concerns.

The relocation was not unprecedented. In November 2024 Beşiktaş played a Europa League home match against Israeli club Maccabi Tel Aviv behind closed doors in Hungary amid heightened tensions between Turkey and Israel over the war in Gaza.

Turkish travel agencies boycott Georgia

Several Turkish travel agencies announced the cancellation or suspension of tours to Georgia in protest, including trips planned for the busy New Year’s holiday period.

Mahmut Arslan of Vaveyla Turizm, based in the northeastern Turkish province of Rize, said his company had decided to suspend all tours to Georgia despite strong demand for New Year’s trips, the Türkiye daily reported Friday.

Gezi Trabzon Turizm announced the suspension of its Batumi tours until further notice, citing violence against Turkish citizens and concerns over passenger safety, the local Haber61 news website reported.

Hüseyin Yalçın, owner of Gözdem Burak Tur in the Black Sea province of Zonguldak, told the İhlas news agency (İHA) that his company would not organize further tours to Georgia until an apology was issued.

The Güncel Antalya news website reported Friday that Liatris Travel had canceled all existing and planned tours to Georgia, while Üni Genç Turizm and Gezi Noktası had canceled their New Year’s trips to Batumi.

The backlash extended beyond the tourism sector, with the Rize Chamber of Commerce and Industry announcing its withdrawal from a trade fair scheduled to take place in Batumi on December 4-6, İHA reported.

Mehmet Ali Tuna, the eastern Black Sea regional chairman of the Association of Turkish Travel Agencies (TÜRSAB), told Haber61 on Friday morning that the association had not received formal notification of any tour suspensions, urging restraint to preserve relations between the neighboring countries.

Georgia’s Interior Ministry denied that the police intervention was linked to the display of Palestinian flags and banners, alleging that some supporters had engaged in violence even before reaching the stadium.

UEFA said it understood that police had intervened to break up clashes among Beşiktaş supporters themselves and that stadium security was the responsibility of local authorities.
