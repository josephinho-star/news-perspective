'Enough': Protesters decry antisemitism after Hunter College student event including Jewish group is canceled

2026-11-21 01:04

A crowd gathered Thursday outside Hunter College to protest after the school's student government seemingly canceled an event due to the participation of a Jewish organization.

On one side of the street, pro-Israel protesters chanted "enough is enough" and waved American and Israeli flags as speakers addressed the crowd. Across the street, a group of counter-protesters gathered waving Palestinian flags while yelling chants of their own.

Passersby also shouted, some in support and others yelling "Free Palestine" and "F--- Israel."

Organizers behind the Stand with Jewish Students at Hunter rally included the Israeli American Council (IAC), Students Supporting Israel (SSI) and #EndJewHatred.

On flyers advertising the rally, they criticized the college's administration for not doing enough in response to what they saw as an act of antisemitism.

"We're saying enough to the marginalization of the Jewish community. We're saying enough is enough to discrimination against Jewish students. And we're saying enough is enough to this obsessive hatred of all of the symbols of Jewish life, whether it's Hillel on campus or the State of Israel," IAC CEO Elan Carr told Fox News Digital, referring to a chapter of the Jewish student group.

Michelle Ahdoot, director of programming and strategy at #EndJewHatred, told Fox News Digital the cancellation of the event demonstrated a problem that went beyond Hunter College.

"When a multicultural event is canceled simply because Jewish students are included, we have a serious problem that goes far beyond one event at Hunter College," Ahdoot said. "The very purpose of multiculturalism is to celebrate diversity and inclusion, not to allow certain groups to decide who is welcome and who is not.

"Any student organization has the right to choose not to participate," he continued. "But when an elected student government responds by canceling the entire event rather than standing up for the inclusion of Jewish students, it sends a dangerous message that Jewish participation is somehow unacceptable."

The Cultural Arts Night was scheduled to take place Nov. 16 but was canceled just days before. Hunter College Undergraduate Student Government (USG) flyers promoting the event showed that clubs involved included Students for Justice in Palestine, the Peruvian Student Association, Hunter Chinese Student & Scholar Association, the Taiwanese Student Association and Hunter Hillel, according to screenshots posted online.

Hillel International is a Jewish organization that can be found on college campuses across the globe. It describes itself as "a space for all kinds of Jewish students — a place where they feel welcomed and included." While its vision includes helping students make "an enduring commitment to Jewish life, learning and Israel," it is focused on Jewish life and culture, hosting holidays and holding events.

Rita Levine Rabin Hillel at Hunter College said in a statement on Instagram it was "looking forward" to the event and that it "had no role in the decision to cancel."

"Antisemitism has no place at Hunter College. Jewish students should never be singled out, scapegoated, or made to feel unwelcome because of who they are and the anti-Semitic response online to the cancellation notice has been egregious and inappropriate," the statement read.

Ilya Bratman, who serves as executive director at Hillel at Baruch College in New York City, said the cancellation of Cultural Arts Night was a clear case of antisemitism and a sign that things could escalate.

"This is so obvious, and antisemitism has nothing to do with Israel. And that's what the main thing you have to say very loudly. This is about Jew hatred and exclusion," Bratman told Fox News Digital. "It's the first step in the process, which begins with physical exclusion of Jewish students from campuses.

"And that is what happened in the 1930s. We know this. This is a slower process here in American campuses, and I believe this is going to keep going if we don't step up in a very serious way to stop it."

Hunter College USG announced the cancellation on Nov. 14 in a statement from Hunter College USG President Sumaiya Ahmed and Vice President Ahmad Waraich. The two student leaders claimed that "lapses in communication kept the details of this event from reaching the people at the top."

"We hear what the student body has said, and we have made the decision to cancel the event as it doesn’t align with our values or the values of the students we represent as the Hunter Community. We apologize to the clubs and students who were affected by this instance," a statement posted on Instagram read.

After the announcement, speculation about the reason for the event's cancellation grew, with some pointing to pressure from anti-Israel activists who objected to Hillel's involvement. While this was not directly addressed, Hunter USG appeared to deny it in a subsequent statement in which it said that the event was canceled over "safety concerns."

Hunter College is a public institution in the City University of New York (CUNY) system, making allegations of discrimination particularly serious since public schools are legally barred from discriminating based on religion or national origin.

Hunter College told Fox News Digital it was "deeply troubled by reports that some student groups pulled from the event because of Hillel’s participation" and said it was investigating the issue.

"There is no place for discrimination and harassment in our college community. We have policies in place to safeguard that principle and will not hesitate to enforce them," the college said in a statement provided to Fox News Digital.

Michal Mari, who is president of SSI at Hunter College, told Fox News Digital during the rally that the environment at the school has been "tough" for Jewish students. She said Jewish students had seen calls for violence against their community, including statements calling to "bring the war home" and "globalize the intifada."

Mari believes the cancellation came after clubs dropped out of the event due to Hillel's participation, which she said was "blatant discrimination against Jewish students on campus."

"I've had professors turn their backs to me while I was speaking to them because they found out that I was wearing a Star of David or that I've been to Israel," Mari told Fox News Digital. "So, it's certainly not the most divided, but it's a place where there's work to be done."

Mari addressed the crowd at Thursday night's rally along with a number of other pro-Israel and Jewish speakers, including Shai Davidai, Ari Ackerman, Shabbos Kestenbaum and Rami Matan Even-Esh (also known as Kosha Dillz), among others, including Hunter College students.

Jayne Zirkle, who serves as director of communications for #EndJewHatred, said the organization was "incredibly proud" of the students who chose to speak publicly despite the pressures they face on campus.

"Their courage matters, and they deserve to know they are not standing alone," Zirkle said.

Davidai, a former Columbia University professor, told Fox News Digital he attended the rally to bring attention to what he saw as the "symptom" of a bigger issue.

"The symptom of canceling an event because a Jewish organization is part of it, that is a problem. And, by the way, it's a problem not just for Jews, it's a problem for everyone. Because when you cancel an event, everyone, Jewish or non-Jews, don't get to go to that event," Davidai said.

While Davidai focused on the broader cultural fallout on campus, Kestenbaum, a former Harvard Divinity School student who sued Harvard over campus antisemitism, framed the issue around civil rights law and public funding.

"You cannot violate civil rights law. Colleges don't get to pick and choose which statute of the Civil Rights Act they want to subscribe to. There is one standard for all universities across the country, and if those universities insist on discriminating against students because of their ethnicity or because of their religious identity, that is illegal," Kestenbaum told Fox News Digital.

Kestenbaum is not the only one who has brought up the possible legal issues with canceling the event. New York City Council Member Eric Dinowitz publicly called for a civil rights investigation.

"[Hunter College] USG cancels a multicultural arts event because it did not align with its 'values.' It appears to be yet another chapter in the troubling history of antisemitism at CUNY and Hunter," Dinowitz wrote on X. "That's why [CUNY] must immediately launch a Title VI investigation."

Along with calls for institutional and legal accountability, speakers at Thursday night's rally encouraged students at Hunter College not to retreat from campus life and stay silent.

"Every student at Hunter or anywhere else, if you speak up, and when you speak up, we've got your back," Davidai said in an interview with Fox News Digital.

"We've got your back with numbers. We've got your back with legal support. We got your back on social media. We have got your backs. But it's up to you to speak up, because if you don't speak up then, you know, the word doesn't come out."

Mari echoed the message, saying that the Jewish community at Hunter College was ready to rally behind those who choose to speak out.

"Courage isn't lack of fear but acting in the face of fear," Mari told Fox News Digital. "You have a community behind you. The Jewish community, especially, is one that is always behind you."

Hunter USG and Hunter Hillel did not immediately respond to Fox News Digital's requests for comment.
