Israeli opposition bloc gathers to see how to oust Netanyahu

2026-11-03 12:34

Leader of the Yashar party and former Israeli military chief, Gadi Eisenkot, greets his supporters as they celebrate the first election results, Tel Aviv, 27 October 2026.

The Israeli opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot gathered on Nov 3 for the first time since elections as they strategies how to oust Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, with divisions on whether to ally with Arab lawmakers.

Opposition party heads gathered in Herzliya, the tech industry hub north of Tel Aviv where former military chief Eisenkot lives, a week after the election produced an inconclusive result.

Yair Lapid, a centrist former prime minister who headed the opposition in the outgoing parliament, said as he entered that Netanyahu was backing an “extreme and dangerous attempt to invalidate the elections.”

“What we need to do now is go and form a government, because the conduct of recent days is just a reminder that we must remove Netanyahu and his people from the Prime Minister’s Office,” Lapid told reporters.

“They have already caused enough damage to the State of Israel and to the people of Israel,” he said.

Netanyahu and his allies have repeatedly alleged major fraud or malfeasance by Arab-majority parties which made historic gains last week, winning 19 seats in the 120-member parliament on the back of anger over the hard-right government’s rhetoric towards Arab citizens of Israel.

No side won a majority. Eisenkot’s diverse opposition bloc won 48 seats while Netanyahu’s camp secured 53, of which 27 are from his Likud party.

The Arab parties could bring Eisenkot to a majority, but the opposition is divided on whether to ally with them, and Arab politicians did not join the talks in Herzliya.

Despite their diverging views on various issues, including the role of Arab lawmakers, all the party leaders in Eisenkot’s bloc have said they would back him to form the next government.

Public opinion polls conducted before the election showed that Jewish Israeli opposition supporters are divided over including Arab parties in government while right-wing Israelis are adamantly opposed.

Yousef Jabareen, the head of the largest Arab-majority party in the next parliament, told AFP that the opposition needed to accept “political reality” and team up to remove Netanyahu.

Arab-Israelis are descendants of Palestinians who remained after Israel’s creation in 1948 and account for around 20 percent of the population today.

On the campaign trail, most of the main Jewish opposition parties rejected partnering with the Arab-majority lists citing a lack of common values, particularly around security and national identity.
