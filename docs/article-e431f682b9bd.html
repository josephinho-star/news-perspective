JD Vance promises Iran won’t turn into ‘forever war’ — and Trump will end it by 2028

2026-11-16 21:06

WASHINGTON — Vice President JD Vance promised the conflict between the US and Iran launched by President Trump earlier this year won’t turn into a “forever war.”

In an interview with Fox News’ Laura Ingraham, Vance said the president was “not going to let this become a forever war” and would, in fact, wrap it up well before they both leave office.

“We told the American people we would do no more forever wars,” he said in an early clip from an interview on “The Ingraham Angle” that will air Monday night.

“Yes, the president felt he had to destroy the nuclear program. And yes, the Iranian nuclear program is a threat to the United States of America,” he added.

“But we are not going to allow this to become a 10- or 15-year commitment, or frankly even a two-year commitment — despite the fact that a lot of neoconservatives and a lot of other people want us to stay there forever.”

Trump launched joint strikes with Israel against Iran on Feb. 28, decapitating members of its leadership — including Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei — obliterating its nuclear facilities and destroying much of its ballistic missile stockpiles and drone capacity.

Iran retaliated with strikes on US military bases and other Gulf allies in the region, as well as by wreaking havoc on commercial shipping vessels in the Strait of Hormuz.

A US naval blockade has since restored commercial passage for non-Iranian ships passing through the critical chokepoint, through which roughly one-fifth of the world’s seaborne oil flows.

Vance was the chief architect of a 14-point Memorandum of Understanding negotiated with the Iranians in June 2026 that collapsed in July — after Tehran broke a shaky cease-fire with further attacks on ships.

The vice president pledged in his speech accepting the Republican Party’s nomination in July 2024 that he and Trump would “send our kids to war only when we must.”

Trump raised his standing in the party during the 2016 primaries by harshly criticizing supporters of the US war in Iraq and has frequently positioned himself as the only president in modern times to not preside over “endless wars.”

In debating the war’s influence on the 2026 midterms, Trump and Vance have offered slightly differing assessments — with the president acknowledging the war partially led to Democrats’ sweep of both chambers of Congress and the vice president claiming it was the primary reason.
