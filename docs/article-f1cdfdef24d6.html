It’s been 40 years, and we still haven’t replicated this New York football magic

2026-11-21 23:20

Forty years ago Saturday — Nov. 22, 1986 — the New York City sports pages looked and read like a dream come true. This was an era of good feeling anyway: The Mets were less than a month into their World Series reign. The Knicks were losing, but Patrick Ewing was young, had two healthy knees and was starting to prove he was equal to — and often beyond — his billing.

It was a good time to be a New York sports fan.

And it was a great time to be a New York football fan. The Giants were 9-2 and fresh off a miracle win in Minneapolis when Phil Simms famously connected with Bobby Johnson on fourth-and-17 late in the game, setting up a Raul Allegre game-winner. We didn’t know it yet, but the Giants were done losing for the season. On Nov. 23, 1986, they would outlast the Broncos, 19-16, in a preview of Super Bowl 21, to keep pace (for the moment) with Washington atop the NFC East.

And in the AFC, all by themselves at the top of the East and the top of the conference, sat the Jets, 10-1, riding a nine-game winning streak and looking unbeatable. Their previous three games had gone this way: 38-7 at Seattle, 28-14 at Atlanta, 31-16 against Indianapolis at the Meadowlands. Even in the Jets’ most glorious years, 1968-69, they’d never been a wagon.
