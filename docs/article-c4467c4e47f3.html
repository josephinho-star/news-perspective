California companies owned by Chinese firms are scooping up Nvidia’s most advanced AI chips: report

2026-11-26 20:41

At least two California companies controlled by Chinese-owned firms are buying Nvidia’s most advanced chips through the company’s partner program, CBS News reported.

Some of those chips were later leased or sold to companies in Southeast Asia, according to the report. The findings raise new questions about whether U.S. export controls meant to keep the technology out of China are working.

Nvidia, which made nearly $20 billion in sales to China last year, can sell lower-powered chips there without a license. Its top-end chips, including the Blackwell line used to build advanced AI models, are under strict export controls.

One of the companies is RiCloud AI, a San Jose data center firm listed as one of Nvidia’s “preferred partners.”

That status gives it priority access to the company’s high-end chips, software and technical support. RiCloud got partner status in December 2025, about 10 weeks after it was incorporated, CBS reported.

RiCloud’s parent company, Yuxing InfoTech Investment Holdings, is headquartered in Shenzhen, China, and listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange. Yuxing CEO Cong Yu has a degree from the People’s Liberation Army Command College, a university run by the Chinese military, according to the report.

Yuxing chairman Li Qiang previously chaired data firm Global Switch Holdings. Australia’s Department of Defence dropped Global Switch as a data center provider in June 2024 over national security concerns.

This year, RiCloud signed a $120 million deal to lease 300 Nvidia servers to Agencia Comercial Spirits, a Taiwanese company that until recently mainly imported whisky. Agencia had $609,000 in net income in 2025 when it listed on the Nasdaq as an AI company. Since Li became its chairman, it has committed to between $488 million and $598 million in data center deals, CBS reported.

The second company, Aivres, is listed as an “elite” partner, Nvidia’s highest tier.

Aivres was previously named Inspur, after its Chinese parent company. It changed its name after Inspur firms in China were added to a federal blacklist in March 2023, according to CBS. Aivres is not on the blacklist. The U.S. government says servers made by Inspur are used by the Chinese military.

The New York Times previously reported that Aivres exported about $3 billion worth of computers containing Blackwell chips to Southeast Asia. Some of them went to Malaysian firms providing services to Chinese tech companies Alibaba and ByteDance.

CBS also linked a third Nvidia elite partner, 2CRSi, to Greg Lui, a California businessman indicted this year in an alleged $300 million chip smuggling case. Lui has pleaded not guilty. 2CRSi, which says it reported the customer to authorities, has not been accused of wrongdoing.

The report also points to a change in how Nvidia reports where its sales go.

Last year, the company stopped sorting sales by customers’ billing locations and started sorting them by where customers are headquartered. Under the new method, U.S.-based customers made up 69% of Nvidia’s 2025 sales and China 9%. Nvidia’s own filings note that a customer’s headquarters may differ from where chips end up.

The report comes as Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang faces scrutiny over his reported affair with Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of being a Chinese intelligence asset.

The Justice Department has said it has not reached a conclusion, and Beijing denies she is a spy. Megaspeed, the Singapore company she ran, is under federal investigation for allegedly getting around export bans and was blacklisted this week. It denies wrongdoing.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren said this week that Nvidia still owes Congress answers about its compliance with export controls. Sen. Chris Coons is expected to investigate whether companies are getting around the chip bans,

The California Post has reached out to Nvidia for comment.
