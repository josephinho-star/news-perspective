Australia news live: Andrew Hastie says he accepts Barnaby Joyce’s explanation over ‘traitor’ video and Pauline Hanson now ‘the only lying coward’

2026-09-10 23:58

Hastie says he accepts Joyce’s explanation over One Nation video

Andrew Hastie says Barnaby Joyce had reached out about the One Nation cartoon that labelled him a “traitor”.

Hastie had this to say to Guardian Australia this morning:

Barnaby Joyce has reached out to me and explained that he didn’t know about the cartoon that called me a traitor eight times. I accept his explanation. I called him a lying coward because I thought he was protecting Pauline Hanson.

I now know that she is the only lying coward.

For context – Hastie told News24 the One Nation cartoon, first posted on social media over a month ago and now pinned to the top of Pauline Hanson’s X account, called him a traitor eight times.

“I would say that Pauline Hanson and Barnaby Joyce are lying cowards, and I’ll say it again, so there’s no ambiguity,” he said.

Pauline Hanson has denied ever calling Hastie a traitor, telling the Nightly: “I will call him a wanker.”

Joyce previously told Guardian Australia he has never called Hastie a traitor, “nor ventured anywhere close to any such narrative about him”.

Woolworths increased prices of at least four ‘family staples’ during ‘Price Freeze’ promotion

Woolworths raised prices for popular items – including a 33% mark up on chicken fillets – within days of promising to freeze prices on 300 “family staples” for three months to help customers with the cost of living.

Pricing data, compiled by comparison website CW Scanner and provided to Guardian Australia, shows the supermarket chain increasing the prices of at least four key products after starting the three-month “Price Freeze” promotion on 1 May.

The supermarket nearly doubled the price of a 420g can of Woolworths “Mexican Style 3 Bean Mix” during the promotional period – from 60c to $1 on 17 June – the data shows. Woolworths dropped the price back to 60c on 17 August

Woolworths has justified the increases because it regarded the previous prices as promotional prices, rather than the “standard shelf price” for each product.

A Woolworths spokesperson said that the end of a temporary promotion did not represent a shelf price increase.

‘Profound’ increase in death rate for welfare recipients, report finds

People receiving an income support including disability, unemployment or carers payments had an average mortality rate more than nine times higher than people who did not access the welfare system, AAP reports.

The report from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare compared mortality rates and causes of death for people receiving and not receiving various income support payments by linking data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics and government systems.

Of the 287,000 people aged 22 to 64 who died between 2012 and 2022, almost three in five received income support payments.

The report showed the main cause of death for people on income support was cardiovascular disease but there were also higher rates of “deaths of despair” associated with alcohol, drugs and self-harm, University of Queensland social science research fellow Mark Robinson said.

The findings point to the profound health inequities in Australia despite its comparatively strong health record.

Bowen: ‘disappointing’ Greens crossed the floor to vote with One Nation on koala deal

Chris Bowen said yesterday was a “good day” for the government after the Greens reached a deal with Labor that will allow big industry to use a new koala national park in New South Wales to offset its climate pollution.

The climate change and energy minister told RN Breakfast this morning it was “disappointing that three Greens crossed the floor to vote with One Nation”.

I think if One Nation and the Greens are voting together, the Greens are voting the wrong way on climate. … I’m very pleased that this passed the parliament. I’m very pleased that the Greens party room worked with me, the majority.

This was a good day yesterday because it’s practical action. … [It’s] good for emissions, and also providing a route for investment and economic development in the regions where this logging has occurred and where now the economy can be diversified.

John Howard says Australia did the ‘right thing’ after September 11 attacks

Former prime minister John Howard, who was in Washington DC during the September 11 terror attacks, spoke to the ABC this morning about his experience 25 years ago.

“It meant that the world would never be the same,” Howard told Radio National Breakfast.

I just knew that it meant a lot to the whole world, and it certainly meant that I quickly came to realise just how important our association with the Americans was because this powerful country had been attacked in an atrocious, cruel manner.

Howard said he believes it was “only right” Australia join the US in Afghanistan, and the White House was “very happy to have our help”. He said:

I think we did the right thing. I was unhappy with aspects of the ultimate withdrawal from Afghanistan. It did look rather chaotic at the time … Overall, I don’t have any regrets. I think we did the right thing in going into Afghanistan.

Former prime minister Kevin Rudd, who also spoke on RN this morning, said he agreed with Howard’s decision to join the allied forces in Afghanistan.

“He got that right. What he got comprehensively wrong was Iraq.”

Marles says One Nation cartoon about Andrew Hastie ‘tasteless’

The defence minister, Richard Marles,said the One Nation cartoon labelling Liberal frontbencher Andrew Hastie a “traitor” is “tasteless” and should be taken down.

Marles just spoke on ABC News Breakfast as many senior Liberals, independent Jacqui Lambie and RSL Australia condemned the cartoon.

The defence minister said this morning:

Andrew Hastie is definitely not a traitor. I know him well and I’ve said this publicly. I very much honour the service that Andrew Hastie has given to our country, wearing our nation’s uniform. I think to use that word in respect of a veteran is extremely tasteless.

No one should be using that word in respect of any veteran and in respect of Andrew.

Australia remembers those whose lives were taken, those who survived, those we’ve lost since, and all those who continue to carry the weight of that day.

Opal Healthcare refunds residents after review into misuse of aged care standards

Opal Healthcare has refunded roughly $190,000 to almost 350 residents under a sweeping review into misuse of new aged care standards, AAP reports.

The standards came into effect in November and were designed to give residents more say over what premium products they opted to pay for.

Guidelines said services that were previously offered for free could not be charged, though they were not made law.

Reports emerged months later of aged care providers wrongfully charging residents for basic amenities, including television and wifi services.

In some cases, services were removed if they were not paid for.

An investigation was launched by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission after the aged care minister, Sam Rae, informed the regulator of claims against Opal Healthcare.

He said at the time the removal of services such as air conditioning and televisions was unacceptable.
