Asio chief reveals 16 terror plots have been foiled since Bondi attack

Australia’s domestic spy agency says it has disrupted 16 terrorist threats in the nine months since the Bondi attack, including an antisemitic attack, a plot to kill Muslims and a violent racist allegedly found with weapons and ammunition.

The Australian Security and Intelligence Organisation’s director general, Mike Burgess, outlined the “evolving threat” in a speech today on the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 terror attacks on the United States.

The Asio chief revealed his organisation had disrupted 16 terror plans since December where the “threat was considered serious but not necessarily imminent”. Burgess previously revealed in June the figure was 14, representing a minor increase. Since 2014, it has foiled 33 major terrorism plots.

Burgess said Sunni violent extremism represented the majority of Asio’s counter-terrorism caseload but that it was “too simplistic to assume there is a single terrorism threat or a most likely terrorist threat as we did in the aftermath of 9/11”.

He said the threats included an individual allegedly inspired by extreme right-wing ideology, others inspired by extreme left-wing ideologies and others inspired by “mixed and seemingly incompatible beliefs”, including one person who allegedly wanted to blow up a school who was allegedly inspired by ideological and extreme Christian beliefs.

These dynamics make the contemporary terrorism environment different – and in some ways, more difficult – than a quarter of a century ago ... Spikes in grievance, intolerance and polarisation are degrading our security environment and making politically motivated violence more likely.

Molly Ticehurst’s killer sentenced to 28 years in jail

Molly Ticehurst’s ex-boyfriend will spend at least 20 years inhas been sentenced to 28 years in jail for her senseless and brutal murder, which was revenge for her reporting allegations of sexual assault to police.

Daniel Billings was sentenced in the Orange Supreme Court today by Justice Natalie Adams, who imposed a non-parole period of 20 years.

Ticehurst, a 28-year-old childcare worker, was found dead in her pyjamas at her home at Forbes, in the state’s central west, in the early hours of 22 April 2024.

Justice Natalie Adams found Billings’ offending was in the high range of seriousness, given he murdered her in the exact way he had long threatened to do.

It must have been absolutely terrifying for Ms Ticehurst to die knowing her worst fears were realised.

Pauline Hanson not backing away from Hastie cartoon, suggesting a ‘chill pill’ for critics

Woolworths increased prices of at least four ‘family staples’ during ‘Price Freeze’ promotion

Woolworths raised prices for popular items – including a 33% mark up on chicken fillets – within days of promising to freeze prices on 300 “family staples” for three months to help customers with the cost of living.

Pricing data, compiled by comparison website CW Scanner and provided to Guardian Australia, shows the supermarket chain increasing the prices of at least four key products after starting the three-month “Price Freeze” promotion on 1 May.

The supermarket nearly doubled the price of a 420g can of Woolworths “Mexican Style 3 Bean Mix” during the promotional period – from 60c to $1 on 17 June – the data shows. Woolworths dropped the price back to 60c on 17 August

Woolworths has justified the increases because it regarded the previous prices as promotional prices, rather than the “standard shelf price” for each product.

A Woolworths spokesperson said that the end of a temporary promotion did not represent a shelf price increase.
