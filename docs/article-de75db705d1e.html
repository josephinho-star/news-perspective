White House press secretary Katie Zacharia dodges questions about where she passed the bar exam, saying only: 'I moved to China'

As she left the White House on Tuesday to accompany Donald Trump to Alaska, the new press secretary, Katie Zacharia, passed on an opportunity to set the record straight on whether she is licensed to practice law anywhere in the United States.

When a reporter asked Trump how he thought Zacharia had done in her first press briefing, during which the self-described attorney delivered an impassioned defense of his record and took only three questions, all from partisan, rightwing outlets, the president replied: “I thought she was very good.”

The White House social media team quickly clipped that answer from the president and posted it online – but left out the rest of the exchange, which began with a reporter asking, “What state did she pass the bar exam in?”

When Trump looked puzzled and stepped closer to the reporter to hear him better over the din of a waiting helicopter, the reporter explained: “You said she’s an attorney. Where did she pass the bar exam? Which state?”

Trump turned to Zacharia and repeated the question: “Which state?”

She stepped forward and said: “I moved to China”.

Two reporters then repeated the question: “Where did you pass the bar exam?”

“I moved to China,” Zacharia repeated, possibly suggesting that when she took a job at a law firm in China in 2011, shortly after she graduated from the Pepperdine Caruso School of Law, she did so instead of taking the bar exam.

On Friday morning, Judd Legum, an independent journalist and a former research director for Hillary Clinton with a law degree from Georgetown, reported that while the new press secretary has described herself as an attorney on cable news shows and podcasts, “there is no evidence that Zacharia ever passed a bar exam or has been admitted to practice law in any state.”

Hannah Brandt, a correspondent for the conservative outlet NewsNation, reported that Zacharia told her on Friday: “I practiced international transaction law, with all the appropriate credentials,” when she held what she called a position as “an associate” at the Zhong Lun Law Firm in Beijing, from 2011-2013.

Six GOP senators condemn Trump deal to lift Russian diesel sanctions

Six Republican senators have condemned the Trump administration’s deal to lift sanctions on Russia’s diesel exports in a new letter to the president alongside Democrats.

Led by Senator Richard Blumenthal, a Democrat, the lawmakers write that the “deal is a gift to our adversary and a betrayal of our Allies” and call for it to be “rescinded”. Four of the GOP senators – Thom Tillis, Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski and John Curtis – joined some of their Democratic colleagues on the foreign relations committee to decry the move over the weekend.

All of them, as well as John Cornyn, the outgoing Republican senator from Texas, and Mitch McConnell, the GOP senator who only recently returned to Capitol Hill after a months-long absence and will also leave Congress at the end of his term, signed on to the letter.

“We also recognize the urgent need to lower energy costs for American families and address the disruptions caused by the war with Iran,” they wrote. “However, allowing Russian-origin diesel into global markets will offer limited to no relief to Americans. It will have a negligible impact on fuel prices, but the revenues the Kremlin will receive from this agreement will undoubtedly finance its desired war of attrition and advance its security cooperation with China, Iran, and North Korea.”

This comes as energy analysts tell the Guardian that the amount of diesel – and the time it will take to arrive in the US – will have little impact on American consumers.
