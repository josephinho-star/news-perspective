Ankara mayor faces criticism over Erdoğan meeting amid opposition crackdown

2026-09-10 14:52

Ankara Mayor Mansur Yavaş faced criticism on social media after meeting with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan at the presidential palace in Ankara on Wednesday, as dozens of opposition mayors remain in prison and Erdoğan’s ruling party assumes control of municipalities won by opposition candidates in the 2024 local elections.

The closed-door meeting was announced by the presidency and Yavaş, who shared a photograph of himself with Erdoğan.

Yavaş said in an initial statement on X that they discussed metro investments, projects under construction and future municipal work. He thanked Erdoğan for supporting projects in the capital and for his role in bringing NATO’s July summit to Ankara.

The post had received more than 2,000 replies and about 4 million views by Thursday afternoon. Several of the most widely liked replies criticized Yavaş for the photograph and his thanks to Erdoğan while elected opposition figures remain jailed. One user accused Erdoğan of jailing Yavaş’s political allies and trying to prevent the mayor from serving Ankara. Another asked why Yavaş had not questioned Erdoğan about reported restrictions on jailed İstanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu.

The timing added to the reaction. Erdoğan received Yavaş on the 103rd anniversary of the Republican People’s Party (CHP), of which Yavaş is still a member, and the mayor did not attend the party’s anniversary reception. Municipal sources said before the event that Yavaş would maintain his current position and avoid taking sides in Turkey’s fractured opposition.

The CHP split after an Ankara court in May annulled Özgür Özel’s 2023 election as party chairman and reinstated his predecessor, Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu. Özel and 90 lawmakers left the CHP in July and established the New Party, now the largest opposition group in parliament. Yavaş stayed in the CHP and has sought cooperation between members of both parties on the Ankara city council. The split followed a legal crackdown that has left hundreds of opposition figures in prison.

The Erdoğan meeting prompted claims that Yavaş might leave the CHP or move closer to the ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP). Yavaş denied those claims in a detailed statement on Thursday, saying he had personally requested the appointment after greeting Erdoğan at an airport.

Yavaş said Ankara was the sole subject of the meeting. He listed metro projects, credit and approval processes, a planned exhibition and congress center and other municipal investments among the subjects discussed.

Yavaş argued that Turkey’s presidential system requires municipalities to seek central government approval or support for some projects. “If I need to meet with Mr. President to solve an Ankara problem … I will meet with him today and tomorrow,” he said.

He denied being invited to join the AKP and said no political subject was discussed. He also said both Kılıçdaroğlu and Özel knew of the appointment in advance.

Özel confirmed that account, saying Yavaş had informed him before requesting the meeting and again before it occurred. “I would not doubt Mr. Mansur,” Özel said, adding that the New Party knew of and approved of the meeting.

Yavaş said his position was intended to preserve cooperation between CHP and New Party members on the Ankara city council. He said he would “do what is necessary” if both party headquarters regarded his stance as a problem, but added that neither leader had objected.

Far right Victory Party leader Ümit Özdağ also defended the meeting, saying contact between a mayor and the president should be routine but that Turkey’s political climate explained the controversy. Former AKP lawmaker Şamil Tayyar urged caution over immediate political scenarios but described the meeting as “a beginning.”

The criticism came one day after the AKP took control of İstanbul’s Üsküdar Municipality in a council vote following the arrest and suspension of elected Mayor Sinem Dedetaş on corruption allegations.

The council had initially elected a CHP member as acting mayor, but a court suspended that decision after an AKP challenge. Four council members elected on opposition tickets later joined the AKP, and two opposition council members were jailed before a repeat vote that the AKP candidate won 23-19.

European Parliament rapporteur on Turkey Nacho Sánchez Amor called the outcome a “blatant power grab” and said arrests, defections and threats had reversed an opposition victory at the ballot box.

A July tally by German broadcaster Deutsche Welle found that at least 39 CHP-run municipalities had faced corruption or bribery investigations since the 2024 elections. Mayors had been detained in 31 of them, and 26 remained in prison.

İmamoğlu, Erdoğan’s main political rival, has been jailed pending trial since March 2025 on corruption charges he denies. The opposition and rights groups say the municipal cases are politically motivated and intended to reverse gains made in the 2024 local elections.

Yavaş, Ankara’s mayor since 2019, is also seen as a possible presidential challenger to Erdoğan. In his Thursday statement he said his role was to reduce political tension and preserve channels for dialogue.
