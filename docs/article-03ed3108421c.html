Erdoğan authorizes 30-year privatization of Bosporus bridges, highways

2026-09-05 11:57

President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan has authorized a 30-year privatization plan for İstanbul’s two state-run Bosporus bridges, eight highways and two beltways, clearing the way to shift their operation and toll revenue to private companies while ownership remains with the state.

The decree, published in the Official Gazette on Saturday, allows the assets to be privatized together or in separate groups through a transfer of operating rights, leases and revenue-sharing agreements or a combination of methods.

Contracts will last 30 years, and the Privatization Administration (ÖİB) must complete the transactions by December 31, 2031.

The package covers the European and Anatolian highway networks run by the Directorate General of Highways (KGM), along with the İzmir-Aydın, İzmir-Çeşme, Niğde-Mersin-Adana, Adana-Gaziantep, Gaziantep-Şanlıurfa and Toprakkale-İskenderun routes.

The KGM will continue operating and maintaining the network until a transfer of operations takes place and will later set the private operators’ maintenance and service duties and inspect their work.

Passenger cars are currently charged 59 lira ($1.20), in each direction on both bridges covered by the decree, which carried about 430,000 vehicles a day in 2024, according to KGM data.

No decision has been made to impose tolls on free roads or increase existing rates because of the privatization, Turkey’s finance ministry said.

The $600 million figure cited by critics was annual revenue rather than profit, with about $300 million going to maintenance, repairs and investment before personnel and operating costs, the ministry added.

Main opposition New Party lawmaker Deniz Yavuzyılmaz called the decision “a sale of Turkey’s future” and argued that private operators would repeatedly raise tolls during the 30-year period.

New Party leader Özgür Özel predicted that highway tolls would rise fivefold and bridge tolls would double, promising to return the highways to public operation if his party takes power in the next general election.

The decree comes after the government hired EY and the BTY Group as financial and technical advisers for the privatization of the bridges.

Government officials and EY also met with Portuguese highway operator Brisa while seeking potential bidders, Reuters reported in April.

A 2012 tender offered both bridges and about 2,000 kilometers of highways for 25 years, but Erdoğan, then prime minister, rejected the winning $5.72 billion bid after arguing that accepting less than $7 billion would amount to “treason,” and the government canceled the tender.
