From ‘Good Time’ to ‘Primetime’: The Best Robert Pattinson Movies

2026-10-06 12:00

Twilight may have made Robert Pattinson a star, but it’s his second decade in the biz that made him a highly credible actor.

Consider his course: From 12th-billed in a Harry Potter movie to four goofy, awkward Twilights (which did nobody any favors beyond their bank accounts), a regrettable 9/11 melodramatic romance (Remember Me) and the dull Water for Elephants, Pattinson couldn’t quite find his footing with the right projects. After that, though? He likely invested his piles of dough wisely and, just as wisely, started taking gigs under highly credible directors: David Cronenberg with Maps to the Stars and Cosmopolis (note: minor Cronenberg is still Cronenberg!), Werner Herzog with Queen of the Desert (note: minor Herzog is still Herzog!), David Gray with The Lost City of Z (note: this is upper-tier Gray, even though not enough people saw it).

And then R-Pattz connected with the Safdie Brothers, and the rest is history — history studded with extra-memorable performances, films in which in-their-prime filmmakers give crucial time to an in-his-prime actor. Which finds Pattinson here in 2026, making his mark deeper with four films, all hits or primed to be so — more on a couple of these in a minute — the newest of which is Primetime. The bio-ish-pic, in which he plays Chris Hansen, host of the controversial 2000s TV show To Catch a Predator, recently debuted to a healthy $19 million, proving that movie stars giving beefy performances still have at least modest drawing power. And next, he’ll join the stupidly talented cast of a little megabucks trilogy wrap-up you may have heard of, Dune: Part Three.

Which leads us to today’s task: Rounding up the last decade of Pattinson’s career, which is ridiculously great, studded with gems and classics-to-be that need to be watched, then watched again:

Pattz headlined the Safdies’ 2017 breakout, a Scorsese-coded crime-thriller (think the greezy urban locales of Taxi Driver and Mean Streets) that plays out over One Crazy Night and leaves us feeling wrung the hell out like we just survived a shark attack. Our guy plays a small-time crook who spends all night trying to scrape together 10 grand — by any means necessary, shady or otherwise — to bail his mentally disabled brother out of the clink after they bungle a robbery. Although the Safdies’ ability to amplify tension to alarming degrees tends to overshadow performances, Pattinson’s full-on commitment to the scuzz and sleaze and uptempo jitteriness of the scenario is terrifically memorable.

Elevated sci-fi was peaking circa 2018 (see also: Brad Pitt-goes-to-Jupiter drama Ad Astra), and this Pattinson vehicle took him to deep space. Clare Denis directs this high-concept saga among the stars, with our guy playing a criminal (alongside the inimitable Mia Goth) subject to deep-space experiments at the hands of Juliette Binoche’s mad-ish scientist. Weird film, and while it’s hard to find our footing at times with this fractured narrative, Pattinson draws us in and keeps us there.

WHere to stream high life

In which Pattz grew sick of Willem Dafoe’s fahhhtz right quick, in a gruesomely funny two-hander about squabbling, old-timey lighthouse keepers in Robert Eggers’ unhinged quasi-supernatural thriller. Our guy holds his own next to Dafoe — no easy feat; the pairing is inspired, to say the least — and fully establishes himself as an eccentric actor capable of brutal comic intensity. He’s never done anything funnier, or weirder: “If I had a steak… I’d f— it.”

WHere to stream the lighthouse

Granted, any actor, no matter who, plays second fiddle to director Christopher Nolan in any movie directed by Christopher Nolan. And Tenet is kinda/sorta Nolan’s “lost” film, released during the pandemic and sandwiched between big hits Dunkirk and Oppenheimer; it’s also incomprehensible if you’re watching it without your slide rule and graphing calculator handy. So yeah — Pattinson is in this movie, in a supporting role that finds him bungee-jumping up a building in what’s a fairly self-evident Memorable Moment. But he does so charismatically, and is at the very least a reminder that we should rewatch Tenet, if we’re feeling ambitious, and aware that any discussion of Pattinson’s part in the film inevitably turns into a conversation about Nolan. So it goes!

I lump these two together for a couple of reasons: One, they’re Netflix originals destined to be forgotten, possibly because they’re Netflix originals. And two, because they’re middling dramas in which Pattinson steals scenes in key supporting roles by skirting the edge of cartoon villainy. He plays the French Dauphin in The King, sneering his way right through co-star Timothee Chalamet in a performance I called “thick and rich ham gravy.” In Devil, he tackles the false prophet role, playing a fire-and-brimstone preacher rich in false-godliness aura, leaving behind “a thin trail of slug grease.” These showy go-rounds should be in Pattinson’s highlight reel.

Pattinson anchors the 1A to The Dark Knight’s No. 1-Batman-movie-ever, giving us an angsty young Batman who’s learning how to wear the cowl not just for himself, but for the whole of Gotham City. It’s a fresh, muscular vigilante-to-hero arc invigorated by Pattinson’s tortured-gothboy smeared-eye-makeup riff on Bruce Wayne. The star and director Matt Reeves proved to be an inspired pairing for a character who was on the verge of being old hat. The long-in-the-works sequel can’t get here soon enough.

WHere to stream the batman

For Bong Joon-Ho’s bazonkers sci-fi epic, Pattinson stretches himself in the classic-Hollywood way, by playing twins. Or, more accurately, clones, described by one character as “habanero Mickey and mild Mickey” (and yes, that character had hopped in the sack with both). Both performances riff on the same intellectually addled dingdong-dope personified by Pattinson’s deeply wacky vocal intonations. In interviews, he said his voices were inspired by Ren and Stimpy, an underrated genius move.

WHere to stream mickey 17

The shadow of Jennifer Lawrence’s searing, feral performance is long and wide, so it’s easy to overlook the depth and eccentricity of Pattinson’s portrayal of a husband who’s at once a catalyst and victim of his wife’s mental breakdown. It’s a key role he plays with wormy complexity and unexpected flourishes; to paraphrase that one guy, you’ll find he’s full of surprises. An underrated, funny-but-not-funny-funny performance in a rare Lynne Ramsey effort that never got its due.

WHere to stream die my love

Pattinson finds himself in a what-if-you-were-in-his-shoes role, playing the fiancee to Zendaya’s character, who reveals on the eve of the wedding that (spoiler alert!) she planned a school shooting when she was a teen, but never went through with it. The film — a sizeable $135 million-grossing hit that shows us star power still puts butts in theater seats — doesn’t necessarily work, but Pattinson keeps it grounded, unafraid to give his character a prickly, jerk-ish quality as he quietly wrestles with whether he’s OK with accepting the bizarre truth about someone he loves.

WHere to stream the drama

Again, a Nolan movie, and the previous assertion about the director again applies: It’s easy to get lost in the melee of super-epic set pieces, a cast consisting of a million-zillion A-to-A-minus-listers and the Endless IMAX 70mm Superior Format Discourse. But Pattzie stands out by rendering Antinous, the lead suitor for Penelope’s hand, as the be-all-end-all sniveling bastard. He gives the character a loud mouth and soft hands, and in the film’s funniest bit, shouts instructions about deploying weaponry against an angry Odysseus with uproarious don’t-want-to-get-my-hands-dirty cowardly energy. It may be time for his first Oscar nom.
