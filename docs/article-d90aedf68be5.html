Turkey condemns alleged Houthi drone attack targeting Mecca

2026-09-17 12:46

Turkey on Wednesday strongly condemned what Saudi Arabia said was a Houthi drone attack targeting the holy city of Mecca, although the Yemeni group denied launching the drone.

Saudi Arabia said its air defenses intercepted a drone heading toward Mecca early Wednesday. The Houthis rejected the accusation as a “lie.”

“We strongly condemn the drone attack carried out by the Houthis targeting the holy city of Mecca,” the Turkish Foreign Ministry said.

Turkey reaffirmed its support for Saudi Arabia’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and security and expressed solidarity with the kingdom.

It also called for an immediate halt to attacks threatening regional stability and urged restraint to avoid further escalating tensions.

Saudi Arabia said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed a drone south of Mecca on Tuesday evening before it entered restricted airspace over the city. Turki al-Malki, spokesman for the Saudi-led military coalition that has fought the Houthis in Yemen since 2015, said the drone had been launched by the group. He described it as the Houthis’ second attempt to target Mecca, citing a ballistic missile launch in 2017.

The Houthis denied targeting Mecca. A Houthi political official described the Saudi allegation as false, while a military source quoted by the Houthi-run SABA news agency said the group posed no threat to Mecca or other Islamic holy sites.

Mecca, home to the Grand Mosque and the Kaaba, is Islam’s holiest city and the destination of the annual hajj pilgrimage.

The incident came amid a sharp escalation in fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis after several years of relative calm.

The Houthis have carried out a series of missile and drone attacks on Saudi territory in recent weeks, while Saudi forces have intensified strikes in Yemen. The group has also expanded its control along Yemen’s Red Sea coast near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, one of the world’s most important maritime trade routes.

The renewed conflict has raised concerns about shipping and energy supplies in a region already facing greater instability. Saudi Arabia has reported attacks on civilian and energy infrastructure, while the Houthis say their operations are in response to Saudi military action in Yemen.

Yemen’s conflict began after the Houthis seized the capital Sanaa in 2014, prompting a Saudi-led coalition to intervene the following year in support of the internationally recognized government.

A UN-brokered truce in 2022 sharply reduced cross-border attacks for several years, although no comprehensive peace settlement was reached.
