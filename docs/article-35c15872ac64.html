Fire crews rescue Californians trapped in vehicles amid flash flooding

2026-11-20 01:13

The Los Angeles Fire Department pulled off a daring aerial recovery of a woman trapped in the LA River channel near the Encino Golf Course. Crews responded to the 6300 block of North Balboa Boulevard, near the Lake Balboa Hiking Trail, around 8:20 a.m., authorities said.

An air operations crew plucked a woman from the riverbed “via short-haul operation to a dry location,” LAFD spokeswoman Lyndsey Lantz said. The victim was taken to a hospital by ambulance.

A video of the rescue released by the department shows a helicopter lowering a firefighter-paramedic into the water and hooking the victim with a carabiner before pulling her to safety.

“The rescue was one of many swift-water operations the department carried out as heavy rain fell across the county Thursday,” the LAFD said.

Lantz said the woman was the only person trapped, but the helicopter crew searched the area from the air to confirm no one else was in danger.

Around 9 a.m., rescuers were called to Vineland Avenue, between Vanowen Street and Sherman Way, where flooding had stranded multiple vehicles.

Firefighters arrived to find seven cars stuck in deep water, some with people still trapped inside. Additional units were dispatched, and every occupant was rescued.

One person was taken to a hospital in unknown condition.

Farther east, a swift-water rescue team was dispatched at about 11:15 a.m. to the swollen San Gabriel River in South El Monte, near North Peck Road and Durfee Avenue, according to the fire department.

The crews rescued five people, at least two of them hoisted out by helicopter, authorities said. A department spokeswoman said one person refused to leave the riverbed. None of those rescued required hospitalization.
