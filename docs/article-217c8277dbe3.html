Turkey, Lebanon revisit gas and power links amid shortages

2026-09-24 14:48

Turkey and Lebanon discussed possible electricity links and pipeline projects in New York on Wednesday, weeks after Beirut said Ankara was interested in supplying gas and investing in Lebanon’s struggling energy sector.

Turkish Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar met Lebanese Energy and Water Minister Joe Saddi on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly, Euronews reported Thursday, citing a social media post by Bayraktar. The ministers discussed electricity connections and cooperation on pipelines and hydrocarbons, he said. His announcement identified no agreed project, route or timetable.

The meeting followed talks in Ankara earlier this month. Lebanon’s energy ministry said the Turkish side had expressed interest in connecting electricity networks, supplying Lebanon with gas, exploring offshore energy blocks and investing in solar power and conventional plants. The two sides appointed teams to follow up, the ministry said, but announced no contract.

Lebanon has struggled for years to supply reliable electricity from its state grid, leaving many households and businesses dependent on costly private generators. The World Bank approved a $250 million project in 2024 to restore grid services, expand renewable energy and support reforms, saying successive crises had weakened the sector’s operations and finances.

The pipeline discussions come as Beirut tries to bring more gas to its power plants. In May, Saddi said Lebanon planned to rehabilitate roughly 30 kilometers of the Arab Gas Pipeline between the Deir Ammar power plant and the Syrian border, while negotiating gas purchases and transit arrangements with Egypt, Jordan and Syria. The ministry said on September 17 Qatar had provided a grant to complete maintenance on the Lebanese section.

Turkey began sending gas to Syria through a border pipeline in August 2025. Bayraktar had said in December 2024 that Turkey could also export electricity to Lebanon if the transmission network allowed it. Neither government has said whether the projects discussed Wednesday would use those connections.
