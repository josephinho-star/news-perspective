Trump to meet with victims of Gavin Newsom’s death penalty reversal as he blasts gov

2026-11-20 18:57

President Trump will meet with the families of murdered loved ones outraged by Gov. Gavin Newsom’s decision to spare California’s death row inmates, as the White House ramps up its attacks on the governor’s controversial move.

The president will thoroughly condemn Newsom’s decision to commute 548 death sentences for California inmates in front of the Department of Justice on Monday afternoon, a White House spokesperson told the California Post exclusively.

Attorney General Todd Blanche, FBI Director Kash Patel, and some of the outraged families who lost family members at the hands of violent death-row criminals will also attend.

The family of 12-year-old Polly Klaas, who was kidnapped and killed by Richard Allen Davis in 1993, is one of the families The Post has confirmed to attend.

Her father, Marc, told The Post that he hopes the president will use his power to fight back against Newsom.

“The president has the world’s largest bully pulpit. I hope he uses it to condemn Newsom’s mass commutations. I hope that he explains the dangers of bringing these dangerous people one step closer to being released from prison. I hope that he impresses upon the public the importance of public safety,” he said.

Davis was convicted of capital murder and sentenced to death in 1996.

The White House expressed rage at Newsom’s decision, which came weeks before he will vacate the governor’s mansion.

“Governor Newsom is failing these innocent victims and their families by refusing to carry out justice against these heinous murderers. He is playing politics with public safety,” White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis told The Post.

“President Trump will always stand with innocent victims and will continue to restore law and order and deliver safer communities,” Bis added.

Federal law enforcement agencies, including the DEA, U.S. Marshals, FBI, ATF, and BOP, will also be present at Monday’s event with Trump.

Newsom said he decided to commute the death penalties — despite California voters rejecting attempts to repeal capital punishment twice — because he felt it was “incompatible” with the state’s values.

“I take this action because I firmly believe that state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California’s values, undermines the foundations of our legal system, and diminishes each of us,” he wrote in a Nov. 5 letter.

“I know we cannot execute people in our imperfect legal system and remain true to our core values, first among them justice and human decency.”

California prosecutors were infuriated by Newsom’s decision, which came days after Democrats claimed the governor’s office again in the midterm elections. Los Angeles County District Attorney Nathan Hochman denounced the move, saying that the death penalty remains legal and that he does not have the power to abolish it.

A collective of victims, prosecutors, and law enforcement leaders announced plans on Nov. 16 to sue Newsom because of the commutations.

“The Governor’s clemency power does not authorize mass commutations that disregard constitutional safeguards, legally required procedures, or victims’ rights,” Anne Marie Schubert, President and CEO of the Criminal Justice Legal Foundation and a former Sacramento County District Attorney, said in a statement.

“By predetermining the outcome for every inmate on death row, the Governor has abandoned his responsibility to exercise meaningful discretion. Blanket commutation is not an exercise of discretion — it is an abandonment of it,” she added.

The Klaas family has been very vocal about their disdain for Newsom’s decision, suggesting it only puts criminals closer to freedom.

“He’s putting these people closer and closer and closer to freedom, and these are absolutely the worst, most disgusting people that have ever lived,” Marc previously told The Post.

The California Supreme Court still needs to approve nearly 400 of the commutations to make them final. Victims have kept a close eye on the court, filing a request for the recusal of at least one associate justice who they viewed as too close to Newsom’s capital punishment policies.

The president previously slammed California’s governor for the commutations, calling it “a disaster for Justice in America.”

“Many of these names, people who have killed, over and over again, can’t believe their luck in having a Governor so incompetent and insensitive that he would not let the long and winding legal system prevail,” Trump wrote on Truth Social.

The California Post reached out to Newsom’s office for comment.
