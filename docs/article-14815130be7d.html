Democrats demand records as investigation into Trump Jr’s investment firm widens

2026-11-24 17:45

Democrats have widened their investigation into Donald Trump Jr’s investment firm, demanding records from four companies that gained its backing shortly before receiving lucrative government contracts or favourable regulatory decisions.

Jamie Raskin, the top Democrat on the House of Representatives’ judiciary committee, sent letters to online firearms seller GrabAGun, defence contractor Hadrian Automation, vape maker Juul Labs, and prediction market platform Polymarket, requesting two years’ worth of communications and financial documents relating to their dealings with 1789 Capital.

The inquiry will examine whether the president’s eldest son and his business partners used political connections or advance knowledge of government decisions to identify investment opportunities, or helped secure contracts and regulatory changes for companies in which they had financial interests.

Trump Jr joined 1789 Capital as a partner four days after his father’s victory in the 2024 presidential election. The Florida-based firm has since grown from about $150m (£113m) in assets under management to about $3bn, while its main fund recorded returns of about 200% by June this year, according to figures cited by Raskin.

The Maryland congressman wrote in the letters that 1789 Capital’s “striking success” in predicting the award of government contracts or release of favourable regulations suggests that either Trump Jr acquired “magical new clairvoyant powers” or there was “a brazen conspiracy … to enrich the first family beyond the wildest dreams of any other presidential family”.

The latest requests extend an investigation first launched by Raskin in August. The four companies have been asked to provide investment agreements, valuations, due diligence material and communications with 1789 Capital and its representatives dating back to 1 November 2024.

The letters identify a series of transactions and government decisions that Raskin says warrant scrutiny.

Hadrian, a California-based defence manufacturer, received a $900m US navy commitment to produce submarine components and an $80m army manufacturing contract in March, two months after 1789 Capital acquired a stake in the company.

Raskin also highlighted donations by Hadrian’s chief executive, Chris Power, who gave nearly $500,000 to the Republican National Committee and its legal fund in July. Four days later, Trump issued an executive order intended to increase the Pentagon’s reliance on domestic manufacturers. Hadrian was subsequently valued at almost $8bn in an investment round in August, up from $1.6bn earlier in the year.

In the case of Juul, 1789 Capital acquired a $5m stake in the e-cigarette maker in March 2025. Four months later, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) reversed an earlier decision that had threatened the company’s survival, authorising five Juul products, including menthol pods. The FDA authorised three more products in August this year.

Raskin noted that the Wall Street Journal reported in May that Trump pressed the then FDA commissioner, Marty Makary, to accelerate approval of flavoured vaping products. Juul has faced longstanding criticism over the appeal of its products to teenagers, although the company says it is committed to helping adult smokers switch from combustible cigarettes.

Polymarket, the prediction market platform, was the subject of a $1.4m penalty in 2022 for operating an unregistered trading platform. The justice department and Commodity Futures Trading Commission closed investigations into the company in July 2025.

Days later, Polymarket announced a $112m purchase of a federally licensed exchange and clearing house, paving the way for its return to the US market. It disclosed an investment from 1789 Capital the following month.

Raskin said the firm’s investment could have increased dramatically in value, with Polymarket reportedly seeking a funding round valuing it at $21bn, compared with a reported $300m when 1789 Capital first invested.

The fourth company, online firearms retailer GrabAGun, went public in July 2025 through a merger with a special purpose acquisition company affiliated with 1789 Capital’s principals. Trump Jr subsequently joined its board.

In May the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives proposed a rule that would make it easier for online retailers to ship firearms directly to customers. GrabAGun told investors it was uniquely positioned to benefit from the change, although the National Shooting Sports Foundation, the industry’s biggest trade association, opposed the proposal, warning it could facilitate illegal firearms sales.

Raskin has asked all four companies to preserve relevant records, including communications on encrypted messaging services such as Signal, WhatsApp and Telegram, and to identify any deleted material.

The investigation comes as Democrats prepare to take control of the House in January following their dramatic gains in the midterm elections. Raskin, who is expected to become chairman of the judiciary committee, has made scrutiny of alleged financial conflicts involving the Trump administration and the president’s family a priority.

Trump Jr, 48, and 1789 Capital, which is based in Palm Beach, Florida, deny any wrongdoing. The firm’s lawyer, Bill Burck, said Raskin had failed to address a September response setting out what it regarded as flaws in his understanding of its investments.

“Facts are facts and Congressman Raskin has them wrong here, but persists in his tall tales against 1789,” Burck told the Associated Press. He described the allegations as personal attacks unsupported by evidence.

Trump Jr, speaking at an Axios event last week, also rejected accusations that he had exploited his father’s political position for financial gain. “I can see what they are saying. It doesn’t make it so,” he said, adding that he should not be expected to suspend his business activities for the duration of his father’s political career.

GrabAGun described Raskin’s letter as a political document and said its allegations were baseless. It said in a statement the proposed firearms shipping rule had been widely discussed in the industry and that its own input had been made through public comments.

Juul said it was reviewing the letter and intended to co-operate with congressional efforts to understand its business.
