Anthony Volpe belts grand slam, gets curtain call in rousing return as Yankees sweep away Rockies

2026-09-11 02:03

Jazz Chisholm Jr. may want to familiarize himself with Wally Pipp.

His injury has opened the door for Anthony Volpe at second base. If Thursday night was an indication, the back-from-the-minors Volpe doesn’t plan on relinquishing the job.

In his first game back in The Bronx, Volpe put on an offensive show: three hits, including a grand slam to the opposite field, as the Yankees completed a three-game sweep of the Rockies with a 10-3 victory to move within three games of the AL East-leading Rays.

After Volpe’s seventh-inning slam, the crowd of 40,706 chanted “Volpe, Volpe, Volpe” until he came out for a curtain call. His five RBIs are a career high. The new position may suit him.

Suddenly, everyone’s favorite whipping boy is a fan favorite.

Chisholm was placed on the 10-day injured list earlier in the day after suffering a sprained right thumb in Tuesday’s win over Colorado.

He was wearing a protective brace on it, and will be shut down for at least a week.

Manager Aaron Boone declined to say if Volpe would be the everyday second baseman, pointing out that he could use José Caballero there as well in Chisholm’s absence.

But, obviously, Volpe is going to stay in the lineup if he hits like this.

Volpe was demoted to Triple-A Scranton/Wilkes-Barre on Aug. 4, replaced by top prospect George Lombard Jr. as the team’s starting shortstop.

He produced in the minors. Volpe was named the International League Player of the Week from Sept. 1-6 after going 11-for-21 (.524) with three doubles, three homers, six RBIs, eight runs scored and five stolen bases.

Over his past 17 games at Scranton/Wilkes-Barre, Volpe was batting .333 with eight home runs and a 1.218 OPS. Teammate Jasson Domínguez joked that he “became Barry Bonds, all the time, every day.”

“I don’t know if physically as much as just mentally [there was a change], having the mindset I wanted to get a lot out of it,” he said before the game. “You have a game every night, try out different stuff. Seeing what works and you feel like it’s gonna translate in case an opportunity like this comes. So there was just a lot of trial and error.”

Luis García Jr. had a two-run shot, Spencer Jones added three hits and two RBIs and five relievers combined to cover 5 ²/₃ innings after an inefficient Max Fried needed 80 pitches to record 10 outs.

During pregame introductions, Volpe received cheers.

In his first at-bat, he singled in a run. He added another hit in the sixth and then came up with the bases loaded an inning later.

He smashed a 1-1 sinker over the wall in right field at 104 mph to put the game away.

“He’s got the ability to be a good offensive player. He’s put together a pretty good run of offense right now,” Boone said. “He’s, I feel like, hitting the ball with authority to all fields and really it comes down to getting yourself in good hitting positions consistently. He’s done a good job of that.”

The Yankees have momentum: a healthy Aaron Judge and winners of eight of 11.

What’s next could be a challenge: a significant Subway Series matchup with the suddenly red-hot Mets and Juan Soto in The Bronx.

The Mets don’t have anything to play for other than pride — and hurting their rivals’ chances of catching the Rays.

The series opener will be played on the 25th anniversary of the World Trade Center terrorist attacks of 9/11, adding an emotional layer to the series.

The Yankees’ three-game AL East deficit is as close as they have been since early August.
