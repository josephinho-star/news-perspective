‘Hot garbage:’ Trader Joe’s fans spot telltale signs of ‘sloppy’ changes to beloved in-store experience

2026-11-13 20:52

Trader Joe’s fans are crying foul after spotting what they believe could be AI-generated artwork on the chain’s latest reusable shopping bags and Fearless Flyer.

The latest controversy centers on a $2.99 “Gardener” mystery bag, one of several limited-edition designs tucked inside the chain’s viral Mystery Packs, which launched earlier this month.

Each sealed brown paper pack contains three random reusable bags, leaving shoppers in suspense until they tear them open. But one customer claims the surprise inside was less than delightful.

In a viral post on Reddit’s r/tjcrew, a shopper shared a photo of the “Gardener” design, which features whimsical doodles of flowers, gardening tools, birds, a watering can and a gnome.

The smoking gun? A garden gnome with six fingers — and, as “The Princess Bride” fans know, that’s grounds for revenge.

“New ‘the gardener’ mystery bag is AI generated,” the Reddit user alleged, adding: “Saw this on TikTok and the gnome has 6 fingers.”

Fellow shoppers were quick to point out other suspicious details.

“Also the watering can doesn’t make sense for several reasons. The spout isn’t coming from the center but the side, the handle suddenly ends, and the poles holding the spout don’t make sense,” one commenter wrote.

“Sloppy! Shame on the design team for using AI on this, we have so many talented artists on board who can do much better.”

Another shopper said the gnome’s unusual digits immediately caught their attention, adding that it was “such a shame that TJs has been resorting to more and more AI art…”

“Mind you they hire artists for sign making, so they very well could have hired someone to design a bag. hate this,” another complained.

Some fans even had a proposed fix for the alleged AI takeover: a design contest that would let employees submit their own artwork for future shopping bags.

“Hey corporate: instead of using AI slop, why not do a bag design contest similar to the recipe one,” one Redditor suggested, proposing that stores submit designs and the company chooses its favorites.

The mystery bag isn’t the only thing raising eyebrows. Shoppers have also questioned the artwork in the chain’s latest Fearless Flyer, with some pointing to animals sporting a few too many appendages.

“There’s a ton of AI imagery in the flyer, too. Hot garbage,” one Reddit user declared.

Another suggested that some recent product descriptions sounded suspiciously robotic, complaining that they were “long winded without saying really anything, and fewer puns.”

The concerns have spilled onto Instagram, too. One Trader Joe’s fan shared an image of the Nov. 2026 Fearless Flyer and flagged a sheep with five legs and a goat with three horns.

“They immediately put out a revised version, but only fixed the sheep… the goat is still left with 3 horns!” the user wrote.

Perhaps most notably, the backlash isn’t coming only from shoppers.

One Instagram commenter identifying themselves as a Trader Joe’s sign artist said the issue had become a source of frustration among employees.

“With over 600 stores and all the artists that are so talented, why would AI even be a consideration?” they wrote, adding that many employees would be “honored to do artwork for The Fearless Flyer!”

According to Julie Averbach, author of the 2025 book “The Art of Trader Joe’s: Discovering the Hidden Art Gems of America’s Favorite Grocery Store,” the chain’s in-store artwork is traditionally created by crew members who also work the registers, stock shelves and bag groceries.

These employee artists reportedly create everything from chalkboard menus to wall murals using paint pens, acrylic markers and paper crafts.

It’s that personal touch that helps set Trader Joe’s apart. Since opening in 1967, the chain has grown to more than 600 stores across 43 states, building a cult following with its affordable finds, cheerful crew and unmistakable charm.

For shoppers who have made a hobby out of hunting down the latest seasonal tote or decoding the Fearless Flyer’s latest puns, the thought of AI taking over the artwork is proving hard to stomach.

Who knew the biggest mystery in Trader Joe’s latest mystery bags would be who — or what — designed them?
