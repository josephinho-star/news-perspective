NBA star Jaylen Brown says in ridiculous interview he's an influencer, not an entertainer for fans

2026-09-10 02:37

If there is one thing Jaylen Brown is truly elite at, besides turning the ball over with his left hand on a crucial drive, it is convincing himself he is the smartest guy in any room he walks into.

The NBA has a long history of foolish quotes, but Jaylen routinely delivers gems so profoundly pseudo-intellectual they belong in a Hall of Fame for tone-deaf vanity.

That is saying something in a league run by LeBron.

Brown, the new Sixers star, recently sat down with Oscar winner Mahershala Ali for a slick Hollywood Reporter feature, and predictable as sunrise, Brown could not stick to basketball.

Instead, we got his latest sermon on why he is far too deep to be categorized as a mere athlete.

"I refuse to adopt the idea that me being an athlete, I’m just here to entertain people," Brown said, apparently rebranding himself from NBA wing to sociopolitical visionary.

"They want our athletes, our musicians, our entertainers, our people of influence to think that we just here for entertainment purposes. I’m going to forever disagree with that."

Right. When millions of fans fork over hard-earned cash for tickets and jerseys, they aren’t looking to kick back on a Tuesday night and watch a game.

They’re obviously tuning in to absorb the profound cultural wisdom of a guy making over $300 million to bounce a leather ball on hardwood.

For years, Brown has cultivated a reputation as the league’s resident philosopher-king, masking questionable judgment behind a wall of academic posturing.

He has never been shy about marveling at his own brainpower. In a 2022 interview with The Ringer, Brown described how people supposedly react when he speaks.

Brown sahred, "When I open my mouth and talk, sometimes people say they are amazed of my intellect. I don’t know if that’s because I truly speak in a way that people can understand or feel a certain way, or because they don’t expect it."

Astonishing stuff.

When reports surfaced that NBA front-office personnel viewed Brown’s "smartest guy in the room" persona as a locker-room turnoff, Brown addressed the criticism during an Instagram Live session.

"No offense to everybody in sports," Brown declared, "but the bar is f***ing low."

NBA media fanboys love to brag that Brown was an "MIT Fellow."

This is the same guy who claimed he was "undecided" on whether the Earth was flat when Kyrie Irving went down that rabbit hole. He defended Irving’s promotion of an antisemitic film, stuck by Kanye West’s Donda Sports long after everyone else fled and wore a Q-Ray-style magnetic necklace marketed as a device that could supposedly alter the body’s energy frequencies through a smartphone app.

Yet Brown still expects the world to treat him like a generational thought leader who transcends basketball, all while complaining about how unfairly athletes are treated.

In a 2017 interview with The Guardian, Brown complained that athletes were still expected to "shut up and dribble."

Nobody is telling Jaylen Brown to shut up. We’re just asking him to stop acting like he split the atom.
