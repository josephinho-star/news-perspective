Florida AG calls for doctor to lose medical license over 149 alleged illegal abortions

2026-11-25 02:21

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier is pushing to strip a North Miami Beach doctor of his medical license over at least 149 suspected illegal abortions, a number he says could rise to 180 as the investigation continues.

Uthmeier said in a video posted by his office on X that preliminary reports from state health officials indicate Dr. Sylvester Braithwaite allegedly performed the procedures between June 2024 and September 2026.

He said some may have been late-term abortions and called on the Florida Board of Medicine to revoke Braithwaite’s license.

"A medical license is not a license to break the law," Uthmeier said.

Uthmeier cited Florida’s Heartbeat Protection Act and said none of the cases fell under the exceptions he outlined for medical emergencies or victims of rape, incest or human trafficking. He said each alleged violation amounts to a third-degree felony.

"My office is investigating this conduct, and we will follow the facts wherever they lead," Uthmeier said. "And we will prosecute doctors who ignore our laws."

Noah Brandt, executive vice president of the anti-abortion group Live Action, told Fox News Digital that Braithwaite should lose his medical license for good and face prosecution if investigators confirm the allegations.

"If these allegations are true, at least 149 innocent preborn children were killed in direct violation of Florida law, and each of those children deserves justice," Brandt said. "The killing of innocent human beings, including through abortion, should be illegal, and the fundamental human rights of every person, born and preborn, must be protected under the law."

Brandt said Braithwaite should be "prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law" if the allegations are proven.

He also praised Uthmeier for "his commitment to enforcing Florida’s pro-life laws and setting an example for attorneys general across the country."

Florida state Rep. Taylor Yarkosky backed Uthmeier in a reply on X, calling the alleged conduct "the Devil at work" and thanking the attorney general "for upholding the law in Florida, and mostly for protecting the lives of the unborn!"

Uthmeier did not say whether any charges had been filed or how far along the pregnancies were in the cases he described as potentially late-term.

Braithwaite is a board-certified obstetrician and gynecologist who serves as medical director of Women & Teens Healthcare in North Miami Beach, according to his professional biography.

The Florida Department of Health lists his medical license as active and confirms his certification in obstetrics and gynecology.

Fox News Digital reached out to Braithwaite’s office for comment.
