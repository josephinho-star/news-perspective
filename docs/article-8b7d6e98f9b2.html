Fiona the hippo dies after ingesting knitted hat at Cincinnati zoo

2026-11-19 22:09

Fiona the hippo, one of America’s most famous zoo animals, has died after efforts to remove a knitted hat that she swallowed after it fell into her enclosure proved unsuccessful.

“It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency,” the zoo wrote in a post on social media.

With surgery too risky for full-grown hippos such as Fiona, who weighed in the region of 2,500lb (1,113kg), veterinarians tried an alternative method of getting the hat to pass through her system.

The hat fell near Fiona when a visitor to the Cincinnati zoo tossed it in the air, after which it bounced off a ceiling fan and fell into the enclosure, according to the Associated Press. Fiona ate the hat and was initially asymptomatic.

Days later, however, she began to lose appetite and staff at the zoo were forced to feed Fiona more in the hope that she would eventually get the hat through her digestive system.

“Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome. The team performed necessary supportive care today in an attempt to save her, but it was not successful and she did not recover,” the zoo statement said.

The zoo announced on Wednesday that the animal care team was using a vibration plate from a horse farm to help move things along, according to the Associated Press.

Fiona was sedated a second time on Tuesday for evaluation. After an ultrasound, veterinarians could see her intestines still trying to push the hat through. They also used a 9ft scope colonoscopy to view her intestines.

“The hope is that the vibrations will help encourage movement through her digestive tract and help the object pass,” the zoo had said on social media. Fiona remained out of her enclosure while she was receiving treatment.

Footage from the procedure released by the zoo on Wednesday shows her snorting and moving around her enclosure, which vets said was a sign of progress. Her hydration levels were being monitored.

“Her care team even offered her a sniff of coffee, a scent she has always liked, and they were happy to see her respond. Thank you for your continued love and concern for Fiona and her dedicated care team,” the zoo said.

This wasn’t the first time Fiona’s health had been at risk. She became a global sensation after her premature birth in 2017, when she couldn’t stand on her own.

Hopes for Fiona’s recovery had garnered support from across the country including, apparently, from Timothy, another hippo at the San Antonio zoo. Timothy had sent well wishes via Instagram. “I know you’ve got some amazing people looking after you. We’re sending lots of love from Texas to the most beautiful hippo in the world. Hope you’re feeling more like yourself soon!”

Zoo staff published a message on social media reminding visitors to be careful when near habitats and to secure belongings.

Fiona was born six weeks premature at just 29lb (13.2kgs), falling below the lowest recorded birth weight for her species at the time. Because she was initially too weak to nurse, Cincinnati Zoo staff hand-milked her mother while documenting Fiona’s recovery through photos and videos that captivated the internet.

Her survival then offered a much-needed positive chapter for the zoo following months of intense public backlash over the fatal shooting of Harambe, the silverback gorilla killed after a child breached his enclosure.

Wildlife facilities have long warned of the dangers posed by foreign objects dropped or blown into animal habitats. Past incidents have demonstrated the fatal risks involved, including a rare antelope that choked to death on a plastic cap of a snack pouch at a Tennessee zoo two years ago, and a polar bear at a German zoo that died in 2014 after swallowing a visitor’s dropped jacket.
