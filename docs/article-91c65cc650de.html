‘Third country’ deportation can resume, US supreme court rules | First Thing

2026-09-30 11:18

Good morning. The supreme court has ruled the Trump administration can resume deporting immigrants to “third countries”, or countries other than their homeland, without giving them any opportunity to contest their removals.

The six-justice conservative majority of the high court temporarily lifted a decision by a lower court that required the administration to provide immigrants sufficient notice and the opportunity to claim fear of torture or persecution in the countries where they were being sent.

What has the administration asked the supreme court to rule on about trans prisoners? The administration asked the justices to allow it to deny gender-affirming care to transgender people in federal prisons. The solicitor general, D John Sauer, wrote that gender-reassignment surgeries, hormone therapy and social accommodations such as undergarments, makeup, padding and wigs “could no longer be justified.”

Why is the EPA union suing the administration? The union, which represents more than 8,000 workers at the Environmental Protection Agency, has filed a lawsuit against the EPA and the agency’s administrator, Lee Zeldin, alleging it illegally terminated a collective bargaining agreement.

Which legal battle has Trump appeared to have conceded? The president has agreed to stop fighting the disbursement of E Jean Carroll’s $5m verdict in her 2023 sexual abuse and defamation civil trial against him, a procedural development as the funds had already been distributed to her.

Trump announces vague ‘morally binding’ AI deal among tech CEOs for ‘tremendous self-policing’

Donald Trump announced Tuesday that the heads of the largest US tech and AI companies had signed on to a “morally binding” agreement to put controls on artificial intelligence. He announced the deal after a White House lunch with top tech executives.

The agreement, which Trump suggested would allow the companies to perform “tremendous self-policing” of their products and technologies, outlines a vague framework for how AI firms would establish safety standards. Trump also signed an executive order renaming artificial intelligence to “superintelligence”, similar to a declaration he made at the UN last week.

What new product has OpenAI announced? Less than 24 hours after OpenAI said it would scrap the launch of a model over safety concerns, the company debuted a whole new suite of artificial intelligence tools, including “dots”, agents it said were “more ambitious” than ChatGPT and a “whole new way to work with AI.”

Why is Greta Garbo making AI headlines? More than a century after her screen debut and decades after she was laid to rest, the actor has been reanimated by AI for a Swedish advert. Peter Bradshaw has given it a damning one-star review.

US Senate blocks resolution demanding accountability over Israeli violence in West Bank

The US Senate rejected along mostly party lines a Democratic resolution that would have forced the state department to report to Congress on violence in the occupied West Bank, including the killings of nine American citizens by Israeli settlers or security forces over the last few years.

Why did an Israeli-bound plane make an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia? A passenger jet travelling from Dubai made an emergency landing, briefly raising concerns about a possible hijacking and prompting Israel to scramble warplanes. Reports said the incident stemmed from a feud between the plane’s two pilots, and everyone onboard was safe.

What has Zohran Mamdani announced about antisemitism? The New York City mayor has debuted a sweeping plan to combat antisemitism. The strategy includes increased security funding for synagogues and an increased focus on incorporating Jewish history into public school education. The plan is seen as a move to assuage critics who see his steadfast denunciations of Israel as antisemitic.

Why are two authors suing the city of Los Angeles? Jenan Matari, a Palestinian-American author, and Nora Lester Murad, a Jewish-American writer, are taking the action after public library officials scrapped their Read Palestine Week book talks.

Texts about alleged Cornell gang-rape released after investigation reopened

Excerpts of text messages reportedly exchanged by a defendant in the Cornell University gang-rape case and the woman who claimed she was assaulted were published by CBS News on Tuesday, a day after New York prosecutors said they were reopening a criminal investigation.

Two of the alleged perpetrators were subsequently expelled after an internal investigation by Cornell – while the other five were allowed to resume their studies at the Ivy League institution after writing an essay, attending a workshop, or serving a short suspension. None were ever arrested or charged.
