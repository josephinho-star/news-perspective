Fresh proof Hochul and Mamdani were clueless about their pied-à-terre tax

2026-11-18 10:00

Published Nov. 18, 2026, 5:00 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

New figures from the Department of Finance make it painfully clear: Gov. Kathy Hochul and Mayor Zohran Mamdani had no idea what they were doing when they pushed through their pied-à-terre tax.

The point was just to give the mayor a “tax the rich” victory; figuring out how anything actually works was irrelevant.

Consider: DOF now says only about 7,500 homeowners will get billed — less than half the 17,000 notices sent to potentially liable taxpayers and far less than the 13,000 the city and state originally expected to pay.

Not to mention the “enemies list” of 900,000 properties — with names and addresses attached — that Mamdani’s team originally released as maybe subject to the tax.

Yes, DOF now estimates that revenue from the tax — which hits second homes in the city worth more than $5 million, or more than $1 million for co-ops and condos — will still meet the $500 million projection (though the city refuses to show its math).

But this means city and state number-crunchers were also way off in their estimates of the tax’s average hit on non-resident homeowners.

Based on the new figures, that comes out to a small fortune: about $67,000 per unit — on top of existing property taxes: 19.843% on a Class 1 condo, so starting at $198,430 for a $1 million pied-à-terre.

It’s also in addition to other taxes — including state income taxes that many of these owners still pay.

Remember: These semi-out-of-towners use far fewer city and state services (no public schools, just for starters) than full-time residents — since they’re not here most of the time.

Yet they’re likely to be important contributors to the local economy — and to the city and state tax base.

And to be donors to local charities.

If the additional tax prompts them to pick up stakes, New York loses all of that.

But Kat and Zo clearly weren’t thinking — or just didn’t care — about that: pandering to childish ideology is all that matters.

Hence Mamdani’s vindictive video slamming Citadel CEO Ken Griffin for the crime of owning a pricey apartment in Manhattan . . . and Griffin opted to expand his operations in Florida rather than New York.

How much more prime business will these crusades drive away? The mayor and gov have no clue.

Fine, the tax seems set to meet Hochul and Mamdani’s initial revenue projections . . . for now.

But none of the intermediate numbers worked out, which means that’s a matter of sheer luck, not of anything resembling a solid understanding of who owns these places and why, or even what the average pied-à-terre is worth.

And when your leaders are designing taxes by throwing darts blindfolded, it’s a pretty safe bet that when their luck runs out, they’ll wind up sticking it to everyday New Yorkers.
