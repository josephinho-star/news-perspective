I voted to open trade with China. 25 years later, it’s time for us to fix that mistake

2026-11-16 10:00

Twenty-five years ago, in December 2001, China officially joined the World Trade Organization (WTO) and fully entered the global economy. This was only possible because a year earlier the United States granted the communist country permanent normal trading relations status, granting China a level of access to our economy normally reserved for friends and allies like Great Britain and Israel. I was one of 83 senators who voted to do so.

I now look back on it as the worst vote I made in 14 years in the Senate.

Since that day, China has grown into an economic and military powerhouse. It has used that status malevolently, conducting genocides targeting Uyghurs, Tibetans and Falun Gong practitioners. It has organized an alliance of authoritarians to menace the free world, and it has empowered human rights abuses across the globe. It is now building up its nuclear weapons capacity to pair with a naval fleet now more numerous than our own.

By allowing China to join the WTO, the U.S. made a fundamental policy error. We believed — or deceived ourselves into believing — that a communist China engaged in the global economy would grow more open and free. We thought a China more exposed to the free world would be gravitationally attracted to freedom.

This has proven to be a tragic miscalculation.

It is long past time to end China’s access to permanent normal trade relations with the United States.

We are locked in a new Cold War. We never granted the Soviet Union favored trading status. We never even considered it. They were our declared enemy and we were in a well-defined struggle for which system — communist dictatorship or capitalist democracy — would prevail.

The Chinese communists went at it differently. They snuck up on us. They feigned friendship until they believed they were strong enough to challenge us. Still, they claim that our systems are not at irreconcilable odds. They complain that the U.S. just wants to limit their growth and deny them their rightful place in a multipolar world.

This is all just a mask for their true aspiration of global dominance. They have been at war with us for decades and we are just now realizing it.

By denying the Soviets access to our markets, we greatly limited their ability to finance their war machine. When they needed more money to compete with President Ronald Reagan’s defense buildup, their economy couldn’t compete and they ultimately collapsed.

We starved the beast then. It is time to do it again.

We can start with a proposition to the Chinese Communist Party: Allow religious freedom for all your people, or we will begin the process of disengaging Western economies from yours.

We never granted the Soviet Union favored trading status. We never even considered it. They were our declared enemy and we were in a well-defined struggle for which system — communist dictatorship or capitalist democracy — would prevail.

Call it a Helsinki Accord 2.0, where the West wants to secure human rights in China in exchange for continuing to allow access to our markets.

This will stir debate and energy inside China for reform. The loss of American markets would be devastating for the Chinese economy. To maintain access to our markets, China must start granting basic human rights to its own people.

The estimated 100 million Christians inside China would relish the right to practice their faith without restraint and the constant watchful eye of Big Brother. Millions of Falun Gong practitioners would be ecstatic at such a prospect. Tibet and East Turkistan (Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region) would rejoice. Members of these groups, including those who have been forced to flee, would be stirring for such a heavenly shift in their homeland.

Furthermore, such a move would simply produce what we anticipated out of the original trade agreement with China — greater freedom for the Chinese people.

If China refuses, it gives more support to America and the West to move forward with untangling our two economies. It helps explain to the American public why we can no longer freely trade with a China run by a brutal communist dictatorship. If the citizens of China cannot freely choose the path for their own souls, we will have no part in funding and assisting such a heartless and cruel government.

One of the great capacities of democracies is the ability to ultimately recognize a mistake and correct it. The free world made one 25 years ago. It is time to fix it.
