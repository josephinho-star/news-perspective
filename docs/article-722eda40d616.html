Tributes flow for Molly Meldrum: Anthony Albanese, Jason Donovan and others remember music industry great

2026-11-23 00:48

Friends and fans of Molly Meldrum have remembered the music industry great, who died on Monday at the age of 83.

Meldrum spent more than a decade hosting the ABC music show Countdown, leaving a lasting legacy on the Australian music landscape.

Anthony Albanese was among those paying tribute. “With an interview style that was inimitable and an enthusiasm that was uncontainable, Molly Meldrum loved music almost as much as we loved him,” the wrote in a post on X.

“On Countdown and beyond, he championed Australian artists and brought international acts to our shores. He gave new artists a leg up and established artists a welcoming embrace. And over the years as he implored us to do ourselves a favour, he became a central part of the Australian soundtrack. We were lucky to have him. May Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum rest in peace.”

Jason Donovan said Meldrum was the “very heart” of the music program while he was growing up, calling the man a “truly unique character, a great Australian and one of this country’s greatest pioneers and ambassadors for music and entertainment”.

“He was loved, recognised and respected around the world for the extraordinary contribution he made to Australian music and the artists he championed,” Donovan wrote on Facebook. “There will never be another quite like him.”

The former Neighbours star shared a memory of Meldrum appearing on an episode alongside Kylie Minogue and Guy Pearce, adding: “What a day that was!”

The television presenter Richard Wilkins remembered his longtime friend on-air on Monday morning. The pair used to travel around the world for interviews, Wilkins said, which led to great memories and hilarious stories.

“Australian music would not be Australian music without Molly’s incredible contribution,” Wilkins said. “I shared so many overseas trips with Molly, which was a joy and a curse. He lost 13 passports, that was the tally I got to.

“We went from a Michael Jackson gig in Munich to see Bruce Springsteen in Paris. That was the sort of thing we used to get up to back in the day.”

Gavin Wood, a longtime friend of Meldrum’s who worked on Countdown, said the host was “one of the craziest, most colourful, funniest and most beautiful people you could ever have the pleasure of meeting”.

“What he has done for the Australian music industry is simply phenomenal,” Wood wrote. “His contribution, passion, enthusiasm and unwavering commitment to Australian artists helped shape an entire generation of music and entertainment.

“There will never be another Molly Meldrum. You simply cannot measure anyone against him.”

Bands and artists echoed those sentiments.

Meldrum produced Russell Morris’s 1969 single the Real Thing, which reached #1 on the charts. “He was a unique, and loveable individual,” Morris wrote on Facebook. “I was lucky enough to call him my friend, and mentor. The journey we went on was pretty surreal – his creative energy and insights were exceptional … He achieved things that were truly great. Things that sometimes seemed impossible.”

“He was such a huge presence in the Australian music industry and was a great friend and supporter of so many up and coming bands and artists – ourselves included,” the band Human Nature wrote. “Thanks for everything Molly. Rest easy.”

The singer Peter Andre posted a photo with Meldrum, praising him for helping to elevate his career and saying “this really hurts”.

“If it wasn’t for this man, I would not of had any of the chances I had to make it in any way in the entertainment industry back when it was near impossible to get noticed,” Andre wrote. “He believed in me from day one and I am forever grateful to Ian ‘Molly’ Meldrum.”

Kate Ceberano wrote: “He had no agenda he wanted, no praise, he never basked in any reflected glory or self pride. He simply wanted the world to hear what he could hear, wanted you to see what he was looking at and then blow it up to 11. Way up sky high. No dream is too big. No cringe, withholding nothing.

“He was the fan all artists wish to have.”

Iva Davies of Icehouse also paid tribute: “He was such a bright, larger than life figure in our Australian music scene for so many years … Like the rest of the music community that owed so much to his support, the band and I are so thankful that Molly was a part of our lives and music. There will never be another like him and he will be sorely missed by so many.”

The Australian Recording Industry Association issued its own praise of Meldrum’s work, noting that both he and the Countdown program were the first inductees into the Aria Hall of Fame who weren’t recording artists, back in 2014.

“It is impossible to imagine what Australian music would look like without Molly’s long and storied career,” said Annabelle Herd, Aria’s chief executive. “He shaped the music taste of a generation of Australians and gave so much of himself for the good of Australian artists, for which our whole industry is eternally grateful.”

The St Kilda football club remembered Meldrum as a “lifelong Sainter”, saying the organisation was deeply saddened by his death.

“An iconic and passionate supporter of our football club, Molly changed and brightened the lives of so many across a storied career,” the club wrote on Facebook.
