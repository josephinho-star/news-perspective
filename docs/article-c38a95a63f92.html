Trump administration asks federal court to dismiss its lawsuit over US Senate election in Alaska – live

2026-11-20 01:44

Trump administration asks court to dismiss its lawsuit over Alaska Senate election

In a legal filing on Thursday, the Trump administration asked a federal appeals court to dismiss the lawsuit it filed before the midterm elections seeking to block the certification of the US Senate race in Alaska should Democrat Mary Peltola win.

Peltola did win the election, with 50.13% of the vote, giving her more than the combined vote totals of all other candidates, including the incumbent Republican senator, Dan Sullivan, and a second Daniel Sullivan, who was reportedly recruited to enter the race by Democrats to siphon votes away from the sitting senator.

The suit was filed at the direction of Donald Trump even though Alaska’s state supreme court had already ruled that the second Sullivan’s candidacy was legal.

The administration moved on Thursday to have an appeals court declare its case moot, two days after the race was decided.

Peltola faced significant controversy on the campaign trail, after the New York Times reported that her staffers were involved in a scheme to recruit the “decoy” Dan Sullivan.

Peltola denied any involvement in encouraging the second Sullivan’s run.

Peltola, who is Yup’ik, will join the Minnesota senator-elect Peggy Flanagan, a member of the White Earth band of Ojibwe, as the first two Native American women to serve in the US Senate.

The legal filing came one day after Alaska concluded its vote-count and used ranked-choice tabulation to decide races where no candidate got more then 50% of the votes cast, including the election for the state’s sole representative in Congress.

In that race, Alaska’s incumbent Republican congressman, Nick Begich, narrowly defeated independent challenger Bill Hill based on the ballots cast for a spoiler candidate, Eric Hafner who was allowed to contest the race as a Democrat, over the objections of the state’s Democratic party, and despite having no connection to the state and currently being incarcerated in federal prison in New York.

Trump pardons Pete Rose, late baseball star convicted of federal tax crimes

Donald Trump, who bragged in a 2016 debate that not paying any federal income tax certain years “makes me smart”, and is now suing the New York Times for defamation over its reporting on his taxes, announced on Thursday that he has pardoned the late baseball star Pete Rose, who served five months in federal prison in 1990 after being convicted of filing false tax returns.

Rose pleaded guilty that year to failing to pay $162,703.15 in taxes on $354,967.60 in undeclared income from personal appearances, baseball card shows, the sale of personal memorabilia and gambling winnings.

The two felony convictions came a year after Rose was banned from baseball for life after admitting that he had gambled on baseball games.

Trump issued his posthumous clemency to Rose two years after the fallen star’s death, and two weeks after the president told 60 Minutes that even if, after his own death, it was a condition to enter heaven that he forgive enemies like James Comey and Nancy Pelosi, “I probably cannot do that, no, because I do hate some of my enemies and I always will.”

Religious messages that US agriculture secretary, Brooke Rollins, emailed to the agency’s 100,000 employees were unconstitutional, and she must be careful to avoid proselytizing when communicating with workers, a federal judge in San Francisco ruled on Thursday.

US district court judge Trina Thompson, who was nominated by Joe Biden, said the emails violated the First Amendment’s establishment clause, pointing to her position of authority, her use of an official email account and the messages suggesting that Christianity is the shared faith of USDA workers.

The Court ordered “that the Secretary must recognize certain boundaries when disseminating messages which convey religious content”.

Rollins sent the messages, which included Christian messages and images, from her official email account on holidays such as Independence Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas and Easter. She did not send messages commemorating holidays from other religions.

“Happy Easter – He is Risen indeed!” Rollins wrote in an April 5 email marking Easter.

“And so, like the very first disciples to encounter our risen Lord in the Upper Room almost two thousand years ago, this Easter let us too be alive with hope, full of Paschal joy, and confident in the mission each of us has been called for,” she added.

On 23 December 23, 2025, the judge noted, “Rollins emailed USDA employees a Christmas message containing a video in which she stated that ‘The spirit of generosity flows from the very first Christmas when God gave us the greatest gift possible, the gift of his Son and our Savior Jesus Christ, who came to free us from our sins and open the door to eternal life. This is the reason for the season’”.

Senator Elissa Slotkin endorses Chris Van Hollen over Chuck Schumer in Senate majority leader race

Senator Elissa Slotkin, a Michigan Democrat, has endorsed her colleague Chris Van Hollen in his bid to replace Chuck Schumer as the party’s leader in the Senate when Democrats take the majority in January.

Slotkin, a former CIA analyst, said in a social media video announcing her support for Van Hollen that that she has been “vocal for many, many years about the importance in the Democratic Party of getting a new generation of leadership”.

“The American public want a new generation, and they want big and bold ideas,” Slotkin said. “I’ve been clear as well that I don’t think that senator Schumer is best positioned to be our next leader.”

In a less-than-ringing endorsement, Slotkin also said that because she wants Schumer out, she is backing Van Hollen.

“I’m encouraged that people like Chris Van Hollen got in the race, and because I’ve been clear that I wasn’t supporting Schumer, I will be supporting Chris Van Hollen,” she said. “But I think no matter the outcome, it’s going to be imperative for our leaders to demonstrate how they’re going to incorporate that next generation and what big and bold ideas specifically they’re going to put forward in the first 100 days.”

Van Hollen responded to Slotkin’s statement by saying that they agree: “the American people spoke, and we need new leadership in the United States Senate. Now is the time to act boldly, and we’re going to work together to deliver results.”

A day earlier, Van Hollen’s Maryland colleague, senator Angela Alsobrooks, said that she would be supporting Schumer, but framed it as a comittment she had made before he entered the race. Calling Van Hollen “a friend and respected colleague” with “an absolute right” to challenge Schumer, she said that she would continue to support Schumer. “I offered him my commitment, and I am a person of my word.”

“I respect her position that she made a commitment prior to when I joined the Majority Leader race,” Van Hollen said in a statement after Alsobrooks came in for some criticism for her decision. “These discussions among our party are critical to move our democracy and our caucus forward. And above all, they must be based in mutual respect and civility – there is no space in this discussion for vitriol or hostility.”

Rapping Republican congressman-elect Anthony Constantino skips orientation to dine with Roger Stone

Anthony Constantino, an incoming Republican congressman who called for Donald Trump to pardon Sean ‘Diddy’ Combs in a foul-mouthed victory speech, confirmed a report on Thursday that he skipped orientation this week to dine with former Trump adviser Roger Stone in Florida instead.

“I let my staff pick the office so I can work on more important things such as eating Chicken Parmesan with Roger Stone while planning how to defend us from Radical Democrats,” Constantino said in a statement to the congressional news outlet Punchbowl.

Stone was seen with Constantino earlier this month at a rally in Rome, New York, when JD Vance endorsed him in a speech late in the campaign.

Constantino, a Trump-endorsed sticker magnet from northern New York, narrowly won the race to represent New York’s 21st congressional district, defeating Democrat Blake Gendebien just 1.5 points in a district former Republican congresswoman Elise Stefanik won by over 24 points in 2024.

Constantino was spotted in Washington DC last week, when he told a TMZ reporter that his call for Trump to free Combs from federal prison after his conviction on prostitution-related crimes, “caught attention.”

“I’m the most talked about person entering Congress right now,” he added.

The newly elected congressman is already facing calls from local Republican officials to focus on his work, instead of promoting his sideline as a right-wing rapper best known for a music video attacking Zohran Mamdani, the mayor of New York City, which is over 200 miles south of his district along Canadian border.

“The City of Plattsburgh Republican Committee is calling on Congressman-elect Anthony Constantino, to immediately change course, and put the urgent needs of NY-21 first, or face a formal call for his expulsion from Congress,” a local party official said in a statement on Thursday.

“We do not care about your rap career. We do not care about anything that does not directly serve the people of NY-21,” David Souliere, the chairman of the Plattsburgh Republican Committee said. “The residents of this district are counting on real results … if this is not your singular priority, then it is time for you to seriously question whether you belong in this role at all.”

California attorney general opens investigation into Nvidia and co-founder Jensen Huang

California’s attorney general, Rob Bonta, said on Thursday that his office has “served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia” that is related to reports that the chip-maker’s co-founder, Jensen Huang, had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

“The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors. My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang’s actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information — and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law,” Bonta said in a statement. “Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors.”

An appeals court decided on Thursday that Democratic New Jersey representative LaMonica McIver will continue to face charges related to a skirmish outside an immigration detention center last year when she attempted to conduct an oversight visit there.

The court’s decision to decline McIver’s request to overturn the court’s earlier ruling comes just a day after she pressed the homeland security secretary for the Trump administration’s aggressive immigration enforcement operations, telling the secretary: “You are killing people.”

Now, with the court’s decision, she is facing a likely trial for the May 2025 incident.

“Because of a weaponized Department of Justice, and because I dared to do my job and conduct oversight of ICE, I am facing 17 years in prison,” McIver said in a statement on Thursday. “Now, due to the disappointing decision by the third circuit, we are all one step closer to a world in which the president of the United States can try to imprison people simply because he does not like what they stand for.”

McIver was charged last year with assaulting, resisting, impeding and interfering with federal officers. The prosecution led to a lengthy legal battle, with McIver claiming the prosecution represented a political attack by the Trump administration.

Democratic US senator Adam Schiff has penned an op-ed in the Wall Street Journal warning about “the systemic risk to the economy” posed by the AI industry – and demanding that Congress rule out a taxpayer-funded bailout should the sector collapse.

Highlighting the turmoil surrounding Nvidia since the spectacular exit of its CEO Jensen Huang, Schiff writes:

This technology [artificial intelligence] has boundless potential to enhance our lives and expand our individual and collective capacity. But as the turmoil around Nvidia reminds us, this swift and massive investment also carries significant risk that an industry downturn or a bubble could destabilize the American economy. In the first six days of the scandal involving Nvidia and its former CEO, the sector lost $500bn in value.

Given the risks to our economy and investors, Congress must make sure that industry understands this:No company is too big to fail, and in the event of a financial collapse of the tech sector, there will be no taxpayer-funded bailout.

The AI industry, after less than five years of market power, is already estimated to represent 3.6% of our nation’s economy. But much of its explosive build-out is financed by an opaque debt structure and expectations of increasing profitability. This may be the biggest economic gamble in American history, and policymakers should make clear that Congress and American taxpayers won’t act as its fail-safe should this industry falter under its own immense weight.

Republican congressman Brad Finstad requests hand recount in Minnesota House race after final count shows him losing by 324 votes

In one of the last US House races to be decided, congressman Brad Finstad, a Minnesota Republican, asked for a publicly funded recount after the state canvassing board’s final count showed him losing his seat to Democrat Jake Johnson by 324 votes, the CBS News affiliate WCCO-TV reported.

Nik Marda, Johnson’s campaign manager, told the outlet that the hand recount in 21 southern Minnesota counties, would take place “likely right after Thanksgiving.”

“What we know from history is over the last 40 years, no full recount in a US House race has moved the vote total by this amount,” Marda said. “Nowhere close to it.”

Trump's nominee to lead cybersecurity agency says January 6 2021 storming of US Capitol was not 'an attack'

Donald Trump’s nominee to lead the US homeland security department’s cybersecurity and infrastructure security agency (Cisa), said on Thursday that the US Capitol was not attacked on January 6 2021.

At his Senate confirmation hearing, the nominee, Michael LoSapio, was asked by senator Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat, “Was there an attack on the Capitol of January 6 2021?”

After initially replying, “I don’t know,” LoSapio said, “No, I don’t believe it was an attack.”

LoSapio, the chief information security officer for Palantir, a technology firm founded by JD Vance’s mentor, Peter Thiel, also refused to admit that Joe Biden had won the popular vote in the 2020 presidential election.

“I’m not going to answer the question about an election six years ago,” LoSapio told Blumenthal. The senator noted that since election security is a central part of the agency’s mandate, by refusing to accept the fact that Trump lost the 2020 election, LoSapio was “disqualified from the position that you’ve been nominated to fill”.

The week after the 2020 election, as Trump falsely claimed that count had been rigged, Cisa was one of a number of federal agencies with oversight of election infrastructure to issue a statement calling the election “the most secure in American history” and noting that there was “no evidence that any voting system deleted or lost votes, changed votes, or was in any way compromised.”

After he returned to the White House last year, Trump signed an April 2025 presidential memorandum directing executive offices to take steps to address supposed “risks from Chris Krebs,” who lead Cisa in 2020. “Krebs, through CISA, falsely and baselessly denied that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen, including by inappropriately and categorically dismissing widespread election malfeasance and serious vulnerabilities with voting machines,” Trump’s memorandum claimed.

House lawmakers announce formation of bipartisan AI safety caucus

Three House lawmakers announced the formation of a bipartisan group focused on artificial intelligence safety, the first of its kind. Republican congressman Nathaniel Moran of Texas and Democrats, George Whitesides of California and Don Beyer of Virginia, unveiled the the House AI Safety and Innovation caucus, which will also focus on the “security risks posed by increasingly advanced AI systems while ensuring the United States remains a global leader in innovative AI technologies”.

“We don’t have to choose between innovation and safety. We can, and we should, do both. Artificial intelligence affects every American, and it should not be a partisan issue,” Moran said in a statement.

Meanwhile, Whitesides added: “Now is the time to build bipartisan consensus and advance commonsense guardrails we know will reduce the most serious risks. Americans are rightly concerned about where this technology is heading, but too often the debate in Washington is framed as a choice between doing nothing and banning AI altogether. That’s a false choice.”

The caucus will, among other things, develop legislative proposals and endorse existing bipartisan bills that create guardrails against the most serious AI risks, as well as meeting with AI developers and technical experts when safety incidents occur.

Democratic lawmakers targeted by Trump for video reminding troops to refuse 'illegal orders' address reporters one year later

The six Democratic members of Congress who Donald Trump accused of “sedition, punishable by death” after they posted a 90-second long video reminding active-duty US service members to refuse “illegal orders”, are speaking to reporters a year after they drew the president’s wrath.

A reminder that senators Mark Kelly and Elissa Slotkin and congressional representatives Maggie Goodlander, Jason Crow, Chris DeLuzio and Chrissy Houlahan all posted a video on social media saying that the Trump administration “is pitting our uniformed military and intelligence professionals against American citizens”.

They added: “You must refuse illegal orders.”

Trump said that “each one of these traitors to our Country should be arrested and put on trial.” He also reposted a message from another user reading: “Hang them George Washington would!”

Federal prosecutors had sought an indictment against the Democrats who participated in the video, but a grand jury in Washington DC declined to indict the six lawmakers, all of whom have military or intelligence backgrounds.

In February a US judge also blocked the Pentagon’s effort to reduce Kelly’s retired military rank and pension pay for his role in the video.

“I think it was important for us to come together a year later to say, number one, we stand behind the video we made. Number two, we won’t be intimidated out of calling out threats to our democracy,” Slotkin told reporters today on Capitol Hill. “We stand behind our men and women in uniform for the amazing work that they do, and I think we feel strongly that if this is the thing we need to do to stand up for the country we love, we have no problem doing that.”

Kelly added: “I will tell you that none of us are backing down. Last year, I said we were the first ones through the breach, and we’re seeing that now. Trump is threatening more members of Congress with prosecution for doing their jobs, and asking for asking questions that he just doesn’t want to answer.”
