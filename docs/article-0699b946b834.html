Senate further advances bipartisan permitting reform bill

2026-11-18 19:31

The Senate took a series of key votes on Wednesday advancing a permitting reform bill that seeks to speed up the nation’s ability to build infrastructure and energy.

The upper chamber advanced the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) 65-31, helping it move toward final passage and overcoming the Senate’s 60-vote threshold.

This is not a surprise, as the legislation has advanced in other recent votes along similar margins, though it represents the formal clearance of a key hurdle to the bill becoming law.

The measure is still expected to go through an amendment process as some lawmakers have insisted on tweaks, including addressing concerns from tribes that its changes to historic preservation law don’t give them adequate input into projects that may impact them. It’s not clear whether these or any other amendments will make it into the bill’s final text.

The legislation seeks to address longstanding gripes from developers that the U.S.’s environmental review and other processes pose significant hurdles to the nation’s buildout.

Its supporters say that the bill will help the American economy and bring down its planet-warming emissions at the same time through significant benefits for renewable energy.

Opponents warn, however, that BAAJA could be bad for the environment as it bolsters fossil fuels and mining, loosens environmental laws and limits the ability of a project’s opponents to challenge it in court.

While the bill appears highly likely to pass the Senate, its path in the House is a bit less certain. It has bipartisan House support, but it also faces some opposition from progressive Democrats. Meanwhile, House Energy and Commerce Chairman Brett Guthrie (R-Ky.) has raised pieces of the bill he’d like to change.

It is competing with a crowded lame duck agenda that includes a lapse in federal funding in December if Congress does not act. But the bill has support from the White House, which could help push it through.

The legislation seeks to boost the nation’s ability to build power lines, which is particularly key to helping renewable energy projects get onto the grid, including by allowing federal regulators to approve the lines if states don’t act on them.

It also contains provisions that seek to prevent presidents from yanking existing permits from energy of all types — combatting both President Trump’s ability to block already-approved wind projects and moves made by Democratic presidents such as revoking approval from the Keystone XL pipeline.

The bill also allows project developers to sue the government if they can demonstrate a pattern of disparate treatment of certain energy sources.

BAAJA also exempts certain activities including expansions to natural gas pipelines from environmental reviews and limits those reviews to only alternatives that meet the goal of a project’s sponsor company.

It restricts lawsuits against projects by mandating that they be filed within 150 days of a project’s approval and only allows people who weighed in during a formal public comment period to sue.

The bill also seeks to rein in increased electricity costs from large data centers, forcing them to pay for any costs stemming from their high electricity demand. However, these facilities may also benefit from the general provisions that seek to help industry build.
