Legendary LA beach bar files for bankruptcy after revealing $6.7M in debt

2026-11-24 19:23

A legendary Venice Beach bar is drowning in $6.7 million of debt — but the owner insists the party isn’t over.

Whaler LLC, the company behind the Venice Whaler, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection Nov. 3 in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Central District of California, public docket records show.

The beachfront watering hole listed just $283,307 in assets — including its $120,000 liquor license — in financial schedules filed Nov. 17, the Santa Monica Daily Press reported Tuesday.

“We’re just restructuring debt,” owner Michael Dobson told the outlet, adding that “not one thing changes in our operations.”

Dobson said the bankruptcy would help refinance expensive, short-term loans.

The mountain of debt includes a $2.25 million federal COVID disaster loan, roughly $379,000 in state tax liens, $131,041 in IRS payroll taxes and $94,536 in unpaid rent.

The company reported $4.79 million in gross revenue in 2025, up from $4.63 million the previous year.

The financial reckoning comes just weeks after a booze-free stretch stunned regulars at the iconic beachfront haunt.

The Whaler temporarily suspended alcohol service in October, urging customers to visit and “help keep our staff working and our doors open,” The California Post previously reported.

Dobson blamed the interruption on paperwork that wasn’t submitted on time.

During the dry spell, the bar offered $3 pizza slices and $10 burger-and-fries combos to keep customers coming through the doors.

The boozer was pouring up again by Oct. 22.

The Whaler traces its history to 1944 and boasts a star-studded roster of past patrons, including The Beatles, The Doors and The Beach Boys.

Dobson took over the two-story watering hole, which sits steps from the Venice Pier, in 2014.

Its sister location in Santa Monica operates under a separate company and is not part of the bankruptcy case, the Daily Press reported.
