Investigation into Indiana dad over sick ‘hot potatoes’ abuse claims launched hours before killing wife, 2 kids in murder-suicide

2026-11-27 15:15

A twisted dad’s shocking “hot potatoes” abuse claims were being investigated just hours before he murdered his family and turned the gun on himself, according to a report.

Thomas Tkach, 51, fatally shot his wife, Julie Tkach, 53, and daughters, Katharyn Tkach, 17, and Lauren Tkach, 16, in their West Lafayette home on Nov. 18.

On the day of the murders, Indiana Department of Child Services received four tips to its hotline of abuse and threats by Thomas Tkach toward his family, according to 13 WTHR, which cited the agency’s Nov. 25 report.

The reports were “substantively similar” and claimed that the sick dad abused one of his daughters in the spring or summer by throwing scalding hot potatoes at her, causing burns.

He also once told his wife he’d “punch her until she can’t call for help,” and his behavior had caused one of the teens “increased emotional distress” and caused her grades to fall, the report said.

A case manager had reached out to Julie Tkach by phone and text on Nov. 18, but the mother didn’t answer, the report said. The case manager also left their card after attempting unsuccessfully to do an unannounced check at the home later that day.

The case manager was able to reach Thomas Tkach by phone Nov. 18, telling him they would need to see his child by the next morning, the report said.

The dad agreed but said he needed to talk to his wife about scheduling while assuring the agent “the children were safe and that he worked for the Tippecanoe County Sheriff’s Department,” the report said.

The following day, the DCS was notified the girls were dead.

Officers had rushed to the home on Nov. 18 after receiving a call from one of the daughters saying “her father had a gun and was threatening to shoot the family.”

When cops arrived, they attempted to intervene by using a loudspeaker to tell Thomas Tkach to come outside. Then they fired less-lethal rounds through the window to create a hole to send a drone in.

Once officers got inside, they found the bodies of Thomas Tkach and one of the girls on the second floor – while his wife and the other daughter were dead on the first floor.

The Tkach girls all died from multiple gunshot wounds, and their deaths are being classified as homicides, according to preliminary autopsy results from the Tippecanoe County Coroner’s Office.

Thomas Tkach died from a self-inflicted gunshot wound to the head, with his death being ruled a suicide.
