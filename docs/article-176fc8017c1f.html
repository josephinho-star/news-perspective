I'm Gen Z: If the GOP wants my vote, stop the speeches and fix the housing crisis

2026-09-04 09:00

I’m going to Dallas next week to attend the 2026 Republican Midterm Convention. I'm not going for the merch and not for the parties. I’m going because Republicans are about to get two nights of primetime television, the first midterm political convention in American history, and if we waste that stage just talking to ourselves, we will deserve a Blue Wave on Election Day, November 3.

Let me say the quiet part at full volume: We are running against a Democratic Party that has stopped pretending. Democratic socialists are winning primaries, and their platform is not "more Medicaid." It is a new constitution, public ownership of the economy, and the end of the United States Senate.

President Trump has called members of the DSA communists, and he wasn’t being cute. If we lose Congress to that coalition, it will not be because America secretly wanted commissars. It will be because we could not look a 20-year-old like me in the eye and tell him why our side will let him buy a house, keep a paycheck, and speak without getting hurt.

Gen Z is not a lost cause. Young men already moved toward the GOP in 2024. Housing is their number-one issue, and the cost of living is the country’s number-one issue. They do not care about our process stories. They care whether this country still works for people who were not born holding a down payment.

Here is what I want to hear from the stage at the American Airlines Center next week – highlight the wins that are real, and stop burying them under slogans.

So here is what I want to hear from that stage at American Airlines Center: Highlight the wins that are real, and stop burying them under slogans.

The border is not a vibe. Illegal crossings collapsed, catch-and-release is dead, and net migration went negative for the first time in half a century. Fentanyl deaths dropped more than 20 percent in a year. That is not "tough talk." That is fewer dead classmates.

The Working Families Tax Cuts made the 2017 rates permanent, with no tax on tips, no tax on overtime, no tax on Social Security, Trump Accounts for kids, and a bigger Child Tax Credit. That is money that stays with a bartender, a welder or a nurse working a double. Do not assume young voters already know, because their algorithm has been feeding them the opposite story.

School choice is expanding, hostages came home, and factories are being announced again. And when we remember Charlie Kirk, who was killed on a campus last September for doing the one thing Gen Z actually respects—showing up and arguing in public—it should not be a 90-second tribute and a cut to commercial. His death is the argument. If a 31-year-old can be shot for debating college kids, free speech is not a branding exercise. It is a survival issue.

Then make promises that sound like they were written by someone who has paid rent in this decade.

Promise a housing offensive that treats supply like a national emergency: Smash the zoning cartel, speed permits, and stop turning every starter home into a museum piece for people who already own one. Promise a first-time-buyer track that is not another 400-page bill named after a senator. Promise the next Congress will ban members from trading individual stocks. Young people think the game is rigged, and they are not crazy.

Promise trade schools and apprenticeships with the same intensity we give tax rates. A generation drowning in degrees and debt does not need another lecture that college is the dream.

They need a path that does not require a $90,000 ticket to learn a job America actually needs. Promise energy abundance like you mean it, not as a culture-war prop but as a cost-of-living policy. Electricity, gas, groceries, and rent move together. If we cannot explain that chain on national television, we will lose affordability to people who will nationalize the grocery store and call it compassion.

Promise campuses will not be permission slips for political violence, that boys are not a problem to be managed, and that we will stop importing labor to undercut the kid who stayed and showed up for work.

That is the speech, not "our values" and not "the swamp": specific, measurable, and aimed at the cohort that can still flip.

Democrats want this convention to look like a circus, and that is fine, because circuses draw a crowd. The question is what the crowd hears when the lights hit. Vice President JD Vance opens Wednesday, and President Trump closes Thursday. Between those two nights, the country will decide whether Republicans are a governing party with a future or a nostalgia act with better production value.

I am tired of hearing that Gen Z is "too far gone." That is what losers say, so they don’t have to do the work. This generation is transactional, anti-incumbent, and allergic to being lied to. They voted against Trump, then they voted for him, and they will vote against us just as fast if we talk like consultants. Next week is the cleanest shot we will get before November: primetime in Dallas.

If we cannot win that argument, we don’t get to blame the media, the map, or the kids. We get to blame the people who had the microphone and used it to congratulate themselves.

See you in Dallas. Don’t blow it.
