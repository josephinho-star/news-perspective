US attorney threatens probe of Garcia over Trump investigation remarks

2026-11-17 15:19

A U.S. Attorney in Texas on Monday sent his fourth letter threatening to investigate a Democratic lawmaker, launching a “preliminary inquiry” into House Oversight Chair Robert Garcia (D-Calif.) and telling him to preserve documents.

Aaron Reitz, the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Texas, has no particular oversight into activities that take place in Washington, D.C. nor of the legislative branch.

But in a late Monday letter he accused Garcia of stretching the power of congressional oversight with his plans to investigate President Trump and those in his orbit.

“A committee’s gavel is not a prosecutor’s commission. Yet you cannot say that the investigations you fantasize about are confined to a lawful legislative end,” Reitz wrote.

Garcia’s team did not immediately respond to a request for comment, but he has defended his plans to investigate the administration as well as Trump’s family members and business dealings – arguing they are rooted in the legislative purpose Reitz says they need.

“Our work is about taking on government corruption, making life more affordable through bringing down costs by actually going after the crooks and the criminals that are causing harm, and then writing good legislation to stop the corruption from happening in the future,” Garcia said last week during an appearance on MSNBC.

“And so it’s outrageous that Donald Trump wants to stop any sort of oversight, and certainly he’s trying to stop us members of Congress from doing our job that is in the Constitution.”

Reitz last week sent a similar letter to Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.), set to be the incoming chair of the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on investigations, as well as Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.)

Over the weekend he wrote a letter to Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) accusing her of being “on the warpath to wield her committee gavel like a tomahawk.” The letter goes on to reference smoke signals while he described the communication as his “first arrow shot.”

A column in Native News Online described the letter as using “insensitive references to Native American culture and history” that draw on “stereotypes that have portrayed Native Americans as violent, primitive or warlike.”

Attorney General Todd Blanche implied last week that he did not have advance knowledge of Retiz’s letter, saying, “I don’t oversee every single thing that’s done.”

But he also took a less aggressive tone when it comes to dealing with congressional investigations.

“The Congress has an extremely important oversight function that allows them and actually requires them to do their jobs in conducting oversight. I have a job as the attorney general to protect the executive’s rights, also to be responsive in the right cases for oversight, which is what we’ve done the last year and a half and that’s something that we’ll continue to do,” he said.

“I am optimistic, as is the president, that we will have a relationship with Congress that is productive.”
