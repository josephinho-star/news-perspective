US envoys to meet Putin and Zelenskyy separately after peace talks see some progress - Europe Live

2026-11-11 12:39

Russia, Ukraine agree on large-scale prisoner swap as some progress made in Abu Dhabi, Russian negotiators say

Russia’s chief negotiator at Ukraine peace talks in Abu Dhabi Vladimir Medinsky said after their conclusion that mutual understanding had been achieved on some issues and that more talks would be held.

Medinsky, in remarks broadcast on Russian state television, said that Russia and Ukraine had agreed on a large-scale prisoner swap, the latest in a long line of such exchanges.

He was cool however on the prospect of European countries joining the talks and said it was unclear what there was for Russia and the European Union to discuss, given what he said was a lack of proposals from the European side, Reuters reported.

Nato countries could sign 'mutual non-aggression pacts' with Russia, Moscow diplomat suggests

Meanwhile, a senior Russian diplomat outlined a series of proposals that could form a part of any future security guarantees for Russia, Reuters reported.

Speaking at a security conference in Moscow, Alexey Drobinin set out three scenarios.

The first option was that of a collective defensive alliance or of bilateral mutual assistance agreements like those Russia has with Belarus and North Korea.

The second possibility he cited included political agreements not to help each other’s adversaries in the event of aggression, like the pact Moscow has with Iran.

And the third option he presented were bilateral mutual non-aggression pacts.

“For example, agreements of this kind could be reached, among other things, with individual Nato member states, which would then receive, for instance, additional individual security guarantees from the Russian Federation,” Drobinin told the conference, according to video of his speech posted online.

G7 looks to address Ukraine's 'pressing financing needs'

The G7 top diplomats stressed the need to secure long-term financial aid for Ukraine, as Kyiv’s allies sought ways to cover its budget beyond 2026, AFP reported.

“We acknowledged the need to address Ukraine’s pressing financing needs for 2027 and beyond in a coordinated way,” said a joint statement issued by foreign ministers of Canada, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, the UK and United States, following a meeting held Monday and Tuesday in Paris.

“We reiterated that negotiations on the future security in Europe must include the Europeans,” it added, as US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine took place in Abu Dhabi.

School bomb alerts pose no real danger, Italy's Meloni says

Meanwhile, just a quick update on Italy: the country’s prime minister Giorgia Meloni said the email bomb alerts sent to school (11:36) pose no danger, but police and other public bodies were monitoring the situation.

“These reports appear to be part of a strategy intended to cause alarm and concern – a matter that is currently under thorough investigation,” she said.

Meloni will chair a meeting on the situation later today to assess what may need to be done after “hundreds” of schools saw their plans for today disrupted by the alarm.

A large number of schools in Northern Ireland were also sent a threatening email, the Northern Ireland Education Authority said, via Reuters, but have been advised to stay open as the authority said police did not regard it as a credible threat.

Kushner also said he thought “it would be great” for Zelenskyy and Putin to meet in person to hold further talks, but acknowledged “the Russians have said they would like to see more progress made before that happens.”

“Clearly, there’s not great trust or love between the leaders, so I think that if we can find a pathway to arrange…I will say this, … these deals do take time.”

US pushing for Europe to be involved in Ukraine war talks with Russia, Kushner says

Kushner also acknowledged that there is “no trust between the sides and that’s to be expected after a conflict like this,” but he hoped he could make progress on that.

He said it is important to have Europe at the table for any discussions on Ukraine and postwar security as “it’s a discussion where they have to be involved,” and pointedly praised his engagement with top Europeans so far.

He said he hoped Putin would “give his blessing” to expanding the format during their meeting in Moscow.

US negotiators to meet Putin in Moscow, Zelenskyy in Dublin next

Speaking after today’s talks, US negotiator (and Donald Trump’s son-in-law) Jared Kushner described the discussions as “very substantive,” even if “pretty tough … after almost eight months of not being together.”

He said he and peace envoy Steve Witkoff would meet Putin in Moscow on Thursday to “go over what was discussed today and try to make progress on some of the issues that we discussed.”

Afterwards, they will fly to Dublin to meet with Ukraine’s Zelenskyy “to debrief with him and further explore some of the ideas that we came up with today,” he said.

Zelenskyy will be in Dublin at the European Political Community summit, alongside other EU leaders.

Kushner said he also hoped to “finalise the humanitarian relief” and “the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners” during tomorrow’s meeting with Putin.

He said the US was also working to “try and see if we can get the Europeans added to the format so that we can make this a multilateral format,” praising already existing discussions with European partners, including the EU, Nato, France, Germany, Italy, and the UK.

Kushner said the key issues revolve around “postwar security” and “security guarantees,” as well as “deconfliction” and monitoring any future ceasefire.

But he acknowledged that “the territorial issues … will be the hardest.”

Meanwhile, David Arakhamia, a close ally of president Volodymyr Zelenskyy and the head of his faction in parliament, said he hoped to keep up “good momentum” in the talks with a view of reaching a solution by the end of the year.

Russia’s chief negotiator at Ukraine peace talks in Abu Dhabi Vladimir Medinsky said after their conclusion that mutual understanding had been achieved on some issues and that more talks would be held.

Medinsky, in remarks broadcast on Russian state television, said that Russia and Ukraine had agreed on a large-scale prisoner swap, the latest in a long line of such exchanges.

He was cool however on the prospect of European countries joining the talks and said it was unclear what there was for Russia and the European Union to discuss, given what he said was a lack of proposals from the European side, Reuters reported.

Zelenskyy also turns to Russia’s demands for security guarantees.

He points out that is not obvious given “no one has attacked Russia for many decades,” and yet “since the second world war, Russia has not gone through even one decade without starting a war or using military force against another country.”

“So when the Russians now talk about a security architecture for themselves, or about security guarantees for Russia, they are hardly talking about real security and peace.

What they really want is to freely interfere in the affairs of other countries, and to use force whenever they want, and to make sure that no one can support liberty inside Russia, because Putin wants to keep his personal power forever and pass on his imperialist and in many ways, simply fascist views to the next generations.”

He also says clearly that Europe “must be at the table” of any talks on ending the war in Ukraine, warning that “whenever Europe does not believe in its own strength, others try to impose their decisions on us.”

He gets warm applause and a standing ovation at the end of his speech.

Zelenskyy also talks about the need for Europe to develop “its own anti-ballistic defence programme,” Freyja to become independent of deliveries from overseas (mostly the US).

He also mentions the importance of drone technologies and learning from Ukraine’s real-life experience.

Zelenskyy brings up the Abu Dhabi discussions, but in quite general terms.

“Yesterday and today our Ukrainian team together with American team has been talking to the Russians in Abu Dhabi.

Putin started this war thinking he could break Ukraine in just days or few weeks. But here we are in the fifth year of the full scale war. The Russians have to sit across from our representatives and have to listen to Ukraine and to talk to us as equals about what comes next. They wanted to decide Ukraine’s future without us, without Ukrainian people, but they failed.”

Zelenskyy says the strait of Hormuz crisis creates a dangerous precedent, asking “what if Russia tries to do the same in Baltic Sea”?

“What if someone tries to repeat what happened in the Strait of Hormuz, this time in other major shipping routes and straits around the world? And who will protect European interests?”

He says the Iran crisis also has an effect on Ukraine, as is experiencing “the shortages of weapons we need for our defence.”

“Today, everyone in the world can see just how connected everything is in our world. Nothing stays local. Nothing. Especially wars and injustice,” he warns.

Zelenskyy then begins his speech proper, with a much more sombre point about the war in Ukraine.

He says that on 11 November, “people in many European countries remember those who lost their lives in war on the anniversary of the end of the first world war.”

“This is not just some distant history, unfortunately. What is happening in the world today brings our thoughts, our feelings, our fears back to those times when Europe and the whole world were turned upside down.”

He says “Russia has fallen for the same illusion as back then more than a hundred years ago. The illusion that it could simply grab more land, a few more colonies, more power and influence at the cost of Ukrainians independence.”

He also points to the events in the Middle East as an additional destabilising element, warning against “rising energy prices” and “one of the worst cost of living crises.”

Zelenskyy also jokes that given the warm relations between Denmark and Ukraine, he regrets the two countries “do not share a border.”

“You have much better neighbours,” he says in a swipe at Russia.

“After all, we have Russia as our neighbour and having a neighbour like Denmark is something any country would dream of right now.”

He then lands another joke about the US and Donald Trump:

“Maybe when someone in America wanted Greenland, he didn’t even realise that what he really wanted was not the land, but to become a little more Danish. And who knows, maybe that was exactly what he needed.”

Back in Abu Dhabi, the Ukrainian negotiating team is now meeting with the European and US envoys for a debrief after the earlier trilateral meeting, a Kyiv delegation source told AFP.
