Hunting, fishing and outdoor recreation fuel $704 billion American industry, new data reveals

2026-11-23 23:05

Turns out, spending a Saturday on the lake or a weekend in the deer stand isn't just good for the soul. It's pretty good for the American economy, too.

According to new data released by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, outdoor recreation contributed a whopping $704.2 billion to the U.S. economy in 2025, accounting for 2.3% of the nation's gross domestic product.

And while that figure includes everything from hiking and camping to RVing and outdoor concerts, fishing and hunting are doing plenty of heavy lifting.

Boating and fishing led all traditional outdoor recreation activities, generating $39.1 billion in economic value added. In fact, it was the largest category across 35 states and Washington, D.C., with Florida ($4.5 billion), California ($3.3 billion) and Texas ($3 billion) leading the way.

Hunting, shooting and trapping weren't far behind, ranking third nationally at $15.1 billion. Texas topped that list with $1.8 billion, followed by California ($849.8 million) and Florida ($831.3 million)

RVing, for those keeping score, came in second at $26.8 billion.

Hunting and fishing support more than just hunters and anglers

Of course, anyone who's ever walked out of a tackle shop $200 poorer than they intended probably isn't shocked that outdoor recreation is a big business.

But the economic impact goes well beyond fishing rods, rifles and bass boats.

Those dollars help support jobs, local businesses and rural communities that depend on visitors coming to hunt, fish and explore the outdoors.

And as the Theodore Roosevelt Conservation Partnership pointed out in its response to the new figures, none of that happens without healthy wildlife populations, accessible public lands and conservation efforts that keep those opportunities available.

The organization cited a National Fish and Wildlife Foundation report finding that conservation spending generates $115.8 billion in economic activity and supports more than 575,000 jobs nationwide.

Meanwhile, the broader outdoor recreation economy continued to grow in 2025, with inflation-adjusted GDP increasing 1.9% and employment climbing 1.7%.

Not bad for a bunch of people who supposedly just want to get away from work.

The bottom line is that hunting, fishing and outdoor recreation aren't just hobbies. They're major economic drivers, and protecting the natural resources that make them possible is an investment that pays off well beyond the woods and water.
