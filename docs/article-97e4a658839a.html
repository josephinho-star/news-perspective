Fantasy buzz: Downs shines in Chiefs debut and who else got the ball in Week 10?

2026-11-16 20:44

The box score doesn't tell you everything. Here are the roles and usage trends from Sunday that stood out.

Rhamondre Stevenson tried to play through his knee injury, but finished with just seven touches and 1.5 fantasy points. He narrowly outsnapped Corey Kiner, who had more rushing attempts (10-6) and ran more routes (14-12). Stevenson handled most of the backfield work early in the game but didn't play in the fourth quarter with the game out of reach. The upcoming bye week gives Stevenson more time to recover and, with TreVeyon Henderson on IR, he should remain the team's lead back. New England has upcoming favorable matchups against the Chargers and Bills, but much will depend on Drake Maye's health.

A.J. Brown returned from a two-game absence against the Lions, finishing with two receptions for 27 yards and 4.7 fantasy points before exiting with a hamstring injury. Brown was already seeing a reduced role following knee and ankle injuries, playing on only 65% of offensive snaps in the first half, down from 92% in Week 7. The bye week gives Brown additional time to recover. Mack Hollins led New England's receivers in routes run, targets (6) and receiving yards (48). He is worth keeping on watch lists in deeper leagues if Brown were to miss more time.

Josh Downs caught four out of five targets for 53 yards and a touchdown in his Chiefs debut, finishing with a season-high 15.3 fantasy points after just three practices with Kansas City. Downs played on only 10 offensive snaps and ran eight routes, lining up exclusively in the slot. His presence also led to Rashee Rice lining up outside more frequently. Downs' playing time should increase as he becomes more familiar with the offense. He warrants flex consideration against the Cardinals in Week 11.

Drake London led the Falcons in targets and ranked third in routes run against the Chiefs. He finished with six receptions for 109 yards and 16.9 fantasy points before leaving late in the fourth quarter due to a possible concussion. It was London's first 100-yard receiving game since Week 5, and Atlanta's bye week gives him time to recover.

Kyle Pitts Sr. led the Falcons in routes, in part because Atlanta was playing from behind. Despite the additional playing time, Pitts has now seen just five targets in four consecutive games and hasn't scored double-digit fantasy points since Week 7 against the 49ers. He remains a TE2 moving forward.

Nico Collins caught nine of a season-high 16 targets for 94 yards and a touchdown against the Browns, finishing with 24.4 fantasy points. Xavier Hutchinson ranked second on the team in routes run and targets (9), scoring a season-high 15.8 fantasy points. Despite Marvin Harrison Jr.'s absence, Tank Dell's route participation remained the same for the second consecutive game. According to Mike Clay's strength of schedule matrix, the Texans have the 10th-easiest remaining schedule and sixth-easiest fantasy playoff schedule for wide receivers. Hutchinson is worth adding, though managers should monitor his health following a concussion evaluation. Dell remains a boom-or-bust flex option.

Harold Fannin Jr. led the Browns in targets (9), receptions (7) and receiving yards (91) against the Texans. He's scored at least 12 fantasy points in each of his last four games, highlighted by a 23.4-point performance against the Steelers in Week 8. KC Concepcion led Cleveland's wide receivers with eight targets and remains a viable flex option. Denzel Boston led the team in routes despite a toe injury but has been held under 50 receiving yards in five of his last six games. As the team prepares for its bye, he becomes expendable in shallow leagues. Jerry Jeudy ran fewer routes than both rookies and gained 65 of his 74 receiving yards on one reception.

Quinshon Judkins finished with 13 touches and 16.1 fantasy points against the Texans, finding the end zone despite a season-low 10 rushing attempts. Dylan Sampson outsnapped Judkins for the third consecutive game and again led the backfield in routes run. Game script has contributed to this trend, with Cleveland falling behind 17-0 on Sunday. Judkins is still the primary rushing option but has averaged just 3.4 yards per carry this season. According to Clay's strength of schedule matrix, Cleveland has the eighth-easiest remaining schedule for running backs. Judkins remains a low-end RB2, while Sampson is worth holding in deeper PPR leagues.

Jordan Mason led the Vikings backfield in both snaps and rushing attempts (16) against the Packers but finished with just 5.9 fantasy points. Aaron Jones Sr. finished with 12 touches and 12.5 fantasy points, handling all four rushing attempts in goal-to-go situations. DeeJay Dallas played on just two snaps, with Jones taking over on third downs. Minnesota had 31 rushing attempts with Kirk Cousins under center after averaging just 15 over its previous two games. The Vikings have one of the easiest remaining schedules for fantasy running backs. Both are viable flex options going forward, but I'd lean toward Jones if this backfield split continues.

Bhayshul Tuten missed two practices last week due to ankle and foot injuries but finished with a career-high 29 touches and 20.3 fantasy points against the Titans. Tuten accounted for 29 of Jacksonville's 33 RB touches, catching three passes for 25 yards and handling all the goal-line work. He has scored six touchdowns over his last four games and surpassed 20 fantasy points in three of them. Tuten is a high-end RB2 with RB1 upside against the Giants in Week 11. Chris Rodriguez Jr. is worth rostering primarily as an insurance back for managers with Tuten.

Tony Pollard led the Titans backfield in snaps, routes run and touches (17) against the Jaguars, finishing with 23.9 fantasy points. Despite the return of Tyjae Spears (knee), Pollard played more on third downs and in two-minute drills, setting season highs in targets (9), receptions (8) and receiving yards (66). He's averaged 18.2 touches per game this season and 23.0 over his last three games, scoring six touchdowns during that stretch. Spears could regain some passing-down work, but Pollard is a reliable RB2 against a Cowboys defense allowing the fourth-most RB fantasy points per game.

After trading away Downs and Keenan Allen, Laquon Treadwell led all Colts wide receivers in snaps and routes run against the Dolphins, finishing with three receptions for 24 yards and a touchdown. Anthony Gould took over as the team's primary slot receiver, while Deion Burks was held without a reception -- despite increased playing time. No Colts wide receiver had over 30 receiving yards. Treadwell is worth monitoring in deeper leagues, while Alec Pierce is worth stashing in an available IR spot.

Ollie Gordon II led the Dolphins backfield in snaps, routes run, touches (24) and fantasy points (15.5). His involvement in the passing game with Kyle McCord under center was encouraging. Miami ranks near the bottom of the league in both yards and points per game, but Gordon has averaged 21.5 touches over his last four outings. That type of volume keeps him firmly on the RB2 radar against a Bills defense that's allowed the ninth-most RB fantasy points per game heading into Week 11.

Travis Etienne Jr. returned from IR against the Panthers and led the Saints backfield with 11 touches. However, he finished with just 6.4 fantasy points. Audric Estime handled third-down duties and led the backfield in both snaps and routes. Alvin Kamara played on the fewest snaps of this backfield trio, but also scored two touchdowns on just five touches, finishing with 16.4 fantasy points. The Saints have favorable upcoming matchups, but the three-man committee makes it difficult to trust any of them. Etienne is a flex option at best. Kamara is a candidate to try to trade away. Estime can be dropped in shallower leagues.

Jordyn Tyson led the Saints with 13 targets, 11 receptions and 19 fantasy points against the Panthers in just his second game of the season. New Orleans more than doubled Tyson's snap total from Week 9, and he ran 31 routes after running just 16 against the Browns. Tyson's involvement in the passing game is particularly encouraging given that Chris Olave was also active. The rookie is rostered in just 42.2% of ESPN leagues and should be a top waiver-wire priority heading into Week 11. Tyson will be on the flex radar against the Bears.

Breece Hall led the Jets backfield in snaps, routes run and touches (15) against the Bills, with 10.7 of his 14.8 fantasy points coming as a receiver. In his two games since returning from injury, Hall has averaged 16.5 touches (compared to Braelon Allen's 9.5). Allen has been the more efficient runner this season (5.0 YPC compared to Hall's 3.4), but Hall's receiving role gives him a higher fantasy floor on a team that frequently plays from behind. Hall remains a low-end RB2, while Allen is worth rostering primarily as an insurance back.

Dalton Kincaid finished with just three targets and 7.8 fantasy points against the Jets, gaining 54 of his 58 receiving yards on one reception. His snap share has declined for three consecutive games as he's dealt with both chest and ankle injuries. Dawson Knox played on considerably more snaps Sunday, though Kincaid still led Buffalo's tight ends in routes run. Kincaid has averaged 11.9 yards per target this season but has seen three-or-fewer targets in five out of nine games. He'll be no more than a high-end TE2 until his playing time and target volume improve.

Ashton Jeanty finished with 13 touches and 10.4 fantasy points against the Seahawks, with 8.6 points coming as a receiver. He has averaged just 6.3 rushing attempts over his last four games, down from 16.3 over his first six. Jeanty hasn't reached 30 rushing yards since Week 4. Meanwhile, Mike Washington Jr. has played more early-down snaps over the last five games. That said, Jeanty continues to dominate the receiving work, keeping him on the RB2 radar. Washington remains game-script dependent but is worth keeping, given favorable matchups against the Chargers, Titans and Cardinals on tap following the Raiders' Week 13 bye.

Zach Charbonnet's touches have increased from nine to 17 to 20 in his three games since returning from an ACL injury. He rushed for 94 yards against the Raiders and led Seattle's backfield in both snaps and routes run. Jadarian Price scored his first career touchdown and finished with 11.4 fantasy points on just 10 touches. Although Charbonnet has yet to score double-digit fantasy points this season, his growing workload should put him on the RB2 radar following Seattle's Week 11 bye. Price is a flex option in deeper leagues.

The Rams relied heavily on the run game against the Cardinals, rushing 46 times and using three tight ends on 63% of its offensive plays. With Puka Nacua, Davante Adams and Jordan Whittington all on IR, Konata Mumpfield led Rams wide receivers in snaps, routes run and targets (4), finishing with 11.1 fantasy points. Rookie CJ Daniels played ahead of Tutu Atwell, but saw just two targets. Mumpfield is worth holding in deeper leagues, though Adams could return in Week 12. Daniels is worth keeping an eye on, while Atwell can be dropped in most leagues.

Blake Corum finished with a career-high 20 touches and 20.9 fantasy points against the Cardinals, while Kyren Williams had 14 touches and a mere 5.2 fantasy points. Both played on the same number of snaps, but Corum started the second half -- a departure from the Rams' usual rotation. Corum has now scored a touchdown in six consecutive games and averaged 5.9 yards per carry over his past three games (compared to Williams' 2.9). Both are on the RB2 radar in an offense that ranks ninth in rushing attempts and rushing yards per game. Williams led the team with five targets and could see more work in closer games.

The 49ers have managed Christian McCaffrey's workload this season, creating more opportunities for rookie Kaelon Black. Black has seen more playing time over the last three games than earlier in the season. He's had at least 11 rushing attempts in each of those games and scored a touchdown in two of them. Black is one of the top insurance backs in fantasy football and should be rostered in more leagues. He also warrants flex consideration in deeper leagues, given his workload in a 49ers offense that leads the league in total yards and ranks second in points per game.

Ryan Flournoy is easy to overlook playing alongside CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens, but he's consistently been involved in the Cowboys' passing game and ranks third on the team in targets. Flournoy has seen at least four targets in five of his last six games, including two with at least seven. He's also scored at least 10 fantasy points in five of those games, including a season-high 19.1 against the 49ers. Flournoy remains a viable flex option in Week 11 against a Titans defense that's allowed the most WR fantasy points per game this season. His outlook is further enhanced by Dallas leading the league in passing attempts per game.

Chase Brown finished with 18 touches against the Steelers, catching eight passes for 39 yards and two touchdowns -- his first scores since Week 1. Brown outsnapped Samaje Perine 48-20 and played on the majority of third-down snaps for the third time in four weeks. He has scored at least seven fantasy points in every game this season and has been a top-10 fantasy running back over the past three weeks. Cincinnati has one of the most favorable rest-of-season schedules for a running back. Brown remains a high-end RB2 with RB1 upside.
