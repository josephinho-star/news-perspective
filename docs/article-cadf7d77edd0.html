FCC approves SpaceX’s plan to launch up to 1 million data centers into space

2026-11-25 20:21

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) approved Elon Musk’s plan for his company SpaceX to launch up to 1 million orbital data centers into space, citing a need to meet growing AI computing demand.

FCC Chair Brendan Carr said in a statement that “by moving compute capacity beyond Earth, SpaceX and the many competitors working on their own orbital data centers are meeting the moment.”

“This is the kind of American ingenuity that can unite our strengths in space and advanced technology, reinforce our economic leadership, and expand prosperity here at home,” Carr continued.

He added that “compute campuses or data centers are essential to sustaining America’s leadership in” artificial intelligence.

The approval gives SpaceX a 15-year permit to accomplish its goals of launching 1 million data centers. The firm must launch its first station into orbit by Nov. 25, 2033, with more deadlines outlined until 2040.

The FCC chair expressed on social media Wednesday that this was the first time the agency had authorized a permit for 1 million orbital data centers, which are set to be arranged in a constellation called “Starmind.”

Musk responded to Carr’s post, writing, “Much appreciated. This will be a great asset to America and the world.”

Data centers have quickly lost favor among Americans, with many cities across the country voting against approving projects or permits for the computing infrastructure in November’s midterm elections. Amid the growing dissatisfaction, Musk’s SpaceX and other tech firms began experimenting with launching orbital data centers that relied on solar power to fuel its computing.

Musk stated earlier this year that putting data centers in space could also work as a potential solution to the rising energy demands of AI.

Jared Isaacman, administrator of NASA, signaled his support for the idea in August, saying the move would “have immense benefits,” as companies could leverage solar energy to power data centers.

“Let’s take our data centers, put them in Earth orbit, take advantage of that free fusion reactor that’s out there for us and use that to ensure we do win the … space race for sure, but the AI race, too,” Isaacman said in an interview with Vinay Simlot, Nexstar Media Group’s Washington, D.C., correspondent.

SpaceX did not respond immediately to The Hill’s request for comment.
