Australia news live: Greens to choose new leader next week after Waters steps down; bogong moths back at parliament

Greens to meet next week to choose new leader after Larissa Waters steps down

In case you missed the news last night, the Greens leader, Larissa Waters, has announced she will step down due to ill health.

A party room meeting next week will be held to choose her successor, with the deputy leader, Mehreen Faruqi, and high-profile senators Sarah Hanson-Young, David Shoebridge and Nick McKim all possibilities to contest the leadership ballot.

Waters said she had been managing a pre-existing kidney disease, but her health had “declined significantly over the course of recent months”. Her prognosis for recovery is positive, but she said the pressure of leadership was detracting from her focus on her health.

I’m really sad that illness means my time as leader has been cut short, but our movement needs a leader in good health to lead us to success at the next election.

Passing the torch on to the next leader of the party will put them in the best position to take the Greens to the next election, fighting for people, not the corporate interests that run Labor, Liberals and One Nation.

18-year-old arrested in NSW on charges of using drone in alleged plot to murder man

NSW police have charged a drone operator over an alleged plot to murder a man in Sydney’s south west.

Officials said the man, 18, was arrested in Silverwater on Monday. Police will allege in court he was operating a drone to conduct surveillance as part of an alleged conspiracy to murder a man.

He has since been charged with conspiracy and agreement to murder a person, participation in a criminal group contributing to criminal activity and other charges linked to the drone.

He was remanded in custody and will appear before court today.

Massive great white shark spotted near whale carcass at popular Perth beach

Multiple sharks, including a five-metre great white, have been spotted swimming near a large, smelly whale carcass that washed ashore at a popular Western Australia beach, drawing hundreds of onlookers. Surf lifesavers found the dead animal at Perth’s City beach at about 7.30am on Monday and raised the alarm. Local authorities closed the beach for several kilometres in each direction of the carcass, which appeared to be about six metres long.

UNSW researchers trained AI models to act ‘drunk’, then spill things they shouldn’t

Researchers at the University of New South Wales say large language models (LLMs) can be manipulated to imitate drunken behaviour … and possibly leak confidential information or answer questions they’re not supposed to.

UNSW researchers said AI models trained or prompted to mimic drunken speech became more vulnerable to privacy breaches than their “sober” counterparts.

The researchers asked a model to role-play as a drunk person, or used a dataset of drunk texts to “fine-tune” the LLM to complete sentences like a drunk person would say them.

A third strategy involved rewarding a model for using language that resembled a drunk text.

The plan wasn’t tested on every LLM available, but UNSW selected a representative sample including OpenAI’s GPT-4 and GPT-3.5.

The researchers said the “drunk” models were consistently easier to manipulate.

“We do observe that particularly with deception and disinformation, most of the language models got jailbroken,” Aditya Joshi, a researcher at UNSW, said in a statement:

It does give out secrets – across the board for all three methods, it is vulnerable.

A timeline of One Nation’s instability: more than half MPs departed party, data shows

More than half of the One Nation MPs elected to state or federal parliaments have left the party before the end of their term, with 28 of the 51 people elected under Pauline Hanson’s banner either resigning midterm or being expelled.

One Nation, which won 6% of the national vote at the 2025 federal election, is now polling above 30% in some surveys, potentially enough to make the party the official federal opposition or even in a position of minority government.

But Guardian Australia analysis of the party’s colourful candidate history shows the party has a history of chaos and instability.

A small number of bogong moths have arrived at Parliament House in Canberra, the first sightings amid an apparent resurgence of the endangered native insect.

Swarms of bogongs have been reported across NSW, part of the annual migration when huge numbers of the insects travel to cool caves in the Australian Alps to escape the summer heat.

The moths were a common sight in Canberra for decades, with particularly large numbers congregating at Parliament House. The lights around the building, including the huge Australian flag, attracted the moths. But in recent years numbers have dramatically dropped.

Dfat providing assistance to family of Melbourne hiker missing in Peru

The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (Dfat) is providing consular assistance to the family of Jordan Yap, a Melbourne man who remains missing after setting out for a difficult trek in Peru.

Dfat said it couldn’t provide further comment owing to privacy obligations.

A GoFundMe page set up by Yap’s family and friends said the man, 30, was meant to return from the 10-day hike on 23 September, but he never checked back into his hostel in Lima.

They have been paying private rescue teams to search for him and are arranging for more crews to head to the area “to cover as much territory as possible”.

“We expect to have these units actively searching in the next few days,” they wrote on the page. “Thank you again for standing with Jordan. Your support means more than we can say.”

Police investigating suspicious deaths of two men in northern NSW

New South Wales police say they are investigating the suspicious deaths of two men in the state’s north.

Police said they found the first man – who has since been identified as a 79-year-old – dead at a property on Summerland Way at Gurranang, near Grafton, on 13 September.

Police said they attended the same property about 3.40pm on Monday as part of their investigation.

After arriving at the house, police said they found the body of the second man, who they believe to be aged in his 50s, and set up another crime scene.

Chalmers: Australia should not ‘carry the can’ for overseas decisions

Chalmers said he doesn’t believe inflation is out of control, but he doesn’t want to see Australians “carry the can for decisions” on the other side of the world.

The treasurer is appearing on what seems like every television and radio program this morning to tout the government’s record on the economy while acknowledging the difficulties reining in inflation amid a cost of living crisis.

He said a lot of Australians are bracing for an almost certain Reserve Bank interest rate increase today, adding the government sees taming inflation and keeping unemployment low as “two important objectives”.

And that’s equally important in the Reserve Bank’s mandate, dual mandate, which talks about maintaining full employment at the same time as they go for price stability, which means inflation in the target band.

He defended his government’s tax receipts as a percentage of GDP, saying they were lower than under the Howard government.

Well, it’s still lower than what we saw under Howard and Costello, is the first point.

When asked if he would accept higher unemployment in exchange for tamed inflation, Chalmers maintained the government was “not for higher unemployment”.

I think that’s pretty clear. And what we’ve seen in recent years is that it’s possible to have low and steady unemployment at the same time as we see inflation moderate to more normal levels.

Wife of missing activist urges Albanese government help

The wife of an Australian activist missing in Cambodia has implored the Albanese government to find him and bring him home, Australian Associated Press reports.

TranHiep, a 50-year-old Australian-Vietnamese democracy campaigner, was due to return to Bangkok, Thailand, to reunite with his wife, ChloeDang, after a short trip to Phnom Penh, Cambodia, to meet human rights defenders.

But he never boarded his flight and has not communicated with his wife since 18 September, leaving her worried for his safety.

My husband deserves protection, and I really hope the Australian government will continue to put pressure on Cambodia to do an investigation to find out where he is right now.

Hiep, a freelance IT professional who founded the Australian Alliance to Combat Human Trafficking, was travelling with another human rights activist, Nguyen Duc Thuan, who is also Vietnamese and holds a Norwegian passport.

The pair travelled to Cambodia to meet with dissident Vietnamese activists for training.

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, said the inflationary challenge in the economy is being made “much worse” by the war in the Middle East.

But we know that there’s always more work today. I think people understand, people who might be bracing for an interest rate rise later today. … I think people understand that there are inflationary challenges in our economy.

But when you see what’s happening with global oil prices … obviously, factually, that is one the big drivers of inflation that people are having other deal with right now.

Chalmers said he knows that people are under pressure and that interest rate rises add to that pressure.

That’s why we accept our part, our responsibility for our part in this fight against inflation. … And it also means dealing with these longer term economic challenges.

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, has defended his economic record on the eve of what is widely expected to be the fourth interest rate rise of 2026, as the Reserve Bank remains concerned about persistent inflation.
