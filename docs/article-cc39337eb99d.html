Olympic trailblazer Anita DeFrantz, longtime advocate for women in sports, dies at 74

2026-11-27 01:41

The sports world is mourning the loss of an Olympic trailblazer.

Anita DeFrantz has died, the International Olympic Committee and U.S. Olympic Committe said. She was 74. No cause of death was not immediately disclosed, although DeFrantz was believed to have been in declining health in recent years.

DeFrantz won a bronze medal in rowing at the 1976 Olympics, later becoming a powerful advocate for women in the male-dominated world of Olympic policymaking. She broke barriers throughout her career, becoming the first American woman and first Black American appointed to the IOC in 1986 before becoming the first woman to serve as an IOC vice president in 1997.

DeFrantzalso was vice president of the organizing committee for the LA Olympics in 1984 and served as chair of the IOC’s Women in Sport Commission for two decades.

IOC President Kirsty Coventry, an Olympic swimmer who has credited DeFrantz with helping her rise to the highest position in the Olympic movement, described DeFrantz as "a mentor, a friend and a true inspiration to me, and to so many others around the world."

"From my earliest days in the Olympic Movement, she showed me what courageous leadership looked like, and what was possible for women in sport," Coventry said.

Edwin Moses, the record-setting hurdler and a contemporary of DeFrantz, said, "every woman who has a high-level position in sports today, they can thank Anita DeFrantz."

DeFrantz won bronze in the 1976 Olympics as captain of the women’s eight team in Montreal. She trained for an Olympic return and, in 1980, gained a sense of the disconnect between athletes and the boardrooms that made decisions on their behalf.

DeFrantz was part of the U.S. team that never made the trip to the boycotted 1980 Moscow Olympics. As spokeswoman for the U.S. athletes’ advisory council, she later led a lawsuit against the U.S. Olympic Committee, which had authorized the boycott under pressure from the Carter administration.

That experience marked the beginning of her effort to earn a seat at the table where those decisions were made.

Before her induction into the Team USA Hall of Fame, DeFrantz recalled realizing how much work remained when she attended her first IOC meeting in 1986.

"I walked in and I thought, ‘This is odd,’" she told The Associated Press in 2025. "It was this cavernous room" and of the 90 or so committee members, she was one of only five women.

"We had to help people open their minds a little," she said.

Women made up half of the 10,500 athletes at the 2024 Paris Games. Today, 46 of the IOC’s 106 members are women.

U.S. Olympic and Paralympic Committee CEO Sarah Hirshland and chairman Gene Sykes remember DeFrantz as "one of the most consequential Olympians our nation has ever known."

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
