Two staff killed in Israeli strike on Gaza medical clinic, MSF says

2026-11-28 10:34

Two workers for the medical charity Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) have been killed in an Israeli strike in Gaza, according to the organisation.

MSF said the strike was a direct hit on one of the charity's clinics, the location of which was known by the Israeli military.

In a statement, the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said the two people were Hamas operatives "acting as security guards at a clinic operated by MSF". The IDF added the strike was "not directed at a hospital" and caused no damage to medical infrastructure.

The head of the World Health Organization (WHO) said that attacks on health facilities and health workers in Gaza were "outrageous and unacceptable".

In a post on X, Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus wrote: "Those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked."

In its statement, MSF - which is also known as Doctors Without Borders - said that the two workers who were killed were watchmen, and added that the strike brought the number of its colleagues killed in Gaza to 17.

The organisation said it was "devastated and outraged" and added: "Our premises were clearly marked with MSF logos, and their location had been communicated to and was known by the Israeli military."

In its own post on X, the IDF said that the two men, who they named as Ahmed Sufyan Muhammad Ali Abu Hasira and Moaz Yousef Alitsbach, were "terrorists in Hamas' military wing".

The Israeli military added it was another example of Hamas "embedding itself within international organizations".

It said that "precautionary measures were taken to minimize harm to civilians" before the strike on Gaza City, adding the men had "advanced plans to carry out attacks against Israeli civilians & IDF troops".

Witnesses told the BBC the two men were sitting at the clinic entrance when an Israeli drone descended and fired a missile.

Video footage from the scene of the strike showed a small crater beside a plastic chair, with bloodstains and limited damage in a courtyard behind the medical clinic.

Hundreds of Palestinians have since attended the funeral of the MSF workers, carrying the two bodies, wrapped in white plastic bags and placed on stretchers, on their shoulders as they made their way through the streets of the city.

Mohammed Imad, a close friend of Abu Hasira, one of the men killed, said he had seen him just hours before the attack.

"He was a peaceful, kind-hearted man. His only concern was looking after his wife, his two children and his mother," he told the BBC.

He said Abu Hasira had no affiliation with any armed group and had worked at the clinic for a long time.

The two workers' deaths follow an increase in Israeli strikes across Gaza over the past week.

Gaza's civil defence spokesman said eight Palestinians were killed in Israeli attacks across the Strip during the day, including the two MSF workers.

Violence has continued in Gaza since Israel and Hamas agreed to a US-brokered ceasefire in October last year. Israeli strikes take place almost daily in areas under the control of Hamas, which are home to 2.1 million people, most of them displaced.

At least 1,705 people have been killed in Gaza over the past 13 months, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN.

The humanitarian situation also remains dire, with heavy rainfall earlier this month causing flooding and damage to tens of thousands of shelters for displaced families.

The UN and its partners have called on Israel - which controls Gaza's crossings - to allow in more supplies to help Palestinians prepare for the winter, including tents, heaters and water pumps, as well as vaccines and medicines to combat infectious diseases.

The Gaza war was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, when about 1,200 people were killed and 251 others were taken hostage.

Since then, more than 74,770 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza, the health ministry says.
