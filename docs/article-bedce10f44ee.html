Bob Newhart faced $6M lawsuit threat after trying to quit hit sitcom: book

2026-11-28 09:08

Stephanie Giang-Paunon, Fox News

Published Nov. 28, 2026, 4:08 a.m. ET

See more of our coverage in your search results.

Bob Newhart could make millions laugh, but a $6 million lawsuit was no laughing matter.

The comedy legend once tried to quit his hit CBS sitcom, “The Bob Newhart Show,” only to discover the network wasn’t ready to let its ratings star walk away.

The contract dispute is detailed in Michael Seth Starr’s biography, “Bob Newhart: The Life and Laughter of an American Treasure,” which chronicles the comedian’s nearly 65-year career.

Newhart wasn’t exactly in a position to face a multimillion-dollar lawsuit.

“I don’t have $6 million,” the comedian later admitted. “And my wife wouldn’t lend me any of her money.”

Although he publicly credited fan support for his decision, Newhart later acknowledged that financial pressure played a major role in his return.

“When I said I wanted to quit, the people in the cast and the production crew were all affected, of course and they helped influence me to change my mind. Then there were letters from fans and people I met on the street. But I guess the main reason I decided to come back was that CBS threatened to sue me for $6 million because there was a year left on my contract.”

According to Starr’s biography, Newhart approached CBS executives Bob Daly and Robert Wussler about leaving “The Bob Newhart Show” before its sixth season.

His attorney warned that if a replacement series failed to generate comparable revenue, CBS could seek damages Newhart estimated at “about fifty or sixty million dollars.”

The comedian also questioned the show’s creative future, with head writers Tom Patchett and Jay Tarses occupied with other projects.

“So, I thought maybe five years was enough. I’d rather go off a year early than a year late,” he recalled. He hoped to negotiate television specials instead, saying, “I’d rather do specials, even if it means walking away from a lot of money.”

CBS ultimately convinced him to return for a sixth and final season, with Starr describing concessions that included better scripts, flexibility to miss one or two episodes and “a modest bump” in his $30,000-per-episode salary.

CBS executive Robert Wussler later recalled Newhart telling him and Daly, “You can sue me and take me to court.”

Wussler recalled their response: “Wait a minute — we don’t want to sue you. We just want you back.”

Newhart eventually agreed, recalling, “Both [“The Mary Tyler Moore Show”] MTM and CBS were so darned nice to me, I got to thinking: ‘Why not go ahead with another year?'”

He added, “What’s so tough about it? People were already starting to say I was quitting because I wouldn’t have [“The Mary Tyler Moore Show”] anymore as a lead-in, which was nonsense.”

Newhart ultimately returned for the final season, which ended in 1978. He went on to star in another hit CBS sitcom, “Newhart,” which ran from 1982 to 1990.

Newhart was married to his wife, Ginnie, for 60 years before she died in April 2023. The couple had four children together.

During an appearance on “Nostalgia Tonight with Joe Sibilia,” Starr spoke about their relationship and how Ginnie’s death affected Newhart.

“He and Ginnie were so close, and I think losing her was just such a big blow. And I mean, he had been slowing down in the last maybe five or six years of his life. I interviewed him maybe about a year and a half or two years before he passed away. And he didn’t sound great. He was coughing a lot, and he sounded a little weak, but he was in his 90s.”

“I mean, he did live a very long life and very fruitful life,” he noted of Newhart. “And we’re still talking about him now, and I think people will be talking about him for years and years after this. So he certainly left his mark on the American public and the American consciousness.”

Newhart died July 18, 2024, at his Los Angeles home at age 94, following a series of short illnesses. His family was by his side.

His career spanned more than six decades, from his Grammy-winning 1960 comedy album “The Button-Down Mind of Bob Newhart” to two iconic sitcoms and a memorable late-career television comeback.

“Bob Newhart: The Life and Laughter of an American Treasure” is available now.
