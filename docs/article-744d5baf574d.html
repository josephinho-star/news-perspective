‘Start being serious’: Canada’s Carney hits back at US officials as tariff talks stall

Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney hit back at US officials who have blamed their northern counterparts for the trade war, saying tariff talks can proceed when “Americans stop doing memes, stop throwing shade, stop trying to be tough, and start being serious about having those discussions…”

When Americans start being serious, “we can have those discussions,” Carney said in an interview posted on X. Seemingly opining on the general chaos of the stalemate, Carney also said: “It’s not constructive, but that’s their democracy.”

Carney’s comments surfaced hours after Politico published an interview with US trade representative Jamieson Greer whonot only downplayed the trade war – but attributed the economically perilous stalemate to Canadians.

“It’s more their emergency than ours,” Greer reportedly said, also stating. “There are no negotiations happening on trade right now.”

US-Canada trade talks collapsed just over one week ago. Donald Trump instituted 50% tariffs on $20bn in goods from Canada – the US’s second-largest trade partner.

Canada, in turn, threatened to levy $20bn in retaliatory tariffs starting on 8 September. The policy could spell financial strain for Americans as key necessities – such as toilet paper – come from Canada.

In the Politico interview, Greer also said: “It’s not clear Canada wants a deal” and claimed they wanted too many things, such as lower taxes on heavy-duty trucks. These asks sank any deal, he claimed.

“Was that political? I don’t know. Was it economic? … I’m not sure how much economic sense it makes because the choice is not between the current tariffs and … you know, duty-free trade. Like, that is that. Those weren’t the options,” Greer also reportedly said.

Donald Trump’s Department of Homeland Security (DHS) is planning to ramp up voter fraud investigations in several states beginning Tuesday, according to CNN.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin has intensified efforts to make his department integral to voter fraud investigations per CNN, which cited “internal federal guidance” in its reporting. The department has allegedly “leaned on” one of its agencies, Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), to pursue this work.

HSI, which traditionally has pursued major crimes such as human trafficking or drug smuggling, now has an “unprecedented number of agents” to work on voter fraud probes. This includes tracking down voting records for people who are believed to be non-citizens, CNN said.

Asked for comment, a DHS spokesperson said: “While ICE Homeland Security Investigations is not able to comment on any active investigations, HSI is actively rooting out and investigating election fraud wherever it can be found. We have repeatedly demonstrated that illegal aliens can and do vote in our elections. Under President Trump, HSI is committed to restoring integrity to our election systems and ensuring that American citizens and only American citizens are electing American leaders.”

While Trump has repeatedly sounded the alarm about voter fraud, experts say that instances of voter fraud are exceedingly rare.

Trump has nonetheless used false claims about widespread election fraud to bolster his crackdown on voting access. He signed an executive order this spring that limits mail-in voting.

That order requires that the US Postal Service to come up with a national voter file. Under this order, the USPS also has to come up with a process requiring states to tell them which voters want a mail-in ballot – and bar them from receiving one if they’re not on the postal agency’s approved voter list.

Democratic senator Jack Reed voiced grave concern about US Army secretary Dan Driscoll’s resignation amid ongoing Defense Department turmoil.

“I do not welcome Secretary Driscoll’s resignation. His departure leaves the Army without its top civilian and uniformed leaders, and a military service whose leadership ranks have been gutted by Secretary Hegseth,” the Rhode Island senator said in a statement, after thanking Driscoll for his service, including work to strengthen the army.

“I am alarmed that Secretary Driscoll’s leadership of the Army stands in stark contrast to Secretary Hegseth’s disastrous tenure atop the Defense Department.”

“It is far past time for President Trump to recognize the lasting harm that Pete Hegseth is inflicting on the Department of Defense,” he also said.

“At this moment, hundreds of thousands of soldiers are deployed around the world, many in combat zones. The soldiers carrying out these missions deserve leadership that is reliable, forthright, and focused on the fight ahead,” the statement continued. “The Army cannot stay ahead of our adversaries while its senior ranks are hollowed out and its chain of command is left in limbo.”

Hegseth has faced continued criticism for his handling of US military activity, such as the war in Iran. The Washington Post reported that senior US military commanders routinely voice formal objections – but that Hegseth goes forward with his ideas despite these objections.

The Post reported, for example, that top military officials opposed prolonging the war in Iran. The Pentagon has rejected this reporting.
