‘Dangerous power grab’: top Democrats decry USPS system that could thwart mail-in voting – live

2026-09-01 21:02

A Democratic senator disclosed a whistleblower statement, which warned of potential chaos as the USPS is set to launch a computer system that could derail mail-in voting

“The Oversight Committee has received whistleblower reports that President Trump’s attack on vote-by-mail for the 2026 election is more serious than previously understood,” Garcia said in a statement. “This new secret tracking system at the US Postal Service is faulty, untested, and threatens to totally disrupt ballot delivery for millions of American citizens.”

“We are fighting in court to protect the right to vote by mail for all and will continue to investigate. This unconstitutional and dangerous power grab must be permanently and immediately blocked.”

Donald Trump in March signed an executive order requiring the creation of a national voter file. Trump’s executive order mandated that the US Postal Service make rules about a process that would require states to apprise them of voters who want mail-in ballots.

Trump’s executive order would require that the Postal Service prevent these voters from receiving mail-in ballots unless they are on a the USPS-approved eligible voter list. While the US Supreme Court paved the way for Trump’s efforts to limit mail-in voting, the issue is still being litigated in lower courts.

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer also slammed what he claimed were Trump-backed efforts to derail the mail-in voting.

“A whistleblower is warning of a ‘catastrophic failure’ in our mail-ballot system—all because Trump is turning USPS into a voter suppression machine,” the New York Democrat said on X. “Vote-by-mail is safe and secure. Trump himself voted by mail just last month. He knows he’s failing the American people, so he’s doing everything he can to rig the midterms.”

Donald Trump’s Department of Homeland Security has apparently taken steps to advance the president’s efforts to limit voting.

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) now has a webpage with a blue box that reads “Coming Soon” and “State Citizen Lists Portal.” According to Democracy Docket, this webpage is newly published.

This portal is in keeping with Trump’s March executive order mandating that DHS and the Social Security Administration gather data and compose lists of eligible voters from all 50 states.

Democracy Docket notes that a DHS memo released in June said the department plans on having this portal up and running by 4 September.

Voting rights advocates have insisted that federal authorities lack the legal power to make and manage lists of voters. A federal voter eligibility database has not existed previously as US states and localities are supposed to manage elections, Democracy Docket explained.

House passes short-term funding bill to avoid shutdown before midterms

The US House on Tuesday passed a short-term funding bill that will keep the federal government operating at current levels until 11 December.

This bill, which has already been green-lighted by the US Senate, will now go to President Trump’s desk. If Trump signs the legislation, this will postpone more permanent funding discussions until after the midterm elections, per NBC News.

Democrats and Republicans have quarreled over the extent of federal spending in their talks to forge a one-year deal. Democrats, for example, rebuffed Trump’s demands for a $1.5bn military budget – up significantly from the record $1 bn military budget approved in 2025.

Trump warns of 'much harder' action if Iran retaliates to 'very justified' US attacks

After the US announced Tuesday that it is striking targets in Iran, President Trump threatened “much harder” action if the country retaliated.

“They will be hit again at a much harder and higher level, but it will not be the biggest attack of them all, that is waiting in the wings and, when it is over, there will be very little left of the Islamic Republic of Iran,” Trump said on Truth Social.

Trump said on his social media platform that the US strikes were in response to Iran’s alleged failed effort to place mines in the strait of Hormuz. He also said the strikes were responsive to Iran’s intercepted attack on a US base in Jordan.

Trump’s comments bring to mind his past statements about attacks on Iran that could constitute war crimes if conducted, such as threatening numerous times to destroy critical civilian infrastructure and to bomb Iran “back to the Stone Ages.”

Trump taps Christian pastor to become adviser on tobacco health concerns

Donald Trump has appointed a Christian pastor to advise his administration on tobacco health concerns.

“I am pleased to announce that Pastor Darrell Scott, a longtime friend, respected Faith Leader, and tireless Advocate for America’s Underserved Communities, will be serving my Administration and me as an Advisor for tobacco health issues on the Advisory Committee to the Director (ACD) in the CDC,” Trump said in a Tuesday afternoon Truth Social post.

Trump said that “for far too long, tobacco-related disease has taken an enormous toll on American Families, including Communities that have too often been overlooked and underserved.” Scott, Trump said, “understands these Communities, and the challenges they face, and he knows how to bring people together to get RESULTS.”

Trump said that in keeping with a campaign promise, Scott’s appointment will support “solutions to reduce harm and SAVE LIVES.” The president wrapped his post by saying “MAKE AMERICA HEALTHY AND SAFE AGAIN!”

Scott has long had a reputation as being one of Trump’s earliest and dedicated Black supporters. Trump in 2016 thanked Scott for encouraging his presidential run.

In 2018, Scott called Trump “the most pro-Black president we’ve had in our lifetime”. Scott co-founded the National Diversity Coalition with Michael Cohen, who was the prosecution’s star witness at Trump’s New York hush-money trial.

The coalition marked a clunky effort during the 2016 race to show that he had support from Black and Latinx voters.
