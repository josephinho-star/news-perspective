‘Start being serious’: Canada’s Carney hits back at US officials as tariff talks stall

Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney hit back at US officials who have blamed their northern counterparts for the trade war, saying tariff talks can proceed when “Americans stop doing memes, stop throwing shade, stop trying to be tough, and start being serious about having those discussions…”

When Americans start being serious, “we can have those discussions,” Carney said in an interview posted on X. Seemingly opining on the general chaos of the stalemate, Carney also said: “It’s not constructive, but that’s their democracy.”

Carney’s comments surfaced hours after Politico published an interview with US trade representative Jamieson Greer whonot only downplayed the trade war –- but attributed the economically perilous stalemate to Canadians.

“It’s more their emergency than ours,” Greer reportedly said, also stating. “There are no negotiations happening on trade right now.”

US-Canada trade talks collapsed just over one week ago. Donald Trump instituted 50% tariffs on $20bn in goods from Canada – the US’s second-largest trade partner.

Canada, in turn, threatened to levy $20bn in retaliatory tariffs starting on 8 September. The policy could spell financial strain for Americans as key necessities – such as toilet paper – come from Canada.

In the Politico interview, Greer also said: “It’s not clear Canada wants a deal” and claimed they wanted too many things, such as lower taxes on heavy-duty trucks. These asks sank any deal, he claimed.

“Was that political? I don’t know. Was it economic? … I’m not sure how much economic sense it makes because the choice is not between the current tariffs and … you know, duty-free trade. Like, that is that. Those weren’t the options,” Greer also reportedly said.

Postal Service is rushing to comply with Trump’s order, with millions of votes at stake

The US Postal Service could soon launch a problematic computer system that could derail mail-in voting, a whistleblower reportedly said.

This whistleblower statement, disclosed Tuesday by Democratic senator Richard Blumenthal, warned of potential mail-in voting chaos. The warning comes as Donald Trump continues to try limiting this manner of casting ballots – on which one-third of American voters rely, according to the Associated Press.

Trump in March signed an executive order mandating the compilation of a national voter file. This executive order also said the US Postal Service must start making rules about a process that would mandate states notify them of voters who want mail-in ballots.

Under Trump’s executive order, the Postal Service would also bar these voters from receiving mail-in ballots unless they are on a USPS-approved roster of eligible voters.

The US Supreme Court sided with Trump’s effort to limit mail-in voting, rejecting a lower court’s injunction against this executive order. Several days later, a lower court once again stopped Trump’s push in a decision that’s expected to reach the Supreme Court.

The unnamed whistleblower alleged that the US Postal Service was rushing to construct a complex system that, in normal circumstances, would require one year or more to build. At times, this work was in contravention of judicial orders to stop this activity, AP said.

With these new procedures, a simple scanning error in a group of tens of thousands of ballots could keep them from arriving at voters’ homes, per AP.

“The Postal Service has designed a system to disenfranchise millions of Americans,” Blumenthal reportedly said prior to disclosing whistleblower information. “This administration seems hell-bent on changing the framework on casting ballots in this country clearly for political reasons.”

Donald Trump this month announced plans to rescind a landmark rule that has protected old-growth forests in the US for 25 years. If finalized, the rollback is expected to benefit the companies that Tom Schultz, chief of the US Forest Service, represented in his previous roles as a timber industry lobbyist and executive, according to research by consumer advocacy non-profit Public Citizen.

The Roadless Area Conservation Rule, more commonly known as the “roadless rule”, was adopted in 2001 to limit development in roughly 45m acres of national forest. Industry interests and Republican representatives have long lobbied for the rule’s rescission, which would open up those swaths of untouched land to road construction, drilling and logging.

In a statement, Schultz described the move as “the best way to restore the balance, reduce wildfire risk, and secure the long-term health of our forests and neighboring communities”. But studies suggest it could put 400 threatened and endangered species at risk and imperil ecosystems.

Industry interests will be the main beneficiaries of the change, advocates fear.

In an emailed statement, a Forest Service spokesperson, who did not provide their name, noted that Schultz had also held positions in Idaho and Montana government.

“Chief Schultz’s appointment reflects the administration’s recognition that the Forest Service’s current challenges require leadership with broad federal land management experience, not just internal tenure,” the person said.

Schultz is the first-ever Forest Service chief who did not previously work within the agency itself. Before Trump tapped him for the job, he served as president of the Federal Forest Resource Coalition, a trade association representing companies that purchase timber from the federal government, and was also vice-president of resources and government affairs at Idaho Forest Group, a major lumber manufacturer.

House Republicans are investigating several US labor unions based on claims from far-right nonprofits alleging union dues are being used for illegal political funding.

Republicans on the House’s education and workforce committee opened the investigation this summer after multiple reports from the conservative thinktanks targeted unions and their spending, claiming that unions are using member dues to support Democratic causes.

While unions are allowed to advocate for political causes, they are prohibited from using union dues to directly fund specific candidates or political action committees (Pacs). Union members can voluntary donate to labor union’s political action committees.

The unions under investigation include the American Federation of Teachers (AFT), United Auto Workers, United Steelworkers, Smart Transportation Division (Smart-TD), Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers and Trainmen (BLET), and the Brotherhood of Maintenance of Way Employees Division (BMWED).

The allegations come from research reports by the American Accountability Foundation (AAF), a “dark money” nonprofit that counts Project 2025 architect the Heritage Foundation as a donor, and the Freedom Foundation, a conservative thinktank whose chairman once protested against Labor Day, claiming “I can’t think of a problem in society that can’t be traced in some way back to the abuses of organized labor.”

Massachusetts primaries test Democratic appetite for generational change

Generational change is on the ballot in Massachusetts on Tuesday as Democratic voters choose whether to renominate 80-year-old progressive senator Ed Markey or replace him on the ballot with 47-year-old representative Seth Moulton.

Markey, who has served in Congress for nearly 50 years, is backed by leading progressives such as senator Bernie Sanders and representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, as well as most members of the state’s congressional delegation, including senator Elizabeth Warren.

No members of the delegation have endorsed Moulton, a moderate US Marine Corps veteran whose supporters include New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill and the Democratic organization VoteVets, Reuters reported.

The Boston Globe’s editorial board is also behind Moulton, casting him as a necessary agent of change with “proven leadership skills” and positions on key issues that align with most voters.

Markey has a nearly $2m fundraising advantage over Moulton, and Markey has led in recent polls by a double-digit margin. The primary winner is heavily favored to win in November.

Democrat Kamala Harris carried the state by nearly 25 percentage points in 2024.

A similar generational challenge is playing out in the Boston area, where moderate representative Stephen Lynch, 71, is facing progressive attorney Patrick Roath, 39.
