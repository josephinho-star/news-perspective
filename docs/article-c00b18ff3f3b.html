Politician hosts 400,000 guests at daughter’s wedding in India’s poorest state

2026-11-26 15:41

It takes a brave soul to try to match the mythic opulence of the wedding arranged by the Indian billionaire Mukesh Ambani for his son Anant in 2024. Estimates of the cost for the months-long celebration ran to at least $600m, with world leaders and celebrities among those on the guest list.

Smaller fry though he is, Anant Singh, a legislator in India’s poorest state, Bihar, gave it his best shot on Wednesday, albeit in a rustic rural setting, for the nuptials of his daughter Shivangi Singh, and Rishikesh Kumar, an MBA student from Georgia State University in Atlanta.

About 400,000 guests were hosted across 2.5 hectares at the family home in the village of Nadawa – a staggering figure even by the standards of India’s famed extravagant weddings. Guests were treated to 500,000 pieces of traditional sweets, and a feast prepared by an army of 500 chefs and cooks, Singh’s confidant Munna Singh told Agence France-Presse. One thousand kgs of mutton were reportedly used for kebabs and curries. Six makeshift helipads were erected for VIPs flying in from across India.

Kumar arrived at the venue in sunglasses and waving an orange scarf in a convoy of 151 cars (Hindus consider uneven numbers to be lucky). Among the gifts for the groom was a Land Rover Defender.

The roads leading to the venue were so choked with cars before the wedding that one guest escaped the gridlock, The Times of India reported, by jumping on a tractor and driving across the surrounding fields.

Guests were also treated to elaborate flower arrangements featuring blooms imported from China, and a fireworks display.

Instead of a DJ for entertainment, Singh planned a horse race. Gatecrashers clambered up into trees and on to electricity towers to catch a glimpse of the race, but the chaos and tumult was so great it had to be cancelled at the 11th hour. Lying around somewhere amid the wedding detritus was the SUV that would have gone to the winner.

Singh is a controversial figure. Elected five times to the state assembly, he has 28 criminal cases pending against him ranging from murder and attempted murder to extortion and kidnapping. All the cases are listed on the website of the Association for Democratic Reforms, an NGO trying to improve governance and strengthen democracy. Singh denies any wrongdoing.

In a television interview before the wedding, he pooh poohed the idea that there was anything unusual about the scale of the wedding preparations. “I laid on exactly the same thing for my elder daughter,” he said.

India’s wedding season has just kicked off. Between now and 15 December, an estimated 5.2m weddings are expected to take place across the country.
