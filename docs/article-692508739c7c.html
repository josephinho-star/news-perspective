At least 68 people dead in high-speed Spain train crash

2026-10-28 15:41

At least 68 people have been killed in a collision between two trains in the Spanish province of Toledo, authorities have said.

The high-speed trains collided shortly before 9:15 a.m. local time (4:15 a.m. ET), when emergency services were deployed to the scene. It occurred on the Madrid to Seville line.

Transport Minister Óscar Puente told reporters in Madrid that the control system at the nearby town of Urda failed and six trains were stopped. One was allowed to restart and travelled 25km before hitting a stopped train at high speed, he added, saying it was "inexplicable" how this had happened.

As well as those killed, 50 people were also injured, the minister said.

Railway infrastructure manager Adif is investigating the sequence of events surrounding the accident, Spanish news agency EFE reported.

The local Castilla-La Mancha government confirmed at least 68 people had died, higher than another high-speed crash in southern Spain that killed 45 people in January.

Emiliano García-Page, the president of the region, said 24 people had died on a train operated by rail company Renfe, with the rest killed on a train operated by Iryo.

More than 500 passengers were on board the two trains, newspaper El País said.

Iryo said its train was "struck from behind" by the Renfe train, saying the cause was "still unknown".

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said in a post on X that he was "shocked" by the accident.

"If there has been any negligence, it will come to light," he later said at a press conference.

"If there have been failures in the safety system, they will be addressed with the utmost firmness. It is clear that we have experienced two terrible tragedies involving high-speed rail within the same year."

Sanchez's political future will be tested in a snap election on Nov. 29, which he called following widespread unrest over Spain's housing crisis.

Santiago Abascal, leader of the far-right Vox party — which is expected to make gains when Spain goes to the polls — pointed the finger at Sanchez's government over the train accident on Wednesday.

"Tragedy again. Rage again over the neglect, the ruin, and the betrayal suffered by the Spanish people," he said in a post on X.

— CNBC's Charlotte Reed contributed to this article.
