Kash Patel reveals FBI's capture of all 10 members of Most Wanted fugitive's list during hearing

2026-09-15 17:45

The FBI has apprehended all 10 members of the FBI's Most Wanted list during President Donald Trump's second term, FBI Director Kash Patel revealed during an appearance in front of the Senate Judiciary Committee on Tuesday.

Sen. Chuck Grassley, R-Iowa, during his opening statements as the committee's chairman, lauded Patel and the FBI for their work in capturing nine of the ten most wanted fugitives on the FBI's top ten list.

Patel, during his opening remarks, corrected Grassley, revealing that his agency had just this past weekend caught the tenth fugitive.

"Mr. Chairman, I'm proud to announce today, and I hate to correct you, we haven't captured nine of the top ten most [wanted] individuals on our list. We've captured ten," Patel said.

"Just this past weekend, we captured a thug down in Venezuela who was on our most wanted list and on the run for years. He has been captured, apprehended, and is on his way back to the United States of America. That's ten of ten. Nobody in the FBI history has done that," Patel said.

"We, the FBI, captured more than two and a half times the number of the top ten most wanted fugitives on the FBI's list from the prior administration," he also said.

While Patel did not say the name of the fugitive during the hearing, the FBI earlier revealed exclusively to Fox News Digital that the 10th capture was that of Anibal Alexander Canelon Aguirre, the FBI's first ever "cyber fugitive."

Aguirre led "a large international conspiracy to steal millions of dollars from financial institutions in support of the Tren de Aragua, through an ATM jackpotting scheme since at least 2024," the FBI told Fox News Digital.

In addition to a clean sweep of the top ten fugitives, Patel also revealed that his FBI has also captured six of the top ten most wanted fraudsters on the top ten fraudsters list, a compilation his FBI created during the current administration.

Patel also outlined the broader progress the Trump administration has made towards making the U.S. safer.

"On top of that, we've captured six of the most wanted fraudsters around the world, taking the murder rate down 20 points is no easy effort. It requires a team left state, local and international partners. This includes results in cities like Baltimore, which retreats its lowest mid-year homicide numbers in recorded history," he said.

"This year in Memphis, we accomplished a 50% reduction in homicide for a city that had the highest per capita homicide rate in the country, thanks to the president's task force. And right here in Washington, D.C., we are on track to have the lowest homicide numbers since 1990," Patel added.

Fox News Digital contacted the FBI for further comment.

Fox News Digital's Bonny Chu contributed to this report.
