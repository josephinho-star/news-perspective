The sneaky tactic drivers are using to avoid SoCal traffic cameras

2026-11-26 00:24

Los Angeles drivers have a new way to dodge speeding tickets — an app that alerts them to the location of the city’s newly installed speed cameras before they get caught.

Polis Assist, a free parking-finder and ticket-protection app, has rolled out a new feature that warns drivers about the locations of LA’s 125 automated speed cameras in real time.

The app, which originally launched to help motorists find free street parking and navigate confusing parking signs, is now expanding its services as the city ramps up its controversial automated ticketing program.

Dennis Baker, founder and CEO of Polis Assist, said the company supports using speed cameras to improve road safety, according to KTLA, but believes drivers should get an immediate warning rather than a surprise ticket weeks after the fact.

“Speed cameras are about safety, and we support that,” Baker began.

“But a ticket that arrives in the mail weeks later doesn’t make anyone safer in the moment. A heads-up before you reach the camera does,” he added.

“We started with parking, but our goal has always been to make city data useful to the people who live here. Speed camera alerts are the next step,” he continued.

Los Angeles officially launched its automated speed-camera program last month, installing 125 cameras across the city. Drivers are currently in a 60-day warning period, but once that expires, motorists caught speeding will receive citations in the mail.

The cameras are expected to be operational across the city by the end of the year, with locations stretching from Sylmar in the north to San Pedro in the south.

The city has already published a map showing where all 125 cameras are installed, but Polis Assist’s new feature aims to give drivers a real-time heads-up while they’re behind the wheel.

Depending on how far over the speed limit they are traveling, motorists could face fines ranging from $50 to $500. The steepest penalty is reserved for drivers caught traveling more than 100 mph.

The new system has also raised questions about the accuracy of automated enforcement in Los Angeles.

The California Post previously reported on a couple who received a $293 automated parking citation after a passing bus camera captured their vehicle in a bus lane while they were waiting for pedestrians. The footage did not show the couple pulling into a parking garage, but the citation was still issued.

The couple is contesting the ticket, though they were warned the review process could stretch into next year.

Polis Assist is available for free through Apple’s App Store. The app does not require users to sign up to access its services.
