Florida detective fatally struck by alleged drunk wrong-way driver on Thanksgiving morning

2026-11-27 02:41

A decorated Florida detective was fatally struck on his way to work by an alleged drunken driver with a revoked license who was going the wrong way on Thanksgiving morning, according to authorities.

Raul Jonathan Espinoza Martinez, 37, was allegedly driving in the wrong direction in the southbound lanes of I-95 in Palm Beach Gardens when he smashed head-on into an SUV driven by Alan Smith, a longtime Palm Beach County Sheriff’s Office detective, according to the Florida Highway Patrol.

Smith, 46, was on his way to work in an unmarked police car just after 5 a.m. when the devastating crash unfolded, WPTV reported.

He was airlifted to a local hospital, where he was pronounced dead.

Espinoza Martinez was also taken to the hospital with incapacitating injuries and arrested in the hospital using handcuffs carried by Smith, police said.

He was allegedly intoxicated during the deadly crash and had an active license revocation for 10 years and three previous DUI convictions.

Smith, a married dad, had been with the Palm Beach County Sheriff’s Office for nearly a decade, joining the Bomb Squad and graduating from the FBI Hazardous Devices School in 2013.

The beloved detective, who was completing his doctoral degree, also served as a Task Force Officer with the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, according to the Palm Beach County Sheriff’s Office.

“He was a college professor, and a lifelong scholar who was in the process of completing his doctoral degree,” the sheriff’s office said in a statement.

“His passion for education, pursuit of knowledge, and commitment to mentoring others were evident throughout his personal and professional life,” the statement said.

“Above all his professional and academic accomplishments, Alan’s greatest pride was his family. He was a loving husband to his wife, Jennifer, and a devoted father to his 14-year-old son, Alexander.”

Espinoza Martinez will be booked on a laundry list of charges upon his release from the hospital, including vehicular homicide, DUI manslaughter, and aggravated battery on a law enforcement officer.

FBI Director Kash Patel added in a statement that the agency’s heart was with Smith’s family and colleagues.

“This is yet another reminder of the sacrifices our law enforcement make standing their posts even on holidays to keep us safe. Never forget them and their families,” Patel wrote on X.

“Our prayers are with Detective Smith’s wife and 14-year-old son, as well as the colleagues he leaves behind.”
