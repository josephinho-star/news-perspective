Liberty’s season comes to crushing end as they’re swept out of WNBA semifinals by Dream

2026-10-10 02:12

Turns out, the entire era of Liberty basketball might need a reset timeout.

A season that began with such high hopes of returning to the top of the WNBA ended in a sweep with an 85-83 Game 3 loss to the Dream on Friday night in front of a disappointed capacity crowd of 17,991 at Barclays Center.

tRY IT NOWIf this was the last stand of this Liberty golden age — heralded by Breanna Stewart’s arrival in 2023 and notarized by a first championship in 2024 — they went down swinging.

“This is just a tough pill to swallow,” Jonquel Jones said. “We know how talented we are, and every year is about being able to play for and ultimately win the championship. We fell short this year.

The Liberty were outmuscled and out-quicked by a determined opponent with its own prime-aged stars making its leap into the WNBA’s elite — where the Liberty were, circa three years ago.

After a Game 2 loss that will be remembered for the Liberty bizarrely not using a reset timeout that was in their pocket before their final possession, Game 3 was a more straightforward kind of nail-biter.

The Liberty flipped the script from Game 1 — when the Dream cranked up the defense to pull away in the third quarter — by finding another level of intensity after halftime.

The Liberty led 71-66 early in the fourth quarter, then went more than five minutes without scoring during a 9-0 Dream run. Their deficit was 81-75 after DeWanna Bonner sank three free throws with 3:15 left.

Trailing by two with 18 seconds left, the Liberty drew up a look for Stewart out of a timeout, but it didn’t go down.

“It’s really hard to win a WNBA championship, and I feel like because we did it, went [to the Finals] twice early on, it was almost like, ‘Oh, this is going to be an every-year thing,’ ” Stewart said. “This s–t isn’t easy. And when you get there and you win, you can’t take it for granted.”

Stewart emptied the tank. After carrying the Liberty throughout the season with vintage two-way play that landed her in the top five of MVP voting and running up major minutes down the stretch, Stewart ran for 39:03. She scored 26 points (on 6-for-15 shooting) and added four rebounds and six assists.

Jones also scored 26 points and added a game-high 14 rebounds.

Sabrina Ionescu, the third member of the franchise’s vaunted Big 3 and a face of the operation since being drafted No. 1 overall in 2020, finished with 12 points on 4-of-12 shooting to cap a down season that she said was hampered “a lot” by a back injury.

Angel Reese led the Dream with 22 points, nine rebounds and nearly as many instances of inviting the crowd’s scorn by tussling with Liberty players.

Jordin Canada tormented the Liberty throughout the series with her quickness on both sides of the ball. She wound up with 19 points and 11 assists.

The Liberty were installed as the betting favorites before the season.

They ended it with a 26-18 record that was good for eighth place and a 2-3 mark in the postseason, where a stirring first-round triumph over the Lynx quickly turned into a nightmare ouster by the Dream.

An offseason of major introspection and potentially seismic moves now awaits a franchise with championship-or-bust standards.

“You don’t know what anything’s going to look like,” Ionescu said, “but obviously like the group that we have, us three poured our hearts into trying to make this work for this organization and this fanbase that showed up for us.”
