Dow tumbles more than 600 points, Nvidia falls again as traders await earnings: Live updates

2026-11-17 15:43

Stocks pulled back Tuesday as the Dow Jones Industrial Average struggled following the release of new corporate earnings reports, and as a chip stocks sell-off ahead of Nvidia's quarterly release led the Nasdaq Composite lower.

The Dow fell 655 points, or 1.3%. The Nasdaq was down roughly 1.4%, while the S&P 500 slipped more than 1.3%.

Home Depot dropped more than 1% after the retailer announced a new CEO would take over next week, weighing on the Dow. That overshadowed better-than-expected quarterly results.

Those moves come as investors brace for Nvidia's quarterly report, due Tuesday after the bell.

Nvidia has been tumbling since last week. Jensen Huang resigned from the CEO post Thursday after reports emerged that he had multiple affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. Further, the chipmaker's board of directors is not considering bringing Huang back as CEO, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC. The stock has dropped 25% this month, putting it on pace for its biggest monthly decline since April 2022 — when it plunged 32%.

"The tape feels increasingly fragile as Nvidia's AI premium continues to unwind and investors reassess the durability of the ecosystem built around its dominance," said Ben Emons, founder of FedWatch Advisors, in a note. "This backdrop has fueled the 'dispersion trade,' gradually eroding the extreme concentration that has defined the S&P 500 and Nasdaq."

Investors are also poring over a slew of economic data on Tuesday, including October retail sales and import prices.

U.S. retail and food services sales rose 0.2% for the month to $748.2 billion, up 0.2% from September and in line with expectations.

Excluding the volatile gas and auto categories, retail sales rose 0.6% from the previous month. That's higher than the 0.4% expected on the Street.

On the earnings front, Disney and Home Depot are set to report before the opening bell.

The 30-stock Dow on Monday slid more than 700 points, or 1.4%, while the S&P 500 lost 1.9%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.5%, its biggest one-day decline since Nov. 5.
