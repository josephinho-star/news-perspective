North Korea expels Swiss diplomat in sanctions row

2026-11-25 21:51

North Korea has ordered Switzerland's diplomatic representative to leave the country within 48 hours and called for the office to close, in a row over sanctions.

It followed Switzerland's decision to place sanctions on the Songdowon international children's camp in the eastern city of Wonsan, which is attended by Russian and Chinese children among other nationalities.

The camp has been accused of involvement in transferring children from Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine.

North Korea's state-run news agency KCNA called the sanctions "a blatant hostile act".

Switzerland is one of the few Western nations to maintain dialogue with Pyongyang.

Confirming the expulsion, KCNA said: "The Foreign Ministry of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea) declared the head of the Swiss Diplomatic Office in the DPRK persona non grata and urged him to leave the country within 48 hours."

The diplomatic rebuke comes weeks after Swiss foreign minister Ignazio Cassis made a rare visit to North Korea, where he officially reopened the office and discussed normalising relations.

Swiss authorities said relations between the two countries would be maintained through the North Korean embassy in Bern, and the Swiss embassy in Beijing.

Diplomatic links with North Korea were first established in 1974 and Switzerland opened a co-operation office in Pyongyang in 1997. It was closed during the pandemic and remained shut for six years.

Switzerland's department of foreign affairs said the country "distinguishes between its sanctions policy and its commitment to maintaining dialogue with all countries".

It added: "The reopening of the office in Pyongyang did not in any way affect Switzerland's implementation of UN sanctions related to North Korea's nuclear programme, nor its implementation of EU sanctions targeting North Korea in the context of Russia's war against Ukraine."

Thousands of North Korean soldiers have been deployed to help support Russia's war with Ukraine.

In August, Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Moscow of using North Korean missiles.
