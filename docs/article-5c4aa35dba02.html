Queen Latifah celebrated female empowerment during her Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction

2026-11-15 23:47

It was an evening of female empowerment for Queen Latifah at the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame on Saturday night.

Megan Thee Stallion and Remy Ma inducted the “U.N.I.T.Y” rapper into the Rock Hall’s class of 2026, and she took the stage, shouting out her family and friends, who populated the crowd at Los Angeles’ downtown Peacock Theatre.

Latifah and fellow inductee MC Lyte performed a medley of their hits, with Remy Ma and Megan joining them to perform Latifah’s “Ladies First.”

Having the women on stage was a full-circle moment for the hip-hop pioneer.

“It’s all that I hoped for out of that record,” she exclusively told Page Six. “I wanted that record to be an anthem for women. I wanted it to be something that made women feel good about themselves.”

“[It’s] not about knocking men down or separating us at all. It’s really about cheering women on, being a cheerleader for us and our own self-esteem and our confidence, and how we should treat ourselves,” she added.

She further told us she’s grateful to have “a bunch of feminist men around me, who let me make all the music I want to, and were happy to hand me lyrics that were really championing women, because they got sisters, mothers and girlfriends and wives and daughters — and they want them to have that type of world.”

And while she may rap “Latifah’s Had It Up 2 Here,” she was overjoyed by the Rock Hall honor. She, MC Lyte and Method Man, who was inducted with the Wu-Tang Clan, were spotted shouting and hugging each other backstage.

“I am feeling blown away by the amount of love in this moment, and how far we’ve come,” she told us.

“I’m feeling good because Black women have been doing [their] thing in the world, and it’s a nice time to feel good about something and to feel hopeful,” she said.

Latifah reflected on the moment during her induction speech.

“There’s a time that I would’ve thought something like this would be inconceivable to be inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. It’s difficult enough being a woman in hip-hop, let alone being a hip hopper in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame,” she said.

Latifah also thanked her family and friends, saying “many times when my dad told me sometimes you would have to stand alone just doing what you do. Tonight is not one of those nights.”

“If you’re here with me tonight, if you’re my family and friends, would you just stand up,” Latifah said to a thunderous ovation from the audience.

Latifah shouted out her son, Rebel, and took a moment to thank longtime partner, Eboni Nichols, and her business partner, Shakim Compere’s wife, Tanya, for the sacrifices they’ve made for them to have their careers.

“I want to thank our families for so much sacrificing that you do, doing it for us to accomplish those dreams,” she said.
