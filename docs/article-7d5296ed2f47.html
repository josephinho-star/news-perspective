Turkish football chief rejects blame over referee corruption scandal

2026-10-05 14:22

Turkish Football Federation (TFF) President İbrahim Hacıosmanoğlu on Monday rejected attempts to blame him and his board for corruption in Turkish football after the country’s top refereeing official and four others were jailed pending trial.

Ferhat Gündoğdu, who has since resigned as head of the Central Referees Committee (MHK), and four others were jailed on Saturday over allegations that they manipulated the management of referees.

They appeared before a judge at the İstanbul Courthouse in Çağlayan on charges including “forgery of official documents,” “breach of trust” and “illegal acquisition of personal data.”

“Don’t try and pin 30 years of corruption in football on me,” Hacıosmanoğlu told the Hürriyet newspaper in an interview.

“Let no one try to blame me and my colleagues on the board … for the corruption, decay and lack of ethics that have existed in Turkish football for 15, 20 or perhaps 30 years,” said Hacıosmanoğlu, who has held the role for just over two years.

“For the future of Turkish football, we will be prepared, if necessary, not only to do our part but to assume the full weight of responsibility.”

Last week Turkish media published leaked WhatsApp exchanges and other messages between Hacıosmanoğlu and Gündoğdu that suggested possible interference in referee rankings and promotions as well as preferential treatment.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek has said prosecutors are examining claims about “interference in the processes of referee classification, assignments, scoring and appointments, as well as the exams on rules.”

Two other referees working in Turkey’s top-flight Süper Lig, who were detained on Thursday night, were due to appear before a judge on Monday.

The investigation appears to be linked to a betting scandal that emerged last year. The TFF suspended 149 referees over betting allegations in October 2025.

In an investigation involving 571 referees from the professional leagues, the federation found that 371 had sports betting accounts and 152 were actively placing bets.

Sports journalist Ayhan Şensoy, who wrote a 2024 book on the subject called “The Gambling Pit,” told Agence France-Presse on Friday that the arrests appeared to be a continuation of last year’s betting scandal.

He said he doubted the current federation leaders had “any chance of staying in office.”
