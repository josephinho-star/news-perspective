Dan’s Week 5 NFL Betting Card

1. Philadelphia Eagles +7½ (-110)

Philadelphia Eagles at Jacksonville Jaguars, Sunday at 9:30 a.m. ET (London)

OK, this is the official "Dan Z.'s Plug Your Nose and Hit 'Place Bet' Bet of the Week." I know I promised to workshop the title and come up with something better, but I'm busy so deal with it. The Eagles have been inconsistent all year and are honestly lucky to even be 2-2. Meanwhile, the Jags have looked dominant. So why in the world would I back the Eagles? Because the second you're sure of something in the NFL (the Eagles stink and the Jaguars are Super Bowl contenders is the common refrain this week), that's when you know you're about to be proven wrong.

Getting more than a touchdown in a neutral-site game is the strongest argument for Philadelphia. Jacksonville’s defense just allowed Joe Burrow to throw for 428 yards, giving Jalen Hurts a route to keeping this competitive. My concern is Philadelphia’s depleted offense: Lane Johnson has retired, Cam Jurgens was traded, and Saquon Barkley and DeVonta Smith missed practice again Thursday with hamstring injuries. At +7½, a seven-point loss still cashes, but those personnel questions kept the Eagles outside my five.

2. Minnesota Vikings-New Orleans Saints Over 42 (-107)

Minnesota Vikings at New Orleans Saints, Sunday at 1 p.m. ET

This is a confusing line. The Saints just allowed 45 points against the Falcons while scoring 24. New Orleans has scored at least 24 points in all four games this season. Yes, the Vikings defense is good. But it's also benefited from a weak schedule and one game played in a monsoon.

Minnesota only managed 15 points against the Dolphins last week, but that's a bit misleading. They couldn't score in the red zone, but that’s largely because they were missing Justin Jefferson and he's expected to play this week. Plus, T.J. Hockenson could have scored a late TD, but slid just outside the end zone so the Vikings could kneel down and end the game. I also expect Kevin O'Connell to have a game plan ready to attack this very vulnerable Saints defense.

New Orleans’s defense allows five yards per rush, giving Minnesota a favorable opportunity to improve its scoring. Tyler Shough has nine touchdown passes and returned to full practice Thursday, while Jefferson expects to play for Minnesota. Those developments strengthen your over case. My hesitation remains the Vikings’ strong defense, limited offensive production and Christian Darrisaw’s uncertain availability. The Saints can contribute points, but Minnesota could still turn this into a defensive game. I see the argument, but passed on over 42.

3. Las Vegas Raiders-New England Patriots Under 45 (-109)

Las Vegas Raiders at New England Patriots, Sunday at 1 p.m. ET

As I have mentioned most weeks, my general NFL betting philosophy is to fade what we saw most recently. The Las Vegas offense has looked explosive through four weeks, especially since Brock Bowers returned. But this is still a team quarterbacked by Kirk Cousins and due for a letdown, especially in an early-afternoon game multiple time zones over.

The Patriots offense has struggled most of the year so far but, aside from a 35-point disaster against Jacksonville, the defense has been elite. They held the Seahawks to 13 points, the Steelers to 3 points and the Bills to 26. Sure, 26 points is a lot, but the Raiders aren't the Bills and that's the only New England game this season that went over 45 points.

Las Vegas’s pass rush and struggling running game give this under a credible foundation. Pressure on Drake Maye could stall New England’s drives, while the Raiders may struggle to generate consistent production on the ground. My concern is Kirk Cousins’s 11 touchdown passes against a Patriots secondary dealing with injuries to Christian Gonzalez and Carlton Davis. Maye’s improvement against Buffalo also raises New England’s scoring ceiling. I prefer Raiders +3½, which can cash in either a defensive game or a shootout.

4. Denver Broncos-Los Angeles Chargers Under 42 (-108)

Denver Broncos at Los Angeles Chargers, Sunday at 4:05 p.m. ET

Where are the points going to come from? The completely broken Chargers offense? The offense led by Bo Nix? I just don't see it. The Chargers defense hasn't been the problem and it has actually been a solid unit this year that has been let down by the offense. The Denver defense isn't as good as last year, but it's still a pretty solid group. In this divisional clash, I see it being very low-scoring where both teams look to control time of possession.

Denver’s offensive struggles and the Chargers’ protection problems make your under understandable. Both Los Angeles starting tackles missed Wednesday’s practice, and Ladd McConkey is also managing an injury. Those concerns could make sustained touchdown drives difficult. The opposing argument is Denver’s secondary: Pat Surtain II and Riley Moss missed practice again Thursday, potentially improving Justin Herbert’s matchup. Defensive injuries on both sides and turnover-created short fields kept this outside my five, although I lean under rather than over.

5. Seattle Seahawks -3 (-110)

San Francisco 49ers at Seattle Seahawks, Sunday at 4:25 p.m. ET

This line is BEGGING for people to jump on San Francisco. After all, I just declared the 49ers the best team in the NFL. So why would I take the Seahawks? Because when Vegas wants you to do something, you should do the opposite. My hunch is that the public is going to hammer San Francisco as an underdog, so this is a simple zig when they zag play.

Seattle’s defense and home field give your pick a solid foundation, especially with San Francisco dealing with defensive injuries. The Seahawks allow just 3.9 yards per play, so this is a serious test for Brock Purdy. My disagreement comes from San Francisco’s offensive efficiency: 6.6 yards per play and a 57.5% third-down conversion rate. Seattle’s own third-down struggles also make separation less convincing. A three-point win only pushes your bet; I prefer San Francisco +3 at -102.

1. Tampa Bay Buccaneers +9 (-105)

Tampa Bay Buccaneers at Dallas Cowboys, Thursday Night Football

Dallas has allowed 28 points and 150.3 rushing yards per game, making a comfortable Cowboys victory less dependable than their offense suggests. Tampa Bay’s running game and defense provide a route to keeping this competitive, even with Baker Mayfield out. Rookie Jalon Daniels’s turnovers and injuries in Tampa’s secondary are the biggest concerns. Still, nine points leave room for Dallas to win without covering. I prefer that cushion to trusting the Cowboys’ defense to create separation.

GPT’s projected score: Cowboys 27, Buccaneers 20

Well, the Bucs have burned me back-to-back weeks (I mean they pushed in Week 4, but ties are like kissing your sister so I'm going to count it as burning me) so there's no way I could back them in Week 5. That being said... I actually don't hate this number and I considered backing the Bucs again. Why? I love pain, I guess?

2. Chicago Bears-Green Bay Packers Under 46 (-110)

Chicago Bears at Green Bay Packers, Sunday at 1 p.m. ET

Green Bay converts just 26.1% of third downs, while Chicago allows opponents to convert only 23.7%. The Packers also struggle to run, making sustained scoring drives difficult against this defense. Chicago averages 39.8 rushing attempts per game, giving the Bears a way to consume time and limit possessions. Green Bay’s vulnerable run defense could allow Chicago to score efficiently, but the matchup still favors the under. A combined 45 points wins this bet, while 46 produces a push.

GPT’s projected score: Bears 23, Packers 17

This is an interesting one. I generally like the idea of going with the under in divisional matchups, so I feel like GPT is taking a page out of my own book here. I also think this play makes a lot of sense. The Bears have a messy QB situation but the defense has been mostly good. The Packers haven't lit up the scoreboard through four weeks, either. I think I like this bet, too.

3. Las Vegas Raiders +3½ (-104)

Las Vegas Raiders at New England Patriots, Sunday at 1 p.m. ET

Kirk Cousins has 11 touchdown passes against four interceptions, while Drake Maye has four touchdown passes and seven interceptions. Maye’s improvement against Buffalo matters, but Las Vegas’s pass rush can challenge him and prevent New England from building a comfortable lead. The Raiders’ weak running game and early East Coast start are concerns. Getting more than a field goal at -104 is the attraction: a three-point Patriots victory still pays this ticket. I prefer that protection to the Raiders’ moneyline.

GPT’s projected score: Patriots 24, Raiders 23

This one I don't love. It feels like the books WANT people to back the Raiders, who are 3-1 and came very close to upsetting the Chiefs last week. New England beat Buffalo so the public isn't exactly fading the Patriots, but this still feels like a bit of a trap line to me.

4. San Francisco 49ers +3 (-102)

San Francisco 49ers at Seattle Seahawks, Sunday at 4:25 p.m. ET

San Francisco averages 6.6 yards per play and converts 57.5% of third downs, giving the 49ers multiple ways to sustain drives. Seattle’s defense is the strongest opposing argument, allowing just 3.9 yards per play. However, the Seahawks’ own third-down struggles make a comfortable victory less convincing. San Francisco’s defensive injuries are a concern, but I expect its offense to keep this competitive. At -102, +3 protects against a narrow defeat and pushes on a three-point Seattle win.

GPT’s projected score: 49ers 24, Seahawks 23

Well, this is the bet where GPT and I go directly head-to-head. I won the first round in Week 3, but GPT got me twice in Week 4. I guess that means pray for me on this one.

5. Buffalo Bills-Los Angeles Rams Under 54½ (-102)

Buffalo Bills at Los Angeles Rams, Monday Night Football

The Rams allow just 4.0 yards per play and a 33.3% third-down conversion rate, giving them a credible way to slow Buffalo’s scoring. Los Angeles also converts only about 53% of red-zone trips into touchdowns, despite producing substantial yardage. Both offenses have enough talent to threaten this under, and improved Rams finishing is the main risk. Still, 54.5 leaves room for a 30–24 game to cash. At -102, the defensive matchup and scoring threshold make this my fifth selection.

GPT’s projected score: Rams 27, Bills 24

I like this one a lot. A primetime game between two teams with explosive offenses screams "UNDER" because the public loves betting overs, particularly in island games. I kinda really like GPT's card this week (well except the two I said I don't like).
