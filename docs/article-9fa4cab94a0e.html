Netflix film boss in firing line amid streaming giant’s layoffs and alleged fall out with Ben Affleck

2026-10-15 13:45

Netflix film chairman Dan Lin was spared in Wednesday’s bloodbath at the streamer, which saw 1,000 staffers receiving pink slips and Lin’s No. 2, film VP Doug Belgrad, step down.

But insiders say Lin — who has held the top film position at the streaming giant since April 2024 — isn’t out of the woods as Netflix’s movie throne is a seat that’s become increasingly hot.

One person with ties to the studio says Belgrad’s exit will only further expose the main criticism of Lin — that his relationships with the talent community are strained. After all, Belgrad, who spent a big chunk of his career at Sony Pictures under the talent-friendly reign of Amy Pascal, was viewed as the good cop to Lin’s bad lieutenant within the loath-to-hear-no agencies.

Lin’s tenure at Netflix has been cast as the inverse to that of his predecessor Scott Stuber, who was known for spending freely and being extremely talent-friendly, paying handsomely for stars. Shortly after Lin arrived, a major reorganization of the film group occurred, and Netflix’s original movie output was reduced by more than 50% from the Stuber era.

On the talent front, amid all the scuttlebutt yesterday, a source close to Ben Affleck told us that the actor griped that he “couldn’t stand” Lin.

That would appear to track with what went down at Netflix’s “Animals” premiere on Oct. 1.

It was Netflix chief content officer Bela Bajaria, and not Lin, who took the stage to introduce Affleck to the audience inside the Directors Village Theatre in Los Angeles. The move raised eyebrows given that “Animals” is a movie and not a TV series.

(While Bajaria’s title is vaguely broad, her own Netflix bio mostly highlights her role overseeing series like “Stranger Things,” “Wednesday,” “Bridgerton,” “Lupin,” “Squid Game,” “Adolescence” and “Beef” — as well as the streamer’s “push into live events and sports with specials such as ‘Chris Rock: Selective Outrage,’ ‘The Roast of Tom Brady‘ and multiyear deals with the NFL and WWE,'” the company info says.)

For his part, Affleck gave a hearty shoutout to Bajaria and made no mention of Lin in his pre-screening speech. (Lin was not spotted inside the theater or after-party, but was seen on the red carpet, a source said.)

A rep for Affleck responded when we reached out: “We’ve had nothing but the best and most supportive partnership with Netflix.” (Netflix reps did not get back to us.)

Sources further tell Page Six Hollywood that David Fincher opted to exit his Netflix deal after clashing with Lin over the upcoming film “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth,” starring Brad Pitt.

Fincher had made three movies in a row at Netflix, including “Mank” and “The Killer.” But “Booth” was the sole title that saw him working with Lin. One knowledgeable source says the A-list director had had enough with Lin’s budgetary shackles.

Meanwhile, Netflix CEO Ted Sarandos is known to be semi-obsessed with scoring an elusive best picture Oscar.

This year, the studio’s two best shots appear to be “La Bola Negra” — which took home the audience award at the Toronto International Film Festival” — as well as “Booth,” a spin-off of Quentin Tarantino‘s “Once Upon a Time… in Hollywood.”

But only the latter is a homegrown title — i.e., one that Lin can take credit for.

All the while, recently ousted Warner Bros. film chiefs Michael De Luca and Pam Abdy are said to be waiting in the wings. There’s no question that the pair — known for their strong relationships with talent gatekeepers, particularly with CAA CEO Bryan Lourd — would provide an antidote to Lin’s approach.

Before David Ellison dispatched Abdy from Warner Bros.’ Burbank lot, she was spotted lunching with Sarandos.

Still, Lin dodged some major bombs that De Luca and Abdy did not.

Sources say Lin — with input from Belgrad — passed on the Tom Cruise disaster “Digger,” which is expected to result in many millions in losses for Warner Bros. Lin also avoided Maggie Gyllenhaal’s pricey flop “The Bride.”

For all the eyerolls over Lin’s penny-pinching ways, he was prescient enough to recognize that an Alejandro González Iñárritu arthouse film featuring an unrecognizable Cruise in prosthetics was a risk too big.
