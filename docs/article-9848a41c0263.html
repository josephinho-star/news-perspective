Tom Hardy shocks fans with surprising career move

2026-08-28 23:09

Tom Hardy’s fans are in disbelief after the actor dropped a rap album with underground group Czarface on Friday.

Under his alias Frankie Pulitzer, the “MobLand” star collaborated with 7L & Esoteric and Inspectah Deck of Wu-Tang Clan on “Czarface Meets Frankie Pulitzer.”

“Tom Hardy and Inspectah Deck collab album is a sentence I never thought I would say,” one stunned fan wrote via X, with another commenting, “Tom hardy really said method acting wasn’t enough.”

A third remarked, “David Bowie may be gone but we still have Tom Hardy,” while a fourth chimed in, “bro really unlocked a secret rap persona.”

“Frankie Pulitzer just confirmed what we already knew: Tom Hardy’s IMDb page is going to keep getting weirder in the best possible way,” another person gushed.

The album contains various references to the Green Goblin, Marvel hero Thor, Shakespeare, Stephen King’s “Misery” and more, and features guest collabs with artists like Busta Rhymes and Method Man.

In “Raw Forever,” Hardy, 48, notably references the Green Goblin, rapping, “I be somewhat of a scientist / like Willem Dafoe / If you know, then you know / Goblin up crumbs.”

He continues, “Doctor Doom / how I’m the Victor / the Scripture.”

A fan reacted to the eclectic cinematic references via X, writing, “MARVEL sipped through his brain like some sort of a symbiotic element… I appreciate that.”

On another track, titled “Grim-Visaged War,” Hardy voices Shakespeare‘s “Richard III” monologue in what sounds like the character of Bane, the actor’s character from 2012’s “The Dark Knight Rises.”

While many fans have expressed surprise at Hardy’s seemingly random career pivot, the actor has been vocal about his passion for rap in the past.

He once shared that he began rapping at the young age of “14 or 15.”

“Because I come from a nice middle-class neighborhood, it was a very hard sell. And I wasn’t very good!” he confessed in 2011, per the BBC. “I’ve recorded loads of stuff but it’s never been released.”

Of course, the “Mad Max: Fury Road” actor isn’t the first celebrity to unveil a musical career change this year. Just last month, Anthony Hopkins released a classical music album, “Life is a Dream.”

The Oscar winner — who is an accomplished pianist and composer — recorded a collection of original compositions with the Philharmonia Orchestra under the supervision of conductor Gustavo Dudamel.

“Music was my first desire, my first wish. I’ve been composing music all my life. Some of these pieces have lived with me for decades, and I still find myself returning to them,” Hopkins, 88, shared in a Facebook post.

“As my mother was passing, she was lonely, and there was only so much the family could provide,” the “Big Little Lies” star explained to a University of San Francisco gathering of students during the school’s Silk Speakers series.
