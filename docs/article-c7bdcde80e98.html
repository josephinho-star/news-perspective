Nassau County unveils first-of-its-kind memorial to cops lost to suicide: ‘They lived as heroes’

2026-11-17 23:59

Nassau County dedicated a monument honoring local police officers who have taken their own lives in what is believed to be a first-of-its-kind memorial in the state.

The memorial, unveiled in front of the Nassau County Police Department’s headquarters on Tuesday, honors the officers’ service, as their families gathered for the dedication alongside the county’s top brass and police union leaders.

Shevlin, who noted there seem to be no similar memorials anywhere in the state, said the memorial will give families a place to remember their loved ones. He added that it should also send a message to officers who are struggling now.

Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman, who made his first public appearance since losing his bid for governor earlier this month, warmly greeted family members of fallen officers and vowed that his county would dedicate “every resource available” to help officers in need of mental health support.

“We remember and honor those officers who served and protected us while fighting battles we could not see,” the inscription on the memorial says.

“No officer needs to bear their burdens alone, or have their family and friends experience this terrible loss. In their memory may we build a community where officers feel safe and are encouraged to seek help.”

Family and friends of the deceased Nassau officers told The Post that the memorial means more to them than words can describe.

“I am so grateful,” Stephen Kear, a former NYPD officer and father of a Nassau officer lost to suicide.

His son, Matthew Kear, was a 27-year-old former Navy veteran and two-year NCPD officer who took his own life in January 2025.

“You see so many bad things in the world, but you don’t get to see the beautiful things, and this is a beautiful thing that they’ve done for the families — Matthew’s smiling down from heaven,” he said.

Nassau’s new memorial opened just one day after neighboring Suffolk County opened the first of seven new “honor walls” that honor its cops who were killed in the line of duty.

It is unclear how many officers or former cops have committed suicide on Long Island this year, however, officials said there have been tragically at least two over the last few months.
