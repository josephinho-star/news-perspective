‘Fake Cartier Heiress’ Andrea Bartzen turned away from NYC event

2026-11-17 23:40

Palm Beach’s “fake Cartier heiress” Andrea Bartzen is in NYC and being turned away at parties.

Bartzen — who was the subject of a scathing NY Mag article that claimed she stiffed bills and made up her lineage to infiltrate elite circles — was spotted attempting and failing to get into a 25th anniversary bash for high-end chocolatier MarieBelle on Monday night.

“She was trying to convince the publicist to let her in,” says a source at the party at the Broome Street store. “They wouldn’t let her in.”

The event was thrown by longtime PR guru Norah Lawlor.

Lawlor is based in NYC, but spends her winters in Palm Beach where she throws events and handles clients like real heiress Consuelo Vanderbilt Costin.

“Andrea showed up,” Lawlor told us when we called to ask about what happened. “She wasn’t on the guest list, she wasn’t invited.”

Bartzen, while failing to sweet-talk her way in to the treat shop, didn’t leave empty-handed.

“I gave her a goodie bag,” Lawlor told us. “She asked to come back later and I said, ‘No. You are not on the list. I will invite you one day when it’s appropriate.'”

Lawlor told us, “Don’t go if you’re not invited. That’s the rules.”

And while that is how the artisanal cookie crumbled, this could all be a big misunderstanding.

Bartzen says she was invited by another guest as a plus-one, which Page Six has confirmed. The guest, however, never got it approved.

“I went to have a business meeting with someone there who was invited,” she told us. “I said please add me as a plus-one and get it confirmed … Norah, the gatekeeper, said ‘You didn’t RSVP personally,’ and so I said ‘Fine’ and didn’t go to the event.”

“The girl that invited me stepped outside, we had a five-minute meeting and then I left,” Bartzen tells us.

Bartzen then threatened to sue us multiple times and accused us of “assault journalism.”

Invited guests included Nicole Miller, Keiko Aoki and lifestyle expert Pamela Morgan.

In March, New York magazine ran a lengthy article called “The Fake Cartier and the Fake Rockefeller,” accusing Bartzen and her then-boyfriend — a magician allegedly posing as a member of the Rockefeller family — of pretending to be blue bloods, crashing parties in Palm Beach, the Hamptons and other wealthy areas. She has lived in New York.

The list of more serious allegations also included renting out an apartment she didn’t own (but had a similar name of the real owner), and promoting events by saying money would go to specific charities, though none reportedly received funds.

Bartzen has denied the allegations and dismissed the magazine’s account as a “hit piece.”

Page Six previously reported in April on another confrontation involving Bartzen, after a guest allegedly accused her of fraud at one of her “Cocktails and Conversation” events, which featured a former professional athlete and a former Navy SEAL.

Bartzen booted the accuser and insisted she wouldn’t be deterred from pursuing what she described as her mission to “heal humanity.”

“I’m not gonna back down. I am here to help humanity and mental health,” she told Page Six at the time. “I won’t let someone try and stop me and lay down dead because they tried to kill me. I have a bigger soul purpose, to heal humanity, and that’s what this event was about.”
