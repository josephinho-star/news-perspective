Skydance CEO David Ellison pressed about his politics, CBS and CNN’s futures at NYT DealBook Summit

2026-11-18 19:23

NEW YORK — Skydance CEO David Ellison was in the hot seat Wednesday at The New York Times DealBook Summit, making his first major appearance since the Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) merger.

Sitting down with The New York Times' Andrew Ross Sorkin, Ellison reiterated his mission for both CBS News and CNN to provide "truth-based, fact-based bipartisan down the middle" coverage, in the aftermath of closing his $111 billion purchase of WBD, putting two major Hollywood studios and dozens of networks, including HBO, CBS and CNN, under one corporate umbrella.

"When you look at what our goal for news is — we're one year into this journey — is to make sure that we can rebuild trust back to at least where it was previously, if not even higher," Ellison said. "We have incredible journalists that are showing up every single day to accomplish that goal. That is our mission today. That is a mission tomorrow. That will be our mission five years from now."

"And fundamentally, we will not stop working until we earn that trust. And from an editorial standpoint, you know, we believe in editorial independence, we don't get involved basically in those decisions, but we trust the journalists to do their jobs," he continued.

The consolidation sparked major concerns within the media and entertainment industry, particularly how he intends to oversee both CBS News and CNN. He has overseen CBS News since late 2025 after Skydance's $8 billion merger with Paramount.

Ellison was asked whether CBS News and CNN will merge as a result of corporate synergy. He teased about potential changes to come but kept details under wraps.

"Right now, we're incredibly happy operating on basically within the status quo," Ellison said. "There might be some news-sharing in the future, but right now we're happy with them with where they are."

The Skydance chief sidestepped questions about his appointment of Bari Weiss as the editor-in-chief of CBS News and the major shakeups at "60 Minutes," namely Scott Pelley's dramatic exit from the magazine program earlier this year, telling Sorkin he's "really proud" of the work of his journalists.

Critics of Ellison and his billionaire father Larry Ellison, who provided financial backing for the multibillion-dollar merger, have taken aim over the Ellisons' perceived chumminess with President Donald Trump.

When asked directly by Sorkin whether he himself voted for Trump, Ellison dodged.

"I've actually never disclosed that," Ellison responded. "And what I'll say is for me, I voted both sides of the aisle basically my entire life... I'm not a partisan."

He noted that, "Half my family's Democrat, half my family is Republican," adding that such dynamics "could make dinner table conversations a little bit challenging."

Ellison also dispelled the rumors that Paramount's development of "Rush Hour 4" was not because of Trump's demand but rather one of his studio chiefs who came up with reviving the Jackie Chan-Chris Tucker franchise.

"You never heard from your father or from the president on anything about 'Rush Hour 4,' that's completely a made-up story," Sorkin followed.

"There's a lot of made-up stories," Ellison replied.

Ellison was also asked about Elon Musk's billion-dollar investment into Skydance. He refrained from saying whether there will be a more formal partnership with the SpaceX founder in the future but said any collaboration would "be a privilege."

Ellison also committed that HBO will distribute "Musk," the scathing documentary about the tech mogul produced by A24, which has a streaming partnership with HBO.

"We believe in independence. We don't believe in censorship of any kind," Ellison said.
