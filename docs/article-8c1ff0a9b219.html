EDF snaps up 1.3m more customers after Utilita takeover

2026-11-19 09:44

EDF Energy will grow its share of the UK’s household energy market to more than 6m customer accounts after snapping up the pay-as-you-go supplier Utilita.

Great Britain’s fourth-largest energy supplier expects to complete the takeover early next year, adding 1.3m accounts to its customer base and taking on Utilita’s 1,500 staff. The price of the deal was undisclosed.

The pair will continue to serve their customers independently until the deal concludes. Utilita customers will be contacted about any changes to their service.

After the deal, EDF has promised to continue to operate from Utilita’s headquarters near Southampton which is the base for about 600of Utilita’s employees.

EDF, which is owned by the French state, set out the takeover plans a year after it completed a deal to take on 47,000 standard credit accounts from Utilita which said it hoped to sharpen its focus on smart pay-as-you-go customers.

The deal is likely to have helped bolster Utilita’s financial position, which briefly fell short of the industry regulator Ofgem’s tougher new financial resilience tests in an industry assessment in September 2025.

Utilita confirmed that it was one of five suppliers which had failed to meet Ofgem’s financial rules last year, but said it was able to comply with the standards within months of the regulator’s red flag.

For EDF, the deal will help to bolster its business as most large suppliers continue to lose customers to smaller rivals. EDF lost about 280,000 customer accounts in the year after July 2025, while British Gas, Scottish Power and E.ON Next were also losing customers. Octopus Energy, the country’s largest supplier, is the only big energy company which has continued to grow its market share.

Utilita was founded in 2003 as a specialist prepay energy supplier, and became the first supplier to install a smart meter in 2008.

Its pioneering approach to smart metering helped the company to modernise pay-as-you-go energy by allowing customers to top-up via an app, website, or by phone rather than visit a corner shop or post office to pay for credit on a plastic card or key.

Simone Rossi, the chief executive of EDF’s UK business, said Utilita would bring “further expertise” to the supplier, marking “a significant step” in EDF’s ambition to grow its UK customer business.

“Together, we will have the scale and skills to keep developing services and support for the households who rely on us,” Rossi said.

Bill Bullen, the founder of Utilita, said the deal would ensure that Utilita could continue to provide “best-in-class smart prepay solutions to Britain’s households”.

Last month, German energy group E.ON Next completed a deal to buy struggling UK rival Ovo after the deal was approved by Britain’s competition watchdog.

The deal comes as households prepare for a sharp rise in energy bills this winter. On Wednesday, the consultancy Cornwall Insight forecast the quarterly industry price cap would jump 26% from January to the equivalent of £2,166 a year for a typical household.
