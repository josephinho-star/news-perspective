Turkish Defense Ministry denies plans to send 15,000 troops to Somalia

2026-11-12 14:11

Turkey’s defense ministry on Thursday denied claims that it planned to send 15,000 troops to Somalia at the Somali government’s request, saying its forces already stationed there would continue military assistance, training and advisory missions, the Anka news agency reported.

“Claims that Turkey will send 15,000 troops to Somalia at Somalia’s request do not reflect the truth,” the ministry said.

Ministry sources, responding to questions after a weekly press briefing, said the Somali-Turkish Task Force and Turkey’s Air Component Command were continuing to help strengthen Somalia’s counterterrorism capabilities.

The denial follows a series of developments that have broadened Turkey’s military role in the Horn of Africa country, including the deployment of fighter jets and a parliamentary vote extending authorization for its troop presence.

Turkey opened its largest overseas military base, Camp TURKSOM, in the Somali capital of Mogadishu in 2017. It has become one of the federal government’s closest security partners, providing training, equipment and economic support.

In July Turkey’s parliament approved a presidential motion extending the deployment authorization for two years, until July 27, 2028. The motion allows President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan to determine the size, scope, timing and rules of the deployment.

Opposition lawmakers questioned the lack of information about troop numbers, equipment and costs, as well as the possibility that Turkish forces could be drawn into direct fighting with al-Shabaab, the al-Qaeda-linked militant group battling Somalia’s federal government.

Turkish officials confirmed in January that the country had deployed F-16 fighter jets to Somalia. They said the aircraft would be operated by Turkish military personnel rather than Somali pilots.

On June 30 Somalia’s defense ministry initially announced that Turkish F-16s had carried out strikes against al-Shabaab positions in the Lower Shabelle region. A subsequent version referred only to support from international partners. Turkish authorities have not publicly confirmed the jets’ involvement in that operation.

Turkey’s military presence has also prompted allegations that it is becoming involved in Somalia’s internal political disputes.

In May the Turkish presidency’s Center for Combating Disinformation denied claims that Turkish forces were being used to pressure opposition groups and support President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud. The center said Turkish personnel had no involvement in Somalia’s elections or internal political affairs.

Further allegations emerged in September during fighting near Baidoa in southern Somalia between federal troops and forces loyal to an ousted regional leader. Forces aligned with former South West State President Abdiaziz Hassan Mohamed Laftagareen accused Turkish aircraft of carrying out strikes. Turkey did not publicly confirm involvement.

Turkey has expanded its engagement in Somalia since 2011 through aid, infrastructure, diplomatic and security programs.

The two governments signed a defense and economic cooperation agreement in February 2024 under which Turkey said it would help strengthen Somalia’s maritime security and protect its territorial waters.
