The Trump administration will formally overturn Biden-era Title IX rules that aimed to protect transgender and LGBTQ students. The Department of Education will return to rules it established in 2020, under Trump’s first administration.

“We will continue to relentlessly champion equal opportunity for all Americans and hold accountable any school or college that violates the rights, privacy, or athletic opportunities of our women and girls,” education secretary Linda McMahon said in a statement.

On its website, the education department wrote: “The Biden Administration’s Office for Civil Rights (OCR) pushed schools across the country to illegally allow males to intrude into women’s sports and intimate facilities, and it investigated school staff for using incorrect pronouns or failing to ask students to declare a preferred gender.”

As the education department website notes, the Trump administration began applying its own Title IX rules after Donald Trump began his second term in January 2025.

Pennsylvania is recording more than 900 measles cases, according to the state’s Department of Health, which updated its measles dashboard with 114 new cases this week. South Carolina and Utah are also reporting significant measles outbreaks.

After Pennsylvania announced two unvaccinated people had died from the disease earlier last month, the first measles-associated deaths in the state in 35 years, health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr. questioned whether the deaths were “fabricated”.

To date, Pennsylvania reports four people have died from measles this year. Less than 1% of those who’ve been infected with measles in the state were fully vaccinated against the disease.

In 2000, the United States eliminated measles, but the disease has experienced a resurgence amid a rise in anti-vaxxing rhetoric. In November, the Pan American Health Organization will consider whether to reevaluate the United States’ measles elimination status.

The company was slated to release GPT-6.1 Astra in October, but safety chief Saachi Jain told the WSJ that it fell short of ChatGPT’s standards in alignment tests, showing more deception than previous models and failing to follow human intent.

The news comes after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for the artificial intelligence agency to slow the development of AI models amid growing safety concerns. In a 12 September blog post, Amodei called for increased global cooperation and oversight of AI.

Last week, the chief executives of OpenAI and Anthropic were called to face an Australian senate inquiry after rogue OpenAI agents hacked Australian and US government websites. Both executives addressed the United Nations security council in separate briefings on AI safety last week as well.

Samuel Alito has recused himself from a major climate-change case before the supreme court after calls he step aside due to his stock holdings in oil companies

The court is expected to hear arguments from local officials in Boulder, Colorado alleging officials at SunCor Energy and ExxonMobil deceived the public about the role of fossil fuels in climate change. After Colorado’s supreme court refused to dismiss the lawsuit, the defendants filed a petition with the US supreme court asking them to shut down the case. In an appeal backed by the Trump administration, the fossil fuel companies argue the lawsuit is a serious threat to the industry and that state courts are not the proper venue to address a global issue.

In a letter posted today, Alito did not specify a reason for recusing himself from the case, though he has recused himself from other cases involving companies where he holds stock in the past. While Alito does not hold stock in SunCor Energy or ExxonMobil, he does have holdings in other oil companies like ConocoPhillips and Phillips 66.

An Arizona investigation into 60 registered voters who were accused of being noncitizens according to the federal Save database has determined that 52 were US citizens. The remaining eight noncitizens were in the United States legally.

Maricopa county attorney Rachel Mitchell conducted the investigation after county recorder Justin Heap informed her of 207 registered voters who were believed to be noncitizens, based on a review utilizing a controversial and often flawed federal database of voters’ citizenship status.

“Our office extends its appreciation to the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and Secretary Kristi Noem for expanding access to the SAVE database,” the County Recorder’s office said in a February statement. “We look forward to continued collaboration to ensure election integrity and that only eligible citizens participate in Maricopa County elections.”

Last week, the US supreme court lifted restrictions on the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (Save) database as Donald Trump seeks to impose tougher restrictions on voting ahead of November’s midterm elections.

In Arizona, the eight noncitizens were registered to vote due to a problem with the state’s motor vehicle division system, which automatically registered people who renewed their driver’s licenses if they had received their first driver’s license before the state required them to present proof of citizenship.

The state department will revoke the visas of 27 Latin American officials, former officials and their families, over allegations of ‘corruption and narco-trafficking’

Those affected include Bolivian attorney general Roger Mariaca, more than 20 other Bolivian prosecutors and judges, two Colombians, two Ecuadorians and one Peruvian judge.

The Trump administration says the visa revocations are intended “to fulfill the commitments to defend democratic governance across our hemisphere against corruption and narco-trafficking”, according to a state department statement. “Undermining a democratically-elected government is a threat to security and rule of law in the Americas and will not be tolerated.”

Earlier this year, Donald Trump convened the “Shield of the Americas”, a coalition of western hemisphere leaders designed “to eradicate the cartels”, just two months after a US military operation to capture Venezuela’s then president, Nicolás Maduro.

In visits to Colombia, Ecuador and Peru this month, secretary of state Marco Rubio pledged support for the right-leaning governments’ efforts to combat drug and human trafficking.

“This is not about being political or nonpolitical, but there needs to be a voice that seeks to promote the values of human dignity, the importance of life, and the possibility of perhaps spending more on health and education and less on weapons.”

Pete Hegseth ordered the defense department to ‘protect and uphold the reliability of America’s voting mechanisms against external manipulation and disruption from foreign actors’ in a 22 September memo first reported by Axios.

In the memo, Hegseth directed the leaders of US cyber command and the combat support agencies to prioritize “intelligence and cyber capabilities to ensure foreign actors do notmeddle in our democratic system”.

He also directed the Defense Intelligence Enterprise to “execute collection and production on foreign threats to our elections” and US cyber command to coordinate with the Department of Homeland Security “to counter potential cyber threats from foreign actors targeting our elections”.

Hegseth’s order comes as concerns about artificial intelligence are on the rise, but also as the Trump administration has spread misinformation about voter fraud. In July, Trump accused China of interfering in the 2020 election, which he lost.
