Ezra Frech flawlessly recovers from prosthetic leg falling off mid-‘DWTS’ finale freestyle to land perfect score

2026-11-25 03:50

Ezra Frech proved nothing can stop him when he flawlessly recovered from his prosthetic leg falling off during his “Dancing With the Stars” Season 35 finale freestyle with his pro partner, Daniella Karagach.

Despite him starting the routine with the prosthetic off, once he put it on he soon lost it after jumping down the stairs onto the ballroom floor as Linkin Park’s “Numb” played.

Frech was able to quickly get his prosthetic leg back on and finish the routine — which included wheelchair dancers on top of incredible choreography — with Karagach.

“His prosthetic was not supposed to come off, but what an incredible recovery,” co-host Alfonso Ribeiro said after the routine.

“Ezra, you are a hero,” Derek Hough said during the judges’ critiques, with Carrie Ann Inaba praising Karagach for “creating a whole new vocabulary” of dance.

The routine earned Frech and Karagach a 30/30, bringing their finale score to a perfect 90/90.

“I want it to be a celebration of what is possible when you’re different,” Frech said in a pre-performance package, getting emotional discussing how Karagach — who called him her “best friend” — has embraced his prosthetic leg.

“The song is about breaking stereotypes. No sob story, this is all about power,” Frech said, revealing the routine will feature “talented wheelchair dancers because we want to change the way people see disability through this dance.”

The routine was the pair’s last dance of the night, following their tango to “Levels” by Avicii in the “Instant Dance Challenge” round and their salsa to “Danza Kuduro” by Don Omar (feat. Lucenzo) in the “Judges’ Choice Round.”

Karagach, 33, previously teased that their freestyle had the potential to make waves in the ballroom.

“It’s definitely never been done before, let’s put it this way, ever on the show,” the former Mirrorball champ previously said on the official “DWTS” podcast.

“The type of dancing we have in it is very, very special … it’s very powerful. And if done correctly, it could definitely go down in, you know, a ‘Dancing With the Stars’ history moment.”

“Coming into this competition, dancing was pretty far out of my comfort zone — let alone ballroom dancing, let alone doing it live on national television,” he said. “I didn’t even know what I was gonna be capable of. I didn’t know what I was gonna be limited by.”

The Paralympian said that, through his partnership with Karagach, he discovered that he’s “able to do things that literally [he didn’t] even think was possible.”

“This show has created so many beautiful memories for me this season and, at the same time, has been one of the most challenging things I’ve ever had to do,” he added.

“The stuff that Dani and I are doing, we’re literally having to rewrite the rulebook every single week and push the boundaries of what a prosthetic leg is even designed to do in the first place,” Frech explained, noting how that has been “really difficult but so rewarding.”

He also shared what winning the Len Goodman Mirrorball Trophy would mean to him.

“Winning the Mirrorball would signify that it doesn’t matter what your circumstances are, it doesn’t matter what adversity you face, it doesn’t matter what challenges come your way. Anything is possible,” he said.
