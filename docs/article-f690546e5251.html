Aria CEO says AI copyright proposal amounts to ‘gaslighting’

Staying with AI, artists have also pushed back on the parliamentary report. The Australian Recording Industry Association (Aria) said it rejected the calls to reform copyright law, saying current law was fine – it just needed to be enforced.

The Aria CEO, Annabelle Herd, said in a statement:

Australian copyright law already gives music rightsholders the ability to sue if an AI company infringes their copyright by training in Australia without a license. Those rights ensure AI companies must come to the table, ask permission and pay an agreed fee.

Proposing to weaken the very right that gives creators commercial control and leverage and calling it additional protection is gaslighting.

Herd said AI companies should be required to disclose what copyrighted material they were using, and help rights holders enforce their rights. She claimed the type of changes recommended by the committee would make things worse.

Giving those companies the benefit of a new commercial scheme to allow them to scrape music without explicit permission from rightsholders will no do nothing to recover a cent for illegal overseas theft.

It will, however, undermine the growing, global AI music licensing market that will deliver control and value to Australian creators.

Copyright owners have asked again and again for government to make AI companies come to the negotiating table.

The parliament must not overturn centuries of copyright law on the fallacy that the law doesn’t work.

News Corp boss among those reacting to parliamentary committee’s AI report

Responses are coming thick and fast to the report of the parliamentary committee into AI, which recommended copyright law changes and new incentives for frontier labs to make commercial deals with artists, musicians, writers and journalists.

News Corp’s executive chair, Michael Miller, said in a statement that he wanted to see more detail on the ideas raised, and that:

Encouraging commercial deals sounds right, but the report lacks the detail needed to give rights holders confidence they can set their own terms of trade and receive fair payment.

Any framework that lets trillion-dollar AI platforms scrape content without voluntary agreements isn’t innovation - it’s state-sanctioned theft.

Guardian Australia responds to parliamentary AI committee’s report

Guardian Australia has welcomed recognition by the parliamentary joint standing committee on AI that creatives and news media must retain control of their work and be paid fairly for its use.

The committee released the 400-page report today, making dozens of recommendations on issues from datacentres to online safety rules.

Copyright was a major piece of the report, with the majority view of the committee – with some exceptions, including senator David Pocock – recommending the government “strengthen Australia’s copyright protections”, including designing a “mechanism to make it easier and fairer for Australian rights holders and creators to be paid by AI companies training frontier and other large language models in Australia”

In a statement, Danika Johnston, Guardian Australia’s interim managing director, said:

Guardian Australia welcomes the committee’s recognition that the creative and news media sectors must retain control of their work and be paid fairly when it is used by AI companies.

But payment is not permission. A strong copyright regime preserves the fundamental right of copyright holders to authorise use of their work and negotiate the terms and price, or refuse use altogether. Any scheme that removes that choice would weaken copyright.

Andrew Hastie warns Australia at risk of falling behind in ‘AI arms race’

The Liberal MP Andrew Hastie, the shadow minister for industry and sovereign capability, says Australia risks “slipping behind” in the “AI arms race”.

Speaking to the ABC’s Afternoon Briefing program a short time ago, Hastie said Australia needed sovereign AI capability – with investment from developers to build AI models here, as well as datacentres.

It’s important we have a level of control over these things. [In the] same way you don’t have private tycoons with their own nuclear arsenal, I think there’s a parallel between the nuclear race and the AI race.

And so I think it’s important that AI ultimately is accountable to the Australian people.

There’s an AI arms race under way and we’re at risk of slipping behind. If we slip behind, it makes us dependant on other countries … it makes us vulnerable to strategic coercion.

Hastie has previously called for AI to be run by Australians, as the government tries to hammer out an agreement with AI developers on copyright protection, as part of a new set of standards for AI companies operating and training models in Australia.

Last week, the Australian Financial Review reported the Albanese government was considering a copyright change under which AI companies would buy permits to train on Australian content but that music may be exempted.

Tennis Australia condemns abuse after graphic threats towards teen player

Eight arrested over alleged assault in the Gold Coast that left 15-year-old fighting for life

Eight teenagers have been arrested over an alleged assault in Surfers Paradise on the Gold Coast that left a 15-year-old fighting for his life.

Queensland police said the incident took place at about 3.20am today.

Eight males were located and arrested, including four 17-year-olds, three 18-year-olds and one 19-year-old. The four 17-year-olds were registered as schoolies attenders with the Queensland government, police said in a press conference.

The 15-year-old boy sustained significant head injuries in what police described as a “prolonged” attack. He was transported to the Gold Coast university hospital in a critical condition.

In a separate incident, a 42-year-old Runcorn man was found with serious injuries to his head and body in Broadbeach, on the Gold Coast, just after midnight.

Three teenagers, two who were registered as schoolies attenders, had charged over the Broadbeach assault, police said. They were scheduled to appear at Southport children’s court today.

Police said the man was assisting a girl who allegedly had her phone stolen by the three teenagers when the attack took place. The man was transported to Robina hospital.

Police are investigating the connection between the incidents and schoolies.
