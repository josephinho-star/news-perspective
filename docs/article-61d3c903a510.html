What is a gold IRA?

A gold IRA is a self-directed, tax-advantaged retirement account that holds eligible physical gold. Like IRAs that hold stocks and bonds, gold IRAs come in two varieties: traditional and Roth. Both kinds of accounts aim to help you save for retirement through tax incentives. With a traditional IRA, contributions may be tax-deductible, and withdrawals are generally taxable. With a Roth IRA, contributions are not tax-deductible, but qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

Gold IRAs have several key differences from IRAs that hold stocks and bonds. Gold IRAs require you to work with a specialized custodian who holds your gold for you. You can’t hold gold in your IRA at home, and if you do, you may be subject to severe penalties from the IRS.

IRAs that hold stocks and bonds are usually held through a brokerage, and these brokers usually don’t charge fees, or charge minimal fees. The fees for gold IRAs include custodial fees, management fees and transaction fees, among others. Selling the gold in your IRA may also involve selling the gold back to the provider.

How does a gold IRA work?

As noted above, you can’t open a gold IRA through a standard brokerage. Instead, you have to establish an account with an IRS-approved custodian that specializes in self-directed IRAs.

Once the account is open, it is typically funded via a tax-free rollover or direct transfer from an existing 401(k) or traditional IRA. Once your funds have been transferred, you select a precious metals dealer to purchase IRS-approved bullion or coins. You may also select the precious metals dealer first who can refer you to a custodian.

It’s important to understand that you never take personal possession of the gold while it’s in your IRA. IRS rules state that while metals remain in the IRA, they must be held by an approved custodian and depository for safekeeping. At distribution, an investor may generally take cash or, where available, an in-kind distribution of the metals. Applicable taxes and IRA distribution rules still apply.

Your custodian should provide regular account statements that track the current market value of your holdings based on the daily spot price. When selecting a custodian, make sure you know how you will monitor your holdings, whether online or through an app.

The first step — as with any financial decision — is to do your homework and do careful research on what precious metals dealer or custodian you want to invest with.

Once you have a custodian, the next phase is funding your account. Many investors choose to fund their new gold IRA through a direct, trustee-to-trustee transfer or a rollover from an existing traditional IRA, Roth IRA, or employer-sponsored 401(k).

You can also buy gold by sending money directly to your custodian, but by moving your retirement funds directly between the financial institutions, you avoid triggering any taxable events or premature withdrawal penalties. The most common method is to fund the account from another account.

It is important to remember that the total annual contribution to all your IRAs, whether traditional or Roth, is capped according to IRS rules.

You can also start the process with the precious metals dealer, which acts as the vendor that you purchase the physical metals from. The IRS enforces purity standards on the metal you buy, so you have to pick IRS-approved bullion bars or coins, such as American Gold Eagles, while strictly avoiding unapproved collectible or numismatic items.

The final step is to arrange for secure storage. The IRS strictly forbids you from storing gold IRA assets in your own home. As recently as 2021, a Rhode Island couple found out the truth of this rule the hard way as the IRS levied punitive taxes for breaking it.

Your precious metals dealer generally helps you establish a relationship with an IRS-approved depository. Your custodian finalizes the transaction by wiring funds to the dealer, who then ships your gold directly to the depository, where it remains fully insured and vaulted until you take distributions.

How a gold IRA rollover works

The benefit of rolling over your conventional IRA or other retirement plan that holds stocks and bonds (like a 401(k), 403(b), TSP) to a gold IRA is the ability to move a large amount of money into a new account without facing a taxable event.

After you have opened your account, you request a rollover through your existing retirement plan’s administrator. Transfers occur through one of two mechanisms: a direct rollover or an indirect rollover.

In a direct rollover, or trustee-to-trustee transfer, your current plan provider sends the designated funds directly to your new gold IRA custodian via check or wire transfer. Because the money never touches your personal bank account, this approach creates no tax liabilities, involves no tax withholding and eliminates the risk of missing regulatory deadlines.

In an indirect rollover, your current plan custodian issues a distribution check payable directly to you. Upon receiving the check, you have 60 calendar days to deposit those funds into your new gold IRA without facing a penalty and tax hit.

In this scenario, the original custodian of a employer plan is legally required to withhold 20% of the distribution for federal income taxes. IRA distributions default to 10% withholding and you can opt out. To complete a full rollover and avoid paying income taxes plus a potential 10% early withdrawal penalty on that withheld portion, you must make up that 10% or 20% out-of-pocket when funding the new account. If you miss the 60-day window, the IRS considers the entire sum a taxable distribution.

After the transferred funds settle in your new self-directed account, your custodian executes the purchase of IRS-approved precious metals from your chosen dealer and arranges secure, insured transport directly to an approved depository facility.
