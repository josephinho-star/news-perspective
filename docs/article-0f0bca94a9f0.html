Burnham promises to bring ‘substantial change’ as he gives first Commons address as prime minister – UK politics live

2026-09-01 14:57

Burnham says country needs 'substantial change', and he will deliver it

Burnham says his approach to the prisoner early release scheme is an example of doing politics differently.

This is politics done differently, problem-solving, over point-scoring. It is what the country needs. It is what the public want more of, and it will characterise my time in office.

He says another example has been starting cross-party talks on social care. Ed Davey, the Lib Dem leader, and Stuart Andrew, who was shadow health secretary until yesterday, participated, he says.

When it comes to the biggest issues facing the country, the economy and the cost of living crisis, people are looking for much more substantial change, as promised in our manifesto, and that is what I will bring.

Britain is not where any of us would wish it to be. Things are not working as they should.

Burnham says he will ensure areas in England without mayors can also benefit from devolution deals

Burnham says he has heard concerns about his devolution plans, and fears that areas without a metro or regional mayor won’t have the same advantages as those that do have one. He says he understands the concerns about the potential emergence of a two-tier England”.

I will not ignore [the concerns]. So I have asked No 10 North to begin work with Cornwall council and its six MPs on a devolution deal, with or without an elected mayor.

And I today extend the same offer to all parts of England without devolved power. The first secretary of state will today write to every member of this house setting out how they can engage No 10 North in the service of the places they represent.

I extended the same offer to the first ministers of Scotland and Wales, and the first and deputy first ministers of Northern Ireland when I met them over the summer, as they rightly pointed out, their situations are different, but there is interest in what a local model of devolution could look like in each of the home nations.

Burnham says he wants to give communities more control over services.

The government I lead will be relentless in giving people and places what they were promised 10 years ago, ownership and control, and from that control over their destiny comes change and growth.

And he says he can say this from his experience as mayor of Greater Manchester.

I say this with confidence. I do. Let me explain why. Because it is backed by a decade of personal experience.

Turning around one of the UK’s former industrial heartlands and turning it into the fastest growing city region in the country, buckingthe low-growth trend.

Burnham claims he has 'clear diagnosis of what has gone wrong' with UK

Burnham says, coming back to the Commons after 10 years, he has “a clear diagnosis of what has gone wrong”.

And let me lay it out clearly.

From the 1980s, this country took a series of wrong turns. Political power was centralised, economic power privatised, the country de-industrialised austerity followed, hollowing out councils and depriving them of the agency to act to reverse any of this.

And then Brexit compounded the damage, ushering in a decade of low growth and stalled regeneration.

Change begins with honesty. I’m not hearing much of it this afternoon. Unless we are blunt about what went wrong, we won’t turn things around.

Far from taking back control, the combination of these policies took control out of the hands of communities up and down Britain.

For 40 years, too many places have been drifting. Too many British high streets tell the story of this decline. People yearn to see them restored to what they once were.

Burnham says problems with indefinite IPP sentences have been 'ignored for too long'

Burnham confirms that the prisoner early release scheme rules are being changed to stop the killers of PC Andrew Harper being released.

And he says part of this plan will involve tackling the number of prisoners still in jail on indefinite IPP (imprisonment for public protection) sentences. This is an issue the Commons has “has ignored for too long”.

Burnham says he visited Dover in August where the government’s efforts to stop small boat crossings are delivering real results. He says numbers arriving over the summer have halved since last year. (See 9.29am.)

Scotland’s NHS boards will be slashed from 14 to two, John Swinney has announced. Ed Miliband says government accepts need for 'step change' in action to stop expansion of illegal West Bank settlements

Ed Miliband is now taking questions in the Commons in his new role as foreign secretary. The first two questions were answered by junior ministers, but Miliband took the third question himself. It was from Labour’s James Naish, who asked what the government would do to stop the expansion of illegal settlements on the West Bank.

Miliband condemned the settlements and said that the government would “not acquiesce in the destruction of the two-state solution”. He said the government was consulting with allies, and that an announcement about the measures it would take was coming soon.

He said Andy Burnham himself spoke recently about the need to do more to ban trade with the illegal settlements.

Asked by Labour’s Martin Rhodes if a trade ban affecting the illegal settlements would apply to the trade in services, Miliband said Rhodes had made an important point. He went on:

We need to look and we are looking indeed at our wider economic relationship with the occupied territories and the different tools we have to tackle it.

I want to be very clear; we do not want British companies financing, constructing or advertising new settlements. The work we are now undertaking is how we can stop that.

When Kit Malthouse (Con) criticised previous government ministers for mouthing “platitudes” on this issue “like goldfish”, and said they had completely failed to stop the expansion of illegal settlements, Miliband said that he agreed with Malthouse “pretty much entirely”.

And when the SNP’s Brendan O’Hara attacked Labour’s record on this, Miliband said the recognition of Palestinian statehood was significant. He went on:

I think we on this side of the house, as the prime minister has said, recognise the need for a step change in our approach, given the urgency and, as I say, he won’t have to wait too long [for an announcement].

Farage takes seat in Commons after winning Clacton byelection

In the Commons Nigel Farage, the Reform UK leader, has been sworn in as MP for Clacton. He was MP for Clacton before the summer anyway, but he triggered a byelection as a protest about the inquiry into claims he broke Commons rules by not declaring a £5m donation from the cryptocurrency billionaire Christopher Harborne.

After he won the byelection in August, Farage gave an interview in which he said “maybe” he should have declared the donation after all – implying he does now accept the inquiry is justified. (But he is also angry about the inquiry being extended to include financial support he received from his friend George Cottrell that also wasn’t declared.)
