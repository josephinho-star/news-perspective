Burnham says Brexit brought ‘decade of low growth’ in first Commons address as prime minister – UK politics live

2026-09-01 17:52

Andy Burnham has declared his government can make “this next decade better than the last” in a confident and relatively non-partisan address to MPs. Speaking in parliament for the first time since becoming PM more than six weeks ago, Burnham gave a wide-ranging opening statement and then took questions from more than three hours. Burnham said:

When I stood outside Downing Street six weeks ago, I said Britain needs a circuit breaker after a decade of turbulence, negativity, point scoring and divisive politics.

This is what I have outlined today.

A clear, coherent, and credible approach to growth that can unlock a feeling of positivity and possibility in every postcode in Britain.

Our job in here is to make it real by taking a place first rather than party first approach.

It starts with reasserting basic standards for Britain, that this is a country where no one sleeps rough and no community is blighted by an illegal waste tip.

And from there, working together to build a new sense of optimism around the country.

We can make this next decade better than the last.

We can lift the prospects of all our people and places.

Burnham repeatedly made a point of stressing his desire to collaborate with opposition parties, as part of his commitment to doing politics in a new way. He was relaxed and good-humoured and, although he faced criticism from some opposition MPs, he was generally given a fair hearing. He did not have any surprise new announcements, and much of what he said repeated arguments had made over the summer. On many issues he indicated that final decisions have yet to be taken. With the statement still running after three hours and 20 minutes, Burnham is still on his feet.

After Burnham had been taking questions for two and a half hour, Jeremy Corbyn told him the session had lasted “longer than the longest World Cup match”. He asked if Burnham would back rent controls.

Burnham said he thought he and Corbyn took part in longer hustings debates during the 2015 Labour leadership contest. On housing, he said “a package of measures to turn the housing market around” was needed.

Julian Lewis (Con) told Burnham he thought the local government reorganisation amounted to “shameless gerrymandering”. Lewis represents New Forest East, and he said his constituency was being “torn apart” with part that looks towards the New Forest being taken over by Southampton.

Burnham said he would ask the community secretary to look at this.

Burnham says he is 'concerned' about plans for Freeview TV to be switched off by 2034

David Mundell (Con) asked Burnham if he would review plans for terrestial Freeview TV to be switched off in 2034. This decision would affect a lot of elderly people in rural areas, he said.

I am concerned about those plans. I know some of my constituents in Makerfield rely on Freeview. There is perhaps an opportunity to look at this as part of the BBC’s BBC charter renewal process.

I can’t give [Mundell] a final answer today, but I recognise it’s a genuine issue that he’s raised. I know it will be of concern to his constituents and let me have a proper look at it.

In response to a question from Labour’s Marsha De Cordova about Europe, Burnham said the next UK-EU summit might take place “the month after next” (ie, in November), but this was still being finalised.

In response to a question from Ellie Chowns, the parliamentary leader of the Green party, Burnham said that he was committed to a carbon neutral future and he was willing to work with the Greens on this.

Burnham said Law had “misunderstood” what Burnham was saying. He said he was ruling out a border poll now. He said he was “very aware of every word in the Good Friday agreement”, which says there should be a border poll if there is clear majority support for one, and he said he would “stick to it 100%”.

He also said he cared about Northern Ireland and visited it often.

Burnham suggests Shropshire could also get devolution benefits without being part of combined authority

Helen Morgan, the Lib Dem MP for North Shropshire, said there are no immediate plans for Shropshire to have a combined authority or mayoral authority. People there were worried this would mean they would lose out under Burnham’s devolution plans, she said.

Burnham said he thought devolution deals would only work with unitary local government. He went on:

I don’t think it will work with a two-tier system, but that’s why I said to colleagues in Cornwall, I’m quite happy to see if a deal can be done for a single unitary authority. [See 5.02pm.]

That is what I understand members of parliament for Cornwall want working with the leader of Cornwall council. If that is the settled view, I’m not going to try and impose something else. Let’s work with that.

And the same could be true in Shropshire, but it needs to be effective and functional and be able to stand on its own two feet.

Burnham urges Scottish government to back bringing Glasgow's bus service back under public control

Zubir Ahmed (Lab) asked Burnham if he could do anything to improve the bus service in Glasgow.

Burnham said he had been surprised to learn how expensive bus fares were in Glasgow. He said he thought there were moves to put the buses under public control in the city, but he had been told the Scottish government was not in favour. He said he hoped they would adopt that approach.

A great city like Glasgow should have public transport under its own control. And what I see in Scotland, there is something of the same problem as England, where the capital city has a public transport system that is way beyond what anywhere else has.

Burnham says he has 'open mind' about beefing up elections bill with caps on political donations

Labour’s Stella Creasy said Reform UK has raised £9m in donations this year, with £8m coming from just three UK-based donors. She went on:

Chequebooks are already circling the next general election, and if we wish to defend our democracy and make sure that all voices can be heard equally, we need to make sure it’s not money that talks.

She urged Burnham to accept amendments to the representation of the people bill that would put caps on how much individuals and companies can donate to political parties.

Burnham said that bill as drafted (which only caps the amount that can be given by donors living abroad) was just a start. He went on:

I don’t think anyone on the front bench is saying it’s the end of this matter. We do need to go further.

I would encourage [Creasy] to engage with the Defending Democracy taskforce.

And certainly I have an open mind about what those further measures might be.

Burnham says he '100%' backs getting social media companies to remove non-consensual explict content more quickly

Wera Hobhouse (Lib Dem) asked about women secretly being filmed on nights out. She said:

In many towns and cities across the country, women are secretly filmed on a night out and the videos are posted online with vile, degrading, misogynistic contents and comments.

And the women only find out once these videos have been shared 100,000 times and more, and they do not know what to do and we need to change something.

Will the prime minister support the campaign to end this vile practice, including looking at reform to make misogyny a hate crime?

Burnham said, as the father of two daughters, this issue was very much at the forefront of his mind. He said he had recently met Georgia Harrison, who was doing “amazing” campaigning in this area.

He said Harrison wanted social media companies to remove non-consensual explicit content immediately, not just within 48 hours, which is the current deadline. A 48-hour deadline was“totally unsatisfactory”, Burnham said. He said he would support changing that “100%”.

Burnham says E1 settlement on West Bank could kill off prospects for two-state solution for Israel and Palestine

Debbie Abrahams (Lab) asked Burnham would he would do to stop the building of illegal settlements on the West Bank.

Burnham said the plans for the E1 settlement were particularly worrying. They would “lead to decisions being made before the Israeli election which, as I understand it, could imperil, if not end, the two-state solution”.

So these matters are urgent. They do need to be confronted. And I believe we will be updating the house soon on those matters. [See 3.04pm.]

Burnham tells Reform UK's Richard Tice he's wrong to think it's either/or with fossil fuels and renewables

Richard Tice, the Reform UK deputy leader, asked when the government would approve the applications for the Jackdaw and Rosebank gas and oil fields in the North Sea. He repeated his claim about the rush to net zero being “stupid”.

Burnham said Tice needed a better grasp of the facts. He explained:

In Greater Manchester it costs £30 to charge a bus with electric charge, £200 plus to fill a tank of diesel. He really does need to look into the facts a little bit more before he makes statements like that in this house.

Solar panels on people’s homes can bring down the cost of their energy and it.

And, yes, we need to be pragmatic. We will still need to rely on oil and gas for some years to come.

The mistake [Tice] is making is saying it’s got to be one or the other for political purposes. The truth is, it’s going to have to be both.
