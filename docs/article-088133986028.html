Young survivor of catastrophic flooding in Nepal reunites with his mother — who feared her 2 kids were dead

2026-08-28 23:00

A young survivor of the deadly flooding in Nepal was reunited Friday with his mother — who had feared both her kids were killed in the horrifying devastation.

Matti Maya Tamang, 28, had desperately searched for her two missing children among the nearly 2,000 people that remain missing — including at least 90 Americans — after catastrophic floods swallowed up Central Nepal’s Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts Wednesday morning.

“I had come to accept that both my children were no more,” the tormented mom said.

“All I could do was weep. I couldn’t hold myself together at all.”

Her eldest son, 8-year-old Zoyal Ghale had been studying at school at the time of the flooding which resulted from a massive chunk of ice snapping off a glacier in the Himalayas that overran the Lendhe River.

Suddenly, “it was so, so black,” he recalled.

The boy said he sprinted from the school into the forest with a friend and climbed the trees in Rasuwa to escape.

Ghale survived but suffered a broken leg and lacerations to his face — before the boy was airlifted to Kathmandu on Thursday.

When Ghale met journalist Sahana Bajracharya at a small hospital in Nuwakot around 11 a.m. on Thursday, nobody knew where his family was.

The resourceful journalist was able to contact Tamang and put her in touch with the hospital — and broke the news that her son was alive.

Tamang could barely believe it. “When you told me you knew where Zoyal was, it didn’t sound real,” she recalled about her conversation with Bajracharya.

“I had already given up hope.”

On Friday, Tamang finally reached the hospital where Ghale was transferred — around a two-hour drive from their home.

Ghale’s father, who works as a construction worker in Japan, was on his way as well, Tamang said.

She later learned her other child was also alive.

“After I found the elder one, I felt my life was back,” she said.

When Ghale was asked whether the unimaginable experience had frightened him, the young boy shook his head: “No.”

There are 1,924 people remain missing as of Friday, according to NBC. Around a quarter of those who are currently missing are foreign nationals.

Rescuers are on high alert for more possible flooding from a newly formed lake in the Himalayan mountains near the Tibetan border.

Experts say the lake — triggered by a glacier collapse — has started to overflow.

Nepalese authorities said all government personnel and members of the public assisting in the rescue efforts have been told to remain alert and take immediate action if necessary.

With Post wires.
