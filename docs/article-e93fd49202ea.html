Stocks open lower as U.S. oil tops $100 amid escalating Iran tensions: Live updates

2026-10-23 13:53

Stocks opened lower on Friday as investors remained uneasy following a Big Tech sell-off and as U.S. oil prices topped $100 a barrel amid rising fears that the U.S.-Iran conflict would flare up again.

S&P 500 fell 0.6% and Dow Jones Industrial Average tumbled 521 points, or 1%. Nasdaq pulled back 5%.

Big Tech shares like Microsoft and Meta were trading lower, while chip stocks were rebounding slightly, with Micron Technology and Advanced Micro Devices ticking up more than 1%. On Thursday, many technology stocks pulled back as traders assessed the impact of outsized corporate borrowing and rising rates on artificial intelligence and other tech names.

CoreWeave, which has become the poster child for data center companies using aggressive amounts of borrowed money, dropped 10% on Thursday. Its shares slipped less than 1% on Friday.

Ed Yardeni, president of Yardeni Research, described the AI trade on Thursday as a "bubble" but said that some depressurization wouldn't be all bad.

"Taking some air out of the AI bubble is a good thing. I don't think it's going to burst and create problems for the economy," he said, pointing to the bond market as a source of capital competition for equities.

The 10-year Treasury yield added 1 basis point to 4.9697% on Friday. The yield on the 30-year was flat and the 2-year yield fell 2 basis points. The upward moves come after Treasury yields fell sharply Thursday as investors looked to U.S. government bonds as a safe haven.

Oil prices also rose on Friday, further pressuring stocks. West Texas Intermediate futures were up 1.4% to $100.90 per barrel. Brent crude prices rose 0.75% to $114. It follows reports that the U.S. and Israel may be drawing up plans for renewed attacks on Iran as part of the regional war that started in late February.

On the week, the S&P 500 is heading for a slim gain, up less than 0.1%, while the Nasdaq is down 0.5% in the period. The Dow is tracking for a 1.7% advance.
