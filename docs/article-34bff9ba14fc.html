Ex-NFLer Nate Burleson calls out Aaron Rodgers: ‘Be on the sideline’

2026-11-27 15:54

Something was missing from Aaron Rodgers’ now-viral diatribe Wednesday.

A day after the now former Steelers QB ripped into his coach over his unceremonious benching — revealing that Mike McCarthy allegedly slept in team meetings, missed training sessions and referred to the team as the “Packers” — ex-NFL star and “NFL Today” analyst Nate Burleson didn’t hold back on Rodgers.

“I have praised Aaron Rodgers throughout his career. I’m a fan like so many other people. He did not handle this right. I don’t care what anybody says,” said Burleson on the CBS show. “These things about Mike McCarthy, they might be true, but that doesn’t remove him from accountability.

“He talked for a long time. You know what I didn’t hear? I could have played better. And I feel like when you’re a quarterback, I played with some great ones, everyone from Daunte Culpepper to Matt Hasselbeck, even Matt Stafford. When things were on fire, those quarterbacks stepped up and said, ‘I could have played better.’”

Rodgers was officially benched late Tuesday night, seemingly the result of an ultimatum he gave to McCarthy regarding offensive playcalling.

In response to the shocking move, Rodgers made an appearance on “The Pat McAfee Show” on Wednesday and set off an NFL firestorm.

“I did hear him say this, which I appreciate,” Burleson noted. “He said, ‘I love my guys.’ And if indeed you do love your guys, show up for your guys. Be a backup. Your team is about to play. Mason Rudolph is about to take the field. Why not step on the sideline with your high football IQ and help him win the game? If you love your guys like you say you do, be on the sideline. At the very least, when we sign a contract and get paid to do our job, we show up.”

Burleson’s critique is far from the only one.

While McCarthy has taken plenty of slings and arrows for his role in the debacle, some of the biggest names in the game have pointed their fingers squarely at Rodgers.

“If it looks like a duck,” Hall of Famer Howie Long said on Thursday’s “NFL on FOX” in reference to Rodgers being an awful teammate. “He’s a guy that I think picked the wrong sport. He probably should have played tennis. It’s an individual sport.”

On the Fox broadcast of Cowboys-Eagles, seven-time Super Bowl champion QB Tom Brady was displeased by the public nature of it all.

“The sanctity of an NFL locker room, it’s a very sacred place,” he said. “We shouldn’t ever lose track of that as players. It’s a privilege to be in that locker room. You’re fighting with your teammates, you’re fighting with your coaches, you’re always acting as a team together. Shouldn’t always be the place for private things to become public, that’s what I’ll say.”

It was a sentiment echoed by Mike Tomlin, the legendary coach of the Steelers whose last season in Pittsburgh overlapped with Rodgers’ first.

“I had a range of emotions watching those course of events yesterday,” Tomlin said. “I think the prevailing one is just general sadness. Sad that we might be looking at the end of a legendary career of a quarterback … to see laundry aired out that way in a public forum, those are things that kind of run counterculture to professional football.”
