Ariana Grande shades ex-manager Scooter Braun as she reveals why she didn’t feel ‘safe’ to create music

2026-11-25 16:34

Ariana Grande claims she felt unsafe making music with her old team, which included her ex-manager, Scooter Braun.

“I also went through a lot of life changes during ‘Wicked.’ I have a whole new… well, not a whole new, but a lot of my team is different now,” the singer, 33, said on Wednesday’s episode of the “Las Culturistas” podcast.

“I have a lot of trust in my life now.”

Grande explained that she “didn’t feel safe to create” music because of “the relationships that surrounded the creation and the putting out and the releasing of and who I was working with.”

The “Petal” singer said she’s more inspired to do her job with her new team.

“I think that when you have safety around you, your body knows, your instrument knows,” she shared. “And you’re just able to share, to kind of start over. And that’s a really beautiful thing. I didn’t think that creating could feel how it feels now with the collaborators and team and so on.”

Page Six has reached out to Braun’s rep for comment.

Grande’s team also revealed she would be “managed exclusively” by Brandon Creed from Good World Management.

Braun confirmed the news on Instagram at the time, writing, “New chapter but always team Ari :).”

That same month, Braun, whose clients also included Demi Lovato and Justin Bieber, retired from artist management to focus on serving as the CEO of HYBE.

On the podcast, Grande described her latest tour as “such a gorgeously corrective experience.”

“And the energy, being able to take it in this time was such a gift because I was really kind of numbed and in survival mode last time that I toured,” she shared. “I, for a million different reasons, was able to feel it all this time and let it in a different way. It was very special, really emotional.”

Last year, Grande admitted that her old team “was a piece” of why she had a “broken” relationship with her music.

“I think it got away from me in a way I didn’t expect,” she told the New York Times last November. “There’s a thing that comes along with your dreams coming true that feels dangerous at times.”

Grande added, “Until quite recently, it was really hard for me to navigate and I think it stripped a lot of joy out of this for me.”
