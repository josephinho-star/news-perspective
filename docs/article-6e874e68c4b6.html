Discord is making its public communities more searchable, and people have concerns

2026-10-27 21:22

Discord announced on Tuesday it will make some conversations in its public servers searchable and easier to find on the internet. The new update targets conversations in “Discoverable Servers,” public Discord servers that administrators have made eligible to appear in the platform’s Server Discovery feature.

The aim is to help people find useful information that might otherwise remain buried in Discord conversations to a wider audience.

According to Discord, more than 63,000 Discoverable Servers host discussions on everything from gaming strategies and fandoms to language learning and niche interests. By making some of these conversations searchable, Discord hopes to extend the reach of its communities beyond the platform itself.

The move also taps into the appeal of traditional internet forums, where users can find answers by searching through publicly accessible discussions. Gaming communities could draw attention to walkthroughs, developers could reach more people with technical advice, and content creators could attract new audiences without relying solely on Discord’s internal discovery tools.

Starting November 16, every Discoverable Server will have its own web page, which may feature automated summaries of discussions in active public channels. Beginning in mid-December, selected messages from public channels may also appear in search engine results.

Server administrators will be able to choose which channels are included, while individual members can opt out of having their messages made publicly searchable. Published messages will not display usernames or avatars. Private servers, direct messages, and group direct messages are excluded.

The announcement has sparked backlash over privacy. Some users are concerned about exposing conversations to audiences far beyond their original context, arguing that posting in a public Discord server does not necessarily mean users want their messages to be discoverable across the wider internet.

Others have raised concerns about whether these conversations could be used to train AI models. Similar arguments emerged during controversies surrounding Reddit’s data-sharing practices and AI training, when users questioned whether their posts and comments should be used to train AI without their explicit consent or compensation.

Discord says users can manage their preferences in settings, while server administrators retain control over which channels participate. Notably, the option to make messages discoverable is turned off by default in the EU, UK, Canada, Brazil, Australia, and South Korea because of legal restrictions. Teen users are also excluded from the feature.
