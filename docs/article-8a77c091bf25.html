Democrats now favored in key midterm races that could give them control of US Senate, new projections find – US politics live

2026-10-07 00:04

Nonpartisan Cook Political Report revises analyses in Kansas, New Hampshire and North Carolina, forecasting Democrats have stronger odds than previously estimated

US Senate race in Kansas becomes 'toss up' in new forecast that puts control 'squarely in Democrats' sights'

The Cook Political Report has revised its predictions for the November midterm elections, saying “A Senate Majority is Squarely in Democrats’ Sights” as it revises three Senate race estimates in Democrats’ favor.

Cook amended its prediction for the Kansas Senate race from “Lean Republican” to “Toss Up” – a shocker for a state where no Democrat has won a Senate election since 1932.

The elections analysis newsletter also forecasted that New Hamsphire will “Lean Democrat” (from a previous “Toss Up” prediction) and that North Carolina is “Likely Democrat” (from a previous “Lean Democrat”) in Senate races there.

“While Republicans continue to have a financial edge in almost every competitive state, President Donald Trump is a drag across the board even in deep red states due to rising fuel prices and the persistent high cost of living,” editor Jessica Taylor wrote.

“At this point, the question isn’t whether Democrats will gain seats, but how many. As such, we are revising our estimate of potential pickups to two to six seats for Democrats — squarely within the range of the four seats they need to net for a majority,” she added.

A nonpartisan coalition of former Nasa astronauts has denounced Donald Trump’s comments at a campaign rally yesterday that Iran could “take out” Los Angeles and San Diego.

In a statement posted to social media, Astronauts For America wrote: “No American city and no American citizen should ever be spoken of as an acceptable price to pay in a conflict with a foreign adversary.”

It added that it called “on elected leaders of both parties to affirm their shared responsibility to protect all Americans.”

Donald Trump said on Tuesday his administration was considering suspending the federal gasoline tax, a sign the president is noticing voter anger over fuel costs a month before the midterm elections.

“We’re thinking about that,” Trump said when asked whether the tax should be suspended.

Any suspension would require an act of Congress. The president cannot unilaterally suspend the federal tax.

Trump first raised the idea in May, as the Iran war pushed fuel prices sharply higher. Pump prices have stayed high since. The national average for regular was $4.3685 a gallon on Tuesday, according to AAA, more than a dollar above where it stood a year ago.

The federal tax is 18.4¢ a gallon on gasoline and 24.4¢ on diesel. Dropping it would cut about 4% from the price of a gallon of regular. The tax also brings in more than $23bn a year for highways and public transit.

In an interview with Reuters, JD Vance said he had not decided whether he would seek the presidency in 2028 and that Iran must make a “meaningful” reduction in its nuclear enrichment capacity.

“I think if they want to show a commitment to not building a nuclear weapon, they would do something meaningful on their enrichment capacity,” Vance said, speaking to the news outlet during a campaign appearance in Alaska.

The vice-president added, “One of the things we picked up on is it’s not totally clear how their country makes decisions.”

Speaking about whether he would run to replace Donald Trump when the president’s term expires, Vance said: “I’m inherently a procrastinator,” adding, “I really haven’t thought about it much.“

A former CIA official has pleaded guilty to a charge that he stole hundreds of gold bars worth more than $40m from the US spy agency and hid them in his basement.

David J. Rush pleaded guilty to one count of wire fraud during a hearing in Alexandria, Virginia today. His sentencing hearing is scheduled for 28 January.

Lawyers for Disney accused the Trump administration of “blatant violations of the US constitution” and censorship on Tuesday in the first court hearing over the Federal Communications Commission’s early review of the media giant’s eight local television station licenses.

District judge Loren AliKhan seemed unswayed by government arguments that the order to force Disney’s ABC to apply years early to renew local television station licenses was tied to an investigation into parent company Disney’s hiring practices.

ABC, Disney and its station group filed suit against the FCC in August, seeking to block the US media regulator from taking any further steps to punish or pressure the network in connection to the order.

In its lawsuit, ABC argued that the FCC’s actions – taken at the behest of the Donald Trump-appointed chair, Brendan Carr – were actually intended to chill the network’s speech, which the president and Carr have argued is tilted against the administration.

Chris Murphy, the Democratic senator from Connecticut, said the Pentagon denied him access to a US military base during a visit to the Middle East. Murphy had intended to visit the Al Udeid airbase, which has been repeatedly attacked during the US war in Iran.
