Whistleblower alleges Trump ordered FBI to investigate protesters in LA over immigration crackdown

Donald Trump ordered the FBI to investigate protesters in Los Angeles who were demonstrating against the president’s immigration crackdown, according to a whistleblower complaint that was submitted to the Senate judiciary committee.

Senator Alex Padilla, a Democrat who represents California and serves on the committee, said his office had received a copy of the complaint.

“These allegations are deeply alarming,” Padilla, who also serves as the top Democrat on the border security and immigration subcommittee, said in a statement. “Using the FBI to target Americans for exercising their first amendment rights directly undermines our nation’s foundational values. I won’t rest until we get answers.”

NBC first reported on the allegations after viewing a copy of the complaint, which identifies the whistleblower as Jill Fields, a former supervisory intelligence analyst in the FBI’s Los Angeles Field Office, who made the complaint following the federal response to anti-ICE demonstrations around the city’s federal buildings, and in residential area, early last year. Fields ultimately resigned from the bureau last year.

According to NBC, Fields alleges that Emil Bove, then the acting deputy attorney general, told the head of the field office to open an investigation into activities outside the federal building even though Fields says the activities were protected free speech. Fields also says the White House gave a directive to FBI Director Kash Patel to investigate a group of protesters using megaphones during immigration enforcement operations after agents had initially declined to open an investigation.

Per NBC, Fields also claims that agents were taken agents based in Los Angeles were taken off active cartel cases to carry out the White House’s requests.

Rubio urges Moscow to share more information about lab technician death over suspected pneumonic plague

Secretary of state Marco Rubio urged Moscow on Tuesday to release more details about the case of a lab technician in Siberia, who died after a suspected case of pneumonic plague.

Darya Shipilova, a 28-year-old employee at the anti-plague institute in the city of Irkutsk, became ill at work. She died two days later on the night of 2 October at a regional hospital in the nearby town of Shelekhov.

It remains unclear how she became unwell. Unconfirmed reports in Russian media have suggested Shipilova may have been infected after a mishap at the laboratory. Officials have not publicly confirmed that account.

“I think it’s incumbent upon Russia to obviously share more information with the world,” Rubio told reporters in Reykjavik. “We’re watching it very closely, hour by hour.”

Donald Trump had said on Monday that his administration was willing to offer assistance. “I hate to see them having it. We’ll help. Everybody who has that kind of a problem, we’ll always help,” he told reporters at the White House when asked what Washington was going to do about the risk of plague coming from Russia.

Christian Lindmeier, a WHO spokesperson, said on Tuesday that there was no sign of a wider outbreak. “All of the patients’ contacts have reportedly been identified and are being monitored for illness, and none to date have shown symptoms of illness,” he said.

California leaders are furious at Trump over his remarks at a Nebraska campaign rally.

Trump is “very intentional about his comments, and especially about targeting Los Angeles,” González said at a campaign event in Koreatown for Xavier Becerra, the Democratic gubernatorial nominee. “He’s been targeting Los Angeles and Southern California since his presidency started on Day One, treating it like a political playground. When you think about ICE, when you think about the policies that he’s carried out or the things that he has said.”

“Using Los Angeles as your collateral damage is un-American. It’s unpatriotic and it’s disgusting,” he said.

Congressman Pete Aguilar, a California Democrat and the House caucus chair, said the president was “just trying to overcompensate for this reckless and irresponsible war” that he started without congressional authorization.

“He might think it’s a joke, but to his supporters, he’s not joking, right?” Aguilar said at the Becerra event. “He wasn’t joking when he told folks to overrun the Capitol either. It’s incredibly dangerous rhetoric. You shouldn’t be making any jokes about giving up California, giving up Los Angeles and San Diego.”

Kilmar Ábrego García expected back in court in as Trump administration continues attempt to deport him to Liberia

Kilmar Ábrego García is expected back in court today as the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) continues its battle to carry out his deportation to Liberia.

The DHS is seeking to vacate a preliminary injunction put in place by US District Judge Paula Xinis, preventing U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement from detaining and deporting Ábrego Garcia.

The Trump administration has asked a federal appeals court to reinstate criminal charges against Kilmar Ábrego García that were dismissed in May by a judge who said the prosecution of the man who unwittingly became a symbol of the White House’s mass deportation campaign was “vindictive in nature”.

In a filing to the US sixth circuit court of appeals, the justice department insisted that human smuggling allegations against Ábrego are genuine – and not retaliation for challenging his wrongful deportation to El Salvador in March 2025.

Ábrego García’s case grew national attention after Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officials admitted to his deportation as an error during the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown.

US justice department moves to strip citizenship of 40 naturalized citizens

Donald Trump’s justice department has moved to strip the citizenship of 40 naturalized citizens accused of crimes in what authorities describe as “the largest” such initiative over a single period during his administration.

US justice department officials on Monday announced the filing of “denaturalization actions” against more than three dozen people alleged to have committed such offenses as “acting as an agent for a foreign government, committing voluntary manslaughter, and aggravated sodomy against a special‑needs man”.

The denaturalization requests in question were submitted in multiple federal courts between 21 August and 2 October, according to the justice department.

The federal Immigration and Nationality Act allows for naturalized US citizenship to be revoked by a federal court if the government proves that it was illegally obtained through concealment of a material fact or willful misrepresentation.

Lawyers for Olympian in dismissed reflecting pool damage case seek inspectors general investigation

Lawyers for the former Olympic canoeist David Hearn, who was charged and dismissed over damage to the Lincoln Memorial reflecting pool, are urging two government watchdogs to examine what they say was a flawed investigation.

Hearn’s lawyers said in a letter to the inspectors general for the departments of Justice and Interior that watchdog investigations were needed “to determine what happened inside their agencies that led to the shocking and indefensible treatment” of their client.

Judge Todd Edelman, of the DC superior court, dismissed the case days ago, siding with Hearn’s lawyers over the office of the US attorney Jeanine Pirro, which had wanted to keep the option of refiling.

Pirro, a Donald Trump ally, publicized the charges at a press conference in July. Later that month her office moved to drop the case, conceding that the damage resulted from flawed installation rather than anything Hearn did.

The New York governor, Kathy Hochul, declared a state of emergency on Monday in response to a dramatic spike in measles cases in the state.

Hochul’s administration said the emergency declaration was in response to a “growing number of measles cases across rural areas of New York and neighboring states”, including Pennsylvania, which has been grappling with a deadly outbreak that is its largest in more than three decades.

The declaration expands the pool of people who can administer vaccines against measles and allows more individuals to order testing.

It comes as there have been 108 confirmed cases of measles in New York so far for the year, up from just one reported case in 2023. In Pennsylvania, which neighbors New York, five people have died after contracting the disease, and there have been 198 hospitalizations.

The Trump administration health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, has been criticized for his response to the measles outbreak. Before his appointment as health secretary, Kennedy was a noted anti-vaccine activist.

Kennedy headlined an anti-vaccine conference in Washington DC in mid-September, as measles cases surged. He told the crowd there that they had a “strong and steadfast friend at the White House”.

Meanwhile, the Pennsylvania governor, Josh Shapiro, has called Kennedy “incompetent”, as the health secretary has publicly questioned whether the state’s recorded fatalities were directly caused by measles. The CDC – which Kennedy oversees – currently lists two measles deaths in the US this year.
