Stocks making the biggest moves midday: SoFi Technologies, Pentair, Royal Caribbean, JetBlue & more

2026-10-27 17:02

Here are some of the companies making headlines in midday trading. Pentair — Shares of the water treatment company leapt 13% after Pentair lifted full-year revenue guidance. Pentair now sees total sales down about 3%, compared to an earlier forecast of 4%-7% decline. Third-quarter results beat on the top and bottom lines. SoFi Technologies — The financial services company jumped 15% after SoFi raised full-year guidance. The company sees revenue totaling $5.04 billion, up from an earlier forecast of $4.75 billion to $4.85 billion, and above the FactSet consensus call for $4.87 billion. SoFi also raised expectations for adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Dell Technologies — The computer and server maker gained 8% after announcing an expansion of its cybersecurity capabilities across artificial intelligence environments. Features include Dell's Resilience Operations for AI service and the PowerProtect AI assistant. AI Infrastructure — Companies providing the backbone of the artificial intelligence build-out climbed Tuesday, helped by improved sentiment after a preliminary filing by Anthropic on Monday to go public and a separate agreement by a group of lenders to finance the purchase of $63 billion in Broadcom technology by Anthropic. Nebius Group surged 11%, Western Digital and Bloom Energy rallied 8% and Astera Labs advanced 9%. Xylem – Shares of the global water tech company sank almost 12%. Xylem cut full-year guidance and now sees adjusted earnings in a range of $5.40 to $5.45 per share, versus an earlier call for $5.55 to $5.70 a share. Profit and revenue in the third quarter also missed the Street's estimates, according to FactSet data. PayPal — Shares rose 2% after the digital payments platform posted third-quarter, bottom-line results that beat estimates. Adjusted earnings of $1.45 per share topped the LSEG consensus estimate of $1.31 per share, and PayPal also lifted its full-year profit forecast. United Parcel Service — The package delivery company gained 3% after reporting third-quarter results that beat Wall Street expectations and raising full-year guidance. UPS projects full-year consolidated revenue of approximately $92.6 billion, up from previous expectations of $91.2 billion, and fourth-quarter revenue of $25.7 billion. Nvidia , Meta Platforms — Nvidia said it will invest $3 billion in Meta . The news came after the social media and artificial intelligence giant on Monday announced plans to raise up to $95 billion to support investments in AI infrastructure. Shares of Nvidia gained 1%, while Meta Platforms dipped 0.8%. Royal Caribbean — Shares advanced 8% after RCL reported third-quarter earnings that exceeded analyst expectations. The cruise line operator earned $6.57 per share, excluding certain items, topping the LSEG consensus forecast of $6.38 per share. The company also raised full-year earnings guidance for 2026. Boeing — The aerospace company slid 9% after reporting mixed financials for the third quarter . While Boeing posted a wider-than-expected loss, revenue came in above the LSEG consensus estimate. Whirlpool — The appliance maker lost 2% after reporting disappointing earnings and guidance. Whirlpool lost an adjusted 21 cents per share on revenue of $3.68 billion in the third quarter. Analysts were expecting earnings of 80 cents per share on revenue of $3.92 billion, according to LSEG. Whirlpool also cut full-year revenue guidance to $14.5 billion from $15.0 billion. JetBlue Airways — The carrier climbed more than 20% after JetBlue said it expects wider fourth quarter margins even as fuel prices are anticipated to rise 85% above year-earlier levels. JetBlue reported an adjusted loss of 60 cents a share in the third quarter, compared to the 55-cent loss that analysts polled by LSEG had estimated. Revenue narrowly missed expectations, as well. Celestica — The supply chain management company surged 12% after Celestica raised full-year guidance. The company anticipates adjusted earnings of $11.55 per share, up from an earlier forecast of $11.30 a share, above the FactSet consensus forecast for $11.16. Cadence Design Systems — The electronic systems design company rose 8% after third-quarter results surpassed estimates and Cadence raised its full-year revenue growth outlook. Cadence earned $2.11 per share on an adjusted basis and posted revenue of $1.63 billion, while analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected $2.05 per share on $1.61 billion. — CNBC's Scott Schnipper, Ananya Chetia, Michelle Fox, Davis Giangiulio, Fred Imbert, Paulina Likos, Amelia Lucas, Darla Mercado, Laya Neelakandan and Kai Nicol-Schwarz contributed reporting
